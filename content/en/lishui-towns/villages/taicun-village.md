## Traditional village listing

In its reply of 28 November 2023 the Lishui District Culture and Tourism Bureau states: "Tai Village in Jingqiao, Luoshan in Hefeng, Yangjia Village in Sunjiaxiang and two others, five villages in all, entered the sixth batch of the traditional village list of Jiangsu." This entry rests on the working account in that reply; the formal document for the sixth batch is not held in this library.

## Read against the current divisions

The district government's administrative divisions page records that Jingqiao Town has 5 residents' committees and 7 villagers' committees, the latter being Shuijing, Dacun, Zhishan, Yunheshan, Kongjia, Xiantan and Tai Village, with Tai Village last of the seven. This agrees with the account of November 2023: Tai Village is both an administrative unit of Jingqiao Town and a traditional village entered in the sixth batch in its own name, with the two sources using the same form of the name and no variant appearing.

The reply names only three of the five villages, so the other two remain unnamed and the list cannot be completed from it.

## Cultural clues at village level

Media reporting supplies further cultural clues at village level for Tai Village. A report of 17 June 2025 by Xinhua Daily Media Network (Jiaohuidian News) records that Tai Village lies on the boundary between Lishui and Gaochun and calls it a "thousand-year old village"; that its lower street was formed in the Hongwu reign of the Ming dynasty, is about a thousand metres long, and has more than ten old houses of grey brick and grey tile on either side, of which five have been assessed as Nanjing historic buildings; and that there are several Ming and Qing wells on the old street whose water still remains. The report also records that Tai Village formerly had such scenes of the "eight sights of Changshan" as "drawing spring water at Shiquan" and "sitting and talking at Jigu", and that the village now builds such "ten new scenes of Tai Village" as "spring lingering at Huiqu", "the century-old street", "sitting and talking at Jigu" and "recalling Yuecheng" on the basis of the old ones.

That report is a second-hand local media source of moderate authority; this entry takes only its checkable material observations, the length of the old street, the number of old houses and historic building assessments, and the Ming and Qing wells, and does not draw conclusions from its rhetorical expressions. "Thousand-year old village" and "formed in the Hongwu reign of the Ming" are the report's own expressions, reproduced and marked as such rather than used as precise dates; the report names only two of the "eight sights of Changshan", so the full list must not be completed from it.

A further official indication can be set against this: in the public notice of 3 June 2026 of the fifth batch of district place-name cultural heritage, "Yuecheng Site" appears under the Jingqiao Town row. That document attaches the name to the town without specifying the village, and this library holds no statement showing whether it is the same as the anti-Jin earthwork known locally as Yuecheng within Tai Village recorded in the report; the two stand side by side and are not merged. That document is a draft seeking comment and shows only that the place name remains in use.

## Points of doubt

- The reply names only three villages while giving a total of five, leaving two unnamed; the list must not be completed on this basis.
- The date of founding, the origin of the name and surviving structures are not recorded in the sources used here; the report's "thousand-year old village" and "formed in the Hongwu reign of the Ming" are its own expressions and are not precise dates.
- The formal document for the sixth batch is not held in this library, so the batch year rests on the timing of the reply of November 2023.
- The present state of the old street and the wells of Tai Village comes from local media reporting; the assessment documents for the "five Nanjing historic buildings" are not held in this library, so those figures are reproduced as the report's own wording.
- "Yuecheng Site" in the district place-name cultural heritage notice is attached to Jingqiao Town without specifying the village; whether it is the same as "Yuecheng" within Tai Village is not stated in this library, and they are not merged on that basis.
- The report names only two of the "eight sights of Changshan"; the complete list of eight is not held here.
