## Traditional village listing

In its reply of 28 November 2023 the Lishui District Culture and Tourism Bureau records that Luoshan entered the sixth batch of traditional villages of Jiangsu together with Tai Village in Jingqiao, Yangjia Village in Sunjiaxiang and two others, five villages in all. This entry rests on that reply's working account.

## Read against the current divisions

The district government's administrative divisions page records that Hefeng Town has 6 residents' committees and 6 villagers' committees, the latter being Sunjiaxiang, Zhangjia, Luoshan, Shenjiashan, Wucunqiao and Zhongyang, with Luoshan among them. Luoshan, Tai Village in Jingqiao and Putang in Honglan share a pattern: each is both a villagers' committee of its town and a traditional village entered in the list in its own name.

At Luoshan the reply and the administrative divisions page use the same form of the name, so the two sources corroborate each other without any mismatch of level. This differs from Sunjiaxiang Yangjia Village in the same reply, which is a settlement inside an administrative village rather than a committee in its own right.

## A lead from the village's intangible heritage

The Luoshan dragon dance is an intangible heritage item of this village. A report by the district media centre of 14 January 2026 says the Luoshan dragon dance "originated in the Ming dynasty and has a history of more than 400 years", consistent with the "more than four hundred years of history" on the national-level project page. Both refer to the age of the dragon dance's transmission, not to the date the village was founded; the village's own founding date is not recorded in the sources used here and the two must not be conflated.

## Division of work with other sites

The grade, safeguarding body and bearers of the Luoshan dragon dance belong to the culture site of this group. This entry records only the village itself and its place on the list.

## Points of doubt

- The formal document for the sixth batch is not held in this library.
- The date of founding, the origin of the name and surviving structures are not recorded in the sources used here; the Ming-dynasty origin of the dragon dance is not the founding date of the village.
- The selecting body and batch of the dragon dance's inclusion among provincial best-practice cases could not be verified, so nothing is written on it here.
