## Three clues

| Village | Subdistrict or town | Ancestral halls | As reported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cangkou | Honglan Subdistrict, Tangxi Village | Rui, Fan and Qiu family halls | "a century old" |
| Shishanxia | Jingqiao Town, Ruijia Village | Liu family hall | built in 1820, listed as a municipal protected relic in 2006 |
| Zhujia | Hefeng Town, Zhangjia Village | Zhu family hall | built some four hundred years ago, three bays by three, 638 sq m |

All three follow one pattern: a settlement dominated by one or two surnames, with the ancestral hall of that surname surviving. Cangkou has three halls for three surnames, Rui, Fan and Qiu; Shishanxia and Zhujia have one each, for the Liu and the Zhu.

## Basis and limits

Both sources are second-hand press reports: the Yangtse Evening Post report carries no publication date, and The Paper report is a local news item of 8 April 2020. Therefore:

- "A century old" and "some four hundred years ago" are the reports' own wording, approximations that this entry does not use as datings.
- The 1820 construction and the 2006 listing of the Liu family hall at Shishanxia are more specific but still come from press reports; the formal documents are not held in this library.
- Whether the villages hold ancestral halls not mentioned in the reports is not recorded in the sources used here.

## Division of work

The form, dimensions, protection grade and publication batch of these halls belong to the history site of this group. This entry offers only a cross-village summary; it neither repeats what the individual village entries already say nor stands in for the history site's treatment of the buildings.

## Points of doubt

- None of the three halls has a first-hand basis such as an inscription or genealogy for its date of construction; a fuller account awaits sources.
- When the three surnames at Cangkou, the Liu at Shishanxia and the Zhu at Zhujia moved into these villages, and from where, is not recorded in the sources used here.
