## How to read the table

Years are given as Western years, calculated from the first year of each reign period; the correspondence between the sexagenary dates and the Western years follows the gazetteer's own text. The reign-year column follows the gazetteer's wording. The basis column gives short source labels: "Kangxi gazetteer, Annals" means Volume One (Annals) of the Kangxi edition of the *Lishui County Gazetteer*; "District government, Historical Evolution" means the "Historical Evolution" page of the Lishui District government website; and "District government, Administrative Divisions" means that site's "Administrative Divisions" page. Where more than one change falls in the same year, the rows are given separately; where a source is silent, the cell is left blank and noted.

## Not in the table

- The Republican period: not recorded in the sources held, and treated in "Lishui in the Republican Period".
- The changes of jurisdiction between 1949 and 1983: not recorded in the sources held.
- Administrative units earlier than the Sui: the gazetteer records that Liyang County was established under the Qin and placed under Zhang Commandery, and that it remained under Danyang Commandery after the Han set that commandery up. All of these precede 591, the starting point of this table, and belong to the entries in the chronological record.

## Uncertainties

First, on the transfer of Lishui to Shengzhou in 890, the annalistic volume cites an earlier gazetteer to the effect that the *Junji* does not record it; the table follows the annalistic volume and adds the note.

Second, the circuit to which Lishui belonged when it was raised to a prefecture in 1295 is not recorded in the annalistic volume; the district government writes Jiqing Circuit, but the name Jiqing dates only from 1329. The table sets the two side by side; see "Lishui under the Song and Yuan".

Third, for the conversion of towns into subdistricts in 2015 the only evidence is a passing remark in the 2018 reply, which is entered here with its nature noted.

Fourth, the Western years are calculated from the first year of each reign period — the fourth year of Daye, for instance, being 608 — while the correspondence of the sexagenary dates to Western years follows the gazetteer's own text; where the two differ, the gazetteer governs.
