## Scope and method

This selection takes the late Ming and early Qing part of the annalistic volume, from the building of a pagoda in 1606 to the imperial order of 1658 commuting the grain tax. The dates in this stretch cross the Ming-Qing transition, and the gazetteer's wording changes with them: the Ming years are given by era name, while the Qing years are prefixed "Great Qing".

The method is that of the four preceding selections: a list of entries first, then six reproduced in full with notes. The six chosen here follow the thread of the grain tax commutation, with one entry from the change of dynasty.

## The quotations and notes

### 34th year of the Wanli era (1606): a pagoda to suppress the light

> 三十四年丙午，正月二夜，县西北隅有赤光一道，直抵东南。知县徐良彦即其地建浮图以镇之。详《永寿寺记》。
>
> *Translated:* In the thirty-fourth year, bingwu, on the second night of the first month, a band of red light ran from the northwestern corner of the county town straight to the southeast. The zhixian (county magistrate) Xu Liangyan built a pagoda on that very spot to suppress it. See the Record of Yongshou Temple for details.

**Note.** This entry ties an anomalous sighting to a building in the same year and links the two with the words "on that very spot". Anomaly records are the gazetteer's own idiom; this site reproduces the text as it stands but treats the red light and the pagoda separately. The pagoda's existence and date rest on the heritage inventory, while the red light remains only what the gazetteer recorded. The pagoda built is the present Yongshousi Pagoda.

### 2nd year of the Tianqi era (1622): the grain tax commuted in perpetuity

> 二年壬戌，应天府尹徐必达据知县张锡命详文，移抚院王象恒、按院易应昌会题，准溧水漕米永远改折，［阖］邑戴之。事载《改折全书》。
>
> *Translated:* In the second year, renxu, Xu Bida, prefect of Yingtian Prefecture, acting on the detailed report of the zhixian Zhang Ximing, referred the matter to the grand coordinator Wang Xiangheng and the censor Yi Yingchang, who jointly memorialised; the grain tax at Lishui was approved for commutation in perpetuity, and the whole county was grateful. The matter is recorded in the Complete Book of Commutation.

**Note.** This is the high point of the county's tax thread in the Ming. Through the magistrate's detailed report, the prefect's referral, and the joint memorial of the grand coordinator and the censor, the grain tax was approved for commutation in perpetuity. The gazetteer closes with "the whole county was grateful" and notes that the matter is recorded in the Complete Book of Commutation. Commutation meant assessing the grain tax in silver; against the transport of grain in kind it saved the cost of carriage, and it was therefore what the county wanted.

### 5th year of the Tianqi era (1625): the commutation is never restored

> 五年乙丑，二月十九日，地震。五月十七日，大风雨，昼如晦。秋旱。十一月二十五夜，地震有声。是年京储告匮，天下改折地方暂借漕米一年。溧水自是不复改折。相沿。
>
> *Translated:* In the fifth year, yichou, on the nineteenth day of the second month, an earthquake. On the seventeenth day of the fifth month, a great storm, and the day was as dark as night. Drought in autumn. On the night of the twenty-fifth day of the eleventh month, an earthquake with sound. That year the capital granary was exhausted, and everywhere that had commutation had a year's grain tax borrowed back. From this time Lishui was never commuted again, and so it continued.

**Note.** This entry records the turning point of the commutation. Granted "in perpetuity" only in 1622, in 1625 the capital granary was empty and a year's grain was "borrowed", and the gazetteer goes on: "from this time Lishui was never commuted again" — the loan became the rule. The gazetteer ends with "and so it continued". In 1644 the local officials petitioned again, but "the dynasty fell before it was granted and the old levy resumed", and only in 1658 did the imperial order commute it. Three reversals, thirty-six years from first to last.

### 13th year of the Chongzhen era (1640): unprecedented famine

> 十三年庚辰，溧水奇荒，绝禾种。拾月十二夜，地震有声。诏自十二年以前民所逋欠粮税悉行蠲免。
>
> *Translated:* In the thirteenth year, gengchen, Lishui suffered an unprecedented famine, and no seed grain was left. On the night of the twelfth day of the tenth month, an earthquake with sound. An edict remitted in full all arrears of grain tax owed by the people from before the twelfth year.

**Note.** This entry records the worst famine of the late Ming; "no seed grain left" means that not even seed remained. The gazetteer puts the earthquake and the remission under the same entry. The remission covers arrears from before 1639, not the current year's tax, and the distinction is clear in the gazetteer's wording; it is reproduced as it stands.

### 2nd year of the Shunzhi era (1645): the whole town surrendered

> 大清顺治二年乙酉，大兵渡江南，溧水知县王观瀛弃官去之，典史方至道率民众以全城降。
>
> *Translated:* In the second year of the Shunzhi era of the Great Qing, yiyou, the great army crossed to the south of the river; the zhixian of Lishui, Wang Guanying, abandoned his office and left, and the dianshi (registrar) Fang Zhidao led the people out and surrendered the whole town.

**Note.** This is the key entry for the county at the change of dynasty: the magistrate abandons office, the registrar leads the people out and surrenders, and the town escapes destruction. Under the same entry the gazetteer goes on to record the proclamation of the general commanding the southern expedition that the people shave their heads, the withdrawal of the garrison, the remission of seven tenths of two taxes, and, in the twelfth month, local bandits scaling the wall and burning the county hall and the treasury, the loyal man Liu Qian dying in the fighting, and troops sent by the Grand Secretariat to retake the town. The records of the transition are tangled; the entries on this site separate what can be verified, and because the record is so brief the confidence is set at medium.

### 12th year of the Shunzhi era (1655): the Register of Equalised Households and Corvée

> 十二年乙未，时值审编，知县闵派鲁执法矢公，迎邑城隍神于县堂，与共莅厥事。详核里甲，分拨差徭，裒多益寡，务使相均。勒成一书曰《均甲平徭册》，锓板传布通邑，豪户不得避役，奸民无由变更，百姓乐之。
>
> *Translated:* In the twelfth year, yimei, at the time of the review of the registers, the zhixian Min Pailu enforced the law with impartiality, received the city god of the county in the county hall and sat with him over the business. Households were reviewed in detail, corvée apportioned, the surplus taken to fill the shortfall, all to make the shares equal. A book was drawn up, called the Register of Equalised Households and Corvée, cut on blocks and circulated throughout the county, so that no powerful household could evade service and no cunning man could alter the register, and the common people were glad of it.

**Note.** This entry records a review of taxation and corvée in the early Qing, whose outcome was the Register of Equalised Households and Corvée. The gazetteer gives the method as "households reviewed in detail, corvée apportioned, the surplus taken to fill the shortfall, so that all were equal", and adds that it was "cut on blocks and circulated throughout the county" — printed and put into circulation everywhere, so that "no powerful household could evade service". The same entry records that the magistrate "received the city god in the county hall and sat with him over the business", a ceremony of the time reproduced here without comment.

## Source limits and search scope

This selection is drawn from a single source, the first juan of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, the annalistic record, and is taken from a second-hand transcription in an online database, not a photographic copy of the original; the text, dates, personal names and the compilers' own notes are reproduced as the gazetteer has them, with nothing rewritten and nothing supplied that the gazetteer does not record. For the background of the grain-tax commutation and of early Qing taxation, the *History of Ming*, "Food and Money II", the study of early Qing ward organisation, and this site's article on taxation (see "Taxation and Corvée in Ming and Qing Lishui") are available; the selection here remains a quotation and is not used to alter the text of the annals.

Layers searched without result, for this span: official web pages (the district government's "Historical Development" and "Scenic Sites") and the published heritage inventories (layer two) record only remains such as the Yongshousi Pagoda, which have nothing to do with these entries on grain transport and anomalies; the academic databases (CNKI, Wanfang, VIP) and the full-text repositories (the National Library's read.nlc.cn and others) yielded no study devoted to the late Ming and early Qing entries of the annals one by one, nor any photographic copy of the gazetteer for checking; the media have nothing to do with this span; the map-survey layer does not apply. All these are "not found in this library's searches", not proof that nothing exists.

## Choices and uncertainties

First, this selection stops at 1658. The Kangxi gazetteer's annalistic volume has entries after that date, but they fall outside this batch and are treated separately.

Second, anomaly records such as the red light, the rice flying from the granary and the calf born with an extra hoof are numerous in the gazetteer. This selection reproduces them as they stand, neither as established fact nor with any scientific explanation.

Third, the records of the change of dynasty, in 1645 and the years after, are tangled and thin in detail, and touch on warfare, the head-shaving proclamation, the garrison and the militia; they belong in a separate treatment. Only the most important entry is quoted here.

Fourth, the three reversals of the grain tax commutation — granted in 1622, borrowed back in 1625, petitioned for in 1644, ordered in 1658 — lie scattered under four entries in the gazetteer. The notes here string them together; the text itself is reproduced entry by entry and not conflated.
