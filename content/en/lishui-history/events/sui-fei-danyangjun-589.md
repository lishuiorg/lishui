## Background

Before the Sui dynasty this area was long part of Liyang County, under Danyang Commandery. In the ninth year of the Kaihuang era the gazetteer records that Danyang Commandery was abolished and Jiangzhou established, with Liyang placed under it — that is, the commandery was replaced by a prefecture as the unit above the county.

## Time and place

The gazetteer records:

> 隋高祖开皇九年己酉，废丹阳郡置蒋州，溧［阳］隶之。
>
> *Translated:* In the ninth year of the Kaihuang era of Emperor Gaozu of Sui, a *jiyou* year, Danyang Commandery was abolished and Jiangzhou was established, and Li[yang] was placed under it.

A character is missing after "Li" in the text, and the old gazetteer supplies "yang" in brackets, giving "Liyang was placed under it". The ninth year of the Kaihuang era is 589 CE.

## The standard history

The *Suishu*, juan 31, "Treatise on Geography, Part 3," entry on Danyang Commandery, records the administration of Jiangnan after the Sui conquest of Chen, and corroborates this entry:

> 丹陽郡（自東晉已後置郡曰揚州。平陳，詔並平蕩耕墾，更於石頭城置蔣州），統縣三，戶二萬四千一百二十五。
>
> *Translated:* Danyang Commandery (from the Eastern Jin onward the commandery here was called Yangzhou. After the conquest of Chen, an edict ordered the city razed to farmland, and Jiangzhou was set up at Shitoucheng instead), with three subordinate counties and 24,125 households.

The treatise's "Jiangzhou was set up at Shitoucheng" agrees with the gazetteer's "Danyang Commandery was abolished and Jiangzhou established". Fudan's CHGIS, citing the *Zizhi Tongjian*, juan 177, the *Suishu* and the *Yudi Jisheng*, dates Jiangzhou to the ninth year of Kaihuang (589), seated at Shitoucheng and governing the five counties of Jiangning, Dangtu, Lishui (from 591), Liyang and Yanling, until it became Danyang Commandery in the third year of Daye (607).

## What happened

Danyang Commandery was abolished, Jiangzhou was established in its place, and Liyang County was transferred to Jiangzhou.

## Consequences

Two years after Liyang passed to Jiangzhou, the gazetteer records that in the eleventh year of the Kaihuang era (591 CE) the northwestern part of Liyang and the former Danyang lands were separated off to form Lishui County, still under Jiangzhou, and that this was the beginning of the county. In 608 CE, the fourth year of the Daye era, Jiangzhou in turn became Danyang Commandery, so that the two names exchanged places.

## Dating basis

This entry is two years earlier than the establishment of Lishui County in 591 CE, the eleventh year of the Kaihuang era. At that time Lishui did not yet exist as a county: what is recorded here concerns its predecessor Liyang County and the prefecture and commandery above it, and the dating follows the Kangxi edition of the *Lishui County Gazetteer* (卷之一·邑纪).

## Uncertainties

First, the entry is a single sentence and gives neither the seat nor the subordinate counties of Jiangzhou. The supplied character in "Li[yang]" was added by the gazetteer's editors, and this entry quotes it in that bracketed form.

Second, the *Suishu*, "Treatise on Geography, Part 3," entry on Danyang Commandery, places "Jiangzhou was set up at Shitoucheng" under the conquest of Chen without giving an exact year; the dating to the ninth year of Kaihuang (589) rests on the *Zizhi Tongjian*, juan 177, which this library read only through CHGIS's citation, **not in the original**.

Third, the subordinate counties and the span 589–606 for Jiangzhou are a reconstruction by CHGIS from the sources, not an explicit statement of the original; it must be read together with the standard history quoted above. On these grounds the confidence level is set to `medium`.
