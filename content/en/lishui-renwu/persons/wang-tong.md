Wang Tong is one of the thinnest entries in the Tang part of the notable-officials biography in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*: the whole biography is a single sentence, and this entry goes no further than it does.

## Origin and term

> 王通，字玄览。长寿间任，增益户口，以清勤名。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

The gazetteer gives his courtesy name Xuanlan and no native place. His appointment is dated only by reign period — "appointed during the Changshou era" is a span, not a fixed year; it is quoted as it stands, and this entry neither converts it into a specific year nor infers one from the dynasty. His post in Lishui is given only as "appointed", with no title stated.

## What the gazetteer records

The one act attributed to him is that he "increased the registered households". How it was done, and by how much, is not recorded. "He was named for clarity and diligence" is the gazetteer's general assessment of his reputation, quoted as an assessment rather than as a record of fact.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of an appointment, and this entry records him through official service. Apart from the increase in registered households, the volume records nothing else he did in Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, his native place is not recorded.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui post only as "appointed" and states no formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, "during the Changshou era" is a span: the gazetteer gives no fixed year, and this entry supplies no year of the common era and infers none from the dynasty.

Fifth, the method and the figures behind "increased the registered households" are not recorded.

Sixth, this volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
