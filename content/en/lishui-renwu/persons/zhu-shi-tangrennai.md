Madam Zhu is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Zhu, her husband Tang Rennai of Shangyuan Township, and records in order her wish to die with him, the manner of her widowhood, her teaching of a son who then died, and finally the magistrate's plaque. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 朱氏，上原鄉民湯仁鼐之妻。年二十喪夫，欲以身殉，絕食。姑知其有遺腹，亟勸慰之。後生男，朱即剪髮跣足，不復修婦容。家無斗筲，紡織自給，課子讀書，幾於有成，而子忽夭，伶仃迨六旬。苦節四十年，內外鄉黨悉稱之，知縣閔派魯給扁旌之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item follows chronological order.

"Madam Zhu, wife of Tang Rennai of Shangyuan Township" gives her natal surname, her place, and her husband: Madam Zhu, wife of Tang Rennai of Shangyuan Township. Shangyuan Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written.

"Widowed at twenty, she wished to follow him in death and refused food" opens her widowhood: her husband died when she was twenty, she wished to die with him, and refused food. Both acts are as the gazetteer records.

"Her mother-in-law, knowing she was with child, urgently urged and comforted her" gives the mother-in-law's intervention. "Mother-in-law" and "posthumous child" are the gazetteer's terms, showing she was pregnant at her husband's death, the ground of her chastity.

"Afterwards she bore a son, and Zhu at once cut her hair and went barefoot, no longer tending her appearance" records the birth and her self-vow. Both acts are as the gazetteer records.

"The house had no grain, and she lived by weaving; she taught her son to read, nearly to success, but he suddenly died young, and she was left desolate into her sixties" records her household, her teaching, and her loss. "No grain" shows her poverty; "lived by weaving" her self-support; her son nearly succeeded in his studies but died young, leaving her alone into her sixties. All are the gazetteer's statements; the son's name, the year of his death, and what he achieved are unrecorded.

"She kept her chastity forty years, all within and beyond her community praised her, and the magistrate Min Pailu gave her a plaque" gives the years and the honour. "Forty years" is the gazetteer's figure; the community's praise is recorded; and the honour comes from Min Pailu, magistrate of this county, in the form of a plaque whose text is not recorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Tang Rennai of Shangyuan Township, keeping her chastity forty years, teaching a son who died, and finally honoured with a plaque by the county magistrate Min Pailu — her community her home and the honouring official a Lishui officer. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Zhu", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at twenty, kept forty years of chastity, and was desolate into her sixties; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Zhu"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her husband's township.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Tang Rennai; his courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and her son's name, studies, and year of death are unrecorded.

Fourth, only the honouring official is recorded, not the plaque text. The gazetteer says only that the magistrate Min Pailu gave her a plaque; the words on it and the year of the honour are unrecorded.

Fifth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. Other items in this volume name other honouring officials such as Min Pailu and Li Zuoyi, and this entry does not borrow their details to fill in hers.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
