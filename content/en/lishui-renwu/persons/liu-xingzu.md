Liu Xingzu is one of the seven men in the "Song" block of the Filial Sons biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. That biography records where a man lived, acts of filiality, and the year of a commendation; it records no birth or death years and no family. This entry writes only what this line records and supplies no life for him.

## How the gazetteer places him

> 劉興祖，表孝坊民也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

The gazetteer gives no township and no village for him and identifies him only by a quarter name, "a resident of the Model Filiality quarter" — the one line of this Song block located by a quarter or tablet name rather than a township. This entry records that place-name as given, supplies no township and no address, and decides nothing about where the quarter lay.

## What the gazetteer records him doing

> 刲股以療父疾，愈而復作，又割腹取肝，雜糜進之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

Cutting flesh from the thigh and cutting open the belly to take the liver are acts of filiality as the gazetteer records them; this entry reproduces the gazetteer's wording and marks their nature: they are the gazetteer's account, and this entry judges neither their medical plausibility nor offers a modern explanation, and presents them as no verifiable fact. The gazetteer also records that the illness "was cured and then returned", which is why the second act follows, and records the manner of administration, "mixed into porridge and given"; both clauses are the gazetteer's own and this entry reproduces them. It does not say what illness his father had, whether the second cure succeeded, or in what years either act fell.

## The commendation the gazetteer records

> 嘉定間旌表。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"In the Jiading era" is the year of the commendation as the gazetteer gives it; this entry reproduces it without converting it to a Common-Era year and infers from it neither his birth and death nor the year of the acts. The line says only "commended", unlike the line on Yi Xiaoyi, which gives the name of the commended ward; this entry supplies no name for the commendation.

## The link with the line on Yi Xiaoyi

The same biography has another line — "Yi Xiaoyi, a man of the town. He cut open his belly and took his liver to cure his mother's illness. In the Qiandao era the matter was reported to the court, and his ward was commended under the name 'Model Filiality'" — and this library holds a separate entry for him; the name matches this line's "Model Filiality quarter". This entry states the link and also states what cannot be decided: first, whether the name of the quarter came from this very commendation of Yi Xiaoyi's ward is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it; second, this line says only "resident of the Model Filiality quarter" and never says whether that quarter and Yi Xiaoyi's ward are the same place, and this library has no material to decide that either; third, the wording differs — the Yi Xiaoyi line says his ward was commended, this line speaks of a quarter — and both wordings are recorded side by side without deciding whether ward and quarter name the same level of place. The two lines are linked only by shared wording; this entry decides nothing and does not merge the two entries.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him by a quarter name, which is a residence, not a birth record. He enters the persons site with the relation "Active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the locality the biography gives is where a man lived, not where he was born — the biography records no birthplace for anyone, only residence and where the act took place, so "born in Lishui" is not available; the gazetteer records no Lishui office, no writing, and no recommendation out of Lishui into office elsewhere, so "official service", "writings" and "Recommended from Lishui" are not available; what it records is that he cured his father at the Model Filiality quarter and was commended in the Jiading era, which is activity in Lishui, so this entry uses "Active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are nowhere recorded, his family is nowhere recorded, and neither his father's illness nor the outcome of the second cure is recorded.

Second, "in the Jiading era" dates only the commendation and cannot be used as evidence of his birth, death, or the date of his activity; this entry converts nothing and infers no year for the acts.

Third, "Model Filiality quarter" is the biography's place-name for him; where that quarter lay and which township it belonged to are unrecorded, and this library has no material to decide them.

Fourth, cutting thigh flesh and taking the liver are the gazetteer's account, and their nature is marked in the body of this entry; this entry judges no plausibility and presents them as no modern fact.

Fifth, the relation between this "Model Filiality quarter" and the commended ward of the Yi Xiaoyi line — whether the quarter's name came from that commendation, whether the two refer to one place, and whether ward and quarter are two names for the same kind of unit — cannot be decided from this library's material, and the doubts stand side by side undecided.

Sixth, the order of the seven Song lines does not follow the commendation years the block gives, since two of them record no year at all; the order therefore cannot be used as evidence of sequence, and this entry infers no dates from it.

Seventh, this entry rests on one line of the biography; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
