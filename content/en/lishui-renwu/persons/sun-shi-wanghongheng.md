Madam Sun is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Sun, her husband Wang Hongheng, and two things: keeping chastity and raising an orphan, and teaching her son Yuchen to enter the county school. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 孫氏，王弘恆妻。廿五歲守節存孤，教子玉臣入泮。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is very short.

"Madam Sun, wife of Wang Hongheng" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"From twenty-five she kept her chastity and raised an orphan, and taught her son Yuchen to enter the county school" gives two things: keeping chastity and raising the orphan from twenty-five, and teaching her son Yuchen to enter the county school as a student. These are the gazetteer's age, record, and fruit of her motherly instruction; the number of years of her chastity, her husband's years, and the year of her son's school entry are unrecorded. Her son's name, Yuchen, is one of two Wang sons in this block (the other being "Chen" in the item on Madam Chen, wife of Wang Hongye); the names are similar but this entry keeps each to its own item and does not join them.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Wang Hongheng, keeping chastity and raising an orphan from twenty-five and teaching her son Yuchen into the county school, the household and her teaching belonging to the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Sun", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her — her son's school entry is his study, not her own recommendation — so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she kept her chastity from twenty-five; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, no number of years of chastity, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Sun"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Wang Hongheng and her son Yuchen; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had children besides Yuchen is unrecorded.

Fourth, the year of her son's entry into the county school and his later career are unrecorded; this entry does not supply his examination year from other volumes such as the examination tables, nor treat his school entry as her own recommendation.

Fifth, other items in this volume name men with the similar names Wang Hongye and Wang Hongheng; this entry draws no link between them nor judges them one household, keeping each to its own item.

Sixth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award, and this entry infers none from other items.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Eighth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
