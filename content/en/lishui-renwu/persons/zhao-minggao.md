Zhao Minggao is a man of Lishui recorded in the "Ming" block of the Filial Sons biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and also recorded in the table of ranks conferred on fathers in juan 2 of the same book. His biography in juan 7 is one of the few in this biography that says something about his learning, his son's examination success and his writings. The two records are not identical, and this entry sets them side by side without taking one over the other.

## His childhood and his stepmother

> 趙鳴皋，字聞野。幼喪父母，事繼母至孝，即累見擯（斤）［斥］而孝勿衰，母亦反為所化。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Courtesy name Wanye" is his courtesy name as the entry gives it. "He lost his parents young" gives his childhood with no year. "He served his stepmother with the utmost filial care" names the person to whom he was filial. "Repeatedly thrust away" records what he met, and why he was thrust away is unrecorded; the transcription's collation brackets are reproduced as they stand and the character inside the square brackets is the one used. "The mother was in the end changed by it" is the entry's account of the stepmother's turn, and belongs to its layer of remark.

## Study and teaching his son

> 貧而力學，試屢不遇，遂篤志教子之驊成名進士。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Poor, and studying hard" and "repeatedly examined without finding a match" record his studies and their outcome; which examinations, and in what years, are unrecorded. "He set himself firmly to teaching his son, who became a successful candidate" gives his son's name — written here in one particular way, the only place in this entry where his son is named. This entry infers neither the year of his son's success nor any office he held.

## Honour through his son: two variant accounts

> 子貴，封文林郎。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

The table of ranks conferred on fathers in juan 2, in its entry under the Tianqi era, is recorded in this library's source card as stating that he was honoured because of a son and given a post as county magistrate of a county named Dehua. The two records must be kept side by side. First, the son's name is written one way in the biography and another in the table; this library decides neither which writing is the man's own name nor whether the two are one person. Second, the form of honour differs: the biography gives a rank conferred, the table gives a post conferred, and "conferred" and "given" are two different favours in this gazetteer's table of ranks on fathers, which must not be merged; this entry sets both forms down and decides neither. The rank in the biography and the post in the table are reproduced as each volume words them, and neither is used to fill out the other. Third, the table places its entry in the Tianqi era, which is the one period this library connects to him; the biography gives no era date, and this entry records only that the honour belongs to the Tianqi period, inferring no birth, death or act from it.

## Standing in the neighbourhood, and his age

> 生平樂施尚義，德（邵）［劭］年高，舉鄉賓者再。壽八十有七。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"He gave gladly and valued rightness" and "his virtue was high and his years many" are the entry's remarks, and the transcription's collation brackets are reproduced as they stand with the character inside the square brackets used. "Put forward twice as a guest of the locality" records a local honour; what that honour was, who put him forward, and when, are nowhere recorded, and "twice" is the entry's count. "His years were eighty and seven" gives his age, not his birth or death year: nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no birth year is inferred.

## Three collections

> 所著有《江州漫興》《樂善齋漫吟》《趙公小言》諸集行於世。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

This is one of the few entries in this biography that credits its subject with writings. The three titles are reproduced as given; whether the collections survive, how many rolls each had, and what they contain are nowhere recorded and this library holds none of their text. The place name in the first title is part of a title, and this entry does not infer from a title where the man travelled; the studio name in the second title is reproduced without deciding where that studio was.

## Relation to Lishui

The biography gives Zhao Minggao no township — most other entries in this biography give one (Shanyang, Sihe, Bailu, Fengqing, Zanxian, Xiantan townships) while this one gives none — and it gives no birthplace. He enters the persons site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: no birthplace is recorded, so "born in Lishui" is not available; the rank and the post both came through his son and the biography records no office of his own, so "official service" is not available; the biography credits him with three collections, but nothing this library holds of their text, and no title, shows them to concern Lishui directly, so "writings" is not asserted; what the biography records is his giving and his standing in the neighbourhood, his putting forward for a local honour, and the teaching of his son at home, so the relation is "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are nowhere recorded, and so is his township. The entry gives only his age, eighty and seven, which is an age and not a date: nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no birth year is inferred.

Second, the biography gives no era date; the only period attached to him comes from the table in juan 2, under the Tianqi era, which is a period the table assigns and proves neither his birth and death nor the dates of his acts. The era value "Ming" here only records that the biography places him among its Ming entries.

Third, his son's name is written two ways, one in the biography and one in the table; this library sets both down and decides neither which is the man's own name nor whether the two writings name one person. The year of the son's success and any office he held are unrecorded.

Fourth, the honour is given two ways, a rank in the biography and a post in the table; "conferred" and "given" are two different favours and this library decides neither. The place named by the post in the table is nowhere located by the gazetteer, and this library does not decide where it was.

Fifth, what the local honour of guest of the locality was, who put him forward, and the dates of the two occasions are nowhere recorded.

Sixth, why he was repeatedly thrust away, and what lay behind the stepmother's change, are unrecorded; "gave gladly and valued rightness" and "his virtue was high" are the gazetteer's remarks.

Seventh, whether the three collections survive, their extent and their contents are not held by this library; no inference is made from their titles about where he travelled or where his studio stood.

Eighth, the biography gives no township for him, and this library has no material to decide where in Lishui he lived.

Ninth, this entry rests on one entry of the Filial Sons biography and on one line of the table of ranks on fathers as recorded in the category summary of juan 2; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
