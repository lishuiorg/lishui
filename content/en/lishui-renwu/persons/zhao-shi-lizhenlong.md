Madam Zhao is one of the figures in the Song section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice gives her husband, the deed of cutting her thigh to cure her grandmother, and the magistrate's petition to reward her. This entry sets apart the question of whose elder "grandmother" was, because the gazetteer leaves it unclear.

## The gazetteer text

> 趙氏，李震龍妻。祖母朱氏病，割股以療。知縣周成之為之申獎。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her station and place

"Madam Zhao" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname and no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Zhao" as its title. "Wife of Li Zhenlong" is the husband the gazetteer records, whose name is Li Zhenlong, reproduced here without alteration. The gazetteer gives neither her home place nor her paternal house, and no residence or native place is supplied here.

## Cutting her thigh to cure her grandmother

"When her grandmother Zhu fell ill, she cut her thigh to cure her" is the gazetteer's account of her deed: her grandmother Zhu fell ill, and Zhao cut flesh from her thigh to cure her. Cutting the thigh is a stock deed in the Chaste and Staunch and Filial Sons biographies of this gazetteer, and this entry reproduces it as the gazetteer's account; the grandmother's illness, whether it worked, and the year of the deed are all unrecorded. One point must be stated as unclear: whether "grandmother Zhu" was Zhao's own grandmother or the grandmother of her husband Li Zhenlong, the gazetteer does not make clear; this entry sets both readings side by side without deciding either.

## The magistrate's petition

"The county magistrate Zhou Chengzhi petitioned to reward her" is the gazetteer's account of what followed: the magistrate Zhou Chengzhi submitted a memorial seeking a reward for her. This entry reproduces the name Zhou Chengzhi and the office "county magistrate" without supplying from other books his years of service or native place, and without recording whether the petition succeeded or what reward followed. This is the notice's account of the honour she received and belongs to the gazetteer.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as the wife of Li Zhenlong, and records her cutting her thigh to cure her grandmother and the magistrate's petition for her. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer gives no home place and records no birth place or native place, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is her cutting her thigh to cure a kinswoman and the county magistrate's petition on her behalf in Lishui, which is her activity in Lishui, so the relation taken is "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; the notice gives no date at all.

Second, neither her personal nor her courtesy name is recorded, and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Madam Zhao.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives only that she was Li Zhenlong's wife and no home place, so no native place can be decided.

Fourth, whether "grandmother Zhu" was Zhao's grandmother or Li Zhenlong's, the gazetteer leaves unclear, and this entry sets the two readings side by side without deciding.

Fifth, the life of her husband Li Zhenlong and the year of their marriage are unrecorded, and Li Zhenlong has no entry in this library.

Sixth, the year of the deed, the grandmother's illness and whether it worked are all unrecorded.

Seventh, the magistrate Zhou Chengzhi's years of service, native place and the outcome of his petition are unrecorded, and nothing is supplied from other books.
