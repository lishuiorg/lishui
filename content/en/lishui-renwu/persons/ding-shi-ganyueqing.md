Madam Ding is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Ding, her husband Gan Yueqing of Zaicheng Township, and her resolve to keep chastity, her refusal to remarry, her raising of an orphan, and the honour she received. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 丁氏，在城鄉甘月卿妻。年十八歸甘，閱一歲為未亡人。有遺腹，立志守貞，父母欲奪其志，泣謂曰：「吾嘗聞，忠臣不事二君，烈女不嫁二夫。吾志決矣，幸父母無復他說。」後遺腹果生男，勤紡績，訓子成家。知縣李作楫旌其廬，博士程之望贈詩以贊，闔邑咸欽仰焉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is layered.

"Madam Ding, wife of Gan Yueqing of Zaicheng Township" gives her natal surname, her place, and her husband's name. Zaicheng Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written; her husband's name is kept as written. Her given name, courtesy name, and paternal family are unrecorded.

"At eighteen she went to the Gan family, and a year later became a widow" gives her marriage and widowhood. "Widow" is the term by which a widow styles herself. This is the gazetteer's sequence.

"She was with child, and resolved to keep her chastity; her parents wished to change her resolve, and she wept and said to them: 'I have heard that a loyal minister serves no second lord and a chaste woman marries no second husband. My resolve is fixed; I beg you, parents, to speak of it no more.'" This speech is the most direct testimony in the item to her will. "I have heard" shows the saying was traditional; the words are those the gazetteer records from her own account.

"Afterwards she bore a son, worked diligently at spinning and weaving, and raised him to establish a household" gives the outcome of her resolve and her householding and teaching. Her son's name and the year he established a household are unrecorded.

"The magistrate Li Zuoyi honoured her gate, and the academic Cheng Zhiwang wrote a poem in her praise, so that the whole county admired her" gives the honour, the poem, and the county's regard. The poem's text and its source are unrecorded; the county's admiration is the gazetteer's report of the local response.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Gan Yueqing of Zaicheng Township, married at eighteen and widowed a year later, refusing to remarry and keeping chastity while raising her orphan, finally with her gate honoured by the county magistrate Li Zuoyi and a poem by the academic Cheng Zhiwang — her community her home and the honouring men both in Lishui. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Ding", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she married at eighteen and was widowed a year later; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, no number of years of chastity, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Ding"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her husband's township.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Gan Yueqing; his courtesy name and status are unrecorded, as are her posthumous son's name and later career.

Fourth, the year of the gate honour and the text and source of the poem are unrecorded; the native place and dates of service of the academic Cheng Zhiwang are also unrecorded.

Fifth, "the whole county admired her" is the gazetteer's report of the local response, not factual record.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
