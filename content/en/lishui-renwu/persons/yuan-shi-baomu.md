Madam Yuan is one of the figures in the Song section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice records her age, her service to her mother, her death in the fire holding her mother, and the exchange between mother and daughter. This entry follows the gazetteer's layers and supplies no name, home place or date.

## The gazetteer text

> 袁氏，年一十五歲，事母嚴氏至孝。母病癱，火延其廬，鄰婦呼之曰：「勢不能俱生矣，女也，盍去之！」女泣謝曰：「使我生而無母，不如無生。」遂抱母死火中。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her station and place

"Madam Yuan" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname; throughout the notice she is called only "the daughter" with no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Yuan" as its title and uses the slug yuan-shi-baomu only as an index, without deciding any name. "Fifteen years old" is the age the gazetteer records; it gives no home place, no paternal house and no father's name, and no residence or native place is supplied. "Her mother, Madam Yan" is the mother's surname as the gazetteer records it, and both surnames, mother's and daughter's, appear in the notice.

## Her service to her mother and her mother's illness

"She served her mother, Madam Yan, with the utmost filial devotion" is the gazetteer's account of her conduct: she served her mother Yan most filially. "Utmost filial devotion" is the gazetteer's appraisal, and no further deeds are given as its basis. "Her mother lay paralysed" is the gazetteer's account of the mother's illness: the mother suffered a paralytic illness. The year of the illness and whether the father was then alive are unrecorded.

## Death in the fire holding her mother, and the exchange

"Fire spread to their house, and a neighbour woman called to her, 'It cannot be that both live; girl, why not leave!'" is the gazetteer's account of her plight: the fire spread to their house, and a neighbour woman called out that both could not survive and urged her to leave. This is the neighbour's speech as the gazetteer records it, reproduced and marked as the neighbour's words. "The daughter wept and answered, 'To live without my mother is worse than not to live'" is the gazetteer's record of her reply: weeping, she answered that to live on without her mother was worse than not to live at all. This is her speech as the gazetteer records it, reproduced as the gazetteer's quotation. "And so she died in the fire holding her mother" is the gazetteer's account of her death: holding her mother, she died in the flames. This entry reproduces it without supplying the circumstances of her death or what followed the burning of the house.

## Relation to Lishui

Of Madam Yuan the gazetteer records her service to her mother and her death in the fire holding her mother. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer records no birth place, native place or township, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; and the biography belongs to the "Chaste and Staunch" section of this county's gazetteer and records her deed of filial devotion, which this library treats as her activity in Lishui, so the relation taken is "active in Lishui". The limits must be stated: the gazetteer records no home place for her, so "active in Lishui" here rests only on her appearing in this county's Chaste and Staunch biography and carries no explicit statement that she acted in Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; "fifteen years old" gives only her age at death, and the notice is undated, so nothing is converted into a Common-Era year.

Second, neither her personal nor her courtesy name is recorded; throughout she is only "the daughter", and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Madam Yuan.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives no home place and no residence, so no native place can be decided.

Fourth, her father's name is not recorded, and nothing of her paternal family or siblings is recorded (the notice concerns only mother and daughter); nothing is supplied.

Fifth, "she served her mother with the utmost filial devotion" is the gazetteer's appraisal, and nothing is recorded as its basis; no deed is inferred from the appraisal.

Sixth, the cause of the fire, the site of the house and the year of the mother's illness are all unrecorded.

Seventh, the neighbour's speech and her reply belong to the speech recorded by the gazetteer, reproduced and marked here, with no further inference beyond what the gazetteer records.
