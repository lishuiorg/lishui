Yang Muhai is one of two national-level representative bearers of the Luoshan Big Dragon known to this library. This entry covers only the man; the history, form and origin tradition of the dragon belong to the entry on the project and are not repeated here.

## Register identity

His bearer page on the China Intangible Cultural Heritage Network gives: number 04-1544, male, Han, born October 1947, a native of Lishui County, Jiangsu; a fourth-batch national-level representative bearer of the project 龙舞 (Luoshan Big Dragon).

The project itself entered the second national batch in 2008, while Yang Muhai belongs to the fourth batch of national-level bearers; the two batches are different and must not be conflated. The bearer page this library holds for Yang Shufan gives the project name as 舞龙, while this page gives 龙舞; both are quoted as written and not harmonised.

## Place in the tradition

The résumé carried on his page records three things: that he is a fourth-generation inheritor of the Luoshan Big Dragon; that in 2007 he assisted Yang Shufan, former president of the Luoshan Big Dragon association and national-level bearer, and mastered the full set of procedures for taking the dragon out; and that from 2007 he raised funds and directed the building of the Luoshan Big Dragon exhibition hall.

The nature of the source matters: all three come from a résumé on his own register page and are self-reported, not independently recorded. "Fourth-generation inheritor" is the only statement of a transmission lineage for the Luoshan Big Dragon known to this library, and it appears only here; it is not used to fix the generation of any other bearer. The site, size and present condition of the exhibition hall are not recorded in this library's sources and are listed as a gap in the entry on the project.

## Relation to Lishui

The page describes him as "a native of Lishui County, Jiangsu", which is the only statement in this library's sources linking him to Lishui by native place, so the relation is recorded as birth. This is a native-place formulation on a register page, not a birth record; whether he was actually born in Lishui is not evidenced by a second source. The village where the dragon is transmitted, Luoshan, has its own entry in the towns station.

## Points of doubt

First, birth is known only to the precision "born October 1947", and it appears on the same page as the native-place statement and the three résumé items. This library has not verified them against a printed edition or a second source; the page changes over time, so the access date (2026-09-26) must be cited.

Second, "fourth-generation inheritor", "assisted Yang Shufan" and "raised funds to build the exhibition hall" are all self-reported, with no independent corroboration in this library's sources. The page does not say from whom the fourth generation is counted or who determined it.

Third, "a native of Lishui County, Jiangsu" is a native-place formulation; it does not establish a birthplace or show that he was born in Lishui.

Fourth, when he began working with the dragon, and whether he was ever listed in a district-level register, are recorded in none of this library's sources. The two district-level "third batch" bearer lists this library holds for 2017 and 2021 contain no "Yang Muhai", but this library has not verified those lists in full, so the absence is not used to conclude that he held no district-level status.

Fifth, this library separately holds an entry for "Yang Baihai", listed in the 2021 district notice. The names Yang Baihai and Yang Muhai are similar; whether they are the same person cannot be decided from this library's sources, and no identification is made. The two entries stand independently.
