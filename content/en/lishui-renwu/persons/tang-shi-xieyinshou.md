Madam Tang is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Tang, her husband Xie Yinshou, and her twice cutting flesh to cure him before keeping chastity after his death. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 湯氏，謝胤綬妻。二十二歲夫病，割股進之，遂愈。後三年夫復病，再割股；夫歿，守節。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item follows chronological order.

"Madam Tang, wife of Xie Yinshou" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"At twenty-two her husband fell ill; she cut flesh from her thigh and gave it to him, and he recovered" records the first healing. The cutting of flesh is the deed the gazetteer records, a practice of the time for healing kin; this entry keeps it as the gazetteer's statement without medical or causal interpretation. His recovery is the recorded result; the illness and what actually happened are unrecorded.

"Three years later he fell ill again and she cut flesh once more; when he died, she kept her chastity" records the second healing and her widowhood. Three years later is the gazetteer's sequence; the second cutting is its record; and her keeping of chastity begins at his death. The year of his death, his age, and the length of her chastity are unrecorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Xie Yinshou, twice cutting flesh to heal her husband and keeping chastity after his death, the household affairs belonging to the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Tang", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was twenty-two at her husband's first illness and that he died three years after the second; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no exact year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Tang"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Xie Yinshou; his courtesy name, status, and whether they had children are unrecorded.

Fourth, the names of her husband's two illnesses and the reality of the flesh-cutting cure are unrecorded; "he recovered" is the gazetteer's stated result, and this library neither draws a causal conclusion from it nor presents it as medical fact.

Fifth, the cutting of flesh is a deed the gazetteer records, and this entry keeps it as such without praise or blame.

Sixth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award, and this entry infers none from other items; nor does this library connect her to others because other items in this volume bear the names Xie Yinshou and Xie Yinquan.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Eighth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
