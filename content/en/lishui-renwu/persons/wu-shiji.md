Wu Shiji is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is short and is one of the few in this volume that states an office title: his Lishui post was that of instructor, and this entry does not write him as a county magistrate.

## Origin and term

> 吴世济，字楫侯。天启丁卯举人，崇祯元年任教谕。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Jihou; the gazetteer gives no native place. He was a *juren* of a *dingmao* year of the Tianqi era, and in the first year of the Chongzhen era was appointed to Lishui as an instructor — a county-school teaching post. The gazetteer states this title plainly, so this entry records him as an instructor and not as a county magistrate. The cyclical date is left unconverted, and the era year gives only his arrival.

## What the record says about his acts

**Scholars and an acting seal.**

> 遇士有礼，久而不懈。曾署县篆，洁白有操，不令人目为代庖者。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He treated scholars with courtesy and did not slacken over time. He "once held the county seal on acting assignment" — that is, he temporarily took charge of the county's seal and business. The gazetteer does not say when, for how long, in what capacity, or what he achieved while doing so, and this entry does not turn that phrase into a county-magistrateship. "Clean and unsullied with principled conduct, such that no one took him for one cooking in another's place" is the gazetteer's judgement, using the idiom about acting outside one's own office; it is reported praise, not a record.

**Promotion and a shrine.**

> 升（大）［太］和县去，邑有祠焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Here the transcription carries its own collation bracket: a wrong character in round brackets followed by the corrected character in square brackets. This entry reproduces the line exactly as transcribed and, in the surrounding text, follows the corrected character inside the square brackets, reading the place as Taihe. He was promoted to Taihe county and left, with no year given; "the county had a shrine" is the gazetteer's record of posthumous worship.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer gives no native place — and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his appointment there as an instructor in the first year of the Chongzhen era, the episode of acting for the county seal, and the county's shrine. The "distinguished officials" of this volume are not limited to county magistrates, and his title must follow what the gazetteer states: unlike most entries in this volume, which give only "appointed" with no title, this one names the instructor post.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded.

Second, his native place is unrecorded.

Third, his arrival is given only as the first year of the Chongzhen era and his departure only as his promotion to Taihe; nothing is converted into a common-era year.

Fourth, the date, capacity and results of his acting for the county seal are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a county-magistrateship from it.

Fifth, the name of the county he was promoted to rests on the transcription's collation bracket and must be checked against the punctuated edition; the year of the promotion is unrecorded.

Sixth, "courtesy toward scholars without slackening" and "clean and principled, not taken for one acting out of place" are the gazetteer's judgements, with no account of what they rested on.

Seventh, the location, date and later history of the shrine are unrecorded.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
