Madam Zhang is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Rui Yuanhua. Hers is the notice in the Ming section recording the "cutting of the thigh" as a cure. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 張氏，芮元化妻。年十九，生子方一日夫死，守節撫孤。翁病割股，姑病又割股。知縣張錫命旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Rui Yuanhua" names husband and wife.

Second, "at nineteen, her son a day old, her husband died, and she kept her chastity and raised the orphan."

Third, "when her father-in-law was ill she cut flesh from her thigh; when her mother-in-law was ill she cut again" records her acts. Cutting flesh from one's own thigh was a conventional act of extreme devotion; the gazetteer records it as the notice tells it, and this entry reproduces it without making any medical or factual judgement.

Fourth, "the magistrate Zhang Ximing honoured her." The year, number and form of the honour are not given.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Rui Yuanhua, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her acts of devotion and the honour she received attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "nineteen" dates her husband's death, not her birth.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Rui Yuanhua are unrecorded, and the son she bore is unnamed, with no account of his later life.

Fourth, the cutting of the thigh is recorded as the gazetteer's account; the circumstances of the illnesses and of the acts are not detailed, and this entry marks them as the gazetteer's report.

Fifth, the magistrate Zhang Ximing's honour gives no year, number or form.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
