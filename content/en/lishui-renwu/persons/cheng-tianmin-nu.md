Cheng Tianmin's daughter is one of the martyred women of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer names her only as "Cheng Tianmin's daughter" and records her as the wife of the county-school student Sun XX, with a single episode: hearing that soldiers had come, she drowned herself. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 程天民女，適邑庠孫□□。聞兵至，竄入村舍，兵追得之，以索系行，近河岸，躍入水，溺不出。兵恨以槍戳其腹，斷索而去。次早，孫尋至，收殮焉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records a single episode, taken sentence by sentence.

"Cheng Tianmin's daughter" is the gazetteer's way of naming her, showing her to be the daughter of Cheng Tianmin. Her given name, courtesy name, and birth order are nowhere recorded, and this entry titles her only as "Cheng Tianmin's daughter".

"Married to the county-school student Sun XX": "married" means she was given in marriage; "county school" is the local county school, and the gazetteer records her husband's status as a student there; "Sun XX" gives his surname Sun with two missing characters for his given name. The transcription marks the gap with "XX", which this entry reproduces without supplying a single character.

"When she heard soldiers were coming she fled into a village house" opens the recorded deed: hearing that soldiers were about to arrive, she fled into a village house. It does not say whose soldiers they were, nor which village of Lishui the house was in.

"The soldiers caught her and led her away bound with a rope" records that she was overtaken and led away tied. The binding is the record of the capture, not something she did herself, and this entry keeps it as the gazetteer's statement.

"Near the riverbank she threw herself into the water and did not come out" is the deed itself: as they reached the bank she leapt into the water and drowned. The river is unnamed, and this entry supplies no name.

"The soldiers, enraged, stabbed her belly with a spear and cut the rope and left" records what followed. Their rage is the gazetteer's wording; what kind of spear it was, and the wound, are unrecorded.

"Next morning Sun came searching and buried her" closes the item: the next morning her husband Sun came looking and laid out her body. This "Sun" is the same man as "Sun XX" above, called simply "Sun" here, and this entry keeps that wording.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners" as people lodging away from home. This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her deed in Lishui: drowning herself when soldiers came, and being buried by her husband's family. Her husband was a county-school student and her father Cheng Tianmin; though her residence is not stated, the deed belongs to the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only as "Cheng Tianmin's daughter", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; and since the record is of a local calamity, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer records only that she drowned amid military disorder; it gives no year of birth or death and no year for the disorder, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Cheng Tianmin's daughter" and says nothing of who her father was or where in Lishui he lived.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. Her husband appears only as "Sun XX", with two characters missing, kept as such; his courtesy name, his status beyond "county school", and whether they had children are unrecorded.

Fourth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award after death, and this entry infers none from other items in this volume.

Fifth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Sixth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
