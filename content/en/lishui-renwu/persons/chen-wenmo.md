Chen Wenmo is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is short but records something rare in this volume — a posthumous enshrinement carried out in the early Qing — and it packs two separate sacrifices into one sentence. This entry splits them, marks every judgement as a judgement, and merges nothing.

## Name and origin

> 陈文谟，字仲谋，别号仰潭，浙江慈溪人。由进士，嘉靖四十一年任。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Zhongmou, with the style name Yangtan, a native of Cixi in Zhejiang, entered office through the *jinshi* degree, and was appointed to Lishui in the forty-first year of the Jiajing era. That year is a definite year given by this volume, and it gives only the year of arrival.

## Acts in Lishui

> 端方正直，不阿不抗，事上敢言；御下明朗，使民通晓，催征得宜，加以劝慰，人尽输纳恐后。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The first half is the gazetteer's judgement of his character. "He pressed tax collection appropriately, added encouragement and reassurance, and everyone paid fearing to be last" is its narration and judgement of how collection was run: it states a manner and a reaction, not a rate, a year or a method, and this entry supplies no fiscal fact from it.

> 听讼务使两得其平，词无滥受，罪弗苛求。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This is its account of his judging: he sought a fair outcome for both sides, accepted no frivolous complaints, and did not hunt for harsh charges. No case is named anywhere in the biography.

## Sacrifices after his death

> 民悉怀之，建祠尸祝，至今称之。顺治十二年春奉主入名宦，其里已祀乡贤。若公者，可谓恭茂无愧者也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Three things are stated here and this entry keeps them apart.

First, the people of Lishui built a shrine and sacrificed to him, and are still praised for it. The gazetteer gives no shrine name, no site, and no year of building.

Second, his spirit tablet was installed among the Distinguished Officials in the spring of the twelfth year of the Shunzhi era. This is one of the few early-Qing posthumous enshrinements recorded in this volume: the gazetteer, written by men of the Kangxi period, dates the installation. Who arranged it and what the rite involved is not recorded; this entry states only the enshrinement date the gazetteer gives.

Third, "his home locality had already sacrificed to him as a local worthy" is a different sacrifice under a different category, elsewhere than the county's Distinguished Officials shrine. This entry lists it separately, does not merge the two into one act, and does not use it to conclude anything about his native place.

"He may be called one who was reverent and admirable without shame" is the gazetteer's judgement.

## After Lishui

> 仕终刑部郎。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Only his final office is given, with no year and no place.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer gives his native place as Cixi in Zhejiang — and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his appointment, the manner of collection and judging attributed to him, the shrine the people built, and his enshrinement among the Distinguished Officials in the spring of the twelfth year of the Shunzhi era. Among the Ming magistrates of Lishui known to this library, his biography is one of the few that records an early-Qing posthumous enshrinement.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; the year he left Lishui is not recorded, and only the year of arrival is given.

Second, the name, site and building date of the shrine raised by the people of Lishui are not recorded.

Third, who arranged the installation of his tablet among the Distinguished Officials, and what it involved, is not recorded; this entry states only the enshrinement date the gazetteer gives.

Fourth, what locality "his home locality" refers to, and the site and date of the local-worthy sacrifice, are nowhere recorded. This entry does not use that phrase to infer his native place or to relate the two sacrifices to each other.

Fifth, the method and rates behind "he pressed tax collection appropriately", and any case he heard, are not recorded.

Sixth, the year and place of his final office as vice director in the Ministry of Justice are not recorded.

Seventh, phrases such as "upright, direct, neither fawning nor defiant" and "reverent and admirable without shame" are the gazetteer's judgements and are not stated as fact.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
