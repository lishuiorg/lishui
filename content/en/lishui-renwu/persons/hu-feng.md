Hu Feng is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and one of the shortest Ming entries in the volume: one clause of courtesy name, one of appointment, four words of judgement. This entry records only that, and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 胡凤，字文明。嘉靖间任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Wenming and was appointed "during the Jiajing era". The biography gives no native place and no route into office — most Ming entries in this volume state a jinshi, juren or clerk origin, and this one states none, so this entry supplies none. The gazetteer gives no definite year, and this entry keeps its wording as an era span: nothing is converted into a Common Era year and no year is inferred from the dynasty. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui. The gazetteer also never gives his title in Lishui, and this entry does not assume that of county magistrate.

## What the gazetteer records

> 廉而果敢。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

These four words are the whole of what the biography says about his term, and they are judgement: clean and decisive. The biography records no building, no fiscal measure, no case and no school measure in Lishui, and none is inferred from the judgement.

## Relation to Lishui

His native place is not recorded, and he enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is the statement of his appointment and the four words of judgement. This entry is all this library has on him.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place and his route into office are nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies neither.

Second, precision of the term: the gazetteer says only "during the Jiajing era", gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts it nor infers a year from the dynasty.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate.

Fourth, the year he left office is not recorded.

Fifth, "clean and decisive" is the gazetteer's judgement, and the acts behind it are nowhere recorded.

Sixth, any concrete act in Lishui, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
