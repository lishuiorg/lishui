Madam Zheng is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer places her and her mother-in-law Madam Mei in a single item, Madam Mei first and Madam Zheng appended; this entry treats Madam Zheng alone, and Madam Mei has an entry (`ls:person:mei-shi-xuyiwei`) that links back to this one. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Zheng, her husband Xu Changzuo, and her mother-in-law Madam Mei. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 梅氏，城西許一唯妻。年廿四守志撫孤，八十五歲而卒。署縣令程萬里以「節儉雙美」獎之。媳鄭氏，昌佐妻，廿五歲守節撫孤，事姑孝，年已七旬矣。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item contains Madam Mei and her daughter-in-law Madam Zheng; this entry reads Madam Zheng alone.

"The daughter-in-law Madam Zheng, wife of Changzuo" gives her relation and status: Madam Mei's daughter-in-law and Xu Changzuo's wife. The gazetteer's "daughter-in-law" marks her as Madam Mei's son's wife, and "Changzuo" gives her husband's name (taking the surname Xu from "Xu Yiwei" above, hence Xu Changzuo). Her given name, courtesy name, and paternal family are unrecorded.

"From twenty-five she kept her chastity and raised an orphan, served her mother-in-law with filial piety, and is now over seventy" gives her chastity, her service, and her age: keeping chastity and raising the orphan from twenty-five, serving her mother-in-law (Madam Mei) with filial piety, and now over seventy. Her care for Madam Mei answers to Madam Mei's own item; her son's name, her husband's years, and his later career are unrecorded.

The gazetteer records no award for Madam Zheng and no number of years of chastity, and this entry does not supply any on her behalf.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Xu Changzuo and daughter-in-law of Madam Mei, keeping chastity and raising an orphan from twenty-five and serving her mother-in-law well, her residence being the city west (as given in Madam Mei's part of the item). But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Zheng", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she kept her chastity from twenty-five and is over seventy; it gives no year of birth, of marriage, or of her husband, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only Xu Changzuo's wife and Madam Mei's daughter-in-law, "Madam Zheng"; her paternal family and native place are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Xu Changzuo and that she had an orphan (her son); her son's name and later career are unrecorded.

Fourth, the item records no award for her and no number of years of chastity, and this entry does not infer them from other items, such as Cheng Wanli's honour in Madam Mei's part.

Fifth, her mother-in-law Madam Mei has her own entry (`ls:person:mei-shi-xuyiwei`), to which this one links; the two share a single item, and separate entries merely give each her due.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
