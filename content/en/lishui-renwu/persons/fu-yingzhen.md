Fu Yingzhen was magistrate of Lishui in the Wanli era of the Ming. He has **no separate biography** in the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*: his name is not among the fifty-five men of the distinguished-officials biography in juan 6. His material is scattered over the establishment record and the filial sons biography. This entry rests on those four places and states their limits.

## Reviving the local covenant: what the gazetteer records of him at greatest length

Juan 3, the establishment record, has a section on the local covenant, which traces the rise and decline of the institution at Lishui; the Wanli period there is his. The page this passage comes from is set in simplified characters, and this library quotes it as it stands:

> 岁久弛废，虚名徒存，不知敦俗化民此其首务。嘉靖间知县王从善行之，后无继者。万历间知县傅应祯复行之，每乡设乡约堂一所，立约长副，置二簿登记善恶，间行劝惩。崇祯戊寅，知县陈汝益再行之，尤重其事。至我国朝，司风化者皆能力持，至今不废云。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 3, establishment record, on the local covenant (from the transcription, which prints this page in simplified characters)

Three points must be stated. First, the gazetteer places him after "Wang Congshan, magistrate in the Jiajing era" and before "Chen Ruyi, magistrate, in the Wuyin year of Chongzhen", so he is of the **Wanli era**, with no year given. Second, the word "revived" shows the covenant was not his creation but a restoration after Wang Congshan's; the content of its rules, and who were appointed covenant heads, are not recorded. Third, this entry does not repeat the year of his covenant, for that year appears in the annals in juan 1 under the second year of Wanli, and this library has a separate event entry, **ls:event:ming-wanli-2-xiangyue-1574**, for that matter; what is recorded here is only what the covenant section adds.

## Ten mu of school land added

The school entry in juan 3, the establishment record, lists six parcels of school land, one of them his. The page is again set in simplified characters, and the passage is quoted as it stands:

> 田十亩。万历间知县傅应祯增置。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 3, establishment record, school entry, appended school land (from the transcription, which prints this page in simplified characters)

The same passage records the other five parcels (108 mu 2 fen, 23 mu 3 fen, 60 mu under He Yigui, magistrate in the Longqing era, 38 mu under Wu Shiquan, magistrate in the Wanli era, and 27 mu under Xu Bida, magistrate in the Wanli era) and adds that the rents from all these lands went "to scholars of good conduct who were poor". All six parcels belong to the Ming; this entry takes only his, and infers nothing from it about the location or the rent of the land he added.

## "The record by Censor Fu Yingzhen": where his censorate office comes from

The biography of Liu Yinglei in juan 6 of the distinguished-officials biography ends:

> 事詳傅御史應禎記。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6, distinguished-officials biography, biography of Liu Yinglei

These six characters are the only place this library learns that he held the office of censor, and they are also a documentary link between him and Liu Yinglei: the affairs of Liu Yinglei, magistrate from the third year of Longqing, were recorded by "Censor Fu Yingzhen", which shows that Fu Yingzhen later rose to the censorate. This entry records the censorate office on that basis but does not infer when he held it or of which circuit, the gazetteer giving only "censor" and no circuit. **"Censor" is the gazetteer's own word, and this entry does not rewrite it as a more specific title.**

## Commending filial sons and engaging Fu Pou: why this library has an entry

The filial sons biography in juan 7 records three things that bear directly on three other entries in this library, and that is what prompted this entry:

> 傅裒，贊賢鄉人。歲貢，不樂仕。母死，廬墓，以孝聞。嘗應傅令應禎聘而出，聞者曰，「傅生既敝屣乎世，而又為傅令屈，則傅令賢可知矣。」
> 虞欽，居北隅。割股療母病，知縣傅應禎旌獎之。
> 黃棖，居南隅。割股療父病，知縣傅應禎旌獎之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, filial sons biography

Three points must be stated. First, the gazetteer calls him "Fu the magistrate, Yingzhen" and "Magistrate Fu Yingzhen", which is where this library learns of his Lishui title of magistrate. Second, **a name trap must be guarded against: Fu Pou and Fu Yingzhen share the surname Fu, but "Fu the magistrate" is Fu Yingzhen and "Master Fu" is Fu Pou**; the gazetteer's entry on Fu Pou distinguishes the two by these two forms, and this entry reproduces them without merging the men. Third, Yu Qin and Huang Cheng were both commended by him for cutting flesh from their own bodies, and these are the two instances this library knows of his commending filial sons while in office.

## Relation to Lishui

The relation here is "official service", and the reasoning must be stated: he was magistrate of Lishui, as this library decides from the title "Magistrate Fu Yingzhen" in the filial sons biography of juan 7 together with the two passages of the establishment record in juan 3; his adding of school land, his revival of the covenant, his commendation of filial sons and his engagement of Fu Pou were all done while in that office. His native place is unrecorded, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; he is not recorded as recommended out of Lishui, so "recommended from Lishui" does not apply; no writings of his are recorded — the record he wrote for Liu Yinglei was written about another man's affairs and is not his own writing — so "writings" does not apply; and there is no record of his sojourning at Lishui, so "sojourn" does not apply.

## Points of doubt

First, his courtesy name, native place, route into office, birth and death years are all unrecorded.

Second, the years in which he held the Lishui magistracy are unrecorded; the year Wanli 2 appears only in the annals in juan 1, which this library records in an event entry of its own, and this entry does not infer from it the beginning and end of his whole term.

Third, the circuit of his censorate office, the year he held it, and his career after leaving Lishui are unrecorded; whether the "record by Censor Fu Yingzhen" survives, and where its text may be read, this library has not recorded.

Fourth, **he has no separate biography in the Kangxi gazetteer's distinguished-officials biography** (his name is not among the fifty-five men of juan 6), so what this library knows of his acts at Lishui is only the four scattered places above; whether he did other things that the gazetteer does not record cannot be decided here, and why he was left out of the distinguished-officials biography the gazetteer does not say.

Fifth, the content of the covenant rules he revived, the sites of the covenant halls he set up, and who were appointed covenant heads are unrecorded.

Sixth, the location and rent of the ten mu of school land he added, and the number of scholars who received it, are unrecorded.

Seventh, the covenant section in juan 3 and the annals entry for the second year of Wanli in juan 1 come from the same source, both being from the Kangxi gazetteer, and so count as one source only; this entry marks that, and does not present two appearances of one source as two witnesses.

Eighth, the four passages this entry rests on come from two transcription pages: the two in juan 3 are set in **simplified characters** (this library quotes them as they stand, without converting them to traditional characters and thereby altering the text), while the two in juan 6 and juan 7 are set in traditional characters; all four are online transcriptions, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription and the page noted.
