Zhou Qiyuan is an appended notice within the notable-officials biography of juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*; the biography ends with the two characters marking it as an appendix. He never held office in Lishui, and this entry does not present him as a Lishui official. The front matter carries no `relation` field, because no value in this library's list of relation types fits; his connection with Lishui is set out in the section below.

## Origin and term

> 周起元，字绵贞，福建晋江人。以解元登万历辛丑科进士，授番阳县，为政有干才。考选御史，历官太仆少卿。巡抚应天，凡属郡县民困多所体恤。于溧水改折一事，公独题疏力言，得报，溧民戴之，请祀名宦。寻以事迕魏珰被害，逮下诏狱死。附记。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was courtesy-named Mianzhen, a native of Jinjiang in Fujian, and took the *jieyuan* place before passing the *jinshi* examination of the *xinchou* year of the Wanli era. The gazetteer gives only the term *jieyuan*, and this entry reproduces it without stating which examination it refers to or in which year; the cyclical examination date is left as it stands and converted to no Western year. He was appointed to the county the gazetteer writes as Panyang, its characters reproduced as transcribed, with no location and no year given. His selection as a censor, his rise to *Taipu shaoqing*, and his appointment as grand coordinator of Yingtian are all undated. He held no office in Lishui, and this entry neither calls him magistrate of Lishui nor supplies any Lishui title for him.

## What the gazetteer records

"He showed ability in government" judges his term at Panyang, and "he was considerate of the distress of the people in all the counties under him" judges his term as grand coordinator; both are the gazetteer's judgements and neither cites an act. The only matter in the notice that concerns Lishui is the sentence saying that on the question of Lishui's tax conversion he alone submitted a memorial and pressed it hard, and obtained a reply. What the conversion consisted of, the year of the memorial, the year of the reply, and what the reply said are nowhere recorded in this volume. "The people of Lishui esteemed him and asked that he be sacrificed to among the notable officials" is the gazetteer's narrative of a posthumous request; the year of the request and whether it was granted are unrecorded. The notice then records his end: he offended *Wei dang* through some affair, was arrested and died in the imperial prison. *Wei dang* is the gazetteer's wording, the gazetteer never states whom it means, and this entry supplies no name; the affair itself is left unexplained, the term for the prison is reproduced as given, and no year is recorded. The closing two characters mark the notice as an appendix rather than a proper biography.

## Relation to Lishui

Zhou Qiyuan never held office in Lishui. The gazetteer appends him, and his connection with the county consists of exactly two things: that he alone memorialised in force on Lishui's tax conversion and obtained a reply, and that after his death the people of Lishui asked that he be sacrificed to among the notable officials. He enters the volume as the grand coordinator of Yingtian who petitioned for Lishui, through a posthumous request for his inclusion, not through service there. This library's list of relation types offers only native birth, official service, writing, sojourning and activity, and none of them fits, so this entry leaves `relation` unset and records the connection only here. The link this entry carries to this library's entry on Xu Bida exists because both rest on the Lishui tax conversion recorded in this volume; each records one man under its own biography, and this entry merges nothing, cross-confirms nothing, and decides nothing about whose effort the conversion was.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, and neither is the year of his arrest and death.

Second, the examination date is given only cyclically and is not converted; which examination the term *jieyuan* refers to, and its year, are unrecorded.

Third, he held no office in Lishui, and this entry does not call him magistrate of Lishui; the front matter leaves `relation` unset for the reason given above.

Fourth, the content of the tax conversion, the year of the memorial, the year of the reply and the substance of the reply are all unrecorded in this volume.

Fifth, the year of the request that he be sacrificed to among the notable officials, who initiated it, and whether it was granted are unrecorded.

Sixth, *Wei dang* is the gazetteer's wording and the person meant is not stated; the affair by which he offended him and the course of the persecution are unrecorded.

Seventh, the county written as Panyang is given without location or date, and the years of his appointment as censor, as *Taipu shaoqing*, and as grand coordinator of Yingtian are all unrecorded.

Eighth, the judgements on his ability and on his consideration for the people are the gazetteer's, and their grounds are unrecorded.

Ninth, this entry and this library's entry on Xu Bida both bear on Lishui's tax conversion, but each rests on a different biography and records a different man, and this entry merges nothing, cross-confirms nothing, and decides nothing about whose effort the conversion was; it also does not use the Xu Bida biography to fill out what Zhou Qiyuan memorialised.

Tenth, this entry rests on one appended notice in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him.
