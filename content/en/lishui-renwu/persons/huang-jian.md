Huang Jian is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Yongle period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. That section is a roster rather than a biography: each line gives a name, sometimes a courtesy name or one clause of kinship, the route into office, and the office held, and gives no account of the man. This entry holds only "presented as a talented man" and "served as a prefectural vice-director", and is among the shortest in the section; everything the gazetteer does not record is placed under Points of doubt rather than supplied.

## Route of presentation and office held

> 黃鑒由人材，任州判。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of talented men" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces the two characters and does not convert them into "jinshi" or any other degree. "Served as a prefectural vice-director" is the office as the gazetteer words it, and it lay in some prefecture, not in Lishui: the gazetteer writes the office in two characters and names no prefecture, so this entry supplies no prefecture and no description of the duties. This block gives no years, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of taking up office can be known.

## What the gazetteer records, and the limit of this entry

The section gives no life: it records no native place, no courtesy name, who presented him or by what procedure, what he did in the prefecture, how he was promoted, what his final office was, or anything after his death. This entry reasons from the words "talented men" and "prefectural vice-director" to no institutional detail, since the gazetteer records none.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his office lay in a prefecture and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Yongle period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Yongle era nor that he was active in any particular Yongle year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of talented men", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; the office is a bare title, the prefecture is not named, and its duties and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Fifth, the same Yongle block carries lines of the same shape for other men, such as a confucian-scholar presentation ending as an instructor and a talented-men presentation ending as a county vice-magistrate; each is a separate person, and this entry reasons from neighbouring lines to no one's native place, date or career.

Sixth, this entry rests on one roster line; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
