Madam Tao is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Wei Shu. Her notice is among the briefest, with only three clauses. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 陶氏，魏樞妻。年二十五夫死，子幼，守節六十三年。知縣朱身修旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Wei Shu" names husband and wife.

Second, "her husband died when she was twenty-five, leaving a young son."

Third, "she kept her chastity for sixty-three years."

Fourth, "the magistrate Zhu Shenxiu honoured her." The year, number and form of the honour are not given.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Wei Shu, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity and the honour she received attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty-five" dates her husband's death, and "sixty-three years" is the gazetteer's figure for her widowhood.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Wei Shu are unrecorded.

Fourth, the text says only that the son was young; his name, number and later life are unrecorded.

Fifth, the magistrate Zhu Shenxiu's honour gives no year, number or tablet inscription.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
