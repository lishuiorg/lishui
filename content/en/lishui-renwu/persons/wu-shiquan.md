Wu Shiquan is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography never dates his Lishui term, yet it names two works with their settings, and records one act that bears directly on this library's account of the county's gazetteers: the recompiling of the county gazetteer. This entry records all three, marks every judgement as a judgement, and keeps its own account and the library's gazetteer entry side by side without mixing them.

## Name and origin

> 吴仕诠，字公择，浙江湖州府归安县人。万历初举进士，令溧水与寻常令异。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Gongze, a native of Gui'an in Huzhou Prefecture, Zhejiang, passed the *jinshi* examination early in the Wanli era, and served as magistrate of Lishui. "Early in the Wanli era" qualifies only his passing the examination; this volume never states the year he took up Lishui, and this entry converts nothing and infers nothing from the dynasty. What follows is the gazetteer's general judgement of his style as a magistrate.

## Acts in Lishui

> 视溧之剧者为简，变令之俗者为雅，动以文章饰吏治。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

All three sentences are judgement: he treated what was burdensome in Lishui as simple, turned what was vulgar in magisterial routine into refinement, and decorated administration with letters. No concrete measure is inferred from them.

> 首崇学校，作《学记》勒于学宫，作《训民四箴》立于堂壁，士之为文者以之风，民之违法者以之劝。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This is the only passage in the biography that names works and their settings. He wrote a "Record of the School", carved at the county school, and "Four Maxims for Instructing the People", set up on a hall wall, using them to give the literati a model and to admonish those who broke the law. The texts of both works are not in this volume, and which hall the "hall wall" is remains unstated. The county school itself has its own entry in this library; this one records only its connection to Wu Shiquan.

> 政暇一意于山川文物，虚贤下士。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

In his leisure he devoted himself to the region's mountains, waters and antiquities, and was humble toward the worthy and courteous toward scholars. No specific act is recorded.

## Recompiling the county gazetteer

> 以邑志为国史，独重其事，考正德、嘉靖二志而重新之，聘山人黄汝金纂辑以成，而自序焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This sentence is one of the gazetteer bases this library has for the making of the Wanli gazetteer. This volume states plainly that Wu Shiquan treated the county gazetteer as national history and valued the task, examined the Zhengde and Jiajing predecessors and reworked them, engaged the recluse Huang Rujin to compile the book, and wrote its preface himself. "Recluse" is the gazetteer's word for Huang Rujin.

The entry "Five Extant Editions of the Lishui County Gazetteer" in this library records, on the basis of this kind of statement, that the Wanli gazetteer was compiled under Wu Shiquan with Huang Rujin, and gives its juan count, first printing and the extant printed copy. The two can be read against each other. But the boundary must hold: this volume records only three things — the reworking from the two earlier gazetteers, the engagement of Huang Rujin, and the self-written preface — and gives no start or end year, no juan count, no arrangement, and no statement of whether the Zhengde and Jiajing gazetteers survive. This entry does not use the other entry to supply what this biography does not record; that entry's juan count and edition information come from bibliographic description, not from this volume.

## Judgements and afterlife

> 有功于溧者德惠不可枚举，至今人思之。公实于万历以后为溧令者有观感云。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Both sentences are judgement. "His services to Lishui, his benefits of virtue, cannot be enumerated" is a general verdict; "people still think of him to this day" is the gazetteer's account of posthumous memory. The final sentence, "he was indeed one who gave later magistrates of Lishui something to look up to and be moved by", is syntactically unclear in the transcription; this entry quotes it as it stands and does not smooth it.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. Three layers tie him to the county: his magistracy; the two works he wrote, one carved at the county school and one set on a hall wall; and his recompiling of the county gazetteer. The third is one of the gazetteer bases for this library's account of the county's older gazetteers, which is why this entry is linked to that entry.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, the year he took up Lishui is not recorded in this volume; "early in the Wanli era" qualifies only his examination, and this entry neither converts it nor infers a term year from the dynasty.

Third, the texts of the "Record of the School" and the "Four Maxims for Instructing the People", the details of their carving and posting, and their later survival are not recorded; which hall the wall belonged to is unstated.

Fourth, the years, juan count, arrangement and division of labour of the recompiling are nowhere recorded. The juan count and edition data in this library's gazetteer entry come from bibliographic description and are not merged into what this biography states.

Fifth, Huang Rujin is described only as a "recluse"; his life is not recorded in this volume.

Sixth, what was kept and what was copied in "examining the Zhengde and Jiajing gazetteers and reworking them" is not recorded; neither of those two gazetteers survives in this library.

Seventh, the year he left office and his later career are not recorded.

Eighth, the final sentence of the biography is unclear in sense; it is quoted as it stands and not rewritten.

Ninth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume. What this library's other sources say about the Wanli gazetteer belongs to the bibliographic layer and is kept separate from the biography.
