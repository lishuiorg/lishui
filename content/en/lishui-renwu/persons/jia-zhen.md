Jia Zhen is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and one of the shortest Ming biographies in the volume: one clause of native place, one of appointment, two of judgement. This entry records only that, and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 贾贞，广平人。永乐间任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was a native of Guangping and was appointed "during the Yongle era". The gazetteer gives no definite year, and this entry keeps its wording as an era span: nothing is converted into a Common Era year and no year is inferred from the dynasty. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui. The gazetteer also never gives his title in Lishui, and this entry does not assume that of county magistrate.

## What the gazetteer records

> 廉明守己，莅政有方。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

These two clauses are the whole of what the biography says about his term, and both are judgement: clean and clear, keeping himself to his own standard, governing with method. The biography records no building, no case, no fiscal measure and no educational measure in Lishui, and none is inferred from the judgement.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Guangping" — and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is the statement of his appointment and the two clauses of judgement. This entry is all this library has on him.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Guangping.

Second, precision of the term: the gazetteer says only "during the Yongle era", gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts it nor infers a year from the dynasty.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate.

Fourth, the year he left office is not recorded.

Fifth, "clean and clear, keeping himself to his own standard; governing with method" is the gazetteer's judgement, and the acts behind it are nowhere recorded.

Sixth, any concrete act in Lishui, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
