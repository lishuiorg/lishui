Madam Sun is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Sun, her husband Xia Zhiyi, and two things: the age at which she began keeping chastity, and her son Xia Lin's passing of the military provincial examination. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 孫氏，夏之益妻。廿四歲守節。中武鄉試夏霖，其子也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is very short.

"Madam Sun, wife of Xia Zhiyi" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"From twenty-four she kept her chastity" gives the age at which her chastity began. This is the gazetteer's age; the number of years she kept it, the year of her husband's death, and the year of her own death are all unrecorded.

"Xia Lin, who passed the military provincial examination, was her son" gives her son's degree and identifies him. Passing the military provincial examination is the degree the gazetteer records for him; "was her son" identifies him as hers. The gazetteer thus appends evidence of her motherly instruction, but the year of his passing and what office he held are unrecorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Xia Zhiyi, keeping chastity from twenty-four, her son Xia Lin passing the military provincial examination, the household and her teaching of the son belonging to the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Sun", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her — her son's military degree is his degree, not her own recommendation — so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she kept her chastity from twenty-four; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, no number of years of chastity, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Sun"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Xia Zhiyi and her son Xia Lin; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had children besides Xia Lin is unrecorded.

Fourth, the year of her son's passing of the military provincial examination and his later office are unrecorded; this entry does not supply his examination year from other volumes such as the examination tables, nor treat his degree as her own recommendation.

Fifth, the item is very short, with almost no deeds beyond her husband's name, the age at which her chastity began, and her son's degree, and this entry does not draw on other items to fill it out.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
