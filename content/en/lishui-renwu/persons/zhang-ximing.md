Zhang Ximing is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is the most concrete account of the conversion of Lishui's grain tax among the biographies of this volume. Apart from that affair, it records only an anecdote about his person and his eye for scholars, and it dates nothing.

## Origin and term

> 张锡命，号月沙，四川潼川州人。登万历丙辰进士，授溧水县。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He went by the style name Yuesha (the gazetteer gives no courtesy name), was a native of Tongchuan Prefecture in Sichuan, passed the *jinshi* examination in a *bingchen* year of the Wanli era, and was assigned to Lishui. The cyclical date is left as it stands: this entry converts nothing and does not infer his year of arrival from it. The gazetteer writes only "assigned to Lishui county" and gives neither a precise office title nor the years of his term.

## What the record says about his acts

**One lost eye, and the county's caution.**

> 公眇一目，邑人相戒，百凡言语书字俱慎之，公略无拘忌，久之民亦相忘。公之聪察纤毫不爽，若有四目之明，至论文阅试，具特识，拔秀异焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He lost the sight of one eye; the county people warned one another and were careful in speech and in writing, while he himself had no restraint about it, and in time the people also forgot it. "His acuteness missed not the finest hair, as if he had the sight of four eyes" is the gazetteer's judgement; "in criticising essays and reading examination papers he had special judgement, and raised up the outstanding" is its account of his discernment in school examinations, with no names or cases given.

**The conversion of the grain tax.**

> 邑有改折田粮事，前令公历历申请未获报可，公曰：『此已有成议，不难。』力请于上，而为民慰。时值嘉兴徐公任大京兆，公亟乘机会，取邑民张晓前疏，申而行之。京兆公曰：『适获我心。』力恳抚按题请，得折改如高淳例。合邑欢呼动地，竭二十年之力，赖公克收其成焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The gazetteer records that Lishui had a matter of converting its field-grain tax; earlier magistrates had applied item by item without obtaining an approving reply. Zhang Ximing said the matter already had an agreed proposal and was not difficult, petitioned upward with force, and so gave the people comfort. At that time a certain "Lord Xu of Jiaxing" held the "great metropolitan" post — the gazetteer names him only by native place and uses only that style of address, giving no office title — and Zhang Ximing at once seized the opportunity, took the earlier memorial of the county man Zhang Xiao and pressed it through. The metropolitan lord said "this exactly matches my heart"; Zhang Ximing further urged the provincial governors and the grand coordinators to memorialise the throne, and a conversion was obtained on the pattern used for Gaochun. "The whole county cheered so that the earth seemed to move" is the gazetteer's account of popular reaction, and "the effort of twenty years, whose completion depended on this lord" is its summary and attribution; neither is stated here as fact. The year the conversion was obtained, its items and its amounts are all unrecorded, and the content of "the Gaochun pattern" is not supplied.

**Two men left unnamed.** In this passage the gazetteer names no one: it refers to "Lord Xu of Jiaxing" by native place and to "the earlier lord magistrate" by sequence only. This volume's biography of Xu Bida says that he "rose in turn to be prefect of the southern capital's Yingtian Prefecture" and that he drafted a memorial for the county people including Zhang Xiao and others asking for the conversion, and Xu Bida was a native of Xiushui in Jiaxing Prefecture, Zhejiang — which matches the native place given here, and the two biographies record the same Lishui conversion and the same county man's memorial. Yet this biography never names him, so this library does not assert that "Lord Xu of Jiaxing" is Xu Bida; the two are kept side by side as a doubt. Likewise the "earlier lord magistrate" is unnamed, and this entry does not identify anyone from the order of the biographies.

**The households of Qi Tai's clan associates and this entry.** The two numbers for those households are dealt with in the section below; this entry adopts neither and does not attribute the affair to Zhang Ximing on the strength of his own biography, which records neither the households nor their exemption.

## The exemption of the first Tianqi year in the Loyalty and Integrity biographies and the annals

His own biography says nothing about Qi Tai's clan associates, but the Loyalty and Integrity biographies of the same volume make him the man who carried through their exemption:

> 至天启元年，知县张锡命伸应天府尹徐必达、监察御史田题准，豁免杨保元、骆应鹏等四十五家，回籍归业。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The volume therefore records that in the first Tianqi year Zhang Ximing pressed the proposal of Xu Bida, prefect of Yingtianfu, and of a supervising censor, obtained approval, and exempted Yang Baoyuan, Luo Yingpeng and the rest — forty-five households — returning them to their native registers and their livelihoods. This entry records the matter on that basis and states four things plainly. First, it comes from the Loyalty and Integrity biographies, not from his own, which covers only the tax conversion, the anecdote about his eye, and his judgement in examinations, and says nothing about Qi Tai's clan associates. Second, the number here is forty-five households; this volume's biography of Xu Bida says "forty-five households enrolled as garrison military", agreeing with it, while the annals in juan 1 of the same book say "thirty-eight households" — two numbers in two juan of one book, kept side by side here, neither adopted. Third, the clause naming the supervising censor is broken in the transcription: whether "Tian" is the censor's surname with his given name lost, or whether the break falls elsewhere, cannot be settled from the text, and this entry neither repunctuates it nor supplies a name, reproducing the wording as it stands. Fourth, the matter is dated to the first Tianqi year, left unconverted. As for Xu Bida's part, this library's entry on him separately records that he "pressed hard for the removal of the registers and asked that descendants be recorded", and the two biographies each tell one side of the affair; this entry does not merge them and does not decide whose effort carried it.

## Other policies and his later office

> 其他善政不能备纪，后擢南台御史。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The gazetteer states that his other beneficial policies cannot all be recorded, and gives only his later promotion to a censorate office in the southern capital, undated.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his assignment there, the anecdote of the county people's caution about his eye and their eventual ease, the grain-tax conversion with its reported popular cheering, and the summary that his other policies cannot all be recorded. This entry is, within what this library knows, the fullest account in this volume of how the Lishui conversion was carried through, its two decisive steps being that he took the county man Zhang Xiao's earlier memorial and pressed it through, and that he urged the provincial authorities to memorialise the throne.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded.

Second, the beginning and end of his Lishui term are unrecorded; the cyclical date is left unconverted and is not used to infer anything.

Third, his Lishui office is given only as "assigned to Lishui county", with no precise title.

Fourth, the year of the conversion, its items and its amounts are unrecorded; the content of "the Gaochun pattern" is not supplied.

Fifth, "Lord Xu of Jiaxing" is unnamed. This volume's biography of Xu Bida matches his native place and career, and both biographies record the same conversion, but the gazetteer does not name him; this library does not assert the identification and keeps both readings side by side.

Sixth, the "earlier lord magistrate" is unnamed, and no one is identified from the order of this volume.

Seventh, whether the memorial of the county man Zhang Xiao mentioned here is the same document mentioned in another biography of this volume is not stated.

Eighth, "the whole county cheered as if the earth moved", "the effort of twenty years, whose completion depended on this lord", "this exactly matches my heart" and "as if he had the sight of four eyes" are the gazetteer's reported reactions, summaries and judgements.

Ninth, his other beneficial policies are left unlisted.

Tenth, the two numbers for Qi Tai's clan-associate households (forty-five in the Loyalty and Integrity biographies and in this volume's biography of Xu Bida, thirty-eight in the annals) are kept side by side, neither adopted. The exemption of the first Tianqi year comes from the Loyalty and Integrity biographies rather than from his own, and this entry records it on that basis while noting where it comes from; the clause naming the supervising censor is broken in the transcription, possibly with one character lost, and this entry neither repunctuates it nor supplies a name.

Eleventh, the year of his promotion to the southern censorate is unrecorded.

Twelfth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
