Madam Tang is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Tang, her husband Zhu Chunren of Yifeng Township, and her chastity, her service to her mother-in-law, and her teaching of her son, closing with an appended note on her son's filial piety. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 湯氏，儀鳳鄉諸純仁妻。年廿一歲夫亡守節，事姑以孝，教子以慈。縣令李作楫以「苦節凌（孀）［霜］」獎之。有子啟駒，復克盡孝，是亦守節之報也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is layered.

"Madam Tang, wife of Zhu Chunren of Yifeng Township" gives her natal surname, her place, and her husband's name. Yifeng Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written; her husband's name is kept as written. Her given name, courtesy name, and paternal family are unrecorded.

"Widowed at twenty-one, she kept her chastity, served her mother-in-law with filial piety, and taught her son with tenderness" gives her age and her conduct. Her service and her teaching are the gazetteer's summary; her son's name and the number of years of her chastity appear elsewhere in the item.

"The magistrate Li Zuoyi honoured her with the words 'Bitter chastity braving the frost'" gives the honouring official and the plaque text. The transcription here carries its own critical brackets, with the erroneous character "widowed" in parentheses and the correct character "frost" in square brackets, that is, the correct text should read "Bitter chastity braving the frost". This entry keeps the brackets in the quotation and reads the text as corrected. The year of the honour is unrecorded.

"Her son Qiju also did his utmost in filial piety, which is likewise the reward of her chastity" is an appended note closing on the gazetteer's judgement, "the reward of her chastity", which is a judgement, not a fact.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Zhu Chunren of Yifeng Township, keeping chastity from twenty-one, serving her mother-in-law and teaching her son, and honoured by the county magistrate Li Zuoyi with "Bitter chastity braving the frost" — her community her home and the honouring official a Lishui officer. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Tang", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at twenty-one; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, no number of years of chastity, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Tang"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her husband's township.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Zhu Chunren and her son Qiju; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had children besides Qiju is unrecorded.

Fourth, "Bitter chastity braving the frost" involves the transcription's critical brackets, with the error in parentheses and the correction in square brackets; this entry keeps the text as written and reads the corrected character. The year of the honour is unrecorded.

Fifth, "which is likewise the reward of her chastity" is the gazetteer's judgement, not factual record.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
