Xu Liangyan is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is one of the two longest in the volume. It is unusually detailed, yet it withholds dates exactly where they matter most: the year of his reappointment to Lishui, and the year of the pagoda he promoted. This entry works section by section, marks every judgement and causal claim as the gazetteer's, and keeps conflicting dates side by side without choosing.

## Origin and term

> 徐良彦，字季良，别号若谷，江西新建县人。登万历戊戌进士，初授婺源县知县，丁内艰去，服阕改补溧水县。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Jiliang, with the style name Ruogu, a native of Xinjian county in Jiangxi, passed the *jinshi* examination in a *wuxu* year of the Wanli era, was first appointed magistrate of Wuyuan, left office for his mother's mourning, and was reappointed to Lishui after the mourning period ended. The gazetteer records only "reappointed on the end of mourning" and gives no year; the cyclical date is left as it stands and this entry does not convert it.

> 居溧五载，所行多仿前令徐公，人称溧有二徐焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Five years in Lishui" gives a duration, not a start and end. That "much of what he did imitated the earlier magistrate Xu" and that people spoke of "two Xus in Lishui" are recorded as contemporary talk; the "earlier magistrate Xu" is not named in this volume.

## Acts in Lishui

**Serving the people and blocking harm: the imperial-brick transport.**

> 公才学政事动皆过人。下车，首权其邑之要务，莫大于便民杜害，允行之。应天八邑有解京皇砖之役，岁佥富户转解，赔累甚重，多至破家，大为民害，公心忧之。因请于田粮少增分毫，不令偏累富户。议令各县共出其费，悉付苏州府惯烧砖式匠作总解，以免驳换之苦，议行而民便。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"He surpassed others in talent, learning and administration in everything he did" is the gazetteer's judgement. What follows is the most concrete fiscal reform recorded in this biography: eight counties of Yingtian Prefecture bore a corvée of transporting imperial bricks to the capital, with wealthy households conscripted each year to relay them, losses so heavy that many were ruined. Xu Liangyan proposed a tiny addition to land tax instead of loading the burden on the rich alone, and proposed that all counties contribute the cost and hand the whole transport to brickmakers of Suzhou Prefecture experienced in the required shapes, sparing the hardship of rejection and re-handling. The gazetteer says the plan was adopted and the people found it convenient, but gives no year and no figure for the tax addition.

**Supplying visiting superiors.**

> 凡上官经临，一切供应什物皆借办于坊长，临期仓卒，多致失误不周，往往吏民相诿，失礼上官。公设处官置，立为定则，不使贻累坊长，上下相安。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Supplies for passing superiors had been squeezed out of ward heads at short notice, causing failures and mutual blame between clerks and people. He arranged them officially and fixed standing rules. What the arrangement and the rules actually were is not recorded.

**Accounts.**

> 每年会计，必清算申明，不使胥吏得一毫影借其间。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

**Judging and penalties.**

> 听讼不形喜怒，使民情得以上达，而奸猾者悉无以自施其巧。罪罚惟轻，赎锾多免，一意保护群黎。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He showed neither joy nor anger in court so that people's feelings could reach the top and the cunning had no room for their tricks; penalties were only the lightest, most monetary compositions were waived, and his single aim was to protect the common people. The last clause mixes narration and judgement; its amounts and scope are unrecorded.

**Promoting a pagoda built by the people.**

> 公素知堪舆之理，每叹溧水形势有不足而易消长。视东南诸水直泻无情，西北风气不聚，倡义民建塔以塞水口，于是民多丰饶，士咸文秀。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Two layers must be separated. "He promoted the righteous people's building of a pagoda to block the water outlet" is the act the gazetteer records — and it gives neither the pagoda's name nor a year. "Thereupon the people were much prosperous and the literati all refined" is the gazetteer's causal claim linking the pagoda to local abundance and literary success; it is a claim recorded by the gazetteer, not a fact stated by this entry. The geomantic reasoning itself is likewise the gazetteer's, and this entry neither endorses nor rejects it.

**The examination field.**

> 万历丙午，分校南闱，多收名士，大宗伯钱谦益其首拔也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

In a *bingwu* year of the Wanli era he served as an assistant examiner in the southern examination and took many later-known men, Qian Qianyi being the first he raised. "Minister of Rites" is the title the gazetteer uses for him; the gazetteer records neither his office at the time nor a year, and this entry converts the cyclical date into nothing.

## After Lishui

> 行取御史台，直言抗疏，以忤魏珰被谪。崇祯初起大理寺卿，寻升工部侍郎，予告归。有《焚余草》诗六卷行于世。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was drawn up to the Censorate, remonstrated directly and defiantly, and was demoted for offending "Eunuch Wei"; in the early Chongzhen period he was raised to Minister of the Court of Judicial Review, soon to Vice Minister of Works, and retired with official leave. A six-juan poetry collection survives in the world. "Eunuch Wei" is the gazetteer's wording and the gazetteer does not name the man; this entry supplies no name. No date is given for any of these steps.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his reappointment, his five years there, the four concrete measures, the pagoda he promoted, and the talk of "two Xus in Lishui".

**Three datings of the pagoda, kept side by side.** This biography says only that he promoted a pagoda to block the water outlet, giving neither name nor year. This library's entry on the Yongshou Temple Pagoda records two further passages: the annals of juan 1 place the building of a pagoda by magistrate Xu Liangyan on that spot, for a purpose the gazetteer states as subduing (zhen), in the thirty-fourth year of the Wanli era, and the construction section of juan 3 records that the Yongshou Temple was built by magistrate Xu Liangyan in the thirty-sixth year of the Wanli era, its founding purpose being to block the outlet of the county's water and to make up a deficiency of the site. The two years differ, and this biography gives none. This entry records all three and selects none, and does not use the phrase "promoted the people's building of a pagoda" to settle that the building in question is the Yongshou Temple Pagoda.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, neither the year of his reappointment to Lishui nor the year he left is recorded; "five years in Lishui" gives a duration only.

Third, the year and name of the pagoda are unrecorded, and this library's other two datings disagree (see above). This entry chooses nothing.

Fourth, the "earlier magistrate Xu" is unnamed. This library's entry on Xu Bida in the same volume also describes a magistrate reappointed to Lishui on the end of mourning, which fits the period, but this volume does not name him, and this entry does not conclude that the "earlier magistrate Xu" is Xu Bida.

Fifth, the year the brick plan took effect, the exact figure of the "tiny addition" to land tax, and the scope of the Suzhou total-transport arrangement are not recorded.

Sixth, the content of the official supply arrangement and of the standing rules is not recorded.

Seventh, the amounts and scope of the waived monetary compositions are not recorded.

Eighth, the place and year of his demotion, the years of his Chongzhen-period appointments, and the year of his retirement are nowhere recorded.

Ninth, the publication history of his six-juan poetry collection, and whether this library holds it, are unrecorded and unobtained.

Tenth, the cyclical dates are given only as cyclical dates; this entry converts none of them and uses none to fix the start and end of his Lishui term.

Eleventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume. The two further datings of the pagoda belong to other juan of the same gazetteer and are kept separate from this biography.
