Madam Xu is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Ding Kebi. Her notice is shared with Madam Yang, wife of Ding Kefu; the two were sisters-in-law and are jointly styled a "pair of chaste women." This entry takes only the Xu branch; Madam Yang has a separate notice, and the two cross-refer.

## Gazetteer text

> 楊氏，丁可輔妻，年二十六；徐氏，丁可弼妻，年二十二。輔、弼俱亡，二氏誓相同守，皆無子，五十餘年冰操堅貞，足稱雙節。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, the notice gives "Madam Xu, wife of Ding Kebi, aged twenty-two," naming her as Ding Kebi's wife and attaching an age. The text does not say whether twenty-two is her age at her husband's death or at the time of writing; this entry keeps the figure without deciding.

Second, "both Fu and Bi died, and the two women swore to keep their chastity together" records that the brothers died and the two wives pledged to remain widows together.

Third, "both were childless" and "over fifty years of icy constancy and firm chastity" record their childlessness and the duration of their widowhood, "more than fifty years" being the gazetteer's approximate figure.

Fourth, "well may they be called a pair of chaste women" is the gazetteer's verdict. It does not say whether either was ever formally commended, or by whom.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Ding Kebi, whose household was her place of residence; her widowhood took place in the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty-two" is a figure the gazetteer attaches without specifying its reference, and this entry keeps it undecided.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, death year and rank of her husband Ding Kebi are unrecorded, and "more than fifty years" is approximate.

Fourth, the text says both women were childless; whether they adopted heirs, and any honour to descendants, is not recorded.

Fifth, the gazetteer does not record whether either was commended, how many times, or when; "pair of chaste women" is the gazetteer's phrase, not the title of an honour.

Sixth, this entry and the one for Madam Yang (ls:person:yang-shi-dingkefu) come from the same notice and cross-refer; only read together do they show the full "pair of chaste women."

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
