Wang Bi is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is short: one clause of origin, one of appointment, two of judgement, one on collection, one on his later career. This entry records it as it stands, keeps judgement as judgement, and quotes the clause on collection without forcing a reading on it.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 王弼，字存敬，黄岩人。成化十一年任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Cunjing, a native of Huangyan, and was appointed in the eleventh year of the Chenghua era, a definite year in this volume covering only his arrival; nothing is converted into a Common Era year. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui. The gazetteer also never gives his title in Lishui, and this entry does not assume that of county magistrate.

## What the gazetteer records

> 有操守，富于学。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Both clauses are judgement: he had moral staying-power, and he was rich in learning. Both are quoted and marked as such, and no act or writing is supplied from them.

> 催科以期，而民无后之者。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This is the only clause in the biography with any administrative content, and it is this volume's record of tax collection in Lishui. The gazetteer says only that he pressed collection "to its term" and that "none of the people were behind": what was collected, what the term was, and by what standard being "behind" was judged are nowhere explained. This entry quotes the wording as it stands, does not force a reading on it, and supplies no method, register or quota.

> 历兴化太守。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This clause records his career after Lishui, and says only that he later held a post at Xinghua under the title the gazetteer writes as *taishou*: no year is given, and this entry does not turn that word into a named office.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Huangyan" — and enters this library through official service. Two clauses tie him to the county: his appointment in the eleventh year of the Chenghua era, and the clause on pressing collection. The tag "Taxation and corvée" answers only to that clause, not to any recorded register, quota or method.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Huangyan.

Second, precision of the term: "the eleventh year of the Chenghua era" is a definite year in this volume and covers only his arrival; the year he left Lishui is not recorded, and nothing is converted into a Common Era year.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate.

Fourth, what "pressed collection to its term, and none of the people were behind" concretely means — what was collected, what the term was, and how being behind was judged — is nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies nothing.

Fifth, "he had moral staying-power, and was rich in learning" is the gazetteer's judgement; the acts and learning behind it are nowhere recorded.

Sixth, the year of the Xinghua post, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
