Yang Zhenxin is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography has three clauses: status, the grave hut, the commendation. Its commender already has an entry in this library, so this entry makes clear which text the commendation belongs to.

## The gazetteer text

> 楊振新，邑庠生。父死廬墓，知縣張錫命旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Status and deed

"County school student" is the only status the biography gives; it is the gazetteer's own wording for a student of the county school, kept as such rather than converted into any other term, and neither the year of admission nor the route into the roll is recorded. "After his father's death he kept a hut at the grave" is the biography's only deed; neither the year nor the number of years of the vigil is recorded, and the burial place of his father is nowhere given.

## The commender Zhang Ximing: this deed comes from the Filial Sons section, not from his own biography

"The district magistrate Zhang Ximing commended him" gives both the commender and his title. Three points must be made clear against an existing entry of this library.

First, this library already has an entry for Zhang Ximing (`ls:person:zhang-ximing`), resting on the officials' biography in juan 6. That biography records his appointment to Lishui, his one blind eye and his acuteness, the commutation of the grain tax, his judgement in examining students and his later promotion, and says nothing anywhere about commending a filial son; the passages in the loyal-martyrs biography and in the chronicle of the county that record the exemption of the Tianqi year are likewise unconnected with it. The commendation of Yang Zhenxin therefore reaches this library only through the Filial Sons section, and this entry records it as such rather than treating it as something Zhang Ximing's own biography says.

Second, the two titles are worded differently: the officials' biography writes only "appointed to Lishui County" and gives no formal title, while this volume writes "district magistrate". This library keeps both wordings, altering neither on the strength of the other.

Third, the year of the commendation is not recorded. What this library knows of Zhang Ximing's dating is the sexagenary "Wanli bingchen" in his own biography and the Tianqi year in other passages of this gazetteer; this entry uses neither to fix the year of Yang Zhenxin's commendation nor to infer his birth, death or active years. The `related` link to the Zhang Ximing entry is a cross-reference for checking only.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Yang Zhenxin no township and no native place; it ties him to Lishui only by making him a student of the county school. That proves study and activity in Lishui, not birth there, so this entry enters him as "active in Lishui" and not as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his status as a county school student, the hut at his father's grave, and his commendation by a magistrate of this county — the last being the only place where the biography ties him directly to a Lishui officeholder.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; neither township nor native place is recorded.

Second, his route into the school roll and the year of admission are unrecorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Third, the year and length of the grave hut and the burial place, birth and death of his father are unrecorded.

Fourth, the year of the commendation is unrecorded; this entry converts and infers nothing, and does not infer his own dates from those of the commender.

Fifth, "district magistrate Zhang Ximing" here and "appointed to Lishui County" in the officials' biography give two different wordings; this library keeps both and decides nothing, and the commendation of a filial son appears only in the Filial Sons section, not in his own biography.

Sixth, the `related` link is a cross-reference only and merges no deeds of the two men.

Seventh, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Eighth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
