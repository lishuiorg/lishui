Madam Zhai is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Zhai, her husband Lu Sihuan of Sihe Township, and records her keeping chastity, raising an orphan, serving her parents-in-law, and refusing to remarry, with an appended note on her son's filial piety and the magistrate's honour. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 翟氏，思鶴鄉陸思瓛妻。年二十一喪夫，子未及周。撫孤守志，事舅姑至孝。值歲歉，或勸以他適，因瀝血自矢，終不二天，冰操四十年而卒。子萬程幼年克孝，苦節之報也。縣令劉登科旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item follows chronological order.

"Madam Zhai, wife of Lu Sihuan of Sihe Township" gives her natal surname, her place, and her husband's name. Sihe Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written. The husband's name is kept as written, without emendation from other items in this library.

"Widowed at twenty-one, her child not yet one" opens her widowhood: her husband died when she was twenty-one, her son not yet a full year old.

"She raised the orphan and kept her resolve, and served her parents-in-law with great filial piety" gives the substance: raising the orphan and holding to her purpose, and serving her parents-in-law with utmost filial piety. Both are as the gazetteer records.

"In a year of famine, when someone urged her to marry again, she cut her flesh and swore an oath, never taking a second husband, and died after forty years of chastity" records her refusal and the years. The famine is the gazetteer's setting for the advice; the oath cut in flesh is the gazetteer's record of her vow; "never taking a second husband" is her constancy; and "forty years of chastity" is the gazetteer's figure and her death.

"Her son Wancheng was filial as a boy, the reward of her chastity" is an appended note closing on the gazetteer's judgement, "the reward of her chastity", which is a judgement, not a fact.

"The county magistrate Liu Dengke honoured her" records that the honouring official was Liu Dengke, magistrate of this county. The form and text of the honour are unrecorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Lu Sihuan of Sihe Township, keeping her chastity forty years and refusing to remarry in a famine, finally honoured by the county magistrate Liu Dengke — her community her home and the honouring official a Lishui officer. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Zhai", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at twenty-one and died after forty years of chastity; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no exact year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Zhai"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her husband's township.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Lu Sihuan and her son Wancheng; her husband's courtesy name and status, and her son's later career, are unrecorded.

Fourth, only the honouring official is recorded, not its form or text. The gazetteer says only that the magistrate Liu Dengke honoured her; the year and the plaque text are unrecorded.

Fifth, "the reward of her chastity" is the gazetteer's judgement, not factual record.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
