Madam Xue is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of the imperial-college student Xu Siyi. Hers is one of the more detailed notices in the Ming section. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 薛氏，年十六，適太學徐思毅，甫生一子，夫卒，誓守節。有窺其色強之者，氏懼迫促，適剪在傍，舉刺強者，即自刺仆地，節大彰。撫按旌其門曰「操貞矢柏」。子可登，貢入國學。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "at sixteen she married the imperial-college student Xu Siyi; she had just borne a son when her husband died, and she vowed to keep her chastity" records her marriage, child and widowhood.

Second, "a man who coveted her beauty tried to force her; in fear and urgency, with scissors at hand, she seized them and stabbed the man, then stabbed herself and fell to the ground, and her chastity became widely known." The gazetteer records this violent episode, reproduced here as given.

Third, "the governor and censor honoured her gate as 'Chaste Vow, Firm as Cypress'" records the honour. "Firm as cypress" is a phrase for constancy like that of the cypress.

Fourth, "her son Kedeng entered the Imperial College by the tribute route" records her son.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Xu Siyi, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her resistance and the honour to her gate attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "sixteen" dates her marriage, not her birth.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Xu Siyi are unrecorded.

Fourth, the assailant is unnamed, and the assault rests only on the gazetteer's account.

Fifth, the governor and censor are unnamed, and the year, number and form of the honour are unrecorded; "Chaste Vow, Firm as Cypress" is the gate-name the gazetteer gives.

Sixth, the career of her son Xu Kedeng beyond entering the Imperial College is unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
