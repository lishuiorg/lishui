Shi Kexing is one of the two men in the "Our Dynasty" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him one clause of status, the year and category of his recommendation, and the clause that he never served, and nothing else. This entry writes only what that line records and supplies no life for him.

## Status and recommendation

> 施可行邑庠生。順治九年授恩詔例，以賢良保舉。方題請，丁母艱，未仕。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"Student of the county school" is the status as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces the three characters and supplies no degree or year of entry. "Ninth year of Shunzhi" is a year the section does give, and this entry reproduces it without converting it to a Common-Era year. "Under an edict category" and "recommended on grounds of worthiness" are the route and the name of the recommendation as the gazetteer words them; this entry does not rewrite them as "jinshi" or any other degree and supplies no text of the edict relied on. The three characters "a memorial was being prepared" stand alone: what was being memorialised, and how far the process had gone, is nowhere recorded. "He was in mourning for his mother" is the section's stated reason he never served; this entry reproduces the three characters and supplies no dates and nothing about what followed.

## The clause "never served" and the role this entry gives

The section states plainly that he never served, and places the reason at his mother's death. On this basis Shi Kexing was recommended from Lishui but holds no office recorded by the gazetteer, so this entry gives him the role "local worthy" rather than "official": the section assigns him no office at all, unlike the Yongle block, whose lines each end in an office. This entry supplies no post on the strength of a recommendation or a memorial, and infers no post he might have held had mourning not intervened.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: what ties him to Lishui is that he was recommended from Lishui — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the status "student of the county school" states a school standing, which evidences a link to the county school but not a birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; the section gives him no office, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Qing" here only records that the section places him in its "Our Dynasty" block. That grouping dates his recommendation, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Qing, and the "ninth year of Shunzhi" is the year of his recommendation, from which this entry infers neither his birth, his age, nor any Common-Era year.

Third, his route into office is given only as "under an edict category" and "recommended on grounds of worthiness", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; the edict relied on is unrecorded.

Fourth, what the memorial concerned, how the process went and what its outcome was, are unrecorded; the dates of his mourning and anything after it are unrecorded.

Fifth, whether "student of the county school" suffices to establish him as a Lishui native cannot be decided from this library's material, and this entry decides nothing and only reproduces the gazetteer's wording.

Sixth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.
