Madam Xie is one of the figures in the Song section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. Her notice is very short, a single sentence, and it is word for word the same as the notice for Madam Li in the same section, only the surname differing. This entry sets out what that sentence records and what it does not, and it does not infer from the shared wording that the two women were of one house or one event.

## The gazetteer text

> 謝氏，割股療母疾。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Reading the single sentence

The notice consists of one sentence, "Madam Xie cut her thigh to cure her mother's illness", and its limits must be set out. First, "Madam Xie" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname and no personal name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Xie" as its title. Second, "cut her thigh to cure her mother's illness" is the deed the gazetteer records: she cut flesh from her thigh to cure her mother's illness. Cutting the thigh is a stock deed in the Chaste and Staunch and Filial Sons biographies of this gazetteer, and this entry reproduces it as the gazetteer's account; of what year it was, what illness the mother had and whether it worked, the gazetteer says nothing. Third, this is the whole notice: **it records no personal name, no husband, no father, no home place and no honouring**, and this entry supplies none of them. Fourth, the same section also has a notice for Madam Li, word for word identical and differing only in surname; this library does not infer from the shared wording that the two record one household, one event, or variant versions of the same person, and keeps them side by side without merging.

## Relation to Lishui

Of Madam Xie the gazetteer records only a surname and the cutting of her thigh, with no home place and no residence in Lishui. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer records no birth place or native place, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; and the biography belongs to the "Chaste and Staunch" section of this county's gazetteer and records a deed of chastity and filial devotion honoured in this county, which this library treats as her activity in Lishui, so the relation taken is "active in Lishui". The limits must be stated: the gazetteer records no home place for her, so "active in Lishui" here rests only on her appearing in this county's Chaste and Staunch biography and carries no explicit statement that she acted in Lishui; a reader must not infer from this entry that she was necessarily of Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; the notice gives no date at all.

Second, neither her personal nor her courtesy name is recorded, and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Madam Xie.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer does not even give a home place, so no native place can be decided, nor may she be called a native of Lishui merely for appearing in this county's Chaste and Staunch biography.

Fourth, neither her husband nor her father is named, and nothing of her paternal family or offspring is recorded; nothing is supplied.

Fifth, the year of the deed, the illness of the mother and her age after the deed are all unrecorded.

Sixth, the gazetteer does not say whether she was ever honoured, and nothing is inferred from other notices in the juan.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single notice in the Chaste and Staunch biography of juan 7, and this library has nothing further to add; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
