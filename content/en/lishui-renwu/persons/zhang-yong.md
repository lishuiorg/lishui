Zhang Yong is one of the Qing filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography tells two acts of self-cutting, one reported vision, a statement of the age at death and two posthumous honours: it is the most complete narrative of the "present dynasty" entries, but one of its passages belongs to recorded marvel. This entry keeps the recorded deeds and the reported marvel apart.

## The gazetteer text

> 張鏞，幼篤於孝行。母王氏病膈，幾不起，鏞刲股和藥進之，而母愈。已而母病復作，鏞復刳胸取肝為寸許者二，投盂中，遂昏仆地。閱三日，忽見一老僧握手，謂之曰：「汝母當無恙，汝今事已畢，可以行矣。」言訖不見。又一日而卒，時年二十一。縣令馮泰運旌獎之，後縣令李作楫為之作傳，題其墓曰「孝子張鏞之墓」。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## What the gazetteer records

"Steadfast in filial conduct from childhood" is the gazetteer's opening summary, and what he was steadfast in is not specified. His mother of the Wang lineage fell ill — the gazetteer uses a single character for an ailment of the chest or diaphragm, whose nature this library does not settle — and nearly died; Zhang Yong cut his thigh, compounded it into medicine and gave it to her, and she recovered. "She recovered" is the gazetteer's statement on this episode. When her illness returned, he cut open his chest and took out liver, two pieces about an inch long, put them in a bowl, and collapsed unconscious on the ground.

Two details are given here without their outcome: the size and number of the pieces, and their being placed in a bowl. How they were used in his mother's medicine, and what became of her second illness, are nowhere recorded. The years of the two acts, and the birth and death of his mother, are unrecorded.

## "He saw an old monk who took his hand": a recorded marvel

The gazetteer continues: after three days he suddenly saw an old monk who took his hand and said, "Your mother will be well; your business is now finished, and you may go"; when the words were done he was no longer seen. A day later Zhang Yong died, aged twenty-one.

Its nature must be stated: the old monk, the hand taken, the words and the vanishing are a marvel recorded by the gazetteer, and this entry reproduces them while marking them as such. It decides neither whether the mother was in fact made well, nor who the monk was, nor whether this was dream or waking. "A day later he died, aged twenty-one" is the gazetteer's record of his death and of his age at it; "twenty-one" cannot yield a birth year, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

## Commendation and biography

"The magistrate Feng Taiyun commended him, and later the magistrate Li Zuoji wrote his biography and titled his grave 'the grave of Zhang Yong the filial son'" gives two posthumous honours. The year and form of the commendation are unrecorded. Where Li Zuoji's biography was collected and whether it survives are unrecorded; this entry does not locate it by way of the tablet inscribed by "the district magistrate Li Zuoji" elsewhere in this volume, and does not decide whether the two are the same man at the same time. This library has no entries for Feng Taiyun or Li Zuoji and no material on their years in Lishui. The title on the grave is a wording the gazetteer records; whether any such stone survives and where the grave was are nowhere recorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Zhang Yong no township and no native place; it ties him to this county through being named in the filial sons section and through the commendation, the biography and the titled grave of two magistrates of this county. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is activity and honour in this county, not birth here, so it does not record him as "born locally".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; "aged twenty-one" is his age at death and cannot yield a birth year, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, township and native place are unrecorded; the biography carries no dates, and the years of the two acts are unknown.

Third, what his mother's ailment was, what became of her second illness, and her birth, death and burial are all unrecorded.

Fourth, how the two pieces of liver were used in the medicine and the course of their handling are unrecorded.

Fifth, the old monk and his vanishing are a recorded marvel, not fact; whether the mother was made well and who the monk was are not decided.

Sixth, the year and form of the commendation are unrecorded; the survival and location of Li Zuoji's biography are unrecorded; the stone and site of the titled grave are unrecorded.

Seventh, this library has no entries for Feng Taiyun or Li Zuoji and no material on their years in Lishui; whether the "district magistrate Li Zuoji" of the examination-recommendation tables is the same man at the same time cannot be determined.

Eighth, his origin is not recorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Ninth, the era field "Qing" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "present dynasty" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
