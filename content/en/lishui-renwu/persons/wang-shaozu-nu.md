Madam Wang is one of the martyred maidens recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her surname, Wang, and her father's name, Wang Shaozu. The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. This entry rests on no more than this single item in juan 7, and supplies nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 汪氏，汪紹祖女。幼能孝，歲乙酉年已及笄，亂兵掠至其村，婦皆驚竄，女嘆曰：「事勢如此，逃恐不免。」即投湖死，鄉人私諡曰「孝烈」。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records her filial conduct, her death and her private posthumous name.

"Madam Wang, daughter of Wang Shaozu" gives her surname and her father's name. Her personal name, courtesy name, native place and maternal kin are nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies none of them.

"She was filial from childhood" is the gazetteer's summary of her early filial conduct, and is recorded as such; to whom she was filial and by what acts the gazetteer does not say.

"In the yiyou year, when she had come of age" gives the year of her death and her age. The year is given only as the cyclical year "yiyou", with no reign name; this entry quotes "yiyou" as it stands, converts it to no year of the common era, and does not identify it with the "second year of Shunzhi" recorded in the item on Madam Zhou in the same section. "Come of age" is the gazetteer's term for a girl of fifteen, old enough to be betrothed; this entry quotes the term as it stands, does not render it as "fifteen", and does not infer her birth year from it.

"Disorderly troops raided her village, the women all fled in alarm, and the maiden sighed, saying that matters being so, flight would scarce save them, and straightway drowned herself in a lake" records the whole of her death: disorderly troops raided her village, the women of the village all fled in panic, and she sighed that matters being as they were, flight would scarce save them, and threw herself into a lake and died. The flight of the women is the gazetteer's record of the other women of the village, set against her own death; the gazetteer names neither the lake nor the village.

"The villagers privately gave her the posthumous name Xiaolie" records her name after death. This name was given by "the villagers", a private posthumous name from her home district, not a court honour or one granted by office; the two characters mean "filial" and "martyred", "filial" answering her childhood filial conduct and "martyred" her death for chastity. This entry quotes the words and marks them as the villagers' private posthumous name, and does not write them as an official honour or a court grant.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is this maiden's filial and martyred conduct at Lishui — she drowned herself in a lake when disorderly troops raided her village; her village must have lain within Lishui, and the villagers who gave her the private posthumous name were villagers of Lishui, so her conduct has to do with Lishui. Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or native place, giving only her surname Wang and her father's name, so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is her meeting troubles at home, not sojourning elsewhere, so "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five values, only "active in Lishui" can be grounded, and on that this entry enters the library.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives the year of her death only as "yiyou" and her age only as "come of age", and that cyclical year carries no reign name; this entry converts nothing to a year of the common era and infers neither her birth nor her death year.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The gazetteer gives only her surname Wang and her father Wang Shaozu, with neither her residence nor her maternal kin; this entry does not infer her native place from her father's name or from other items in this volume.

Third, apart from her father's name Wang Shaozu, her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her father, and records neither her mother, her brothers, nor whether she was betrothed, though it says she had come of age.

Fourth, the honours may not be complete. The gazetteer records only that the villagers privately gave her the posthumous name Xiaolie, and does not record whether she received an honour from office; a private posthumous name is not an official honour, and this entry quotes the private name as it stands and does not infer a further official honour from other items in this volume.

Fifth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer alone. It is taken from the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription".

Sixth, this library has searched its other sources and found no material that would supplement this person.
