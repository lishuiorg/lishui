Hu Yue is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is very short: two clauses of origin, one of appointment, two of judgement. This entry records it as it stands, and marks one thing the biography does not keep — it calls his benefits of rule worth recording and records none of them.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 胡玥，字朝重，襄阳人。由进士，弘治十三年任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Chaozhong, a native of Xiangyang, a *jinshi* holder, and was appointed in the thirteenth year of the Hongzhi era, a definite year in this volume covering only his arrival; nothing is converted into a Common Era year. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui. The gazetteer also never gives his title in Lishui, and this entry does not assume that of county magistrate.

## What the gazetteer records

> 恺悌得民，惠政足纪。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Both clauses are judgement: affable and winning the people, and his benefits of rule worth recording. One thing must be marked: the gazetteer says his benefits were worth recording, yet the biography records not one of them — no building, no fiscal measure, no case, no school measure. The judgement is quoted and marked as such, and nothing is supplied from the phrase "worth recording", nor from any other source on his behalf.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Xiangyang" — and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is the statement of his appointment and the two clauses of judgement. This entry is all this library has on him.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Xiangyang.

Second, precision of the term: "the thirteenth year of the Hongzhi era" is a definite year in this volume and covers only his arrival; the year he left Lishui is not recorded, and nothing is converted into a Common Era year.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate.

Fourth, the benefits of rule that "worth recording" refers to are nowhere specified, so what he did in Lishui is unrecorded.

Fifth, "affable, winning the people; his benefits of rule worth recording" is the gazetteer's judgement, and what it rested on is not recorded.

Sixth, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
