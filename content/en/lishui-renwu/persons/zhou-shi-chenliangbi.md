Madam Zhou is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Zhou, her husband Chen Liangbi, and three things: keeping chastity and raising an orphan, living past sixty, and an honour from the magistrate Li Zuoyi. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 周氏，陳良弼妻。廿二歲守節撫孤，年逾六旬。知縣李作楫旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is very short.

"Madam Zhou, wife of Chen Liangbi" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"From twenty-two she kept her chastity and raised an orphan, and lived past sixty" gives the start of her widowhood and her years: keeping chastity and raising the orphan from twenty-two, and reaching her sixties. These are the gazetteer's ages and matters; the orphan's name and later career are unrecorded.

"The magistrate Li Zuoyi honoured her" gives the honouring official as Li Zuoyi, magistrate of this county. The form and text of the honour, and the year, are unrecorded, and this entry supplies nothing.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Chen Liangbi, keeping chastity and raising an orphan from twenty-two, living past sixty, and finally honoured by the county magistrate Li Zuoyi — her community her home and the honouring official a Lishui officer. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Zhou", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she kept her chastity from twenty-two and lived past sixty; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Zhou"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Chen Liangbi; his courtesy name and status, and the orphan's name, are unrecorded.

Fourth, only the honouring official is recorded, not its form or text. The gazetteer says only that the magistrate Li Zuoyi honoured her; the year and the plaque text are unrecorded.

Fifth, the item is very short, with almost no deeds beyond her husband's name, the years of her chastity, and the honouring official, and this entry does not draw on other items to fill it out.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
