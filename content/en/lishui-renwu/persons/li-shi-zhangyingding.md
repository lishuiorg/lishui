Madam Li is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Li, her husband the farmer Zhang Yingding of Bailu Township, and two matters: early widowhood with a posthumous child, and the raising of that child together with her care for her mother-in-law. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 李氏，白鹿鄉農人張應鼎妻。年十八，適兩月而夫死，有遺腹。苦節三十餘年，撫子成人，歷盡艱楚，且養姑尤盡孝焉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is short and works in three layers.

"Madam Li, wife of the farmer Zhang Yingding of Bailu Township" gives her natal surname, her place, and her husband's status: Madam Li, wife of Zhang Yingding, a farmer of Bailu Township. Bailu Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written. "Farmer" is the occupation the gazetteer records for her husband, kept as such rather than turned into scholar or merchant.

"At eighteen she married, and two months later her husband died, leaving her with a posthumous child" gives her age and the start of her widowhood: married at eighteen, widowed two months later, and already with child. "Posthumous child" shows she was pregnant at her husband's death, the ground of the gazetteer's account of her chastity and of her raising the child.

"She kept her chastity for over thirty years, raised her son to adulthood through great hardship, and served her mother-in-law with particular filial piety" gives the years of her chastity and the raising of the child. "Over thirty years" is the gazetteer's figure; "raised her son to adulthood" is her posthumous child brought up; "through great hardship" is the gazetteer's summary; and her care for her mother-in-law is noted as especially filial. All are the gazetteer's statements; the beginning and end of the thirty years, the son's name, and the mother-in-law's age are nowhere recorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Zhang Yingding of Bailu Township, widowed young, keeping her chastity over thirty years, raising her orphan and serving her mother-in-law, the township being her home. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Li", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at eighteen and kept her chastity over thirty years; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Li"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her husband's township.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Zhang Yingding; his courtesy name and dates, and her son's name and later career, are unrecorded.

Fourth, "over thirty years" is the gazetteer's approximate figure; the start and end are unrecorded, and this entry infers no death year from it.

Fifth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award, and this entry infers none from other items.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
