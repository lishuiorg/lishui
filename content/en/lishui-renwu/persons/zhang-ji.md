Zhang Ji is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography has one sentence of origin, one of appointment, two clauses of judgement and one clause of popular reputation, and records no particular measure. This entry writes only what it contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 章籍，浦城人，枢密衡之孙。绍兴间任，为政清勤，且平易而法不挠，民颂之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was a native of Pucheng and is called "a grandson of the Privy Councillor Heng": here the gazetteer refers to his grandfather by office and gives only his given name, supplying no surname, and this entry reproduces the wording as transcribed without supplying a name or recounting any deeds. No father is recorded. "Appointed during the Shaoxing era" gives a span, not a year; this entry converts nothing, infers nothing from the dynasty, and fixes no start or end of his term. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own.

## What the gazetteer records

Two clauses describe how he governed. "Clear and diligent in government" judges his clarity and his diligence. "Easy-going, yet not bending the law" judges his manner and the firmness of his legal practice; the gazetteer offers no gloss on the word rendered here as "bending", and this entry adds none. The further clause "the people praised him" is the gazetteer's account of popular reputation — a claim about praise on the streets rather than a verifiable record. The biography cites no act and records no measure, and this entry infers no building, no case and no tax matter from the judgements.

## Relation to Lishui

Zhang Ji was a native of Pucheng, not of Lishui, and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is the sentence giving his appointment during the Shaoxing era, the judgements on his government, and the reported praise; nothing he did in Lishui is recorded beyond that, and neither his leaving nor his later movements are given.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Pucheng.

Second, the appointment is given only as "during the Shaoxing era", a span rather than a year, and the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, the gazetteer supplies no surname for the grandfather it calls "Privy Councillor Heng"; his name, rank and career, and the name of Zhang Ji's own father, are all unrecorded, and this entry supplies none.

Fifth, the judgements on his government and the reported praise are the gazetteer's, and the grounds for them are unrecorded.

Sixth, any concrete acts in Lishui, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him.
