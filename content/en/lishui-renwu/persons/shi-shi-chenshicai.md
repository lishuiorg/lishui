Madam Shi is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Shi, her husband Chen Shicai, and the record of her chastity and of the magistrate Min Pailu's petition on her behalf, closing with the gazetteer's own comment. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 施氏，陳時才妻。苦節三十五年，知縣閔派魯以通庠公舉核申學憲，其略云：「節婦施氏，于歸一載即失所天，茹苦多年，又傷無後。勤織養高堂之老，毀容翻歷代之書，所當亟賜旌表，風勵末俗者也。」其見重於縣令如此。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is layered.

"Madam Shi, wife of Chen Shicai" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"She kept her chastity thirty-five years, and the magistrate Min Pailu, on a petition by the whole county school, forwarded the case to the education commissioner" gives the years and the petition. "Thirty-five years" is the gazetteer's figure; the petition and its forwarding are recorded, with "whole county school" meaning the county's students and "education commissioner" the provincial education officer. The year of the petition and the commissioner's reply are unrecorded.

"The petition said in substance: 'The chaste widow Shi, who lost her husband a year after marrying and has endured hardship for years, and grieved that she has no heir, wove diligently to support the elders of the household and, marring her face, read through the books of the dynasties; she should be promptly honoured, to encourage the fading customs of the age.'" This is the gazetteer's epitome of the petition, and it is the core of the item, giving the concrete circumstances of her chastity: widowed a year after marriage, no heir, supporting her elders, and reading. The words are the petition's, kept as written.

"That she was held in such regard by the magistrate" is the gazetteer's comment, a judgement rather than a fact.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Chen Shicai, keeping chastity thirty-five years, and finally with the county magistrate Min Pailu forwarding her case to the education commissioner on the county school's petition — her community and the petition both in Lishui. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Shi", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only thirty-five years of chastity; it gives no year of birth, of marriage, or of her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Shi"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Chen Shicai; his courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and while the petition states that she had no heir, whether she adopted one is unrecorded.

Fourth, the petition is given only in epitome ("in substance"), not in full; the year of the petition, the commissioner's reply, and the outcome of the honour are all unrecorded.

Fifth, "that she was held in such regard by the magistrate" is the gazetteer's comment, not factual record.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
