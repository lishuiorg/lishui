Guan Qi is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is one of the shorter ones among the Song entries: one sentence of origin and appointment, two clauses of judgement, and one sentence about the promotion that followed his term. This entry goes no further than that paragraph.

## Origin and term

> 关起，字蔚宗。崇宁二年以太子中允出知溧水县，机务整饬，肇兴学校。任满，擢广西常平太守。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was courtesy-named Weizong and, in the second year of the Chongning era, left the office the gazetteer calls *Taizi zhongyun* to take charge of Lishui. Unlike most entries in this volume, this one does state the Lishui office: the gazetteer writes *zhi Lishui xian*, and this entry renders it as it stands. *Taizi zhongyun* is the office he is said to have held before coming to Lishui, and the verb rendered here as "left" is the gazetteer's own wording. The Chongning year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty. His departure is stated only as "the end of his term", with no year.

## What the gazetteer records

All that is said of him in Lishui is two clauses: his ordering of affairs and the starting of the schools. The first is the gazetteer's judgement of how he ran things and cites no act and no measure. The second is the only clause in the biography that points at a concrete matter in Lishui, but it says only that he began something concerning the schools — which school, what was done, whether anything was built — none of this is recorded, and this entry supplies no detail about schools.

After Lishui the biography says only that he was promoted to *Guangxi changping taishou*. The promotion is not dated; the title is given in the gazetteer's own words, reproduced here as a transliteration, and this entry neither recasts it as another office nor adds anything of his later career or his death.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records no native place for him, and this entry enters him through official service. What ties him to the county is the sentence giving his appointment and the two clauses of judgement; nothing he did in Lishui is recorded beyond them.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, and neither is his native place.

Second, the appointment is given only as the second Chongning year, which is the year of arrival; his leaving is given only as the end of his term, undated. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer states his Lishui office as *zhi Lishui xian*, and this entry keeps that formulation rather than substituting another title.

Fourth, what "the starting of the schools" actually consisted of — building, endowment, teaching — is not recorded.

Fifth, "his ordering of affairs" is the gazetteer's judgement, and the grounds for it are not recorded.

Sixth, the year of the promotion, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded; the title *Guangxi changping taishou* is the gazetteer's wording and this entry makes no guess about its formal name.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
