Guo Yun is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and the first Ming entry in the volume. His biography is comparatively long for this volume: it gives his appointment, the condition of the county when he arrived, the building of the county office, his integrity, and his reassignment and hereditary succession after leaving Lishui. This entry records all of that, and separates out the institutional change the entry carries.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 郭云，随州人。洪武二年任溧水州，寻改知县。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was a native of Suizhou, appointed to Lishui Prefecture in the second year of the Hongwu era, and "shortly afterwards changed to county magistrate".

What this line records is an institutional change. The gazetteer places Guo Yun at the moment Lishui was changed from a prefecture into a county: his first appointment is written as service in "Lishui Prefecture" and the later one as "county magistrate", so a single term spans both stages. This is the only record this library has of the prefecture-to-county change of Lishui that comes from a person's biography rather than from an administrative narrative, and its value lies in *who was in post at the change*, not in the dates of the change itself. Two boundaries hold. The gazetteer gives no year for the change, and this entry neither infers one from "the second year of the Hongwu era" nor converts anything into a Common Era year. The gazetteer also never states his formal title in the prefecture stage, and this entry supplies none.

## What the gazetteer records

> 会兵燹之后，邑甚凋敝，云至力任之。建置县署，百废具兴，而益持以廉，士民咸悦。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"After the fires of war" is the gazetteer's account of the county's condition on his arrival; it names no event and no year, and this entry identifies no specific war with it. "The county was much wasted" and "Yun took it up with all his strength" are the gazetteer's narration of his conduct. "Built the county office" is one clue this volume yields for the building of the Lishui county office; its site, layout, date and later history are nowhere recorded. "What had collapsed was all revived" is the gazetteer's general verdict and "the literati and the people were all pleased" is its account of popular response; neither is a verifiable record, and both are quoted and marked as such, with no concrete measure inferred from them.

> 后以膂力谋略被召，改为南阳卫指挥。抚绥军民，俾以乐业。子孙世袭其职。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

These three sentences concern his life after Lishui: summoned for his strength and strategy, made commander of the Nanyang Guard, pacifying soldiers and people so that each might keep to its livelihood, and his descendants inheriting the post hereditarily. The Nanyang Guard was not in Lishui; the year of the summons and the course of the hereditary succession are not recorded, and this entry only transcribes them.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Suizhou" — and enters this library through official service. Three things tie him to the county: the appointment written first as prefecture and then as county; the building of the county office and the reviving of what had collapsed; and his integrity together with the reputation recorded as pleasing the literati and the people. The first is this library's person-side record of the prefecture-to-county change of Lishui; the second is this volume's clue for the county office. The tags "Administrative history" and "Military affairs" answer respectively to the first of these and to the single sentence on the condition of the county after the fires of war — not to any recorded course of a battle.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Suizhou.

Second, precision of the term: "the second year of the Hongwu era" is a definite year in this volume and covers only his first appointment; the two words rendered "shortly afterwards changed" carry no year, and this entry neither infers the year of Lishui's change of status from the Hongwu year nor converts it.

Third, his title in Lishui: the gazetteer writes first service in the prefecture and then county magistrate; this entry keeps the two stages as the gazetteer has them and does not merge them into "county magistrate from the second year of the Hongwu era". His formal title in the prefecture stage is not given.

Fourth, what event and what year the "fires of war" were is not recorded.

Fifth, the site, layout, completion year and later history of the county office he built are not recorded.

Sixth, the year of the summons, where the Nanyang Guard was, what his pacifying consisted of, and how the hereditary post passed down are all unrecorded.

Seventh, "what had collapsed was all revived" and "the literati and the people were all pleased" are the gazetteer's verdict and narration, and what they rested on is not recorded.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition.
