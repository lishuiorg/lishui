Madam Zhu is one of the chaste widows recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her surname, Zhu, and her husband's name, Wang Zhongju. The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. This entry rests on no more than this single item in juan 7, and supplies nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 朱氏，王仲舉妻。年二十六夫歿，毀容守節，茹素衣布，機杼不輟，凡四十一年。卒能教子揚名中順治辛卯武闈鄉試。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records her chastity and her teaching of her son.

"Madam Zhu, wife of Wang Zhongju" gives her surname and her husband's name. Her personal name, courtesy name, native place and paternal kin are nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies none of them.

"Her husband died when she was twenty-six" is the gazetteer's record: her husband Wang Zhongju died when she was twenty-six. "Twenty-six" is an exact figure and is quoted as given; this entry does not infer her birth year from it.

"She disfigured herself and kept chastity, eating vegetarian food and wearing plain cloth, never ceasing to weave, for forty-one years" is the gazetteer's record of her conduct and the length of her widowhood: she disfigured her face as a sign that she would not remarry, kept a vegetarian diet, wore plain cloth, and never stopped weaving, for forty-one years in all. Each of "disfigured herself", "vegetarian food and plain cloth" and "never ceasing to weave" records her conduct as the gazetteer gives it; the forty-one years is the length of widowhood the gazetteer gives.

"In the end she taught her son, who made his name by passing the military provincial examination in the xinmao year of Shunzhi" is the fruit of her teaching: her son finally made his name and passed the military examination. The year is given as "the xinmao year of Shunzhi", a reign name and a cyclical year together, and this entry quotes it as it stands, converts it to no year of the common era, and does not reword it. "The military provincial examination" is the degree route the gazetteer records; the gazetteer does not give her son's name.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is this woman's conduct of chastity at Lishui — forty-one years of widowhood and the teaching of her son to pass the military examination; her son, a man of this county, is set in this county's biographies of chaste and martyred women, so her conduct has to do with Lishui. Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or native place, calling her only by her surname Zhu, so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is her keeping widowhood at home, not sojourning elsewhere, so "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five values, only "active in Lishui" can be grounded, and on that this entry enters the library.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that her husband died when she was twenty-six and that she was chaste for forty-one years, without her birth or death year, and this entry makes no reckoning from them.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Zhu" and records neither her paternal kin nor her residence; this entry does not infer her native place from her husband's surname or from other items in this volume.

Third, apart from her husband's name Wang Zhongju, her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. The gazetteer records that she had a son who passed the military provincial examination, but gives his name, rank and later life nowhere, nor her parents and brothers.

Fourth, the honours may not be complete. This item does not record whether she received honours, and this entry does not infer it from other items in this volume.

Fifth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer alone. It is taken from the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription".

Sixth, this library has searched its other sources and found no material that would supplement this person.
