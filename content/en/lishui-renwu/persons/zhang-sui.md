Zhang Sui is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is among the more complete Ming entries in the volume: it gives origin and post, reports an exchange of words, records his death and what he left, names two structures he built, and adds a kinsman of the same clan. This entry records all of it, and separates out the two things that cannot be settled — a possible same name, and his title.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 张璲，顺天府霸州人。弘治间由吏员任县丞
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was a native of Bazhou in Shuntian Prefecture, reached by way of clerk origin, and appointed county vice-magistrate *xiancheng* "during the Hongzhi era". The post must be stated as the gazetteer states it: the biography says expressly *xiancheng*, not county magistrate; the "distinguished officials" of this volume are not limited to magistrates, and this entry never writes him as magistrate of Lishui. The gazetteer gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts the era span nor infers a year from the dynasty. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself. It also never gives a courtesy name for him.

## What the gazetteer records

> 倜傥有大志，而守甚介。或讽云，世方用人以格，君何自苦为也？应之曰：格能限璲官耳，能限璲乎？
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Unrestrained and with great ambitions, yet very strict in keeping" is the gazetteer's judgement. What follows is a reported exchange: someone remonstrated with him, saying the age employs people by a fixed standard and asking why he made things hard for himself, and he answered, in which he names himself. This entry quotes it as speech reported by the gazetteer, not as a verbatim record of what was said. What the "standard" of "employs people by a standard" was, the gazetteer never explains, and this entry does not interpret it; who remonstrated is not recorded.

> 后升州同，不及赴而卒。萧然遗一囊也，官为之殓。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was later promoted to a post the gazetteer writes only as *zhoutong*, but died before he could take it up, leaving nothing but a single bag, and an official buried him. Which post that promotion was, in what year it came, and the year and place of his death are nowhere recorded; which official "an official buried him" refers to is likewise not recorded, and this entry infers no place or post from the word.

> 尝建泮宫坊、永丰仓。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

These two clauses are two clues this library has for buildings of Ming Lishui: an archway named for the school, and a granary named Yongfeng. The gazetteer records only that he "once built" them; it gives no location, form, building year or account of their survival, and this entry supplies none, nor does it identify either with any surviving remain.

> 后有张令名锡者，其族子也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The biography adds that later there was "Zhang the magistrate, named Xi", a kinsman of his clan. This library has a separate entry for a Zhang Xi, whom this volume records as appointed in the first year of the Zhengde era, courtesy name Weifan, a jinshi holder. Whether that "Zhang the magistrate named Xi" is the same man is nowhere stated by the gazetteer, and this entry does not identify them; the two are kept side by side without choosing (see the entry on Zhang Xi). What generation the gazetteer's word for kinsman means, and the career of the man named Xi, are also unrecorded.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Bazhou in Shuntian Prefecture" — and enters this library through official service. Three things tie him to the county: his post as county vice-magistrate; the archway and granary he built; and the manner of his death and burial together with the kinsman who followed. The second is this library's two clues for buildings of Ming Lishui — one archway, one granary, named by the gazetteer as school-related and as Yongfeng, with no site and no account of survival. The tag here is only "Granaries", answering to the granary clause; the biography records nothing on taxation or corvée, so no such tag is given.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; the gazetteer gives him no courtesy name; his native place is given only as Bazhou in Shuntian Prefecture.

Second, precision of the term: the gazetteer says only "during the Hongzhi era", gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts it nor infers a year from the dynasty.

Third, his post: the gazetteer says expressly county vice-magistrate, not county magistrate, and this entry never writes him as magistrate of Lishui; what post the promotion written as *zhoutong* was is not recorded.

Fourth, the location, form, building year and survival of the archway and the granary are nowhere recorded.

Fifth, whether "Zhang the magistrate named Xi" is the same person as the Zhang Xi of this library's separate entry — whom this volume records as appointed in the first year of the Zhengde era — is nowhere stated, and this entry does not decide; what generation the gazetteer's word for kinsman means and the career of the man named Xi are also unrecorded.

Sixth, which official buried him, and the year and place of his death, are nowhere recorded.

Seventh, what the "standard" in the reported exchange meant is left unexplained by the gazetteer, and who remonstrated is not recorded.

Eighth, "unrestrained and with great ambitions, yet very strict in keeping" is the gazetteer's judgement, and the reported exchange has no recorded context.

Ninth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
