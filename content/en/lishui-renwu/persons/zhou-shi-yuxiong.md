Madam Zhou is one of the martyred women recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her surname, Zhou, and her husband under the two characters "Yu Xiong". The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. This entry rests on no more than this single item in juan 7, and supplies nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 周氏，愈熊妻。順治二年，亂兵經其鄉，挾之馬上，氏誓不受污，伺過橋，奮躍入水死，時有婢從，亦同死。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records a single matter.

"Madam Zhou, wife of Yu Xiong" gives her surname and her husband's name. The gazetteer gives her husband only as the two characters "Yu Xiong", without a surname, a courtesy name or a status; this entry quotes the characters as given and supplies neither his surname nor his career. Her personal name, courtesy name, native place and paternal kin are nowhere recorded.

"In the second year of Shunzhi, disorderly troops passed through her village" gives the year of the troubles and the march of the troops. The reign-year phrase "the second year of Shunzhi" is the gazetteer's own wording and is quoted as it stands, not converted to a year of the common era. The troops "passed through her village", and that village must have lain within Lishui; but the gazetteer does not say which village or which district it was.

"They seized her and put her on a horse, and she vowed not to suffer defilement" is the gazetteer's record of her capture and her resolve: the disorderly troops seized her and set her on a horse, and she vowed not to be defiled.

"Watching for a bridge, she leapt into the water and died" is the deed recorded in this item: she waited for a bridge, sprang into the water and died. The word "watched" records her waiting for the moment; the gazetteer names neither the bridge nor the water, and this entry supplies neither.

"A maidservant was with her at the time, and died likewise" is an appended matter the gazetteer records: a maidservant attended her and died with her. The maidservant's surname and age are not recorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is this woman's martyrdom at Lishui — in the second year of Shunzhi disorderly troops passed through her village, seized her on horseback, and she leapt into the water and died; her village must have lain within Lishui, so her conduct has to do with Lishui. Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or native place, calling her only by her surname Zhou, so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is her meeting troubles at home, not sojourning elsewhere, so "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five values, only "active in Lishui" can be grounded, and on that this entry enters the library.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer ties the year of the troubles only to "the second year of Shunzhi", without her birth or death year, and this entry infers neither.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Zhou" and records neither her paternal kin nor her residence, saying only that the troops passed through her village; this entry does not infer her native place from her husband's name or from other items in this volume.

Third, apart from her husband's name "Yu Xiong", her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband no surname, courtesy name or status, and records no children, parents or brothers.

Fourth, the honours may not be complete. This item does not record whether she received honours after death, and this entry does not infer it from other items in this volume, nor extend to her the honour recorded for another woman in the same section as conferred by the county, prefecture and province.

Fifth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer alone. It is taken from the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription".

Sixth, this library has searched its other sources and found no material that would supplement this person.
