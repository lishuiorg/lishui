Madam Chen is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui recorded in the "Biographies of the Chaste" (zhenlie zhuan) in juan 7 of the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer. The gazetteer names her as the wife of Wang Shi and devotes only eight characters to her, making her the briefest notice in the Ming section. This entry rests on that single record and adds nothing to it.

## Gazetteer text

> 陳氏，王仕妻。夫死守節，辛苦存孤。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

The notice says only that she "kept her chastity after her husband's death and toiled to preserve the orphan." The first half records that she did not remarry after her husband died; the second half records that she brought up the child left behind. Whether there was one child or several, their names, and whether they later prospered are all unrecorded. The text gives no birth or death years, no biography, rank or death year for her husband Wang Shi, no number of years she lived as a widow, and no commendation. Unlike many notices in the same juan, it does not even name an official who honoured her. Nothing here is inferred from other notices or other books.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Wang Shi, whose household was her place of residence; her conduct as a chaste widow took place in the Lishui countryside. The text states neither where she was born nor that she was a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based solely on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; this entry neither converts nor guesses them.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name, and no other passage permits a determination.

Third, the husband's lineage, the children, and any honorific titles are entirely unrecorded; the text says only that she preserved the orphan, without number, name or outcome.

Fourth, whether she was ever commended, and if so how many times and in what years, is not recorded.

Fifth, this entry rests solely on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, which has no counterpart elsewhere; the text used is a network transcription, not checked against a critical or printed edition.
