Xiao Shuyu is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him two items — the route of presentation and the office held — and nothing else. This entry writes those two items, adds the one clause of kinship the section itself supplies, and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 蕭叔裕由人材，任四川夔州府經歷。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of persons of talent" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces it and does not convert it into "jinshi" or any other degree. "Served as recorder of Kuizhou prefecture in Sichuan" is the office as the gazetteer words it, and it lay in Sichuan, not in Lishui. What a recorder did, and when he took up and left the post, is nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies no duties and no dates. This block gives no years at all, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of appointment can be known.

## The kinship clause the section itself supplies

A further line in the same block inserts a clause of kinship between the name and the route into office:

> 蕭文舉叔裕孫。由太學，任翰林院孔目。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

The name "Shuyu" appears in this section only in this one place. On that basis the section takes Xiao Wenju as a grandson of Xiao Shuyu, and this entry therefore links to this library's entry for him. Three points must be stated. First, this is the gazetteer's own statement of kinship: this entry reproduces it and marks it as such, and reasons from it to no birth or death year and to no sequence of dates between the two men. Second, both lines stand in the same Hongwu block, which carries no dates, and no sequence is inferred from the order in which the two lines appear. Third, the line quoted here is a roster line of the Hongwu block, not text from the arts-and-literature section of juan 8.

## The biography in juan 8, arts and literature

This library has also seen, in the arts-and-literature section of juan 8 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, a biography of this man by the Ming writer Duanmu Xiaowen, written at the request of his grandson Xiao Wenju (the text says Wenju brought him his grandfather's life-record and begged him for a biography). It records far more than the Recommendations examination does, and three things bear on this entry.

First, his origins and his move. The biography says:

> 公諱叔裕，先本蘭陵，自建炎扈遷，後世為金陵人。金陵地沃，漸六朝侈靡習，公性嗜澹朴，弗愜也，遂卜遷於我溧。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 8, arts and literature

"By origin of Lanling" gives his forebears' seat; "following the imperial progress in the Jianyan reign, his descendants became men of Jinling" places the family's southward move in the Southern Song; and "he then chose to move to our Lishui" states plainly that he settled his household in Lishui. The Recommendations examination records no native place for him, and this biography adds the household's establishment in Lishui — the only old-gazetteer text this library knows to give his origins and his settling in Lishui.

Second, his career. The biography says he entered office by summons as recorder of the Kuizhou prefecture, which agrees with the examination's "by way of persons of talent, recorder of Kuizhou prefecture in Sichuan", the two corroborating each other. It further records his work in Kuizhou: the prefecture fronted the river and was a haunt of bandits whom the clerks could not check, and he took their chief by a stratagem, so the robbers vanished; when Shu had just been pacified and Kuizhou, at the mouth of the gorge, was a throat of the frontier, he oversaw the garrison's military farming and, following and restoring Meng Gong's old methods, worked the water conservancy and spurred on the idle, so that his fields yielded the most of any such farm; and when Taizu held to harsh law and punished petty faults of officials with branding and death, he, who had never cared for office, pleaded illness and went home. None of this appears in the examination.

Third, his family and character. The biography calls him a lifelong lover of books and of the great principles, and says that his grandson Xiao Wenju was already a remarkable child while still wearing his hair in tufts, and that the grand censor Yao, esteeming him, wished to marry his daughter to Wenju. This corroborates the examination's clause making Xiao Wenju his grandson.

One limit must be stated. The biography breaks off mid-sentence at "余方欲勞管城力，" — as the transcription this library relies on gives it, the text stops there and what followed is lost. Whether it recorded anything further, and how it closed, is therefore unknown; this entry supplies nothing from the broken text and does not rewrite the examination's two items on its strength.

## Relation to Lishui

The Recommendations examination gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui; the juan 8 biography, by contrast, gives his forebears at Lanling, the family's later move to Jinling, and his own settling in Lishui (see the section above). He nevertheless enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his office lay in a Sichuan prefecture and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available. Though the juan 8 biography has him settle in Lishui, only one value is taken per entry, and the relation stands on the examination's roster line. The clause of kinship states his relation to Xiao Wenju, not his relation to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded; his native place and family are given only by the juan 8 biography (forebears of Lanling, descendants men of Jinling, and his own settling in Lishui — see the section above), not by the Recommendations examination.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of persons of talent", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "recorder of Kuizhou prefecture in Sichuan" is an office title whose duties and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, nothing is recorded about what the section's kinship clause rests on. The other line writes only "a grandson of Shuyu"; this entry takes the two "Shuyu" references to be one and the same man, but that judgement rests on the section's own wording alone and no other material allows it to be checked.

Fifth, the arts-and-literature section of juan 8 of the same book carries Duanmu Xiaowen's biography of him, which is the source of the section above. That biography breaks off mid-sentence at "余方欲勞管城力，" and is not complete: as the transcription this library relies on gives it, the text stops there. Whether it recorded anything further, and how it closed, is therefore unknown, and nothing is supplied from the broken text. An earlier draft of this entry said this library had not recorded that text; the source card now transcribes it, so that statement is superseded.

Sixth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Seventh, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block and takes one further line of the same block only for the kinship clause; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him. The section used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
