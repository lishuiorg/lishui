Zhang Wenxing is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is short but, unlike most Ming entries in the volume, adds a stretch of career before Lishui — demotion for remonstrating as a censor — and a popular nickname. This entry records it as it stands, keeping judgement and nickname as judgement and nickname.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 张问行，字子书，内黄人。由进士，以御史言事被谪，嘉靖九年任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Zishu, a native of Neihuang, a *jinshi* holder, demoted for remonstrating while serving as a censor, and appointed in the ninth year of the Jiajing era. The clause on demotion is the biography's account of his life before Lishui: that he held a censor's post and was punished for what he said. What he said, when he was demoted and to what post are nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies nothing. "The ninth year of the Jiajing era" is a definite year in this volume covering only his arrival; nothing is converted into a Common Era year. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui. The gazetteer also never gives his title in Lishui, and this entry does not assume that of county magistrate.

## What the gazetteer records

> 善察，屡摘民间阴事，呼为神明宰。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Good at inspection" is the gazetteer's judgement. "Repeatedly uncovering hidden matters among the people" is its narration: what the matters were, what cases resulted and by what method they were uncovered are nowhere recorded, and no case or investigation is supplied from the clause. "People called him the godlike magistrate" is a name attributed to him by others in the gazetteer's report — contemporary assessment and a nickname, not to be stated as fact; the word for magistrate inside that nickname is the gazetteer's wording within a reported epithet, and this entry does not use it to supply his formal title.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Neihuang" — and enters this library through official service. Three things tie him to the county: the statement of his appointment; the narration of his inspection and of uncovering hidden matters; and the nickname reported for him. The last two are the gazetteer's judgement, narration and reported epithet, not verifiable records.

One relation to this library's entry on Wang Congshan must be stated. This volume separately records Gao Chong as appointed in the seventh year of the Jiajing era and Zhang Wenxing in the ninth, and the entry on Wang Congshan uses those two to set out three incompatible accounts of the lower limit of his term in Lishui. This entry records only what this volume says about Zhang Wenxing's appointment: the volume never gives the year his predecessor handed over, so this entry does not use "appointed in the ninth year" to conclude that Wang Congshan had left office by then. A successor's arrival shows only that someone took up the post that year; it records nothing about when the previous holder left.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Neihuang.

Second, precision of the term: "the ninth year of the Jiajing era" is a definite year in this volume and covers only his arrival; the year he left Lishui is not recorded, and nothing is converted into a Common Era year.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate; the word for magistrate inside the reported nickname is not used to supply it.

Fourth, what he said as a censor, when he was demoted and to what post are all unrecorded.

Fifth, what the "hidden matters" were and what cases they produced is not recorded; "people called him the godlike magistrate" is a reported epithet and assessment, and what it rested on is unrecorded.

Sixth, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this volume records no year of handover by his predecessor, and this entry does not use his appointment year to fix any predecessor's year of departure.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
