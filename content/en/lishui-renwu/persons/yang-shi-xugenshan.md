Madam Yang is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the second wife of Xu Genshan, magistrate of Pengze. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 楊氏，彭澤令許根善繼室。年十六歸許，生子甫周，夫即歿於官。截髮自矢，紡織教子，持冰霜操四十餘年。督學陳給米帛，直指屢旌其廬。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "second wife of Xu Genshan, magistrate of Pengze" names husband and wife. "Magistrate of Pengze" is Xu Genshan's office; "second wife" shows she was not the first.

Second, "she married into the Xu family at sixteen, bore a son barely a year old, and her husband died in office" records her marriage and the death of her husband at his post.

Third, "she cut off her hair as a vow, wove and taught her son, and kept a frost-like constancy for over forty years" records her widowhood. Cutting her hair was a vow not to remarry; "frost-like constancy" is the gazetteer's metaphor, and "over forty years" is approximate.

Fourth, "the education commissioner Chen gave her rice and cloth, and the circuit censor repeatedly honoured her gate" records her commendations. The commissioner surnamed Chen is unnamed; the circuit censor repeatedly honoured her gate, but the number and dates are not given.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Xu Genshan, whose household was her place of residence; her widowhood, her teaching of her son, and the honours she received all attach to the Xu family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "sixteen" dates her marriage, not her birth.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the career and death year of Xu Genshan beyond his office are unrecorded, and it is not stated whether he died in Pengze or elsewhere.

Fourth, whether the infant son reached adulthood is unrecorded, and the descendants are not fully described.

Fifth, the education commissioner Chen is only partly named; the repeated honouring by the circuit censor gives no number, year or tablet inscription.

Sixth, "over forty years" is approximate.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
