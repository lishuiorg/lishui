Madam Yin is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the concubine of Mao Kangqu. She is the only one of the four women of the Mao household distinguished as a concubine. This entry takes only the Yin branch and cross-refers to the others.

## Gazetteer text

> 傅氏，庠士茆康宇妻；胡氏，庠士茆康年妻；陳氏，茆康衢妻，妾尹氏，俱年二十餘，相率苦節，世所罕有。幸各有子，庠生吉旋，傅出，吉常、吉相，咸秉母訓，能自樹立。貞潔之氣萃於一門，足風閨閫者也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "Madam Chen, wife of Mao Kangqu; the concubine Yin-shi" gives their status: Madam Chen was his principal wife and Madam Yin his concubine, the latter attached by the gazetteer under "wife of Mao Kangqu."

Second, "all in their twenties, keeping a bitter chastity together, which the world rarely sees" describes the four women, and the gazetteer counts the concubine alongside the wives. "In their twenties" is approximate.

Third, "each fortunately had a son." The gazetteer later names only Jixuan as Madam Fu's; whether Madam Yin's son is Jichang or Jixiang it does not say, and this entry does not assign it.

Fourth, "the spirit of chastity gathered in one household" is the gazetteer's verdict on the family.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Mao Kangqu, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity attaches to the Mao family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "in their twenties" is approximate and is not converted.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life and death year of Mao Kangqu and his son's name are not clearly recorded; as a concubine she is recorded without any account of her origins or the time she entered the household.

Fourth, Jichang and Jixiang are not assigned by the gazetteer to a particular mother, so this entry does not assign Madam Yin's son.

Fifth, the gazetteer does not record whether any of the four was honoured, how many times, or when.

Sixth, this entry cross-refers to the notices for Madam Fu (ls:person:fu-shi-maokangyu), Madam Hu (ls:person:hu-shi-maokangnian) and Madam Chen (ls:person:chen-shi-maokangqu).

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
