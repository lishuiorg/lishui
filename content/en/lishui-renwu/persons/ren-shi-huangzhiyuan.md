Madam Ren is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer. The gazetteer names her as the wife of Huang Zhiyuan and devotes barely four clauses to her. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 任氏，黃志遠妻。少而寡，誓存遺腹，年八十終。歐陽撫台獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "widowed young" records that she lost her husband early; her exact age is not given.

Second, "vowed to preserve her posthumous child" records her resolve to keep the child unborn at her husband's death. "Posthumous child" means a child not yet born when the husband died; the text says only that she vowed to preserve it, without stating whether it was a boy or a girl or whether it was raised to adulthood.

Third, "lived to eighty and died" gives the only age in the notice: she was eighty at death. The text gives no birth year and no count of the years she lived as a widow.

Fourth, "a provincial governor surnamed Ouyang honoured her" records the commendation. "Governor" renders the colloquial term for a provincial governor used here; the honoree's surname was Ouyang, his personal name is not given, and neither the year nor the number of the honours is recorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Huang Zhiyuan, whose household was her place of residence; her conduct as a chaste widow belongs to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given beyond the single figure of eighty at death; this entry does not derive a birth year from it or convert it to a Western date.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the sex of the posthumous child, whether it reached adulthood, and its name are unrecorded; the text says only that she vowed to preserve it, and the outcome is left unstated.

Fourth, the rank, life and death year of her husband Huang Zhiyuan are unrecorded.

Fifth, the governor Ouyang is only partly named, and the year, number and form of the honours, including any inscription on a tablet, are unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
