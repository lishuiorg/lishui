Madam Jing is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Jing, her husband Yu Jiajing, and two things: her widowhood and the entry of her two sons into the county school. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 經氏，俞嘉景妻。二十六歲孀居。長子衷恆、次子衷直，俱業儒入泮。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is very short.

"Madam Jing, wife of Yu Jiajing" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"Widowed at twenty-six" gives the age at which her widowhood began. This is the gazetteer's age; the number of years she kept her chastity, the year of her husband's death, and the year of her own death are all unrecorded.

"Her eldest son Zhongheng and second son Zhongzhi both studied the classics and entered the county school" gives her sons' names and their studies: both engaged in classical study and entered the county school as students. This is the fruit of her motherly instruction as the gazetteer records it, and naming two sons is rare in this volume; the year of their entry and their later careers are unrecorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Yu Jiajing, widowed at twenty-six and raising two sons who both entered the county school, the household and her teaching belonging to the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Jing", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her — her sons' school entry is their study, not her own recommendation — so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at twenty-six; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, no number of years of chastity, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Jing"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Yu Jiajing and her two sons Zhongheng and Zhongzhi; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had other children is unrecorded.

Fourth, the year her two sons entered the county school and their later careers are unrecorded; this entry does not supply their examination years from other volumes such as the examination tables, nor treat their school entry as her own recommendation.

Fifth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award, and this entry infers none from other items.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
