Madam Liu is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer. She is the only notice in the Ming section that quotes the text of an imperial grant. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 劉氏，贈承德郎主事王崇妻。生子數月即喪夫，撫孤名登成進士，具疏陳情。萬曆間敕贈安人，謂其「青年砥節，白首流慈。厥天無年而有年，厥子無父而有父。延宗脈，勤課訓，刻刻皆辛苦，字字皆血淚」雲。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Wang Chong, posthumously titled Vice Director" names husband and wife. The word "posthumously granted" shows that Wang Chong's titles, Chengdelang and Vice Director, were conferred after his death; the notice gives no rank or career held in life.

Second, "a few months after bearing a son she lost her husband" dates the husband's death to a few months after the birth of the son.

Third, "she raised the orphan, named Deng, who became a metropolitan graduate; he petitioned on her behalf" records the son Wang Deng's degree and his memorial seeking recognition for his mother.

Fourth, "in the Wanli era she was granted the title of Anren" records the honour. Anren was an honorific title for the wife of an official. The reign era is kept as given and not converted.

Fifth, the quoted grant — "in youth she tempered her chastity, in old age she spread kindness; her husband had no years yet through her he had years, her son had no father yet through her he had a father; she extended the lineage and laboured at instruction, every moment toil, every word blood and tears" — is quoted from the grant as the gazetteer gives it. This entry reproduces the wording without rewriting it, including the final "yun" (it is said).

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Wang Chong, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her son's degree and petition, and the grant she received all attach to the Wang family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "a few months after bearing a son" describes the child's age at the father's death, not her own.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the career, death year and titles of her husband Wang Chong, and the career of her son Wang Deng beyond his degree, are unrecorded, and any honorific titles may not be complete.

Fourth, "in the Wanli era" dates only the grant; this entry does not convert it or infer the length of her widowhood from it.

Fifth, the quoted grant is likely an excerpt; this library has neither the full text nor the original edict.

Sixth, the only honour recorded is the title of Anren; whether there were other commendations, and how many and when, is not recorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
