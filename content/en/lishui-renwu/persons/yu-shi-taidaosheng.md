Madam Yu is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, and the last notice in the Ming section. Her notice has only three clauses. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 俞氏，邰道昇妻。年二十二歲而寡，教子邰從龍入泮。壽八十六歲而終。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Tai Daosheng" names husband and wife.

Second, "widowed at twenty-two" dates her widowhood.

Third, "she taught her son Tai Conglong to enter the county school" — that is, to become a licentiate. The gazetteer records only his entrance, not his later career.

Fourth, "she lived to eighty-six and died."

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Tai Daosheng, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity and her teaching of her son attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given beyond her age at widowhood (twenty-two) and at death (eighty-six); this entry does not convert them orderive a birth year.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Tai Daosheng are unrecorded, as is the career of her son Tai Conglong beyond entering the school.

Fourth, the gazetteer does not give the number of years she remained a widow; this entry does not compute it from her widowhood and death ages.

Fifth, the gazetteer records no commendation for her, and the notice carries no dates at all.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
