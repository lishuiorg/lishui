Madam Fu is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of the licentiate Mao Kangyu. Her notice is shared by four women of the Mao household; this entry takes only the Fu branch and cross-refers to the others.

## Gazetteer text

> 傅氏，庠士茆康宇妻；胡氏，庠士茆康年妻；陳氏，茆康衢妻，妾尹氏，俱年二十餘，相率苦節，世所罕有。幸各有子，庠生吉旋，傅出，吉常、吉相，咸秉母訓，能自樹立。貞潔之氣萃於一門，足風閨閫者也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of the licentiate Mao Kangyu" gives Madam Fu's status; "licentiate" renders the county-school student rank held by her husband.

Second, "all in their twenties, keeping a bitter chastity together, which the world rarely sees" describes the four women. "In their twenties" is approximate.

Third, "each fortunately had a son; the licentiate Jixuan was born of Fu" records that each had offspring, and names Jixuan as Madam Fu's son — the only son in the notice expressly assigned to a mother.

Fourth, "Jichang and Jixiang both followed their mothers' instruction and could establish themselves" records two further sons; the gazetteer does not say which mother each belonged to.

Fifth, "the spirit of chastity gathered in one household" is the gazetteer's verdict on the family.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Mao Kangyu, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity and her teaching of her son attach to the Mao family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "in their twenties" is approximate and is not converted.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life and death year of Mao Kangyu and of her son Jixuan are unrecorded, as is Jixuan's career beyond being a licentiate.

Fourth, Jichang and Jixiang are not assigned by the gazetteer to a particular mother, and this entry does not assign them.

Fifth, the gazetteer does not record whether any of the four was honoured, how many times, or when.

Sixth, this entry cross-refers to the notices for Madam Hu (ls:person:hu-shi-maokangnian), Madam Chen (ls:person:chen-shi-maokangqu) and Madam Yin (ls:person:yin-shi-maokangqu-qie); only read together do they show the household's "spirit of chastity."

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
