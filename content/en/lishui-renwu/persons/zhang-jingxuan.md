Zhang Jingxuan is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Yongle period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. That section is a roster rather than a biography: each line gives a name, sometimes a courtesy name or one clause of kinship, the route into office, and the office held, and gives no account of the man. This entry holds only "presented as a talented man" and "district magistrate of Pengcheng County in Zhejiang", and is among the shortest in the section; everything the gazetteer does not record is placed under Points of doubt rather than supplied.

## Route of presentation and office held

> 張景宣由人材，任浙江彭城縣知縣。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of talented men" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces the two characters and does not convert them into "jinshi" or any other degree. The office lay in Pengcheng County, not in Lishui; the duties and term of a district magistrate are unrecorded and this entry supplies none. This block gives no years, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of taking up office can be known.

## The wording of his post in the gazetteer

The gazetteer words his post in five characters naming Zhejiang, Pengcheng County and the district-magistrate title, and this entry reproduces them character for character, changing neither the province nor the county. This library has no material to decide which province Pengcheng County belonged to and borrows no other book to correct the gazetteer; the doubt about this wording is recorded side by side under Points of doubt and settled by nothing.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his office lay in Pengcheng County and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Yongle period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Yongle era nor that he was active in any particular Yongle year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of talented men", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; the duties and term of his district post are unrecorded.

Fourth, the five characters naming his post are as the transcription gives them; the province assigned and the county's affiliation cannot be decided from this library's material, and this entry neither corrects them nor replaces them.

Fifth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Sixth, the same Yongle block carries another line ending as a district magistrate, for a different man in an unnamed district; sharing an office title proves nothing about a link between them, and this entry infers none.

Seventh, this entry rests on one roster line; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
