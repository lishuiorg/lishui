Madam Wang is one of the chaste widows recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her surname, Wang, and her husband's name, Tai Shaosheng; it records nothing of her personal name, her courtesy name or her native place. The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing, the age in which the compiler lived; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. This entry rests on no more than this single item in juan 7, and supplies nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 汪氏，邰紹盛妻。子生數月夫死，家貧，全節教子四十餘年。縣府藩皆旌之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records four things.

"Madam Wang, wife of Tai Shaosheng" gives her surname and her husband's name. Her personal name, courtesy name, native place and paternal kin are nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies none of them.

"Her son was a few months old when her husband died" is the gazetteer's record: her husband Tai Shaosheng died a few months after their son was born. The year of his death, the son's name and whether he lived to adulthood are all unrecorded; this entry does not infer the start of her widowhood from "a few months old", nor her birth year.

"The family was poor" is the gazetteer's description of the household.

"She kept her chastity whole and taught her son for over forty years" is the gazetteer's summary of her widowhood and of her raising of her son, and is recorded as such. The "over forty years" is the length of widowhood the gazetteer gives, without start or end years; whether the son she taught was the one described as "a few months old" the gazetteer does not say, and this entry does not decide, quoting only the words "taught her son".

"The county, prefecture and provincial authorities all conferred honours on her" is the gazetteer's record of honours. It names no year, no officer and no inscription. "County", "prefecture" and "province" are the gazetteer's own words and are quoted as they stand, not changed to modern equivalents, and this entry does not determine the levels or forms of the honours.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is this woman's conduct of chastity at Lishui — keeping her widowhood, teaching her son, and receiving honours from the county, the prefecture and the province; the honours issued from this county, this prefecture and this province, and her village must have lain within Lishui, so her conduct has to do with Lishui. Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or her native place, calling her only by her surname Wang, so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is her keeping widowhood at home, not sojourning elsewhere, so "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five values, only "active in Lishui" can be grounded, and on that this entry enters the library.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives neither her birth year nor the year of her husband's death, and this entry infers neither.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Wang" and records neither her paternal kin nor her residence; this entry does not infer her native place from her husband's surname or from other items in this volume.

Third, apart from her husband's name Tai Shaosheng, her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. The gazetteer records only her husband and the fact that her son was a few months old; the son's name, number and later life, and her parents and brothers, are nowhere recorded.

Fourth, the honours may not be complete. The gazetteer gives only the one sentence that the county, prefecture and provincial authorities all conferred honours on her, with no year, no officer and no inscription, and it does not say whether the honour was for chastity, for chastity and filial piety, or under another name; this entry quotes it as it stands and does not supplement it from other items in this volume.

Fifth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer alone. It is taken from the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription".

Sixth, this library has searched its other sources and found no material that would supplement this person.
