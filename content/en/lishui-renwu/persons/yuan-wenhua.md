Yuan Wenhua is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography tells no episode with a course of events; it lists several ways he conducted himself and closes with an inscription composed for his grave. This entry writes nothing beyond that list and that inscription.

## The gazetteer text

> 袁文化，白鹿鄉人。父死，廬墓九載。三十喪妻，終身不再娶。足不履公庭，未識官長面。鄉人重其孝義，相率銘其墓，有雲「三十鰥居敦大義，九年廬墓重天彝」。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Township

The gazetteer calls him a man of Bailu Township, one of the township names this volume records for Lishui; this entry keeps the wording, and this library has no material linking the township to a present-day settlement.

## How he conducted himself

The gazetteer lists three things, none dated and none given a course of events. First, after his father's death he kept a hut at the grave for nine years; the years at which the vigil began and ended are not recorded, and "nine years" is the number the gazetteer gives. Second, "at thirty he lost his wife and never remarried" — what "thirty" refers to is not made clear by the text: it may be read as his age when his wife died, but it may refer otherwise, and this entry decides neither and infers no birth year from it. Third, he never set foot in a court yard and never knew the face of an official — a statement about his conduct, and whether he avoided litigation or something else is nowhere recorded. The biography gives no origin and says nothing about any award.

## The villagers' grave inscription: reported praise

The gazetteer records that the villagers valued his filial and righteous conduct, that they together composed an inscription for his grave, and it quotes the line "widowed at thirty, he honoured the great bond of righteousness; keeping the grave hut nine years, he set store by heaven's constant order". Its nature must be stated: this is an inscription the gazetteer reports as the villagers', a popular praise recorded by the gazetteer, not a record of official commendation, and this entry does not treat it as fact. The couplet answers the two items above (the loss of his wife at "thirty" and the nine-year vigil), but it is verse, and this entry uses it to supply nothing the gazetteer does not record. Whether the inscription was cut on stone and whether any such stone survives are unrecorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in a named township, and this entry enters him as "active in Lishui". A township is the place of residence the gazetteer records, not a birth record: the text says only that he was a man of such-and-such township and gives no birthplace, so this entry does not record him as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his residence in Bailu Township, his vigil and his refusal to remarry, his keeping away from courts and officials, and the grave inscription — composed by men of this county, and the only place in the biography where a local response is recorded.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; "thirty" and "nine years" are undated, and this entry converts nothing and infers no birth or death from them.

Second, what "thirty" refers to is not made clear, and this entry does not decide whether it is an age or something else.

Third, the years at which the vigil began and ended, and the burial place of his father, are unrecorded.

Fourth, his origin is not recorded, this entry supplies no examination title, and his livelihood and family — beyond his wife — are unrecorded.

Fifth, "never set foot in a court yard, never knew an official" is reported conduct; what lay behind it is unrecorded.

Sixth, the grave inscription is reported popular praise, not a record of official award; whether it was inscribed on stone and whether it survives is unrecorded.

Seventh, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Eighth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Ninth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
