Wu Yu is recorded in the Supplementary Notices to the notable-officials biography of juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, not in the biography itself. The value of this entry lies not only in the man but in the gazetteer's self-reproach: the supplement exists precisely because the gazetteer judges that the earlier records lost this man. This entry writes only what the entry contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 吴裕，洪武间为溧水令，有声。上作《褒贤颂》送之，此见王祎《造邦勋贤录》。如此显名异宠，而前三志俱未载，《官师表》亦不列其名，无乃疏乎？故补焉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦补遗

He served as *ling* of Lishui during the Hongwu era. The gazetteer gives his Lishui office as *ling*, and this entry keeps that formulation rather than rendering it as "magistrate of the county" or supplying another title. "During the Hongwu era" is a span marked by an era name, not a year; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty. No courtesy name, no native place, no route into office, and no year of leaving are recorded.

## What the gazetteer records

The gazetteer says he "had a good name" — a judgement, with no act cited. It then says that *shang* composed an *Ode to Praising Worthies* to send him off: the gazetteer uses only the term *shang*, never states whom it means, and this entry supplies no name and no date; the text of the ode, its date and whether it survives are nowhere recorded. The gazetteer further states that this account is found in Wang Yi's *Zaobang xunxian lu*, and this entry reproduces the book title and the author's name as given. Whether that book survives, in which juan, and what its wording is, has not been obtained by this library, which holds no source card for it; this entry therefore quotes nothing from that book and reports only that this volume points to it. "Such a distinguished name and such unusual favour" is the gazetteer's judgement.

## Relation to Lishui

He is added to the notable officials on the strength of his being *ling* of Lishui during the Hongwu era, and this entry records him through official service. What ties him to the county is his term there, the reported reputation, the ode said to have been sent off with him, and the omission that follows; nothing he did in Lishui is recorded.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the term is given only as "during the Hongwu era", a span rather than a year, and the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office as *ling*; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, whom the gazetteer means by *shang* is not stated, and this entry supplies no name; the content, date and survival of the ode are unrecorded.

Fifth, the survival, juan and wording of Wang Yi's *Zaobang xunxian lu* have not been obtained by this library, which has no source card for the book; this entry relies on this volume's statement of where the account is found and quotes nothing from the book itself.

Sixth, which gazetteers are meant by "the three earlier gazetteers" is not stated, and this entry does not guess which three; the *Table of Officials* is the name of a table in earlier gazetteers, and neither its juan nor whether this library has read it is recorded.

Seventh, "had a good name" and "such a distinguished name and such unusual favour" are the gazetteer's judgements, and their grounds are unrecorded.

Eighth, the sentence "none of the three earlier gazetteers carries him, and the Table of Officials does not list his name — is this not an oversight? so he is added here" is the piece of self-criticism about omission in earlier Lishui gazetteers known to this library. Its value is that the gazetteer admits its own lapse. This entry records that as the gazetteer's claim; it does not conclude that the other gazetteers certainly lack him, nor that every detail of this case must be factual.

Ninth, this entry rests on one supplementary notice in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
