Wei Zijing is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him one clause of kinship, the route into office, and two successive offices, and nothing else. This entry writes only those four items and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 魏資敬良臣後。以通經，薦棗強丞，擢監察御史。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

Every other line of this section introduces the route of presentation with the same particle ("by way of persons of talent", "by way of confucian scholars", and so on), while this line uses a different one, rendered here as "through proficiency in the classics". This entry reproduces the gazetteer's wording rather than harmonising it with the other lines, and does not convert it into "jinshi" or any other degree. One clause says he was recommended into the post of vice-magistrate of Zaoqiang; the gazetteer writes the place and office together without a county marker, and this entry adds none, and supplies no prefecture or province. The other clause says he was raised to supervising censor; in what year, and when he took up and left either post, is nowhere recorded. Neither office lay in Lishui. This block gives no years at all, so neither the year of presentation nor the years of either post can be known.

## The kinship clause the section itself supplies

Between the name and the route into office the section inserts the clause "a descendant of Liangchen"; this is the gazetteer's own statement of kinship. Three points must be stated. First, it is the gazetteer's claim: this entry reproduces it and marks it as such, and reasons from it to no birth or death year and to no sequence of dates. Second, the section never says who this "Liangchen" was — no surname, no office, no generation in the line — and this library has no entry for such a man to check against. Third, the same block carries a line reading "Tang Liangchen, by way of knowledge of the classics, appointed magistrate of Dazu county in Sichuan", for which this library has a separate entry; the section never says that the "Liangchen" of this clause is that man, makes no link between the two, and nothing is decided here, so this entry declares no related link.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his two posts lay in Zaoqiang and in the censorate, and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available. The clause "a descendant of Liangchen" states his kinship with an unnamed man, not his relation to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "through proficiency in the classics", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "vice-magistrate of Zaoqiang" and "supervising censor" are office titles whose duties and terms are unrecorded.

Fourth, "a descendant of Liangchen" is the section's own claim, and nothing is recorded about what it rests on or about the man it names; whether the "Tang Liangchen" of another line in the same block has anything to do with it cannot be decided from this library's material.

Fifth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Sixth, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
