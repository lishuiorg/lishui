Duanmu Xiaowen is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Yongle period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him three items — the route of presentation, one clause of kinship, and the office held — and nothing else. This entry writes only those three items and supplies no life for him.

## Route of presentation and office held

> 端木孝文以善子。由儒士，任翰林院待詔。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of confucian scholars" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces the three characters and does not convert them into "jinshi" or any other degree. "Served as a Hanlin Academy drafter" is the office as the gazetteer words it, and it lay in the Hanlin Academy, not in Lishui; what a drafter did, and where he served, is left as a bare office title, and this entry supplies no duties and no post. This block gives no years, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of taking up office can be known.

## The kinship clause the section itself supplies

Between the name and the route into office the section inserts the clause "a son of Yishan"; this is the gazetteer's own statement of kinship. On this basis the section takes Duanmu Xiaowen as a son of Duanmu Yishan, whose name appears elsewhere in the same section, in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block:

> 端木以善由儒士，任尚書。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

This entry records the kinship on that basis and states four points. First, "a son of Yishan" is the gazetteer's claim; this entry reproduces it and marks it as such, and reasons from it to no birth or death year and to no sequence of dates between the two men. Second, this library has a separate entry for Duanmu Yishan (`ls:person:duan-mu-yishan`), resting on this same Hongwu line, and the two entries link to each other; the link records only the kinship the section itself states and signals no judgement beyond it. Third, the Hongwu and Yongle blocks stand next to each other, but the order of the blocks carries no dates, and this entry infers no sequence from "Yishan in Hongwu, Xiaowen in Yongle". Fourth, the summary of the earlier part of the arts-and-literature section (juan 8) in this library's source card lists a biography of the prefecture recorder of Kuizhou attributed to Duanmu Xiaowen; that section is not the verbatim gazetteer text used here, so this entry quotes none of it and only notes the clue that this library holds about him as an author.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his office lay in the Hanlin Academy and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available. The clause "a son of Yishan" states his kinship with Duanmu Yishan, not his relation to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Yongle period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Yongle era nor that he was active in any particular Yongle year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of confucian scholars", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "Hanlin Academy drafter" is a bare office title, and its duties, post and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, "a son of Yishan" is the section's own claim and the gazetteer records nothing of what it rests on; this library's entry for Duanmu Yishan rests on the same Hongwu line, so the two entries cross-reference one another without adding any evidence beyond the section's own wording, and neither how the line continues nor what the section relied on can be decided.

Fifth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Sixth, this entry rests on one roster line in the Yongle block and takes one line from the Hongwu block only for the kinship clause; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
