Madam Yu is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of the licentiate Xu Shiheng. Her notice is one of the fuller ones in the Ming section and ends with a couplet given by a kinsman. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 俞氏，庠生徐時亨妻。年十七，歸徐一年，遺腹三月而夫亡。悍翁、戾伯逼之改醮，氏投水懸樑，誓死不易，惟子秉完是視。家貧極，氏孤燈紡績課子，娶媳，得孫繞膝，享年八十。知縣鄺洪照旌雲「松柏堅貞」。族紳徐文煒有聯贈雲，「十七歲居孀，彩袖喜添萱草色；六十年苦節，清冰儼睹柏舟心。」
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of the licentiate Xu Shiheng" names husband and wife.

Second, "at seventeen, a year after marrying in, her husband died leaving her three months pregnant" records her marriage, pregnancy and widowhood.

Third, "her harsh father-in-law and violent brother-in-law pressed her to remarry; she tried to drown herself and to hang herself, vowing never to change, and thought only of her son Bingwan." This violent episode is reproduced as the gazetteer gives it.

Fourth, "the family was utterly poor; by a single lamp she wove and taught her son, took a daughter-in-law, and saw grandchildren around her knees; she lived to eighty."

Fifth, "the magistrate Kuang Hongzhao honoured her as 'Firm as Pine and Cypress'" records the honour. The office, name and wording are reproduced as given.

Sixth, the couplet by the clansman Xu Wenwei, "widowed at seventeen, glad to add the hue of day-lily to her coloured sleeves; sixty years of bitter chastity, clear as ice, a heart like the cypress boat," is quoted as the gazetteer gives it.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Xu Shiheng, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her resistance and the honour she received attach to the Xu family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings of her own, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "seventeen" dates her marriage and "eighty" her lifespan, but neither yields her birth year precisely.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life and death year of Xu Shiheng are unrecorded, as is whether her son Bingwan prospered.

Fourth, the father-in-law and brother-in-law are unnamed, and the pressure to remarry rests only on the gazetteer's account.

Fifth, "sixty years of bitter chastity" and "lived to eighty" are both given by the gazetteer; this entry does not reconcile them or derive a birth year.

Sixth, the magistrate's honour is recorded with the wording "Firm as Pine and Cypress," but the year, number and form are not given, and the clansman Xu Wenwei cannot be traced further here.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
