Zhai Fengming is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography has one clause of status and one episode of fetching his father's coffin: it records facts of action but dates nothing. This entry writes nothing beyond that clause and that episode.

## The gazetteer text

> 翟鳳鳴，山陽鄉人，為諸生。父卒於姚州吏目，土官攘其妾，因匿其柩。鳳鳴往迎之，徒步悲號，白其冤於上，始迎柩還。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Township and status

The gazetteer calls him a man of Shanyang Township and a student in the schools. Shanyang Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, and this entry keeps the gazetteer's wording; this library has no material linking it to a present-day settlement. "Student in the schools" is the gazetteer's own term, kept as such rather than converted into any other rank, and the gazetteer gives neither the year of his admission nor the route by which he entered.

## What the gazetteer records: fetching his father's coffin

The gazetteer records that his father died while serving as a sub-official at Yaozhou; that a "native official" seized a concubine and on that account hid the coffin; that Zhai Fengming went to fetch the coffin, travelling on foot and wailing; that he pleaded the injustice "upward"; and that only then was he able to bring the coffin home.

Four points here must be kept as written and held in doubt. First, "Yaozhou" is a place name as the gazetteer gives it; this library has no material on its location or its administrative attachment, and this entry does not fix it anywhere. Second, "sub-official" is the post the gazetteer gives for his father; this library has no material on its rank or duties, and this entry supplies none. Third, the possessive in "seized his concubine" is not resolved by the text — whose concubine it was is not determined, and this entry does not decide. Fourth, "upward" is only a term for a superior authority or superior office: which office or which person is not recorded, and neither the course of the appeal nor how the case was settled is recorded. The year of his father's death, the year of the fetching and the burial place after the coffin's return are all unrecorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in a named township, and this entry enters him as "active in Lishui". A township is the place of residence the gazetteer records, not a birth record: the text says only that he was a man of such-and-such township and gives no birthplace, so this entry does not record him as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his residence in Shanyang Township, his status as a student in the local schools, and the fetching of his father's coffin home — the gazetteer does not name where the coffin was buried, but the placing of the episode in the filial sons section is itself the gazetteer's stand that it belongs to this county.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor are his father's.

Second, the biography carries no dates at all: the year of the father's death, the year of fetching the coffin and the burial place are all unrecorded, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Third, this library has no material to determine the location of Yaozhou or the rank and duties of the sub-official post.

Fourth, who the "native official" was, whose concubine was seized, and the course of the hiding of the coffin are unrecorded.

Fifth, which authority "upward" refers to, how the appeal proceeded and how it was settled are unrecorded.

Sixth, the year of his admission to the schools and his route into them are unrecorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Seventh, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Eighth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Ninth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
