Tang Xueshen is one of the Qing filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography is the densest of the "present dynasty" entries of this volume and one of the few in it that gives a year of award. It is dense in events but unclear in several clauses, so this entry sets out what the gazetteer gives and what it withholds.

## The gazetteer text

> 湯學紳，白鹿鄉人。三歲失母，撫於繼母劉。患癰，紳吮之，繼以嘗糞。劉視紳若離里，而紳報劉若乳哺。劉逝，又事鄭如之。父死，廬墓盡哀。順治十一年有司上聞，建坊表其閭。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Township and age

The gazetteer calls him a man of Bailu Township, one of the township names this volume records for Lishui; this library has no material linking the township to a present-day settlement. "He lost his mother at three" is the only age the biography gives, and it attaches to the loss of his mother, not to his birth; this entry infers no birth year from it and converts nothing.

## What the gazetteer records, and four unclear clauses

He lost his mother at three and was raised by a stepmother of the Liu lineage. From here on, four points must be kept as written and held in doubt.

First, "an ulcer was present; Xueshen sucked it, and afterwards tasted foul matter": the gazetteer never says whose ulcer it was. It may be read as the stepmother's, for whom he sucked and then performed the fouler service, or as his own. The text gives no basis for the reading; this library keeps both and decides neither, and this entry writes neither as fact.

Second, "Liu regarded Xueshen as if apart from the household, while Xueshen repaid Liu as if she had nursed him": the phrase for "apart from the household" is hard to construe, the transcription reads so, and this entry reproduces it without emendation; its sense is beyond this library's material. "As if she had nursed him" is the gazetteer's simile for the way he repaid her.

Third, "when Liu died he served a Zheng in the same way": the gazetteer gives only the surname Zheng and never says who this person was or by what relation she entered his household — whether another stepmother or his father's later wife is nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies nothing. "In the same way" is the gazetteer's statement that he served her as he had served Liu.

Fourth, "when his father died he kept the grave in full mourning": neither the year nor the number of years of the vigil is recorded, nor the burial place; his father is not named.

## The eleventh Shunzhi year: a date of award only

"In the eleventh Shunzhi year the authorities reported him upward, and an archway was built to mark his gate" gives the only date and the only favour in the biography. "Eleventh Shunzhi year" here dates the report and the archway, not his birth or death and not the year of any deed; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers neither his birth and death nor the span of his activity from it. "The authorities" is a term for offices, and the gazetteer names no one; what this library knows of the district magistrate in Lishui in that year comes from the chronicle of the county in this gazetteer and from existing entries of this library (such as `ls:event:qing-shunzhi-11-chongjian-xiantang-1654`), but the Filial Sons section does not name anyone here, and this entry does not assert that it was he who did it. The site of the archway, its name and the offices through which the report passed are nowhere recorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in a named township, and this entry enters him as "active in Lishui". A township is the place of residence the gazetteer records, not a birth record: the text says only that he was a man of such-and-such township and gives no birthplace, so this entry does not record him as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his residence in Bailu Township, the household matters of his upbringing and his service to his stepmothers, the vigil at his father's grave, and the report and archway of the local authorities — the last being the only place in the biography where an act of the county government is recorded.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; "at three" is the age of losing his mother and "eleventh Shunzhi year" is a year of award, and neither can yield a birth year; this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, the subject of the ulcer is not fixed by the text — stepmother or Tang Xueshen himself — and this library decides neither.

Third, the phrase "as if apart from the household" is hard to construe; this entry reproduces it unchanged and its sense is beyond this library's material.

Fourth, the person "Zheng" and her relation to his household are unrecorded, and this entry supplies nothing; his father's name, birth, death and burial place are unrecorded.

Fifth, the year and length of the vigil are unrecorded; apart from the Shunzhi year the biography carries no dates.

Sixth, "the authorities" are unnamed; what this library knows of the district magistrate of Lishui in that year comes from the chronicle of the county, but the Filial Sons section names no one and this entry does not identify them.

Seventh, the site and name of the archway and the offices through which the report passed are unrecorded.

Eighth, his origin is not recorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Ninth, the era field "Qing" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "present dynasty" block of this biography section together with the year of award the biography gives, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
