Madam Guan is one of the virtuous women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Jing Guoyou. She was honoured as "chaste and true" for taking her own life. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 管氏，經國猷妻。歸經歲余，夫死，有遺腹。自計生男當撫孤，即女死未為晚。及生女也，遂縊死。萬曆間表其門曰「貞烈」。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Jing Guoyou" names husband and wife.

Second, "a little over a year after marrying into the Jing family her husband died, leaving her with an unborn child" records her marriage and the death of her husband.

Third, "she reasoned that if she bore a son she should raise the orphan, but that if she bore a girl it would not be too late to die" reports the gazetteer's account of her inner reasoning. The text presents this as her calculation; this entry reproduces it and marks it as such rather than as an outward fact.

Fourth, "when she bore a girl, she hanged herself."

Fifth, "in the Wanli era her gate was honoured as 'Chaste and True'" records the honour. The reign era is kept as given and not converted; the text does not name who conferred it.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Jing Guoyou, whose household was her place of residence; her self-sacrifice and the honour to her gate attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; this entry does not convert the Wanli date or infer her dates from it.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of her husband Jing Guoyou are unrecorded.

Fourth, the fate of the daughter she bore is unrecorded; the notice says only that she gave birth and then died.

Fifth, the reported reasoning is the gazetteer's account of her thoughts, not a verifiable fact, and is marked as such.

Sixth, the only honour recorded is the Wanli-era naming of her gate as "Chaste and True"; the conferring authority, the number and the dates are unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
