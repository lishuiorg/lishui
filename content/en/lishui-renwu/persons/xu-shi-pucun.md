Madam Xu is one of the martyred maidens recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives her surname, Xu, her maternal family as the Wu family of Pucun, and her father as the county student Xu Mengming. The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. This entry rests on no more than this single item in juan 7, and supplies nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 許氏，蒲村武氏之甥女。年十六未字人，隨父庠生許夢明避亂於外家，值搜山兵至，挾之馬上，氏紿之曰：「我有金鐲諸飾在敗屋壁中，願取以相從。」兵利其有，牽馬縱往，勒轡在門候，久不出，入視之，則女已投屋後塘中死矣。兵怒，斫其屋而去。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records the whole course of her flight from disorder and her death.

"Madam Xu, niece of the Wu family of Pucun" gives her surname and her kin. The gazetteer makes her the "niece" of a family surnamed Wu at Pucun; "niece" is a term of the maternal kin, and the gazetteer uses it to record that her maternal family lived at Pucun. This entry quotes the place name "Pucun" as it stands and does not supply its modern administrative affiliation from elsewhere. "The Wu family" is her maternal family, not her husband's.

"At sixteen, unmarried" is the gazetteer's record: she was sixteen and had not yet been betrothed. "Unmarried" is the marital state the gazetteer gives, quoted as it stands and not reworded; this entry does not infer her birth year from it.

"She went with her father, the county student Xu Mengming, to shelter with her maternal family from disorder" records her father's name and status and where they took refuge. Her father was the "county student Xu Mengming" — a student of the county school, a degree holder the gazetteer records; they took refuge with her maternal family, the Wu of Pucun. The gazetteer does not attach a year to this flight.

"Searching troops arrived and set her on a horse" records the troops' search and her seizure. "Searching troops" is the gazetteer's term and is quoted as it stands.

"She deceived them, saying ... that she was willing to go with them if she might fetch them", and "the troops, coveting her goods, led and released the horse, checked the reins and waited at the gate; when she did not come out for a long time they went in to look, and the maiden had already thrown herself into the pond behind the house and died" record her escape and her death: she told the troops that she had gold bracelets and other ornaments in the wall of a ruined house, and would go with them willingly if she might fetch them; the troops, greedy for her goods, led and released the horse, held the reins and waited at the gate; when she did not come out for a long time they went in and found that she had thrown herself into the pond behind the house and died. "She deceived them" records her ruse, as the gazetteer has it.

"The troops were angry and, chopping her house, went off" is a further matter the gazetteer records.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is this maiden's martyrdom at Lishui — her maternal family was the Wu of Pucun, Pucun being a place name of Lishui recorded in this volume, and both her flight and her death took place there, so her conduct has to do with Lishui. Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or native place, giving only her surname Xu and her father's name, so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is her flight from disorder, not sojourning elsewhere, so "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five values, only "active in Lishui" can be grounded, and on that this entry enters the library.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was sixteen when she died, without the year of the deed or her birth year; this entry converts nothing to a year of the common era and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The gazetteer gives only her surname Xu and her father Xu Mengming, with neither her native place nor her residence; "Pucun" is where her maternal family lived, and this entry does not use it to conclude that she was a person of Pucun or of Lishui.

Third, apart from her father's name Xu Mengming, her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her father as the county student Xu Mengming and her maternal family as the Wu, but records nothing of her mother, her brothers or other branches of the Xu family; as she died unmarried, she had no offspring.

Fourth, the honours may not be complete. This item does not record whether she received honours after death, and this entry does not infer it from other items in this volume.

Fifth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer alone. It is taken from the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription".

Sixth, this library has searched its other sources and found no material that would supplement this person.
