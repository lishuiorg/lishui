Huang Mingzhao is one of the Qing filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography names more commenders than any other entry of the "present dynasty" block: four magistrates and one instructor appear in it. This entry separates what the gazetteer records from what this library can and cannot say about those commenders.

## The gazetteer text

> 黃明詔，父病割股以療，舉鄉飲介賓。其子世紀復效之，母疾亦瘳。邑令閔派魯、學諭吳鼎玫給扁旌獎，一曰「孝萃一門」，一曰「父子克孝」。後縣令馮泰運、李作楫俱旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## What the gazetteer records

He cut his thigh to treat his father's illness. No year is given, no illness is named, and the text says only that he did it "in order to treat" — it does not state that his father recovered, and this entry does not add a recovery. The gazetteer also records that he was chosen as a guest of the village drinking rite; this is a designation as the gazetteer gives it, and this library has no material on the rite's arrangement, its year or why he was chosen, so this entry does not turn it into any title or office.

The gazetteer further records that his son Shiji repeated his father's deed and that a mother's illness was cured. "Shiji" is the son's name as the gazetteer gives it. Here doubt must be kept: which mother is not made clear — it may be read as Shiji's mother, that is Huang Mingzhao's wife, but it may refer otherwise; which relative each of the two men treated is left to a single word for "mother", and this library keeps the ambiguity without deciding. Shiji's birth and death and his later life are unrecorded, and the biography names no other relative.

## The five commenders, one by one

First, the magistrate Min Pailu and the instructor Wu Dingmei awarded inscribed tablets, one reading "filial piety gathered in one household" and one "father and son both truly filial". The gazetteer gives two tablets and two men but does not make the correspondence clear — who inscribed which tablet, and which tablet belongs to the father and which to the son: this library decides none of it and reproduces the two texts only.

Second, Min Pailu appears in this volume only as "magistrate of the county". This library has no person entry for him. He is recorded in this library as one of the compilers of the Shunzhi edition of the Lishui gazetteer — on the basis of bibliographic description — and he appears as district magistrate in the county chronicle of juan 1 of the Kangxi gazetteer (rebuilding the county hall in the eleventh Shunzhi year, compiling the record of equalised corvée in the twelfth year, as existing entries of this library record). This entry records only that he commends Huang Mingzhao here as county magistrate; it borrows no years of office from the chronicle, and it does not cite the text of the Shunzhi gazetteer — what this library holds of that edition reaches only its table of contents and the opening of its juan 6, which cannot be used to establish this matter.

Third, "instructor" is the title as the gazetteer gives it; this library has no entry for Wu Dingmei and no material on his years in Lishui.

Fourth, "later the magistrates Feng Taiyun and Li Zuoji both commended him": this library has no entries for Feng Taiyun or Li Zuoji and no material on their years in Lishui; the sequence implied by "later" and the form of the commendation — tablet, grant, or other — are nowhere recorded. Elsewhere in this volume, in the examination-recommendation tables, there is a tablet inscribed by "the district magistrate Li Zuoji"; whether that is the same man at the same time cannot be determined from this library's material, and this entry does not merge the two.

Fifth, the biography carries no dates at all: the year of the cutting, of the rite, and of the three commendations are all unrecorded, and this entry does not infer any of them from the years known for Min Pailu in the chronicle. The `related` link to this library's entry on the rebuilding of the county hall in the eleventh Shunzhi year is a cross-reference for Min Pailu alone, not a dating of this matter.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Huang Mingzhao no township and no native place; it ties him to this county through being named in the filial sons section, through commendation by magistrates and an instructor of this county, and through his being a guest of the local drinking rite. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is activity and commendation in this county, not birth here, so it does not record him as "born locally". His son's repeating of his deed is a matter of one household in this county.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; township and native place are unrecorded; the biography carries no dates, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, what illness his father had and whether he recovered are unrecorded, and this entry does not add a recovery.

Third, the meaning, year and reason of the village-drinking guest designation are beyond this library's material.

Fourth, which mother "a mother's illness was cured" refers to is not made clear, and this library does not decide; Shiji's birth, death and later life are unrecorded.

Fifth, the correspondence between the two tablets, the two commenders and the father and son is not made clear; this entry reproduces the tablet texts only.

Sixth, Min Pailu appears here only as county magistrate; this library has no person entry for him, supplies no years of office, and may not use the text of the Shunzhi gazetteer to establish this matter.

Seventh, this library has no entries for Wu Dingmei, Feng Taiyun or Li Zuoji and no material on their years in Lishui; whether the "district magistrate Li Zuoji" of the examination-recommendation tables is the same man at the same time cannot be determined.

Eighth, the `related` link is a cross-reference only and decides nothing about the year of the commendation or the years of office.

Ninth, the era field "Qing" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "present dynasty" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
