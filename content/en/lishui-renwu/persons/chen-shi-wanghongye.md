Madam Chen is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Chen, her husband Wang Hongye, and her widowhood, the years of her chastity, and her son's status. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 陳氏，王弘冶妻。生子三月夫亡，堅守冰操四十餘年。邑廩生臣，曰其子也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is very short.

"Madam Chen, wife of Wang Hongye" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"Three months after bearing a son her husband died, and she kept her chastity over forty years" gives the start of her widowhood and its length: her husband died three months after she gave birth; she kept her chastity over forty years. The three months is the gazetteer's sequence, and "over forty years" its figure; her son's name (given below as Chen) and her husband's years are recorded elsewhere in the item.

"A stipendiary student of the county school, Chen, said to be her son" gives her son's status and identity: one "Chen", a stipendiary student of the county school, whom the gazetteer marks as her son. The status is as the gazetteer records it; her son is named Chen. The gazetteer writes "said" where the sense requires "namely"; the transcription reads "said", and this entry keeps the text unemended, noting the point under points of doubt.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Wang Hongye, widowed three months after bearing a son, keeping chastity over forty years, her son a stipendiary student of the county school, the household and her teaching belonging to the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Chen", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her — her son's stipend is his study, not her own recommendation — so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that her husband died three months after her son's birth and that she kept chastity over forty years; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Chen"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Wang Hongye and her son Chen; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had children besides Chen is unrecorded.

Fourth, the "said" before her son's identity is, by sense, an error for "namely"; the transcription reads "said", and this entry keeps it unemended as a point for further checking. The year her son became a stipendiary student and his later career are unrecorded.

Fifth, another item in this volume gives the son of Madam Sun, wife of Wang Hongheng, as Yuchen, a name close to this item's "Chen"; this entry does not judge the two to be one household or one person.

Sixth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award, and this entry infers none from other items.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Eighth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
