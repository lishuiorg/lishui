Madam Xia is one of the three women in the "Three Staunch Women" notice in the Yuan section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice opens with "the Three Staunch Women" and covers all three in one entry; this entry deals with Madam Xia alone. The agreement or disagreement of Madam Liu's husband Zhao Kai and of Madam Heng's husband is treated in their own entries; here it is stated that the name of Madam Xia's husband Zhao Dong agrees in both notices.

## The gazetteer text

> 三烈婦，衡氏，趙雷澤妻；劉氏，趙楷妻；夏氏，趙棟妻。時汝（穎）［潁］兵起，攻建業，城陷，三氏相向誓，無何同赴水死，時號「三烈」。翰林學士陳祖仁有傳，潞國公張翥有贊。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her station: one of the Three Staunch Women, not the other Madam Xia

The subject here is the Madam Xia of the "Three Staunch Women", recorded as the wife of Zhao Dong. The other two are Madam Heng, wife of Zhao Leize, and Madam Liu, wife of Zhao Kai; this library holds separate entries for them, and this entry is linked to both. "Madam Xia" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname and no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Xia" as its title. A distinction must be stated: the Song section of the same Chaste and Staunch biography has another Madam Xia, "daughter of the Xia family of Qiqiao", for whom this library also holds a separate entry (`ls:person:xia-shi-qiqiao`); the two are different women and the two entries are not merged.

## The three women drowning themselves

"When the Yu-Ying forces rose and took Jianye, and the city fell" is the gazetteer's account of the occasion: the Yu-Ying forces rose, attacked Jianye, and the city fell. Neither era nor date is given, and this entry reproduces the text without converting it into a Common-Era year and without deciding on whose side those forces fought. The collation brackets are the transcription's own and are reproduced as they stand, the character inside the square brackets being the one used. "The three women swore facing one another, and soon drowned themselves together" is the gazetteer's account of their deaths: the three pledged themselves to one another and shortly drowned themselves together. "They were then called the 'Three Staunch'" is the gazetteer's account of how the name arose, and the words "Three Staunch" are reproduced. Their oath and the place where they drowned are not recorded.

## The biography and encomium for the "Three Staunch"

"The Hanlin academician Chen Zuren wrote their biography and the Duke of Lu, Zhang Zhu, their encomium" is the gazetteer's account of later men writing a biography and encomium for them. This entry records the existence of the biography and encomium, their authors and their offices; the two pieces are now transcribed in juan 8, Literature (see this card's sources) and may be briefly quoted. Neither Chen Zuren nor Zhang Zhu yet has an entry here, so nothing is supplied about their lives from this.

## Reading the biography and encomium in juan 8: Madam Xia as Dong's wife

The biography by Chen Zuren and the encomium by Zhang Zhu that this notice points to are now transcribed in this card's source `src:kangxi-lishuixianzhi`, juan 8, Literature, and this entry reads them alongside the notice. Zhang Zhu's "Encomium on the Three Staunch" opens "歸德趙雷澤二子棟之婦夏" — Madam Xia, wife of Dong, the son of Zhao Leize; Chen Zuren's "Biography of Righteousness" has Zongze's wife Heng say to "棟婦夏" (Dong's wife Xia) that they should die, and Madam Xia answer "吾意亦然" ("my thought is the same"), whereupon the two "共赴池水" (went together to the pond water). On Madam Xia being Dong's wife the two juan-8 pieces agree with the juan-7 Chaste and Staunch biography, and no variant arises.

## Compared with the Loyalty and Integrity biography: Madam Xia's husband agrees

The "Zhao Longze" notice of the Loyalty and Integrity biography in juan 6 (this library already holds the entry `ls:person:zhao-longze`) reads "the wife Xia of Dong, the elder son of his younger brother Leize; the wife Liu of Kai the second son; and the wife Heng of his younger brother Zongze". For Madam Xia the comparison is this: juan 6 has "the wife Xia of Dong, the elder son of his younger brother Leize", agreeing with this juan's "Madam Xia, wife of Zhao Dong" — both the husband's name Zhao Dong and the wife's surname Xia being consistent. So no variant arises here, unlike the case of Madam Heng where the two disagree. The three women are the ones of Zhao Longze's house who died with him, and this entry is linked to the other two and to Zhao Longze for cross-reference only.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as the wife of Zhao Dong and records the three women's drowning themselves. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer gives no home place and records no birth place or native place, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is her death keeping her chastity in Lishui, and the biography belongs to this county's gazetteer, so this library treats it as her activity in Lishui and takes the relation "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; "when... the forces rose and took Jianye" gives no year, so nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no age is inferred.

Second, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives only that she was the wife of Zhao Dong and no home place, so no native place can be decided.

Third, the life of her husband Zhao Dong and the year of their marriage are unrecorded, and Zhao Dong has no entry in this library.

Fourth, the oath of the three women, the place where they drowned, and the year of their deaths are all unrecorded.

Fifth, the biography by Chen Zuren and the encomium by Zhang Zhu are now transcribed in juan 8, Literature (see this card's sources) and may be briefly quoted; neither man yet has an entry in this library, so nothing about their lives is supplied from this.

Sixth, this Madam Xia and the other Madam Xia of the Song section of the same juan, daughter of the Xia family of Qiqiao, are two people; this library keeps them in separate entries and does not decide any relation between them.

Seventh, whether any others of the Zhao house died with them besides the three women and the named Zhao Longze and his son Quan is unrecorded, and no total for the household is inferred.

Eighth, this entry rests on a single notice, the "Three Staunch Women" of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7, and this library has nothing further to add; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
