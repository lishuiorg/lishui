Qian Wenxiang is one of the Qing filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography tells no episode with a course of events; it lists several ways he conducted himself and closes with one commendation. This entry writes nothing beyond that list and that commendation.

## The gazetteer text

> 錢文象，豐慶鄉人，素有孝行。父母亡，廬墓三載，見親友絕口不言。力田祭掃，足跡不入城市，教子孫以孝為先。鄉黨中稱為長者，知縣饒應元獎［之］。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Township

The gazetteer calls him a man of Fengqing Township, one of the township names this volume records for Lishui; this entry keeps the wording, and this library has no material linking the township to a present-day settlement.

## How he conducted himself

The gazetteer opens by saying he was long known for filial conduct, without saying what that conduct was. Four items follow, none dated and none given a course of events. First, after his parents died he kept a hut at the grave for three years; the years at which the vigil began and ended and the burial place of his parents are unrecorded, and "three years" is the number the gazetteer gives. Second, he met relatives and friends without uttering a word — on what subject he kept silent is not recorded. Third, he worked hard at the land and kept the grave rites, and never set foot in a town; where his land was and which grave he tended are unrecorded. Fourth, he taught his descendants to put filial piety first; the names of his descendants and their later lives are unrecorded.

"The neighbourhood called him a man of worth" is a reputation the gazetteer reports, a matter of local opinion rather than verifiable record; this entry reproduces it and marks its nature.

## The commendation and a supplied character

"The district magistrate Rao Yingyuan commended [him]" is the only honour the biography gives. One textual point must be reproduced as printed: in the transcription the object of the verb is missing in the source and supplied by the transcriber in brackets, and this entry quotes it in that form, neither deleting nor merging it. The year of the commendation and its form are nowhere recorded, and this entry does not infer the form from other entries in this volume that mention tablets, archways or grants of grain and cloth. This library has no entry for Rao Yingyuan and no material on his years in Lishui, and this entry infers no date for him from the order in which magistrates appear elsewhere in this volume.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in a named township, and this entry enters him as "active in Lishui". A township is the place of residence the gazetteer records, not a birth record: the text says only that he was a man of such-and-such township and gives no birthplace, so this entry does not record him as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his residence in Fengqing Township, the vigil and his work at the land and the grave rites, his teaching of his descendants, the local reputation, and his commendation by a magistrate of this county — the last being the only place where the biography ties him directly to a Lishui officeholder.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; the biography carries no dates at all, and the year at which the three-year vigil began is unknown; this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, this library has no material on the location of Fengqing Township or its correspondence with any present-day settlement.

Third, his origin is not recorded, this entry supplies no examination title, and the names, birth, death and burial of his parents are unrecorded.

Fourth, what he kept silent about, and the location of his land and of the grave he tended, are nowhere recorded.

Fifth, the names and later lives of his descendants are unrecorded; the biography names no other relative.

Sixth, "the neighbourhood called him a man of worth" is reported local opinion, not fact.

Seventh, the year and form of the commendation are unrecorded; the object of the verb is a character supplied by the transcriber in brackets, reproduced as printed.

Eighth, this library has no entry for Rao Yingyuan and no material on his years in Lishui, and infers none from the order of magistrates in this volume.

Ninth, the era field "Qing" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "present dynasty" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
