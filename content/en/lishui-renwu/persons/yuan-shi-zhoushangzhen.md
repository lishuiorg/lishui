Madam Yuan is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer first gives her paternal family and her husband, then her chastity and honours, and finally an appended note on her son Qiding. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 袁氏，白鹿鄉敦淑之女，周尚貞妻。少寡，辛苦存孤。前膺嘉獎五次，縣令劉登科復旌其閭。子啟鼎，年二十六妻卒，誓不再娶，縣令李作楫獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item has three layers.

"Madam Yuan, daughter of Dunshu of Bailu Township and wife of Zhou Shangzhen" gives her natal surname, her paternal family, and her husband's name. Bailu Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written; her father's name is kept as written, without supplying a surname or courtesy name from other books.

"Widowed young, she raised an orphan through hardship" opens her widowhood: widowed young, she raised the orphan through toil. "Widowed young" is the gazetteer's summary of her age, and "through hardship" its summary of her raising the child; the year of her widowhood and the child's name are unrecorded.

"She was honoured five times before, and the magistrate Liu Dengke again honoured her gate" records her honours: five prior commendations, whose givers and texts are unrecorded, and then a further honour to her gate by Liu Dengke, magistrate of this county. The word "again" marks it as following the earlier honours, and the text of the gate honour is unrecorded.

"Her son Qiding, widowed at twenty-six, vowed never to remarry and was honoured by the magistrate Li Zuoyi" is an appended note on her son: at twenty-six he lost his wife, vowed never to remarry, and was honoured by Li Zuoyi, magistrate of this county. This is appended matter on her son's chastity; the subject of this entry is Madam Yuan, and the son's affairs are secondary.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: daughter of Dunshu of Bailu Township and wife of Zhou Shangzhen, widowed young, raising an orphan, repeatedly honoured, finally with her gate honoured by the county magistrate Liu Dengke, and her son Qiding likewise honoured by the magistrate Li Zuoyi — her community her home and the honouring officials Lishui officers. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only by her father's township and "Madam Yuan", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed young and repeatedly honoured; it gives no year of birth, of marriage, or of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only a daughter of Dunshu of Bailu Township and "Madam Yuan"; her paternal family's residence and native place are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives her husband Zhou Shangzhen and her son Qiding; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had children besides Qiding is unrecorded.

Fourth, the givers, forms, and texts of the "five prior honours", and the texts of the gate honour and the son's honour, are all unrecorded, and this entry supplies nothing.

Fifth, her son Qiding's affairs are appended matter; the years of his vow and his honour, and his later career, are unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
