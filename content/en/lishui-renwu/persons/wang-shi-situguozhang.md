Madam Wang is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Wang, her husband Situ Guozhang, and matters of chastity, support, and household preservation, closing with its own judgement. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 王氏，司徒國章妻也。適二年夫亡，誓死靡他，人莫奪其志。以織紝奉舅姑，養撫幼孤亦賴十指，五十餘年如一日。能終舅姑喪葬，不損夫遺田宅，保家完節，是才而賢者也。年七十有一，猶強健。天實相之，人以為節孝之報雲。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item works in two layers, chastity and preservation of the household.

"Madam Wang, wife of Situ Guozhang" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. The husband's surname is Situ, a two-character surname, his given name Guozhang, kept as written; his courtesy name, status, and career are unrecorded.

"Two years after her marriage her husband died; she vowed never to turn to another, and none could shake her resolve" opens her widowhood: she was married two years when her husband died, vowed never to remarry, and none could shake her will. "Marriage" and "never to turn to another" are the gazetteer's words.

"She supported her parents-in-law by weaving, and raised and fed her young child also by the work of her ten fingers, for over fifty years as though it were a single day" records the substance of her widowhood. "Parents-in-law" and "young orphan" are the gazetteer's terms, and the "ten fingers" show she lived entirely by weaving.

"She could bury her parents-in-law to the end and never diminished the land her husband left, preserving the household and completing her chastity; she was both capable and worthy" gives the fruit of her chastity with a judgement: she saw her parents-in-law buried, kept intact the land her husband left, preserved the household and completed her chastity, being both capable and worthy. The three acts are recorded, and "both capable and worthy" is the gazetteer's judgement.

"At seventy-one she was still strong; Heaven truly aided her, and people took it as the reward of chastity and filial piety" gives her age and the gazetteer's comment. The age is recorded; "Heaven truly aided her" and "the reward of chastity and filial piety" are the gazetteer's own statements, not verifiable facts.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Situ Guozhang, widowed, supporting her parents-in-law, raising her child, and preserving the household for over fifty years, her parents-in-law, land, and community being the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Wang", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she lived over fifty years after her husband's death and was strong at seventy-one; it gives no year of birth, of marriage, or of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Wang"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Situ Guozhang; his courtesy name and status, and the name of the "young orphan", are unrecorded, as is her son's later fate.

Fourth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item records only that "people took it as the reward of chastity and filial piety", not whether she received an official award, and this entry infers none from other items.

Fifth, the closing words "Heaven truly aided her, and people took it as the reward of chastity and filial piety" are the gazetteer's judgement, not factual record.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
