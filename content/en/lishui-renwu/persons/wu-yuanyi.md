Wu Yuanyi is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him one clause of kinship, a period label inside the line itself, and two successive offices, and nothing else. This entry writes only those four items and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 吳原頤柔勝六世孫。洪武間授宣城訓導，累官至國子博士。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

This is one of the few lines of the block that carries its own period label, and this entry reproduces it; most lines of the block carry none. One clause places his first post in Xuancheng; the other says he rose in due course to a professorship of the Imperial Academy. "In due course" is the section's summarising word: in what years he served, through what ranks, and how many steps are nowhere recorded, so this entry supplies no intermediate offices and arranges no sequence. The period label is the gazetteer's own; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers from it no year of taking up office. "Assistant instructor" and the Imperial Academy professorship are office titles whose duties are unrecorded. Neither post lay in Lishui. This line, unlike others in the block, gives no route of presentation at all, and this entry supplies none.

## The kinship clause the section itself supplies

Between the name and the offices the section inserts the clause "a sixth-generation descendant of Rousheng"; this is the gazetteer's own statement of kinship. Three points must be stated. First, it is the gazetteer's claim: this entry reproduces it and marks it as such, reasons from it to no birth or death year and to no sequence of dates, and does not use the "sixth generation" to measure the years between the two men. Second, the section never says who this "Rousheng" was — not even whether he shared the surname, nor his office, nor his place in the line — and this library has no entry for such a man to check against, so no related link is declared. Third, the period label in this line dates his appointment at Xuancheng, not the time of his ancestor, and no sequence within the lineage is arranged from it.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his two posts lay in Xuancheng and at the Imperial Academy, and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available. The tag "Education and culture" is given here only because both his posts were school posts, and implies no educational activity of his in Lishui. The clause of kinship states his relation to an unnamed man, not his relation to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and the period label inside the line reaches only his appointment at Xuancheng.

Third, this line records no route of presentation, and this entry supplies none; "assistant instructor of Xuancheng" and the Imperial Academy professorship are office titles whose duties and terms are unrecorded.

Fourth, the ranks and years behind "rose in due course" are unrecorded, and this entry supplies and orders none.

Fifth, "a sixth-generation descendant of Rousheng" is the section's own claim, and nothing is recorded about what it rests on or about the man it names.

Sixth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Seventh, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
