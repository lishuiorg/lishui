An Yingsui is one of the Qing officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and this volume places him last of its four Qing entries. His biography records several acts during his term and his death in office from exhaustion, and is the most complete of the Qing entries in its account of posthumous worship.

## Origin and term

> 安应（晬）［睟］，字玉吾，辽东人。由旗下生员，顺治六年任。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

His name in the transcription carries its own collation bracket: a wrong character in round brackets followed by the corrected character in square brackets. This entry reproduces the line exactly as transcribed and takes the corrected character inside the square brackets for its own title and name. He was courtesy-named Yuwu, a native of Liaodong, entered as a student-member of the banners, and was appointed to Lishui in the sixth year of the Shunzhi era. What "student-member of the banners" precisely means is unrecorded, and "Liaodong" is given only as two characters of origin with no locality. The gazetteer writes only "appointed" and gives no office title.

**"Our dynasty" means the Qing.** This volume lists An Yingsui together with Luo Jiashi, Yang Guozhen and Wang Dingyin under the heading "our dynasty". The gazetteer was written by men of the Kangxi period, who called the Qing "our dynasty"; this entry treats him as a Qing official and notes the original wording.

## What the record says about his acts

**Judgement of his term.**

> 在官勤敏平和，事无弗善。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Diligent, agile and mild-tempered in office; there was no matter he did badly" is the gazetteer's general judgement, naming no act.

**Detached garrison troops and local militia.**

> 时分防兵肆虐，民苦之，至有杀人以祭马神庙者，公力请于上，徙去之。自立乡勇，亲以操练，贼不敢窥。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

At that time, the gazetteer records, detached garrison troops ravaged the county and the people suffered from them, to the point that a man was killed to make sacrifice at the temple of the horse deity; he pressed the matter upward with force until those troops were moved away. He also raised local militia on his own initiative and drilled them in person, and bandits dared not look in. Which formation those detached troops belonged to and when they were there, where the horse-deity temple stood, and how the militia was organised and how large it was — all unrecorded. "Bandits dared not look in" is the gazetteer's statement of the result.

**Courtesy to scholars and ritual entreaty.**

> 且虚贤下士，逢丁祭必斋宿循礼，旱祷则徒跣吁神，立应雨泽。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He humbled himself before the worthy and was courteous to scholars; at every ding sacrifice he kept fasting and lodging and followed the ritual; and in drought, praying for rain, he went barefoot to entreat the spirits, and rain immediately followed. "Immediately responded with rain" is the gazetteer's account of a numinous response, recorded here as the gazetteer's narration, not as fact.

**Death in office and a shrine.**

> 以劳瘁致病，九年正月卒于官，民建祠祀焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Worn out by labour he fell ill and died at his post in the first month of the ninth year, and the people built a shrine to sacrifice to him. The gazetteer records his death in office, with the month attached to "the ninth year"; that year depends on the sixth year of Shunzhi given above, and this entry transcribes it as it stands, converts nothing into a common-era year and does not supply the era name.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his appointment in the sixth year of the Shunzhi era, his success in having the detached garrison troops moved away and his raising of local militia, his conduct toward scholars and in ritual, his death in office, and the shrine the people built. His death at his post and the people's shrine are, among the four Qing entries of this volume, the only case where an official is recorded as dying in office and being shrined by the people.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded; the gazetteer records only that he died in office, with the month attached to "the ninth year", which this entry neither converts nor completes with an era name.

Second, the name "An Yingsui" rests on the transcription's collation bracket and must be checked against the punctuated edition.

Third, his origin is given only as "Liaodong", with no locality, and the meaning of "student-member of the banners" is unrecorded.

Fourth, his Lishui office is given only as "appointed", with no precise title.

Fifth, the formation and dates of the detached garrison troops, the location of the horse-deity temple, and the organisation and size of the militia are unrecorded.

Sixth, the case of the man killed for sacrifice at the horse-deity temple and its outcome are unrecorded.

Seventh, "immediately responded with rain" is the gazetteer's account of a numinous response, not a verifiable record.

Eighth, the location, date and later history of the shrine are unrecorded.

Ninth, this volume lists him under "our dynasty", meaning the Qing; the original wording is noted above.

Tenth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
