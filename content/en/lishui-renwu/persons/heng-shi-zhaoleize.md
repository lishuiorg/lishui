Madam Heng is one of the three women in the "Three Staunch Women" notice in the Yuan section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice opens with "the Three Staunch Women" and covers all three in one entry; this entry deals with Madam Heng alone and sets apart one variant concerning her husband, because the two notices of this gazetteer disagree.

## The gazetteer text

> 三烈婦，衡氏，趙雷澤妻；劉氏，趙楷妻；夏氏，趙棟妻。時汝（穎）［潁］兵起，攻建業，城陷，三氏相向誓，無何同赴水死，時號「三烈」。翰林學士陳祖仁有傳，潞國公張翥有贊。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her station: one of the Three Staunch Women

The subject here is the Madam Heng of the "Three Staunch Women", recorded as the wife of Zhao Leize. The other two are Madam Liu, wife of Zhao Kai, and Madam Xia, wife of Zhao Dong; this library holds separate entries for them, and this entry is linked to both. "Madam Heng" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname and no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Heng" as its title. The gazetteer gives neither her home place nor her paternal house, and no native place is supplied.

## The three women drowning themselves

"When the Yu-Ying forces rose and took Jianye, and the city fell" is the gazetteer's account of the occasion: the Yu-Ying forces rose, attacked Jianye, and the city fell. Neither era nor date is given, and this entry reproduces the text without converting it into a Common-Era year and without deciding on whose side those forces fought. The collation brackets are the transcription's own and are reproduced as they stand, the character inside the square brackets being the one used. "The three women swore facing one another, and soon drowned themselves together" is the gazetteer's account of their deaths: the three pledged themselves to one another and shortly drowned themselves together. "They were then called the 'Three Staunch'" is the gazetteer's account of how the name arose, and the words "Three Staunch" are reproduced. Their oath and the place where they drowned are not recorded.

## The biography and encomium for the "Three Staunch"

"The Hanlin academician Chen Zuren wrote their biography and the Duke of Lu, Zhang Zhu, their encomium" is the gazetteer's account of later men writing a biography and encomium for them. This entry records only the existence of the biography and encomium, their authors and their offices; this library has not transcribed them, and neither Chen Zuren nor Zhang Zhu has an entry here, so nothing is supplied about their lives from this.

## One variant: her husband "Zhao Leize" or "Zongze"

The "Zhao Longze" notice of the Loyalty and Integrity biography in juan 6 (this library already holds the entry `ls:person:zhao-longze`) reads "the wife Xia of Dong, the elder son of his younger brother Leize; the wife Liu of Kai the second son; and the wife Heng of his younger brother Zongze". The comparison must be stated: Madam Liu wife of Zhao Kai, and Madam Xia wife of Zhao Dong, agree in both places; but Madam Heng's husband is "Zhao Leize" in this juan's Chaste and Staunch biography and "Zhao Zongze" in juan 6's Loyalty and Integrity biography, and the two disagree. This library sets the two readings side by side without deciding which is right and without altering the gazetteer's own characters. The three women are the ones of Zhao Longze's house who died with him, and this entry is linked to the entries for Madam Liu, Madam Xia and Zhao Longze for cross-reference only, without deciding any further relation beyond the correspondence with the Loyalty and Integrity biography.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as the wife of Zhao Leize and records the three women's drowning themselves. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer gives no home place and records no birth place or native place, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is her death keeping her chastity in Lishui, and the biography belongs to this county's gazetteer, so this library treats it as her activity in Lishui and takes the relation "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; "when... the forces rose and took Jianye" gives no year, so nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no age is inferred.

Second, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives only that she was the wife of Zhao Leize and no home place, so no native place can be decided.

Third, her husband's name disagrees in the two places — "Zhao Leize" in the Chaste and Staunch biography and "Zhao Zongze" in the Loyalty and Integrity biography — and this library sets them side by side without deciding and without altering the gazetteer's characters.

Fourth, the oath of the three women, the place where they drowned, and the year of their deaths are all unrecorded.

Fifth, the biography by Chen Zuren and the encomium by Zhang Zhu are not transcribed here, and neither man has an entry, so nothing about their lives is supplied from this.

Sixth, whether any others of the Zhao house died with them besides the three women and the named Zhao Longze and his son Quan is unrecorded, and no total for the household is inferred.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single notice, the "Three Staunch Women" of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7, and this library has nothing further to add; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
