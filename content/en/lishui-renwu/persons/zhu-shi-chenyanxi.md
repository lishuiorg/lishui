Madam Zhu is one of the virtuous women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the daughter of the county licentiate Jiashi and betrothed to Chen Yanxi. Hers is the notice in the Ming section of one who kept faith with a betrothal to the death. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 朱氏，邑庠生嘉試女。幼受陳彥璽聘，及笄，陳溺死，哭請於母求往吊，其父弗許。後有徐姓者欲委禽，女聞之，以筆書几上曰「願葬陳墓側」，即縊死。陳族數百人迎柩歸，與彥璽合葬史塘凹。先，氏有祖姑未嫁守節，五十歲卒。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "daughter of the county licentiate Jiashi" gives her paternal family; the gazetteer gives only her father's given name, Jiashi, and her surname is Zhu, so her father was of the Zhu family.

Second, "betrothed to Chen Yanxi in childhood; when she came of age, Chen drowned, and she wept and begged her mother to let her go and mourn, but her father would not allow it."

Third, "later a man surnamed Xu sought a match; when she heard of it she wrote on a table with a brush, 'let me be buried beside Chen's grave,' and hanged herself."

Fourth, "hundreds of the Chen clan brought her coffin home and buried her with Yanxi at Shitangao."

Fifth, "earlier, her grandfather's sister had kept her chastity unwed and died at fifty" is a supplement. That maiden is given no further detail and is not made a separate entry here.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as the daughter of a Lishui county licentiate and betrothed to Chen Yanxi; her self-hanging, the bringing home of her coffin and her burial at Shitangao all lie in the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "coming of age" refers to the capping ceremony at about fifteen, not an exact date, and this entry does not convert it.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her father's given name.

Third, the life of her father Zhu Jiashi and the life and death year of Chen Yanxi are unrecorded.

Fourth, the man surnamed Xu is unnamed, and his suit rests only on the gazetteer's account.

Fifth, Shitangao is the burial place the gazetteer gives, and this library cannot locate it in any other material.

Sixth, the grandfather's sister who kept her chastity unwed is only a supplement and is not given a separate entry here; her name is unrecorded.

Seventh, the gazetteer records no court commendation for her, and the notice carries no dates at all.

Eighth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
