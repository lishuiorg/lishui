Mei Hong is one of the filial sons of Lishui listed in the "Ming" block of the Filial Sons biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His is the longest entry in this biography: it gives his childhood, two episodes of family life, a period of mourning, an exchange with a county magistrate, his burial and the popular name of his grave, and then adds three episodes taken from another biography. The entry gives no era date, no township and no birth or death year, and this entry writes only what the gazetteer records, supplying no dates and no family history.

## Childhood and the gazetteer's remarks

> 梅洪，少孤，未嘗學，所行概有儒者風，而尤以孝著。洪自奉甚約，母膳必以甘脆進，其婦勿樂也，洪出，輒有後言。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Orphaned young" and "never studied" are the two facts the entry gives about his childhood; it says nothing of when he was orphaned or why he did not study. "His conduct had broadly the air of a confucian" and "he was noted above all for filiality" are the gazetteer's remarks, and the character the gazetteer uses for "broadly" is reproduced here as it stands; nothing is inferred from a remark back into an act. "He kept himself very sparingly" and "for his mother's meals he always had the sweet and crisp things served" are two everyday details, and they are the most concrete record this library has of how a household in Lishui fed its elder. The phrase about his wife being unhappy and speaking afterward uses the gazetteer's own word for putting her away, which this entry does not rewrite as "divorce".

## Putting away his wife and living with his mother

> 洪覺之，一日呼其婦數之曰：「新婦無禮於姑，尚何論其它！若亟出，願從此絕矣。」親舊力為解，卒不聽。自是洪獨與母居，餘二十年不言娶，奉母唯謹。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

The verb used for his speaking to his wife is reproduced as the gazetteer words it. Relatives and old acquaintances pressed to reconcile the two and he would not listen. "More than twenty years without speaking of taking a wife" is a span the entry gives, not a date: this entry converts nothing, fixes no beginning or end, and infers no age from it.

## His mother's blindness clearing: the gazetteer's account of a marvel

> 後其母瞽，行必洪負之，且泣曰：「天乎！安得使吾母一見孤兒哉？」無何，目忽一明，人皆異其孝感雲。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"When she walked he always carried her" is an act the entry records. "Her eyes suddenly became clear" is the gazetteer's account of a marvel won by filial feeling; this entry does not write it as fact. "Everyone took it as a marvel of his filial influence" is what the gazetteer reports people to have said, reproduced and marked as their opinion, with no judgement on whether the event was real.

## Mourning at the grave, and a magistrate who found him a wife

> 母九十五而卒，洪悲號若嬰慕然。廬墓終三歲，還，縣令聞其賢屢及門，洪輒避去。令語其鄰曰：「孝子薄令耶？且吾欲成孝子孝耳，孝子其有意乎？」洪聞之，始與見。令曰：「惜哉，子未學也！子志於孝而勿娶，是不知有無後憂；不知有無後憂，是使子父母傳子而絕矣！奚其孝？」於是洪大動。令心知其貧，為娶婦，內之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"His mother died at ninety-five" gives her age, not the year of her death; this entry converts nothing and infers no date. "He built a hut at the grave for a full three years" gives the length of his mourning. The entry writes only "the county magistrate" and gives no name, and this library has no material to decide which magistrate it was. That the magistrate knew he was poor and arranged a marriage for him uses the gazetteer's own wording. The entry thus records an exchange between an unnamed magistrate and an unnamed filial son, which is one record this library has of contact between a local official and a household of Lishui.

## His wife leaving, his son dying, and his burial

> 無何，生子矣，而益貧，婦亦勿樂也，數求去。洪嘆曰：「吾昔也子而婦，今也父而母，固命也，復何言！」婦竟去。後數歲其子卒，洪亦隨卒。邑人憐之，收葬於城北十里許，後有監司宋某者題之曰「孝子梅洪之墓」，而人則呼為梅孝子墓而不名。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"A few years later his son died, and Hong died soon after" gives an order, not a year. "Buried about ten li north of the city" is a direction and a distance, not a place that can be located, and this entry does not decide where that would be today. The circuit official is given only as "a certain Song", unnamed, and the year of his inscription is not recorded. "People called it the grave of Mei the filial son and did not use his name" is the popular name of the grave, and on this the entry records one layer in which his name survived locally: the grave was known by a title rather than by his name.

## "A recent hill-dweller of Huangshan wrote his biography": where the last three episodes come from

> 近黃山人汝金為之傳，則又謂：洪嘗有鄰人兄弟爭產者，洪願分己產以償所爭，爭者服其義而解。洪嘗倡新白侯廟，諸富人爭餉之，洪日以半升水糒自隨，諸富人不敢復言餉，議者又服其介。里中兒有目攝其母者，洪惟罪己不已，其人大慚，洪之能下人又如此。又聞洪兩遣婦皆處以禮焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

Three points must be stated. First, everything after "and further says" is the gazetteer quoting another man's biography of Mei Hong, not the gazetteer's own layer: the first part of the entry is the gazetteer's account, while the three added episodes — sharing out his own property to settle a neighbour's inheritance dispute, promoting a temple while carrying only half a sheng of dry food with him each day, and blaming himself when a village boy looked greedily at his mother — come from that other text, and this entry keeps the two layers apart. The words rendered "it is also heard" mark the gazetteer's own language of hearsay, reproduced as it stands. Second, all this library knows of the author is one line: the entry for Wu Shiquan in the Distinguished Officials biography records that Wu engaged "the hill-dweller Huang Rujin" to compile the gazetteer, and "hill-dweller" is the gazetteer's word for him. The related link here points to that entry only as a cross-reference for who this biographer was; it asserts no direct connection between Mei Hong and Wu Shiquan, and it does not assert that this biography is part of the Wanli gazetteer's text. Third, the opening word of the phrase is the gazetteer's own and this entry does not decide whether it means "recently" or "nearby", nor how far apart the two men's dates were.

## The temple name appears elsewhere in the same gazetteer

> 唐时故址即今城隍庙也。元和间改县为白侯庙，而遂为城隍。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 3

This entry says Mei Hong once promoted the "new Marquis Bai temple", while juan 3 of the same gazetteer records that in the Yuanhe era the county seat was changed into the "Marquis Bai temple" and so became the city god's temple. The two names are the same except that this entry adds the word "new". Whether they are one temple, and what "new" means (newly built, rebuilt, or a second temple), is nowhere recorded, and this library does not decide. A separate entry in this library, on Bai Juyi and Lishui, sets out the two dates this gazetteer and the Guangxu gazetteer give for that temple; this entry quotes none of it and only notes that the act recorded here and the temple named elsewhere may be held side by side for later checking.

## The gazetteer's closing remark

> 嗚呼！洪果不學而身備諸善乎哉？然而可以名洪者，則無如孝雲。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

This is the gazetteer's own closing judgement on the entry, reproduced and marked as such; nothing is decided from it about whether Mei Hong "possessed all the other virtues".

## Relation to Lishui

The entry gives no township for Mei Hong — most other entries in this biography give one (Shanyang, Sihe, Bailu, Fengqing, Zanxian, Xiantan townships) while this one gives none, and only "about ten li north of the city" at his burial — and it gives no birthplace. He enters the persons site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: no birthplace is recorded, so "born in Lishui" is not available; no office and no recommendation out of Lishui are recorded, so "official service" and "recommended from Lishui" are not available; no writing of his is recorded, so "writings" is not available; what the entry records is his conduct in Lishui — supporting his mother, mourning at her grave, his dealings with the magistrate, promoting a temple, episodes in his neighbourhood, and where he was buried — so the relation is "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his courtesy name and his township are nowhere recorded. The entry gives no era date — in this "Ming" block only three entries give one, Yu Zhou in the Tianshun era, Ding Ying in the Chenghua era and Zhou Shiyi in the Hongzhi era — so the era value "Ming" here only records that the biography places him among its Ming entries; it proves no particular Ming year, and nothing is converted into a Common-Era year.

Second, the county magistrate is unnamed and this library has no material to decide which magistrate it was; this entry does not use era dates from other entries in the same biography to fix the post he held.

Third, the circuit official is given only as "a certain Song", unnamed, and the year of his inscription is unrecorded; the word "later" records order only, not a date.

Fourth, the clearing of his mother's blindness is the gazetteer's account of a marvel, and what "everyone" said is their opinion; neither is written as fact.

Fifth, "orphaned young", "never studied", "the air of a confucian" and "noted for filiality" are two facts of his childhood and two remarks of the gazetteer, and nothing is recorded about what they rest on.

Sixth, the last three episodes come from the other biography the entry quotes, not from the gazetteer's own layer; of that author this library knows only the two characters "hill-dweller", and his life, his dates and whether his text survives are all unknown.

Seventh, where the "new Marquis Bai temple" stood, and whether it is the temple named in juan 3, cannot be decided from this library's material.

Eighth, "about ten li north of the city" cannot be located; whether the grave called the filial son's grave still existed when this gazetteer was compiled, and where it is today, is nowhere recorded.

Ninth, this entry rests on one entry of this biography and takes two entries from other volumes of the same gazetteer only as cross-references for a name and a temple name; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him. The biography used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
