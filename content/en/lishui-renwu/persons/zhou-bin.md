Zhou Bin is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Distinguished Officials" biography section. His biography runs to barely twenty characters, one of the shortest among the Song entries of the volume: one clause of origin and appointment, one judgement, one report of popular feeling. This entry writes nothing beyond that clause, and places everything the gazetteer does not record under Points of doubt rather than supplying it.

## Name and appointment

> 周邠，字开祖。元丰四年任，节俭爱民，民德之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Kaizu and took up office in the fourth year of the Yuanfeng era. "Fourth year of Yuanfeng" is a fixed year given by the volume, and it dates only his arrival; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers neither the start nor the end of his term from the dynasty. The gazetteer writes only "appointed" and gives no formal title for his Lishui post; this entry does not write "district magistrate" and does not borrow one from neighbouring entries, some of which read "administered Lishui as district magistrate", others "served as a constable", others "served as vice-magistrate".

## What the gazetteer records

"Frugal, loving the people" is a judgement, and it cites no act: the gazetteer does not say what earned the praise, what expenditure it cut, what corvée it lightened, or what it stopped building, and this entry reasons back from the judgement to no policy. "The people were grateful to him" reports popular feeling — the gazetteer uses the character for "virtue" as a verb, and this entry reproduces its wording — and is a report rather than a verifiable fact.

The biography records no building, case, tax measure or school matter during his Lishui term, and nothing about his career, final office or death afterwards.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place, and he enters this library through official service. What ties him to Lishui is the single clause dating his appointment, together with the judgement and the report of popular feeling attached to it. Apart from being named in the biography section, the entry touches no Lishui affair.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; neither his native place nor his route into office is recorded.

Second, his appointment is dated only as "fourth year of Yuanfeng", which gives the year of arrival; the year he left is not recorded, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer writes only "appointed" for his Lishui post and gives no formal title; this entry does not write "district magistrate" and supplies no other title.

Fourth, what "frugal, loving the people" and "the people were grateful to him" actually rest on is nowhere recorded.

Fifth, the order of the Song entries in this volume does not follow the appointment years the volume itself gives. The volume places this entry after "Guan Qi", whose biography dates his appointment to the second year of Chongning, and before "Zhou Bangyan" (eighth year of Yuanyou) and "Zhang Ge" (second year of Daguan) — so this entry's year is earlier than one it precedes and later than the entry it follows. The order of the volume therefore cannot be used as evidence of sequence in office, and this entry infers no appointment year or seniority from it.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him. The volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
