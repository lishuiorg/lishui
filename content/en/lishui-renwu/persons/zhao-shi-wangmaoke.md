Madam Zhao is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Wang Maoke. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 趙氏，王懋柯妻。年二十七柯亡，家貧，苦節，紡織撫孤。萬曆間知縣董懋中旌其閭曰「貞節」，邑進士王名登有詩，載《藝文》。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Wang Maoke" names husband and wife.

Second, "when Ke died she was twenty-seven; the family was poor, she kept a bitter chastity, and wove to raise the orphan."

Third, "in the Wanli era the magistrate Dong Maozhong honoured her gate as 'Chaste.'" The office, name, reign era and gate-name are reproduced as given; the reign era is not converted.

Fourth, "the local graduate Wang Mingdeng wrote a poem, recorded in the 'Literature' chapter." That poem has not been obtained here.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Wang Maoke, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her raising of the orphan and the honour to her gate attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings of her own, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Not merged with the "Zhao lineage hall" notice

This entry, a person, shares a de-suffixed title with the lishui-history place "Zhao lineage hall" (ls:place:zhaoshi-zongci), but the two are distinct: this entry is a Ming-era chaste widow recorded in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, while the other is a Qing ancestral hall in Guotangtou village, Honglan. ## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty-seven" dates her husband's death.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Wang Maoke are unrecorded, and the orphan she raised is unnamed.

Fourth, the Wanli reign dates only the honour; the only honour recorded is the magistrate Dong Maozhong's naming of her gate, and whether there were others is not stated.

Fifth, the poem by the local graduate Wang Mingdeng is recorded in the literature chapter but has not been obtained.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
