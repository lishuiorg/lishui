Yan Huang is one of the seven men in the "Song" block of the Filial Sons biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and the longest-mentioned of them: a degree, one clause on his childhood reputation, one episode during the Yuan crossing, a lifelong injury, and his age at death. The biography records no residence and no birth or death years, and this entry writes only what this line records, supplying no life for him.

## The degree the gazetteer records

> 嚴晃，宋武進士。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Song military jinshi" is the degree as the gazetteer words it, and this entry reproduces the four characters without rewriting them as plain "jinshi" — the military and the civil routes are distinct and the gazetteer's wording stands. The gazetteer records no year of his passing and no office of his, and this entry supplies no post and no year from the degree.

## What the gazetteer records him doing

> 幼以孝聞，元兵渡江，晃負母避，（與）［遇］兵，值兵刃其母，晃以身蔽之，刃中其頸，終身不能仰視。壽七十八而終。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Known for filiality from childhood" is a judgement and cites no act, and this entry reasons back from it to no deed. "Yuan troops crossed the river" is the background event as the gazetteer records it, with no year attached, and this entry converts and infers nothing. The collation bracket in the transcription is reproduced exactly as given, neither deleted nor resolved. Carrying his mother to shelter and shielding her with his body are the gazetteer's account of his conduct; the blade in his neck, his lifelong inability to raise his head, his age of seventy-eight and his death are likewise the gazetteer's record — all are the gazetteer's account, and this entry reproduces them, marks their nature, presents them as no modern fact, and reasons from "seventy-eight" to no birth year.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records no residence for him, the only man of this Song block given no place. He enters the persons site with the relation "Active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: he stands in a Lishui gazetteer's Filial Sons biography, and his tie to Lishui is that biography's placement of him together with the filial act it records of him, yet the biography records no birthplace and no township for him, so "born in Lishui" is not available; the gazetteer records no Lishui office, no writing, and no recommendation out of Lishui into office elsewhere, so "official service", "writings" and "Recommended from Lishui" are not available; what it records is his filial conduct and his lifelong wound, which is activity as this library knows it, so this entry uses "Active in Lishui". As for his role: the biography places him by his filial conduct and records no office, and the gazetteer names only a degree, so this entry keeps the role "local worthy" rather than "official".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are nowhere recorded, no residence is recorded, no family is recorded, and neither the year of his degree nor any office of his is recorded.

Second, the gazetteer calls him a Song military jinshi while placing the event that maimed him at the crossing of the river by Yuan troops, and his lifelong injury and his death at seventy-eight follow it: his life spans Song and Yuan, and this is a tension the gazetteer's own wording creates. The era values available to this library include no "Song-Yuan transition", so this entry uses "Five Dynasties, Song and Yuan", while the biography lists him in its Song block and words his degree as Song, and this library follows the gazetteer's own Song label. This entry decides neither whether he was born under Song or Yuan nor any Common-Era year.

Third, "Yuan troops crossed the river" is a background event with no year attached; this entry infers from it neither his birth and death nor the year of his wound, and converts nothing.

Fourth, "died at seventy-eight" is the gazetteer's figure for his age; this entry reproduces it and reasons from it to no birth year and to no birth-and-death range.

Fifth, "known for filiality from childhood" is a judgement whose basis is unrecorded; the episodes of carrying his mother and shielding her are the gazetteer's account, and their nature is marked in the body of this entry, where no plausibility is judged.

Sixth, the tag "Yuan" in this entry rests only on the phrase "Yuan troops crossed the river" and states no separate conclusion about his dynastic affiliation; the two-fold dating is recorded above.

Seventh, the order of the seven Song lines does not follow the era years the block gives, since two of them record no year at all; the order therefore cannot be used as evidence of sequence, and this entry infers no dates from it.

Eighth, this library also holds an entry for a Ming instructor of the same surname recorded in the Recommendations examination of juan 2; the two are different men in different eras and different sections, no kinship is recorded, and the two entries must not be merged.
