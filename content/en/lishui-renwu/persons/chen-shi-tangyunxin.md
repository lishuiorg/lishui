Madam Chen is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer names her only through "the mother of the county-school student Tang Yunxin" and records her chastity, her encouragement of her son's studies, and her long and healthy old age, closing with the gazetteer's judgement. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 陳氏，生員湯運新之母。夫之俊早喪，家貧，誓死［不］再醮。和熊勵子，辛勤歷五十年。歲七十矣，既壽且康，人以為苦節之報雲。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is layered.

"Madam Chen, mother of the county-school student Tang Yunxin" gives her natal surname and her son. "County-school student" is the rank the gazetteer records for him, a student of the local county school; the gazetteer names her through her son, so her given name, courtesy name, and residence are unrecorded.

"Her husband Zhijun died young, the family was poor, and she vowed never to remarry to the death" gives her husband's early death, her poverty, and her vow. The gazetteer gives the husband only as "Zhijun", without a surname; "to remarry" means to take a second husband, and the character "not" in her vow is a supplement marked by square brackets in the transcription. This entry keeps the brackets in the quotation and reads the text as corrected. Her birth year, her husband's years, and her death year are all unrecorded.

"She encouraged her son in his studies and toiled fifty years" gives her teaching and the length of her chastity. "Encouraged her son" renders the gazetteer's allusion to a traditional practice of preparing bear-gall pills to keep a son at his studies, whose details the gazetteer does not set out; this entry keeps the words without expanding them. "Fifty years" is the gazetteer's figure.

"At seventy she was long-lived and healthy, and people took it as the reward of her chastity" gives her age and the gazetteer's judgement. "People took it as the reward of her chastity" is a judgement, not a fact.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: mother of the county-school student Tang Yunxin, poor and widowed, vowing never to remarry, encouraging her son in his studies and keeping chastity fifty years, her son a student of the county school and the household affairs in the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Chen", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her — her son's student status is his study, not her own recommendation — so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was long-lived and healthy at seventy; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no exact year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer names her only through "the mother of the county-school student Tang Yunxin" and "Madam Chen"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, her husband is given only as "Zhijun", without a surname, and the gazetteer does not state his courtesy name or status; whether she had children besides Tang Yunxin is unrecorded.

Fourth, the character "not" in "vowed never to remarry to the death" is a supplement marked by square brackets in the transcription; this entry keeps the text as written and reads the corrected character. Since the transcription supplies a character, the original text here must be defective.

Fifth, "encouraged her son in his studies" is the deed the gazetteer records, its details unrecorded, and this entry keeps the words without expanding them.

Sixth, "people took it as the reward of her chastity" is the gazetteer's judgement, not factual record.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Eighth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
