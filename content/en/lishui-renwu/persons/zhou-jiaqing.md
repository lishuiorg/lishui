Zhou Jiaqing is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in the "Ming" block of the Filial Sons biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His is one of the longest entries in this biography and the one most heavily coloured by marvels. The entry gives four facts of his person — courtesy name, township, father, mother — and then records his nursing, his dreams, his travels, the ages of himself and his mother, and a commendation. This entry keeps the layer that can be recorded (courtesy name, township, parents, the manner of his nursing, that he was commended) apart from the layer that cannot be written as fact (dreams, marvels, lamps on the sea, a monk's blessing), and supplies no dates.

## What the gazetteer records of his person and home

> 周家慶，字承善，仙壇鄉荷塘人。父尚禮，邑庠士，早卒。事母沈氏以孝聞。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Courtesy name Chengshan" is his courtesy name as the entry gives it. "Hetang in Xiantan township" is his home as the entry gives it, and it is the most usable fact in the entry. "His father Shangli, a county school student, died early" gives his father's degree and the manner of his death, with no year. "He served his mother of the Shen clan and was known for filiality" gives his mother's clan and why he was noted. The entry gives no birth or death year, no degree of his own, and nothing further of his family.

## Nursing his mother: the acts the gazetteer records

> 母嘗病篤，百藥不瘳，慶衣不解帶，水不入口，徹夜祈斗，願以身代。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"His clothes stayed on him" and "no water passed his lips" describe how he nursed her. "Through the night he prayed to the Dipper" records the manner of his praying; what rite this was and to which practice it belonged is nowhere recorded, and this entry decides nothing about it. "He wished to take her place in his own body" records his wish. These four clauses belong to the layer of acts, and are distinct from the dreams and marvels that follow.

## Dreams and marvels: the gazetteer's accounts

> 恍惚夢有人騎白象而來，曰，「爾心可嘉，增母壽一紀。」又夢一女人以盞水噀母面，又噀慶面，曰，「令汝母子同壽。」覺而母忽清涼，沉疴立起。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

The opening word marks what follows as seen in a dream. This entry marks the rider of the white elephant, the woman with a cup of water, and their words as the gazetteer's account of dreams, and writes none of them as fact. "Adding one cycle to his mother's lifespan" and "making mother and son equal in lifespan" are the words of the dream, reproduced as given. "On waking, his mother suddenly felt cool and rose from a long illness" is the gazetteer's account of the turn in her illness, part of its layer of marvels, and is not written here as fact. The word used for "cycle" and its number are reproduced as the gazetteer words them, with no conversion into a number of years.

## Mount Wutai and the southern sea: the travels the gazetteer records

> 慶自忖曰：「夢乘象者，必五台山文殊佛也。」誓往謁謝，冒險至山。時有夜台禪師，名重朝野，每夜必週遊五台。一見慶至，摩頂受記曰：「汝一點孩心，來得清淨，子母俱全。」同人數百，一無所語。慶又忖曰：「以水沃面者，或南海觀音耶？」即乘筏渡南海，黑夜至捨身岩，跪祝海神曰：「慶遠歷山海者，為祈母壽耳，願見海燈兆母壽數。」
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

The two passages of "he reasoned to himself" record his own reading of what the dreams meant; these are his views, reproduced and marked as his. Mount Wutai, the southern sea and the cliff named for the giving up of the body are all outside Lishui, and on these the entry records a journey, which is one record this library has of a man of Lishui travelling far. Whether he actually reached those places, and what his crossing to them involved, is nowhere recorded and is not written here as fact. "A monk named Yetai, whose reputation weighed with court and country" is a named monk and a claim about his renown, and this library has no material to identify him. The laying of a hand on the head and the words spoken, and the hundreds of companions who said nothing, belong to the gazetteer's layer of marvels.

## The number of lamps and the equal lifespans

> 跪至丙夜，果見波濤上燈大如月者七。慶又叩曰：「豈母壽止七袠耶？願求其餘。」後又見燈小如星者九，因拜謝之。後母果七十九齡，無疾而終。於是，慶日究佛道變化之由，其後慶亦七十九齡無疾而終，正應夢中母子同壽之數。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

The word used for the watch of the night at which he knelt is reproduced as the gazetteer words it. "Seven lamps as large as the moon on the waves" and "nine lamps as small as stars" are numbers in the gazetteer's layer of marvels. The character used for the decade in "seven decades" is reproduced without being rewritten, and this entry does not assert that the gazetteer means a decade by it. "His mother at seventy-nine, dying without illness" and "he too at seventy-nine, dying without illness" give the ages of two people, not their birth or death years: nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no birth year is inferred. "This exactly matched the number in the dream of equal lifespans" is the gazetteer's own arithmetic, reproduced and marked as its claim. "From then he daily investigated the way of the transformations of the buddha teaching" records the direction he took afterwards.

## Commended, but with no year and no title recorded

> 前後邑大夫聞而異之，俱旌其孝雲。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Successive great officers of the county" is the gazetteer's wording: it names no one and gives no year of their posts. "All commended his filiality" records that he was commended, with no year and no title of the commendation. Other entries in this biography give a title for a commendation (Yi Xiaoyi's ward named "model of filiality", Xia Mou's ward named "bright filiality"), give the name of the commending official (Yu Qin and Huang Cheng, commended by the county magistrate Fu Yingzhen), or record an arch raised (Tang Xueshen), while this entry records none of those particulars, and this entry borrows none from the others.

## Cross-reference to Zhou Shiyi in the same biography

This entry places him at "Hetang in Xiantan township", while the entry for Zhou Shiyi in the same biography places him at "a man of Hetang village, first district of Xiantan township, in the Hongzhi era". Both give Xiantan township and Hetang, but this entry gives no era date while that one gives the Hongzhi era, and their fathers (Shangli and Yongsi) and their mothers (of the Shen and of the Zhu clans) differ. Whether the two are of one family, whether Hetang and Hetang village are one place, and whether their dates meet, are nowhere recorded, and this library does not decide. The related link points to the entry for Zhou Shiyi only as a cross-reference for the same township and village, and asserts no decision about any relationship between them.

## Relation to Lishui

The entry places him at Hetang in Xiantan township, which is where the gazetteer says he lived. He enters the persons site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the township is a place of residence, not a birth record — this biography records no birthplace for anyone, so "born in Lishui" is not available; no Lishui office, no recommendation out of Lishui and no writing of his are recorded, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui" and "writings" are not available; what the entry records is his nursing, his praying and his commendation in Lishui, so the relation is "active in Lishui". His journeys to Mount Wutai and the sea record travel away from Lishui, and this entry does not use them to switch the relation to "sojourn".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are nowhere recorded. The entry gives only the ages of his mother and of himself, seventy-nine each, which are ages and not dates: nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no birth year is inferred.

Second, the entry gives no era date — in this "Ming" block only three entries give one, for Yu Zhou, Ding Ying and Zhou Shiyi — so the era value "Ming" here only records that the biography places him among its Ming entries, and proves no particular Ming year.

Third, "successive great officers of the county" are unnamed, and the year and title of the commendation are unrecorded; this library has no material to decide which magistrates they were.

Fourth, the rider of the white elephant, the woman with the cup of water, the laying on of the head, and the seven and nine lamps are all the gazetteer's accounts of marvels, and the matching of the numbers with two lives of seventy-nine is the gazetteer's own claim; none is written here as fact.

Fifth, the monk Yetai, Mount Wutai, the southern sea and the cliff are places and persons this library cannot identify; whether the journey actually reached them is unrecorded.

Sixth, what the prayer to the Dipper was, and to which practice it belonged, is nowhere recorded, and this entry decides nothing about it.

Seventh, the words used for "cycle", for the decade in "seven decades", and for the watch of the night are the gazetteer's own and its own numbers, reproduced without conversion into years or hours.

Eighth, whether this entry and the entry for Zhou Shiyi in the same biography concern one family, and whether the two place names are one place, cannot be decided from this library's material; the related link is a cross-reference only.

Ninth, this entry rests on one entry of this biography; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
