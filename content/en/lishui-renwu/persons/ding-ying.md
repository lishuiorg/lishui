Ding Ying is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in the "Ming" block of the Filial Sons biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The entry is short but contains something none of the other entries in this biography has: a filial son refusing a commendation. It also bears directly on an entry of the Distinguished Officials biography, since the "magistrate Wang Bi" named here is Wang Bi. This entry writes only what the gazetteer records and supplies no life for him.

## Studentship and mourning

> 丁溁，成化間為諸生，事母孝，母死，廬墓側。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"The Chenghua era" dates the period in which he was a student, not his birth or death; nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no age is inferred. "Student" is the degree the entry gives; which school and in what year he entered it are unrecorded. "He built a hut beside the grave" is where he mourned, but the entry gives no length of mourning — other entries in this biography do give lengths (Mei Hong three full years, Yuan Wenhua nine years, Qian Wenxiang three years) while this one gives none, and this entry borrows no length from them.

## "The magistrate Wang Bi" is Wang Bi: this entry supplies his Lishui title

> 時王令弼稱長者，甚悉溁哀毀狀，乃過慰之。與之語，若將厭厭從地下者，令為嗟嘆不已。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Magistrate Wang Bi" is this entry's wording. This library has a separate entry for Wang Bi (`ls:person:wang-bi`), resting on the Distinguished Officials biography of juan 6, whose roster records him as "courtesy name Cunjing, a man of Huangyan" and "appointed in the eleventh year of Chenghua". The dates agree — this entry places Ding Ying as a student in the Chenghua era, the roster places Wang Bi's appointment in the eleventh Chenghua year — and this entry attaches the word "magistrate" to his name. On that basis this library decides that the "magistrate Wang Bi" here is the Wang Bi of the Distinguished Officials biography, and states plainly that this is this library's decision from two volumes read side by side, not a statement the gazetteer makes. The decision has a further use: the Distinguished Officials biography gives Wang Bi no title in Lishui, and the word "magistrate" here is where this library knows that title from. This biography repeatedly supplies the Lishui titles of sitting magistrates in the words "magistrate" and "county magistrate", which is one way this biography can support the Distinguished Officials biography.

## Refusing the commendation: the crucial part of the entry

> 令凡三過，溁卒無異曩所見，令去溁亦無所報謝，令益高之。將聞其事於朝，溁遜謝曰：「使君意良厚，獨奈何使溁以大不幸者就旦夕名，溁且愧死矣！」令亦驚謝曰：「吾欲以旦夕名重輕子，曩實非知子者。」
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"The magistrate came three times" records three visits. In the phrase "in the end Ying was no different from what he had been seen as before", the word rendered "in the end" means "finally, after all" and describes his mourning; it does not record his death, and the entry records no death here. "He gave the magistrate no thanks" is Ding Ying's manner toward him. "He meant to report his case to the throne" is the magistrate's plan to seek a commendation, and "he declined with humility" is Ding Ying's refusal. The words of that refusal — asking why a man bearing a great misfortune should be made to win a name overnight — are what this entry records Ding Ying as saying. Other entries in this biography record commendations received (Tang Xueshen, whose case an official reported to the throne and who was given a arch at his gate; Huang Mingzhao, given inscriptions; Ren Shiqi, commended by the magistrate Yang Guozhen), while this one records a refusal, and it is the one record this library has of a man of Lishui declining such an honour.

## Going to the capital as a paid official, and the record Wang Bi wrote him

> 後歲余，溁為貲郎入都，王令時在比部，因撰《丁孝子廬墓記》贈之，雅非溁意也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7

"Paid official" is this entry's wording; this entry reproduces it and decides neither what office it was nor how he entered office. "Went to the capital" records that he travelled; where he went and what he did there are unrecorded. "The magistrate was then in the Board of Punishments" records Wang Bi's whereabouts at that time, using the gazetteer's own term. "Quite against Ying's wishes" is the gazetteer's remark, reproduced and marked as such. The *Record of Ding the Filial Son's Mourning Hut* is a text this entry says Wang Bi wrote and gave him; this library has not recorded that text. The arts-and-literature section of juan 8 as recorded in this library's source card breaks off in the middle of the biography of the recorder of Kuizhou, so the absence of this record from that part cannot be taken as evidence that the record does not exist.

## Relation to Lishui

The entry gives Ding Ying no township and no birthplace. He enters the persons site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: no birthplace is recorded, so "born in Lishui" is not available; the office "paid official" is given with no place and it is not said to be a Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the entry records no writing of his, so "writings" is not available — the *Record of the Mourning Hut* was written by Wang Bi and given to Ding Ying, not written by Ding Ying; what the entry records is his mourning in Lishui, his dealings with the magistrate and his refusal of a commendation, so the relation is "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his courtesy name and his township are nowhere recorded.

Second, "the Chenghua era" dates his studentship, not his birth or his mourning; nothing is converted into a Common-Era year.

Third, that "the magistrate Wang Bi" is the Wang Bi of the Distinguished Officials biography is this library's decision from the agreement of dates and the wording of two volumes; no passage anywhere states that the two are one man. If the decision fails, the magistrate of this entry is someone else and this library has no material to identify him.

Fourth, the two volumes give different accounts of Wang Bi after Lishui: this library's entry for Wang Bi records the Distinguished Officials biography as saying he went on to be prefect of Xinghua, while this entry says he was then in the Board of Punishments. The order of the two, and whether they are one post, are left undecided with both wordings set side by side.

Fifth, what office "paid official" was, how he obtained it, and what his going to the capital involved are nowhere recorded; this entry does not decide from it whether he held a post in Lishui.

Sixth, whether the *Record of Ding the Filial Son's Mourning Hut* survives, and where its text may be read, is unrecorded; the arts-and-literature section of juan 8 as this library holds it does not mention it, but that section is itself truncated, so its absence proves nothing.

Seventh, "quite against Ying's wishes" and "the magistrate esteemed him all the more" are both the gazetteer's remarks, and nothing is recorded about what they rest on.

Eighth, this entry rests on one entry of this biography and takes one entry from the Distinguished Officials biography only to identify the magistrate and to set his later career side by side; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him. The biography used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
