Fu Pou is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography is short but has three layers: township and origin, the grave hut, and his coming out in response to a summons from "Magistrate Fu", with what listeners said attached to it. The third layer is one of the places where this library learns Fu Yingzhen's Lishui title, so it is treated in a section of its own.

## The gazetteer text

> 傅裒，贊賢鄉人。歲貢，不樂仕。母死，廬墓，以孝聞。嘗應傅令應禎聘而出，聞者曰，「傅生既敝屣乎世，而又為傅令屈，則傅令賢可知矣。」
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Township and origin

The gazetteer calls him a man of Zanxian Township, one of the township names this volume records for Lishui; this entry keeps the wording, and this library has no material linking the township to a present-day settlement. "Tribute student" is the route into status as the gazetteer records it, kept as such rather than converted into "recommended graduate" or any higher title, and the year of the tribute is not given. "Took no pleasure in office" is the gazetteer's statement about his unwillingness to serve; what he declined is nowhere recorded.

## What the gazetteer records

After his mother's death he built a hut at her grave and became known for filial piety. The hut is an act as recorded by the gazetteer; neither the year nor the number of years is given. "Became known for filial piety" is the gazetteer's general judgement; among whom he was known, and what was said of him, are unrecorded.

## "Magistrate Fu Yingzhen": where this volume gives Fu Yingzhen's Lishui title

The gazetteer records that he once responded to a summons from "Magistrate Fu Yingzhen" and came out. Four points must be made clear.

First, "Magistrate Fu" is this biography's way of naming Fu Yingzhen; the entries on Yu Qin and Huang Cheng in the same volume both read "the district magistrate Fu Yingzhen commended him". Fu Yingzhen's Lishui title of district magistrate is given by this volume, and these three passages are what this library knows it from.

Second, this library already has an entry for Liu Yinglei (`ls:person:liu-yinglei`), whose biography in juan 6 contains the clause "the matter is set out in the record by Censor Fu Yingzhen", and the man referred to is this Fu Yingzhen. But that passage writes "Censor Fu", while this volume writes "Magistrate Fu" and "district magistrate": the two titles differ, one censor and one district magistrate. This library keeps both wordings side by side, decides neither that the two titles are the same office nor that the man named in juan 6 must be the man named here. The `related` link to the Liu Yinglei entry is a cross-reference for checking only, not an identification of the two as one person.

Third, this library has no person entry for Fu Yingzhen. What this library knows of him as district magistrate sits in the text of the Liu Yinglei entry and in the entry recording the establishment of the village compact in the second Wanli year (`ls:event:ming-wanli-2-xiangyue-1574`). This entry borrows no appointment years or career steps from those entries and fixes no date for Fu Pou's coming out.

Fourth, "since Master Fu treats the world as worn shoes, yet yields to Magistrate Fu, the worthiness of Magistrate Fu may be known" is a judgement the gazetteer attributes to "listeners" — the comment of people who heard of it, not verifiable record. This entry reproduces it and marks its nature. What lies behind "treats the world as worn shoes" and "yields to Magistrate Fu" is nowhere recorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in a named township, and this entry enters him as "active in Lishui". A township is the place of residence the gazetteer records, not a birth record: the text says only that he was a man of such-and-such township and gives no birthplace, so this entry does not record him as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his residence in Zanxian Township, his origin through the local tribute system, the hut at his mother's grave, and his response to a summons from a magistrate of this county — the last being the only place where the biography ties him directly to a Lishui officeholder.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor are his mother's.

Second, the year of his tribute status is not recorded; this entry converts and infers nothing, and the biography carries no dates at all.

Third, the year and length of the grave hut and the burial place of his mother are unrecorded.

Fourth, what office or what business he declined when he "took no pleasure in office" is unrecorded.

Fifth, the date of his response to the summons, what he was summoned to do, and what he then did are nowhere recorded; this entry does not identify the business by way of the village-compact and school-land matters recorded elsewhere in this gazetteer.

Sixth, "Magistrate Fu" and "district magistrate" here and "Censor Fu" in the officials' biography give two different titles; this library keeps both and decides nothing, and the `related` link is a cross-reference only.

Seventh, "listeners said" is reported comment, not factual record.

Eighth, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Ninth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
