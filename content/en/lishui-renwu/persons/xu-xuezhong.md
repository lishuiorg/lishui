Xu Xuezhong is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography is one of the shortest in the volume: one clause of local reputation and one of award. This entry writes nothing beyond those two clauses and places everything the gazetteer does not record under Points of doubt rather than supplying it.

## The gazetteer text

> 徐學忠，以純孝稱於鄉里。天啟間奉旨旌，賜粟帛。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Local reputation and award

"He was known in his neighbourhood for pure filial piety" is the biography's only account of his conduct — the gazetteer records only that this reputation attached to him locally, and cites no act: not what he did, not which relative he treated, not any vigil at a grave. This entry reasons back from the phrase "pure filial piety" to no specific deed.

"In the Tianqi period he was commended by imperial decree and granted grain and cloth" gives the only date and the only favour. "Tianqi" here dates the award by decree, not his birth or death and not the year of any deed; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers neither his birth and death nor the span of his activity from it. "Commended by decree" states the source of the favour as an imperial order, and "granted grain and cloth" states the goods given; how much grain and cloth, whether a tablet or archway accompanied it, and how the commendation was carried out are nowhere recorded, and this entry does not infer the form from other entries in this volume that mention tablets or archways.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Xu Xuezhong no township and no native place; it records only that his reputation was local and that he is listed in this volume's filial sons section. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is that he was praised in the neighbourhoods of this county and recorded in its biography section, not that he was born here, so it does not record him as "born locally". Apart from the reputation and the award, the biography touches no Lishui affair.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; neither township nor native place is recorded.

Second, "Tianqi period" dates only the award by decree; this entry converts nothing and infers no birth, death or active period from it.

Third, his origin is not recorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Fourth, the specific conduct behind the reputation for "pure filial piety" is nowhere cited.

Fifth, the quantity of grain and cloth, whether any tablet or archway was involved, and how the commendation was executed are all unrecorded.

Sixth, which decree was followed and through which office it passed is unrecorded, and this entry supplies nothing.

Seventh, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Eighth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
