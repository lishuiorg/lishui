Madam Chen is one of the martyred women recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her surname, Chen, and her husband's name, Qian Hualong, together with his status as a student of the county school. The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. This entry rests on no more than this single item in juan 7, and supplies nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 陳氏，邑庠生錢化龍妻。乙酉歲亂，兵入其家欲犯之，婦憤然赴水死。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records two things.

"Madam Chen, wife of Qian Hualong, a student of the county school" gives her surname and her husband's name, and records her husband's status as a student of the county school, a degree holder of this county. That is the status the gazetteer gives; this entry quotes it as it stands without changing it to "licentiate" or another degree. Her personal name, courtesy name, native place and paternal kin are nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies none of them.

"The disorders of the yiyou year" is the year of the troubles the gazetteer records; it gives the cyclical year name "yiyou" alone, with no reign name. This entry quotes "yiyou" as it stands, converts it to no year of the common era, and does not identify it with the "second year of Shunzhi" recorded in the item on Madam Zhou in the same section. "The disorders" is the gazetteer's summary of the troubles of that year; its causes and course are not recorded.

"Soldiers entered her house intending to violate her, and she resolutely drowned herself" is the deed recorded in this item: disorderly soldiers entered her house intending to violate her, and she drowned herself in her fury. The gazetteer says not to whom the soldiers belonged, not where in Lishui her house was, and not into what water she threw herself; this entry supplies none of these. "Resolutely drowned herself" is the gazetteer's record of the deed and is quoted as the gazetteer's account, without further interpretation.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is this woman's martyrdom at Lishui — in the troubles of the yiyou year soldiers entered her house and she drowned herself; her house must have lain within Lishui, and her husband was a student of this county's school, so her conduct has to do with Lishui. Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or native place, calling her only by her surname Chen, so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is her meeting troubles at home, not sojourning elsewhere, so "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five values, only "active in Lishui" can be grounded, and on that this entry enters the library.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives the year of the troubles only as "yiyou", and that cyclical year carries no reign name; this entry converts it to no year of the common era and infers neither her birth nor her death year.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Chen" and records neither her paternal kin nor her residence; this entry does not infer her native place from her husband's surname or from other items in this volume.

Third, apart from her husband's name Qian Hualong, her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. The gazetteer records only that her husband was a county school student; his courtesy name and career, and whether they had children, are nowhere recorded.

Fourth, the honours may not be complete. This item does not record whether she received honours after death, and this entry does not infer it from other items in this volume.

Fifth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer alone. It is taken from the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription".

Sixth, this library has searched its other sources and found no material that would supplement this person.
