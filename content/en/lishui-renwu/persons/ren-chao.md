Ren Chao is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography records one deed and then adds a hard clause; this entry keeps the recordable layer (cutting his thigh for his father) separate from the undecidable one.

## The gazetteer text

> 任超，篤於行孝，父病危，超籲天刲股，密調劑。而父愈，年八十餘，垂老始得聞之，天欲彰隱德也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## What the gazetteer records

"Steadfast in filial conduct" is the gazetteer's opening summary, and what he was steadfast in is not specified. "His father was critically ill; Ren Chao cried to heaven, cut his own thigh, and privately compounded the treatment" is the biography's only deed. "Privately compounded" is the gazetteer's wording for preparing the medicine; how it was compounded and with what is not recorded. "And his father recovered" is the gazetteer's closing statement on the episode. The year of the illness, the father's later lifespan and Ren Chao's own age at the time are nowhere recorded.

## "Aged over eighty, only at extreme old age did he hear of it": the sense is unclear

The gazetteer continues: "aged over eighty, only at extreme old age did he hear of it; heaven wished to make hidden virtue manifest". Two things cannot be settled, and this library keeps the ambiguity rather than choosing. First, the subject of "aged over eighty" is not fixed by the text — it may be read of the father, who recovered and lived past eighty, or of Ren Chao himself; the gazetteer gives no basis for the reading, and this entry decides neither. Second, who "heard" and what was heard is equally unclear — whether the father only learned of his son's cutting at extreme old age, or whether someone else learned of Ren Chao's act — and this entry supplies nothing.

"Heaven wished to make hidden virtue manifest" is the gazetteer's own explanation of the episode, not verifiable record; this entry reproduces it and marks its nature. The biography says nothing about any award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Ren Chao no township and no native place; it ties him to this county only by placing him in the filial sons section. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is that he is recorded in this county's biography section, not that he was born here, so it does not record him as "born locally". Apart from the cutting of his thigh for his father, the biography touches no Lishui affair.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; township and native place are unrecorded; the biography carries no dates at all, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, the subject of "aged over eighty" is not fixed by the text — father or Ren Chao himself — and this library does not decide.

Third, who heard and what was heard in "only at extreme old age did he hear of it" is unclear, and this entry supplies nothing.

Fourth, "heaven wished to make hidden virtue manifest" is the gazetteer's explanation, not fact.

Fifth, his origin is not recorded, this entry supplies no examination title, and his father's name, birth and death are unrecorded.

Sixth, the method and ingredients of the "private compounding" are unrecorded.

Seventh, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Eighth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.
