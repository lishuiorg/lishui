Zhao Longze is one of the Yuan figures in the category of loyal martyrs in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His entry records his family descent, his bearing, his resignation from a professorship, and how seven members of his household died when the capital fell. It closes with the gazetteer's own question and exclamation. This entry keeps those layers apart and cross-references the entry for Qi Tai in the same volume, the two being merely two of the figures in the one category, both dead in war, with nothing else between them.

## His descent: three generations the gazetteer records

> 趙龍澤，字萬里，宋隱逸趙林之玄孫，元鄉賢趙鑒之子。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Four points must be stated. First, "courtesy name Wanli" is his courtesy name as the entry gives it. Second, "great-grandson of Zhao Lin, recluse of the Song" names one of his forebears: Zhao Lin is recorded as a recluse of the Song. This library has no entry for Zhao Lin and has not consulted other gazetteers, so the line of descent is simply reproduced as the gazetteer gives it and no life of Zhao Lin is decided from it. Third, "son of Zhao Jian, notable of the Yuan" names his father; this library likewise has no entry for Zhao Jian. Fourth, both statements are the entry's own opening account and belong to the gazetteer, not to this library's research elsewhere; because Zhao Jian is called a notable, this library does not enter Zhao Longze among the notables, nor treat him as belonging to that category. **The related link here points only to Qi Tai, not to Zhao Jian or Zhao Lin**: this library holds no entries for the latter two and so cannot link to them.

## His bearing, and the professorship he resigned

> 為人骯髒自負，有司舉教授，一就職即謝去。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Of a lofty and self-respecting character" is the entry's remark on his conduct and bearing; the wording is the gazetteer's own, and what it rests on is not recorded. "The authorities proposed him for a professorship, and as soon as he took it up he resigned" records the nomination and the resignation. No reason for the resignation is inferred here; the gazetteer records none.

## Seven dying when the capital fell: the crucial part of the entry

> 會汝（穎）［潁］兵陷建業，龍澤不屈死之，子權亦從父死。弟雷澤長子棟婦夏氏、次子楷與其婦劉氏、弟宗澤之婦衡氏，同赴水死，獨雷澤與棟以他故得免。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This must be taken sentence by sentence. First, "when the Yu-Ying forces took Jianye" gives the occasion; neither a year nor a date is given and the entry writes only that it was "when", so nothing is converted and no year is determined, and on whose side those troops fought is not stated either. The collation brackets are the transcription's own and are reproduced as they stand, the character inside the square brackets being the one used. Second, "he died unconquered" records his death, and "unconquered" is the gazetteer's account of how he died, reproduced as its own account. Third, "his son Quan also died with his father" records the death of his son Zhao Quan. Fourth, the gazetteer then turns to his brothers' household: the wife Xia of Dong, the elder son of Leize, the wife Liu of Kai the second son, and the wife Heng of his brother Zongze "all drowned themselves and died", while Leize and Dong alone "were spared for other reasons". Two points must be recorded: the gazetteer names seven dead — Zhao Longze, his son Quan, Xia, Kai, his wife Liu and Heng — and names Leize and Dong as spared, and this entry reproduces those counts without inferring any others unmentioned; and **what the "other reasons" were is not recorded**, so nothing is supplied here.

## The gazetteer's question and exclamation: a judgement, not a fact

> 議者謂趙氏上世多賢者，何為一旦酷烈至此！嗚乎，此正天之所以不死趙氏一門者歟！
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This part is the gazetteer's own commentary and must be marked layer by layer. First, "those who discuss say" marks what follows as the opinion of others rather than the compiler's own assertion; who these discussants were and when they spoke is nowhere recorded, and the words are reproduced and marked as theirs. Second, "the Zhao house had many worthies above it" is the discussants' recollection of the forebears recorded earlier as Zhao Lin and Zhao Jian, and "why such ferocity at one moment" is their question. This entry neither answers the question nor decides what the Zhao household in fact did. Third, the exclamation and the closing words "this is precisely the way Heaven did not let the Zhao house die out" are the gazetteer's own exclaimation and speculation, marked as such and not written as fact.

## Relation to Lishui

He enters the persons site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: as to Zhao Longze the gazetteer records only his bearing, his resignation from a professorship and his death when the capital fell, none of it in Lishui; it gives no native place, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no Lishui office, so "official service" does not apply; it records no writings of his, so "writings" does not apply; it records no recommendation out of Lishui, so "recommended from Lishui" does not apply; and "sojourn" has nothing to rest on. The relation recorded is therefore "active in Lishui", meaning only that he appears in the gazetteer of Lishui: juan 6 lists him among the single Yuan figure in that category, and that is the sole basis on which he is included. It carries no implication that he acted in Lishui, and a reader must not infer from this entry that he ever came to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are nowhere recorded; the entry gives only "when... the forces took Jianye" with no year, so nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no age is inferred.

Second, neither his native place nor his residence is recorded, and no inference is drawn from the two generations of descent through Zhao Lin and Zhao Jian.

Third, the appraisal of his conduct and the discussants' remark about his forebears have nothing recorded as their basis.

Fourth, the reason for his resignation from the professorship and what the "other reasons" for the two survivors' escape were are nowhere recorded.

Fifth, whether any others of his clan perished besides the seven the gazetteer names is unrecorded, and no total for the household is inferred.

Sixth, this library has no entries for Zhao Jian or Zhao Lin, so the related link points to neither; nothing is known here about their lives, and no entry may be built for them on the strength of this one line of descent.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single biography of this gazetteer, and searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
