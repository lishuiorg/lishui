Madam Xia is one of the figures in the Song section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice gives her place, her deed of cutting out her liver to cure her mother, and the honouring of her neighbourhood as "Zhaoxiao" in the Chunxi period. This entry sets it beside "Xia Mou" in the Filial Sons biography of the same juan, because the two agree on the name given and the era, and that must be made clear.

## The gazetteer text

> 夏氏，漆橋夏家女。年十三，母病篤，剖脅割肝，和藥以進，母遂愈。淳熙間表其里曰「昭孝」。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her place and station

"Madam Xia" is the designation the gazetteer gives; only a surname is recorded, with no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Xia" as its title. "Daughter of the Xia family of Qiqiao" is the gazetteer's statement of who she was — a daughter of the Xia family at Qiqiao, which is the residence and paternal house this notice supplies. "Thirteen" is the age at which she did the deed, not her year of birth, and no birth or death years are computed from it.

## Cutting out her liver to cure her mother

"When her mother fell gravely ill, she cut open her side and took her liver, mixing it into medicine and giving it to her, and her mother recovered" is the gazetteer's account of her deed: with her mother gravely ill, she cut open her side and drew out her liver, mixed it into medicine and offered it, and her mother recovered. The phrases are the gazetteer's own and are reproduced as its account; cutting the thigh and cutting the liver are stock deeds in the Chaste and Staunch and Filial Sons biographies of this gazetteer, and this entry neither judges their medicine nor decides whether the deed truly happened. The mother's illness, her age afterwards, and whether the deed fell in the year she turned thirteen are all unrecorded.

## The Chunxi honouring, matching the "Xia Mou" notice in the Filial Sons biography

"In the Chunxi period her neighbourhood was honoured as 'Zhaoxiao'" is the gazetteer's account of the honour: in the Chunxi period her neighbourhood was given the name "Zhaoxiao". One relation must be stated: the Filial Sons biography of the same juan has a separate "Xia Mou" (this library already holds the entry `ls:person:xia-mou`), whose notice reads that he was "most filial to his mother, and in the Chunxi period his neighbourhood was honoured as 'Zhaoxiao'". Both the neighbourhood name "Zhaoxiao" and the era "Chunxi" agree, so the two are two ways of recording one act of honouring. This library sets them side by side and links them for cross-reference only; it does not decide that the "Madam Xia" of the Chaste and Staunch biography and the "Xia Mou" of the Filial Sons biography must be one person, nor treat their agreement as proof of a single event. Moreover, the "Xia Mou" notice states that his name is lost (the gazetteer writes "his name is lost"), and this notice likewise gives no name, so no identification can be made by name. Whether the two are one person or one event must await the printed text and other records.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as a daughter of the Xia family of Qiqiao, and she enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui". The reasoning must be stated: her township is the place of residence and paternal house the gazetteer gives, not a birth record, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; the gazetteer records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers, and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is her cutting out her liver to cure her mother in Lishui and her neighbourhood's being honoured for it, which is her activity in Lishui, so the relation taken is "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; "thirteen" states only the age at which she did the deed, and no birth, death or period of activity may be founded on it.

Second, neither her personal nor her courtesy name is recorded, and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Madam Xia.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: "Qiqiao" is her residence and "the Xia family" her paternal house, but to what township these belonged and where her house stood are unrecorded, and nothing is supplied.

Fourth, neither her husband nor her father is named.

Fifth, "the Chunxi period" dates only the honouring of the neighbourhood; nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no year of the deed is inferred from it.

Sixth, "Zhaoxiao" agrees with the neighbourhood name in the "Xia Mou" notice of the Filial Sons biography; this library sets the two side by side and links them, without deciding that they are one person or one event, and any connection must await the printed text.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single notice in the Chaste and Staunch biography of juan 7 and merely cross-refers the "Xia Mou" notice; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
