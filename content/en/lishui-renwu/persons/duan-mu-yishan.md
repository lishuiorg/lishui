Duanmu Yishan is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him two items — the route of presentation and the office held — and nothing else. This entry writes those two items, adds the one clause of kinship the section itself supplies, and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 端木以善由儒士，任尚書。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of confucian scholars" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces it and does not convert it into "jinshi" or any other degree. "Served as a minister" is the office as the gazetteer words it — two characters only, naming no ministry, no post and no term. This entry supplies no ministry and does not fill in his career from other volumes of this gazetteer or from other books. This block gives no years at all, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of appointment can be known.

## The kinship clause the section itself supplies

Two lines in the "Ming, Yongle period" block of the same section insert a clause of kinship between the name and the route into office:

> 端木孝文以善子。由儒士，任翰林院待詔。
> 端木孝思以善子、孝文弟。由儒士，任兵部員外兼翰林院侍書。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

The name "Yishan" appears in this section only in these two places. On that basis the section takes Duanmu Xiaowen and Duanmu Xiaosi as sons of Duanmu Yishan, and this entry therefore links to the two entries this library already has for them. Three points must be stated. First, this is the gazetteer's own statement of kinship: this entry reproduces it and marks it as such, and reasons from it to no birth or death year and to no sequence of dates between any of the three men. Second, the Hongwu and Yongle blocks stand next to each other, but the order of the blocks carries no dates, and this entry infers no sequence from "Yishan in Hongwu, the two in Yongle". Third, the lines quoted here are roster lines of the Yongle block, not text from the arts-and-literature section of juan 8.

## The migration and the burial place recorded in juan 8

The arts-and-literature section of juan 8, as recorded in this library's source card, lists a tomb epitaph for him written by Song Lian, entered under a four-character attribution, and quotes one sentence of its text on the migration of the family; the card also records the burial place the epitaph gives.

> 一遷於大梁，再遷金陵烏衣巷，三遷於溧水縣之巉山。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 8, epitaph by Song Lian

The epitaph also gives his ancestry as men of Wei and traces the line to a disciple of Confucius. Four points must be stated. First, what is quoted here is the fragment the card's summary of juan 8 records, not a roster line of the Recommendations examination and not the verbatim gazetteer text transcribed in this reading; the citation is to juan 8. Second, the four characters under which the card enters the epitaph are reproduced as they stand, and this entry does not decide which of them is the given name and which the courtesy name; the card notes that the name, the courtesy name and the migration in the epitaph agree with this section, and this entry records that as a cross-reference without deciding that the subject of the epitaph is the man of the Hongwu line. Third, the descent from a disciple of Confucius is the epitaph's claim about a remote ancestor; it is reproduced and marked as such, not written as fact. Fourth, the third migration into a hill in Lishui County and the burial at the field east of that hill are the one Lishui place-name this library connects directly to him; this entry records the migration and the burial on that basis, infers neither that he was born there nor where that place is today.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; the office it gives him lay away from Lishui and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available. The clause of kinship states his relation to Duanmu Xiaowen and Duanmu Xiaosi, not his relation to Lishui. The epitaph quoted above places his family's third migration at a hill in Lishui County and buries him there; that is a record of where the family settled and where he was buried, not a record of his birth, so "born in Lishui" is still not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of confucian scholars", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "minister" is a bare office title, and its ministry, post and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, nothing is recorded about what the section's kinship clause rests on. The Hongwu line writes the full name "Duanmu Yishan" while the Yongle lines write only "a son of Yishan"; this entry takes the two "Yishan" references to be one and the same man, but that judgement rests on the section's own wording alone and no other material allows it to be checked.

Fifth, apart from this volume, the arts-and-literature section of juan 8 of the same book carries writing connected with this man; this library holds only that section's category summary and the epitaph fragment quoted in it, not the epitaph in full, and has not checked its title or its attribution in the original. What is taken from juan 8 is used for the migration and the burial place only, and adds nothing to his career or his life.

Sixth, the epitaph in juan 8 gives his burial place as the field east of the hill in Lishui County, and where that place is today cannot be decided from this library's material; whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block, takes two lines from the Yongle block only for the kinship clause, and takes one fragment from the category summary of juan 8 for the migration and the burial place; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him. Both the section and juan 8 are online transcriptions, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
