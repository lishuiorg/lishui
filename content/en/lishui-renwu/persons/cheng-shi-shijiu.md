Madam Cheng is the last item recorded in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section is headed "Guochao" (the present dynasty) in the original, that is, the early Qing, the age in which the compiler lived; this library renders it as the Qing period and notes the gazetteer's wording, so the `birth_era` of this entry is "Qing", which takes the gazetteer's dynastic grouping and is not a record of birth. The transcription on which this entry rests breaks off immediately after the two words "Shijiuba", and its continuation is not available; there is thus no deed to read here, and the entry can record only her surname, her place and the fact of the break. That is the whole of the confirmed information on which it enters this library.

## Text of the gazetteer

> 程氏，石湫壩……
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

(The ellipsis marks the point at which the transcription breaks off, and is not part of the gazetteer's text.)

## Reading

The item preserves only the five characters "Madam Cheng, Shijiuba".

"Madam Cheng" records her surname. Her personal name, courtesy name, age and paternal kin are all lost, because the gazetteer's text breaks off.

"Shijiuba" records that she was a person of Shijiuba, the sole locality this item preserves. Shijiuba is one of the place names of Lishui recorded in this volume, namely the place recorded under "dams" in the survey of mountains and waters in juan 5 as "Shijiuba, twenty-five li to the west". This entry quotes the three characters as they stand, neither supplying its distance and form from the survey of mountains and waters nor its family history from other items.

After "Madam Cheng, Shijiuba" the gazetteer breaks off, and her husband's name and her deed — chastity, death for chastity, teaching a son, receiving honours, or the like — are all lost. This entry does not invent a husband or a deed for her on the pattern of other items in the same section (such as "Madam So-and-so, wife of So-and-so ..."), nor infer from the category of "Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women" that she must have been a chaste widow or a martyred woman: if the gazetteer does not record it, this entry does not write it.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "Active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. The gazetteer records only the five characters "Madam Cheng, Shijiuba", and the only thing that can be grounded is her locality: Shijiuba belonged to Lishui, and on that her residence has to do with Lishui, so she enters this library as "active in Lishui". Yet the gazetteer never records her birthplace or native place, giving her only as "Madam Cheng" and her place as "Shijiuba", so "native of Lishui" is not filled; and it has no office, no recommendation and no writings on which "official service", "recommended from Lishui" or "writings" could rest; what it records is a residence and not a sojourn, so "sojourn" does not apply either. It must be stated that "active in Lishui" is filled here only to tie her to the library by her locality, and does not mean that the gazetteer records any deed of hers in Lishui — the text breaks off at this point and her story is lost.

## Points of doubt

First, the gazetteer's text breaks off, and her husband's name and her deed are entirely lost. The transcription on which this entry rests breaks off at "Madam Cheng, Shijiuba", and its continuation is not available; this entry knows only that her surname was Cheng and that she was a person of Shijiuba, and knows neither her husband nor her story. This is the most important limit of the entry, and no husband or deed may be supplied from the category or from the pattern of other items.

Second, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The item preserves only five characters, with no age, no cyclical year and no reign year, and this entry infers no birth or death year.

Third, her birthplace and native place are not expressly recorded. The item gives only "Shijiuba", a locality; Shijiuba is a place name of this county, but the gazetteer does not state whether she was born and raised there, and this entry does not conclude from "Shijiuba" that she was a native of Lishui.

Fourth, her paternal kin and her offspring may not be fully recorded. With the text broken off, her paternal kin, her husband's family and her offspring are all lost, and this entry supplies none of them.

Fifth, the honours may not be complete. Because the text breaks off, whether she received honours cannot be known, and this entry makes no inference.

Sixth, this entry rests on one item of this gazetteer, and on a transcription that breaks off at "Shijiuba"; it has not been checked against a punctuated edition or the printed book, so all quotations are marked "from the online transcription". Anyone wishing to complete it must first obtain the full text of the "Madam Cheng" item in the "Guochao" section of the Biographies of Chaste and Martyred Women in juan 7 from the printed book; this entry must not be extended from other items or other gazetteers.
