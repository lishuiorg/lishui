Yan Que is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Yongle period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. That section is a roster rather than a biography: each line gives a name, sometimes a courtesy name or one clause of kinship, the route into office, and the office held, and gives no account of the man. This entry holds only "presented as a confucian scholar" and "served as an instructor", and is among the shortest in the section; everything the gazetteer does not record is placed under Points of doubt rather than supplied.

## Route of presentation and office held

> 嚴恪由儒士，任助教。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of confucian scholars" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces the three characters and does not convert them into "jinshi" or any other degree. "Served as an instructor" is the office as the gazetteer words it, written in two characters with no institution attached: where he instructed, in what office or school, is nowhere recorded, and this entry supplies nothing. This block gives no years, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of taking up office can be known.

## What the gazetteer records, and the limit of this entry

The section gives no life: it records no native place, no courtesy name, who presented him or by what procedure, whom he assisted in teaching, how he was promoted, what his final office was, or anything after his death. This entry reasons from the words "confucian scholars" and "instructor" to no institutional detail, since the gazetteer records none.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his post is not placed in Lishui and the gazetteer records no Lishui post for him, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Yongle period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Yongle era nor that he was active in any particular Yongle year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of confucian scholars", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; the office is a bare title with no institution named, and its duties and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Fifth, this library also holds an entry for a filial son of the Song recorded in juan 7 of the same gazetteer who shares the surname; the two are different men in different eras and different sections, no kinship between them is recorded, and the two entries must not be merged.

Sixth, this entry rests on one roster line; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
