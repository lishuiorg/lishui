Han He is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is short but unusual among this library's Ming Lishui figures in naming two affairs — promoting a new school building, and being charged with settling corvée and taxes — and in reporting an opinion about him. This entry records it as it stands, and states his post as the gazetteer states it: he held a school office, not a county magistracy.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 韩和，字介之，江西铅山人。天顺间由举人任教谕
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Jiezhi, a native of Qianshan in Jiangxi, reached by the *juren* degree, and appointed instructor at the county school "during the Tianshun era". The post must be stated as the gazetteer states it: the biography says expressly *jiaoyu*, an educational office, not county magistrate; the "distinguished officials" of this volume are not limited to magistrates, and this entry never writes him as magistrate of Lishui. The gazetteer gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts the era span nor infers a year from the dynasty. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui.

## What the gazetteer records

> 尝倡新学宫，置祭器甚备而无少污也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"He once promoted a new school building" is one clue this volume yields for the building of the Lishui county school: the gazetteer says only that he promoted a new school, and records no year, site, layout or later history, so this entry does not decide which building campaign of the Lishui school it corresponds to. "He provided sacrificial implements, very complete and without the least taint" records the implements he provided; their names, numbers and survival are nowhere recorded.

> 上官委以定徭赋，众皆服，议者谓韩先生又优于吏如此。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"His superiors charged him with settling corvée and taxes" records one commission; what corvée and what taxes, what registers were made and how it was carried out are nowhere recorded. "Everyone submitted" and "commentators said Master Han was in this way better even than the clerks" are opinion and judgement reported by the gazetteer — the talk of "commentators" and the claim that he surpassed the clerks are contemporary assessments, quoted and marked as such, not stated as fact.

> 无何，竟致仕去，人益啧啧云。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Before long, he in the end retired and went" records the close of his career; the year and the reason are not recorded. "People murmured all the more" is the gazetteer's report of talk about it; whether the murmuring was admiration, regret or surprise at how quickly he went is left unclear by the text, and this entry does not decide.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Qianshan in Jiangxi" — and enters this library through official service. Three things tie him to the county: his post as school instructor; his promoting a new school building and providing sacrificial implements; and his being charged with settling corvée and taxes. The first two are this volume's clue for the Lishui school and its implements; the third is its clue for the settling of Lishui corvée and taxes — and it is a school officer who was given an administrative commission, which is why the commentators said he surpassed the clerks, itself a reported judgement. The tags "Education and culture" and "Taxation and corvée" answer to the first two and to the third.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Qianshan in Jiangxi.

Second, precision of the term: the gazetteer says only "during the Tianshun era", gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts it nor infers a year from the dynasty.

Third, his post: the gazetteer says expressly *jiaoyu*, not county magistrate, and this entry never writes him as magistrate of Lishui; which school in Lishui he served is not stated in the biography, which never uses the word "Lishui", and follows only from the volume.

Fourth, the year, site, layout and later history of the new school he promoted, and the names, numbers and survival of the sacrificial implements, are nowhere recorded.

Fifth, what the settling of corvée and taxes consisted of, what registers it produced and how it was applied is nowhere recorded.

Sixth, "everyone submitted", "commentators said Master Han was better even than the clerks" and "people murmured all the more" are reported opinion and judgement; what they rested on is not recorded, and the sense of the murmuring is unclear.

Seventh, the year and reason of his retirement, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
