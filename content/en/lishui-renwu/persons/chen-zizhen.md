Chen Zizhen is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is mostly judgement: a few clauses on his style of government, a county shrine after his term, a passage recounting his return journey through the county, and his final rank with shrines wherever he served. It records no concrete act and no dates for his Lishui term.

## Origin and term

> 陈子贞，字怀云，江西南昌人，万历十六年进士。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Huaiyun, a native of Nanchang in Jiangxi, and passed the *jinshi* examination in the sixteenth year of the Wanli era. The gazetteer gives the year of his degree but not the year of his Lishui post; this entry does not infer his arrival or departure from the degree year and converts nothing. For his Lishui office the gazetteer writes only that he "administered Lishui", and this entry follows that wording without supplying a title.

## What the record says about his acts

**His style of government in Lishui.**

> 令溧水以实心为政，爱民如子，待士若师。劝善简讼，讲学课文。不矜清俭，不尚虚文。在任相安，有恩无怨，邑祠祀之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The gazetteer describes his conduct in balanced clauses: government with a solid heart, love of the people as of children, treatment of scholars as of teachers, encouragement of good conduct and simplification of litigation, lectures and set lessons, no display of austerity and no empty formalities. "During his term all were at peace; there was favour and no grievance" is the gazetteer's judgement, and "the county built a shrine to sacrifice to him" is its record of posthumous worship. The biography names no act, no building, no fiscal item.

**After leaving office, passing through Lishui.**

> 行取御史去，旋督学江南，巡试诸郡，道出濑上，登徽恩阁，赋诗题扁，如归故乡，不胜有感。犹询民苦士风，一一不忘，有长者气度，称为召父杜母。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He left Lishui on promotion to the Censorate, soon superintended studies in Jiangnan and toured the prefectures to examine candidates; passing through Lai, he climbed the Hui'en pavilion, composed a poem and inscribed its board, and the gazetteer says he felt as if returning to his home place and could not contain his emotion. It adds that he still asked after the people's hardships and the scholars' manners, forgetting none of it. "He had the air of a man of virtue, and was called a father and a mother of the people" is the gazetteer's reported praise — the paired names are those of two Han officials famous for caring for the people — a phrase of commendation, not a record. What "Lai" refers to, where that pavilion stood and whether it survives are all unrecorded; this entry draws no inference from the resemblance between that place-name and the county's own name.

**Final rank and shrines.**

> 官至福建巡抚，所在有祠，永思无。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He rose to provincial governor of Fujian, and every place where he served had a shrine. The last three characters are transcribed exactly as the online transcription has them; their punctuation and sense must be checked against the punctuated edition, and this entry neither alters, adds nor deletes a character.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his government there, the county's shrine, and the passage in which — after leaving — he passed through, climbed a pavilion, wrote a poem and asked again after the people's hardships. The biography deliberately places this after his departure, which is unusual among the biographies of this volume.

This entry is a cross-reference with this library's entry on Xu Bida: another biography of this volume recalls an earlier magistrate Chen Zizhen's handling of a fiscal matter (the wording is quoted in that entry), and the two biographies stand in the same juan. This entry uses only its own biography and supplies no fiscal act for Chen Zizhen from the other one.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded.

Second, the year of his Lishui post is unrecorded; only his degree year is given, and it cannot be used to fix his arrival or departure. Nothing is converted.

Third, his Lishui office is given only as "administered Lishui"; the precise title and the years of his promotions are unrecorded.

Fourth, his move to the Censorate, his Jiangnan school superintendence and his touring examinations are all undated.

Fifth, what "Lai" means and where that pavilion stood, when it was built and whether it survives are unrecorded.

Sixth, "as if returning to his home place", "the air of a man of virtue" and "called a father and a mother of the people" are the gazetteer's narration and reported praise, not verifiable records.

Seventh, the closing three characters are kept exactly as transcribed; their punctuation and meaning await the punctuated edition, and this entry offers no interpretation.

Eighth, the location, date and later history of the county shrine are unrecorded.

Ninth, another biography of this volume recalls a fiscal measure of his term; that is a different matter from this biography, and nothing is supplied from it.

Tenth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
