Shi Migong is an entry in the Song part of the notable-officials biography in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His year of appointment is given precisely, and the biography covers both his business in Lishui and his later career.

## Origin and term

> 史弥巩，字南叔，鄞县人。嘉定进士，丞相浩之从子，丞相弥远之从弟。绍定元年任，为人好学强记，性刚介而政精明，人服之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was courtesy-named Nanshu, a native of Yin county, and entered office as a jinshi of the Jiading era. The gazetteer records his kinship with two chancellors: "a nephew of the chancellor Hao, a younger cousin of the chancellor Miyuan" — it supplies no surname for either man, and "nephew" and "younger cousin" are the kinship terms the gazetteer itself uses. His appointment is dated precisely, "the first year of the Shaoding era", one of the entries in this volume that gives a fixed year; the source dates by reign period, and this entry reproduces that dating without adding a year of the common era. His Lishui post is given only as "appointed", with no formal title stated, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county". "Studious with a good memory", "rigid in character and clear-sighted in administration", and "people were convinced by him" are the gazetteer's assessments, quoted as such.

## What the gazetteer records

> 首严庠序，累官提点江东刑狱。岁大旱，活饥民百余万，后知婺州，卒年八十。真德秀尝称其『皭然不污』。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He "put the school first and was strict about it" — the county school was what he took up first and enforced, but the gazetteer records no measure and no result. He then "accumulated offices up to judicial intendant of Jiangdong"; the gazetteer does not say whether that post came before or after Lishui, nor whether it was held concurrently. "In a year of great drought he kept over a million starving people alive" — the gazetteer names neither the region of the drought nor the office under which he acted. "Later he administered Wuzhou, and died at the age of eighty" gives his age at death but no year of death. "Pure and unstained" is a remark by Zhen Dexiu quoted by the gazetteer, whose own source for it is not given.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in Lishui through his appointment in a fixed year and through what he put first in the county, and this entry records him through official service. His main connection with Lishui is what this volume records of him there: the county school as his first concern, and an administration so clear-sighted that "people were convinced by him". The judicial intendancy, the relief of the drought and the prefecture of Wuzhou lie outside Lishui and are reported here only as the sequence of his career.

## Points of doubt

First, no year of birth and no year of death are recorded; the gazetteer gives only that he "died at the age of eighty".

Second, the gazetteer never states the formal title of his Lishui post; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Third, the year and details of the jinshi degree of the Jiading era are not recorded.

Fourth, the sequence between the judicial intendancy of Jiangdong and his Lishui post, the region of the great drought, and the office under which the relief was carried out are all left unstated.

Fifth, the measures and results behind "put the school first and was strict about it" are not recorded.

Sixth, the source of Zhen Dexiu's remark "pure and unstained" is not recorded.

Seventh, this volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
