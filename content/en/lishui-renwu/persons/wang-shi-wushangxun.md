Madam Wang is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer. She is one of the few notices in the Ming section to carry an exact commendation year. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 王氏，舉人武尚訓繼妻。年十七夫故，氏誓死，屢自經，以姑陳氏救之。守節四十四年，百苦備嘗，撫子成名。萬曆三十六年奉旨旌表貞節。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "second wife of the provincial graduate Wu Shangxun" names husband and wife. Wu Shangxun was a provincial graduate, and "second wife" shows she was not the first. His first wife and children are unrecorded.

Second, "widowed at seventeen" gives her age at Wu Shangxun's death.

Third, "she swore to die, repeatedly hanged herself, and was saved by her mother-in-law Chen-shi" records her first days of mourning. The mother-in-law's surname was Chen; she is recorded as the one who saved her.

Fourth, "she remained a widow for forty-four years, enduring every hardship, and raised her son to distinction" is the gazetteer's summary. It does not name the son, nor say whether he was her own child or the first wife's.

Fifth, "in the thirty-sixth year of Wanli she was honoured by imperial order for chastity" gives the year and the title of the honour. The reign year is kept as given and not converted.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Wu Shangxun, whose household was her place of residence; her widowhood, her raising of the son, and the imperial honour all attach to the Wu family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "seventeen" dates her husband's death, not her birth.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the career and death year of Wu Shangxun are unrecorded, and the son she raised is unnamed and of unstated parentage; what "distinction" means is not explained.

Fourth, the mother-in-law Chen-shi is otherwise unrecorded, and this library cannot fill in her life.

Fifth, the only honour recorded is the imperial commendation for chastity in the thirty-sixth year of Wanli; whether there were other honours is not stated, and no tablet inscription is given.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
