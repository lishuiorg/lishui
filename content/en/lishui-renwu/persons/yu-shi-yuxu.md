Madam Yu is one of the virtuous maidens of Lishui recorded in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, and the longest notice in the Ming section. It records her descent, her vow to remain unwed, her gift of land for a bridge and a nunnery, and a legend about her after death. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 俞氏，宋狀元俞栗五世孫女也。居贊賢鄉，父俞旭，母陳氏，生子七人，女行三。幼字庠生周輅，將適，輅夭，氏立志不貳，父母亦不能強，撥田八畝與氏守貞。勤苦紡織，積金數十，舍造神山庵橋。將終，以所撥田亦舍在庵以延香火。享年八十餘卒，葬父母側。亡不數日，各村祭祀，有神附人言曰，「俞氏為閃電娘娘，爾各村宜俱祀焉。」女故成化丁亥十月，有墓碑。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "fifth-generation descendant of the Song top laureate Yu Li" gives her family background. Yu Li has no entry in this library, so this entry neither fills in his genealogy nor assigns her to a particular branch.

Second, "lived in Zanxian township" gives her residence; the text does not say which ward or village.

Third, "her father was Yu Xu and her mother Chen-shi; she had seven brothers and was third among the daughters" gives her parents and rank among the siblings. The text reads "bore seven sons"; this entry keeps the wording and does not alter it. Whether all seven were male, and their names, are unrecorded.

Fourth, "betrothed as a child to the licentiate Zhou Lu; as the marriage approached, Lu died" gives her betrothal. "Betrothed" means an engagement made in childhood.

Fifth, "she resolved not to change, her parents could not force her, and they gave her eight mu of land so that she might keep her vow" records her resolve to remain unwed.

Sixth, "she toiled at weaving, saved several tens of cash, and gave it to build the Shenshan Nunnery bridge" records her deed. "Several tens" is the gazetteer's approximate figure.

Seventh, "as she neared the end, she also gave the land to the nunnery to sustain its incense" records a further gift at death.

Eighth, "she lived past eighty and died, and was buried beside her parents."

Ninth, "she died in the tenth month of the dinghai year of Chenghua, and there is a gravestone." The reign year is kept as given and not converted. "Nü" is the gazetteer's term for an unmarried woman.

Tenth, "within days of her death the villages made offerings, and a spirit spoke through a man, saying: 'Yu-shi is Auntie Lightning; each of your villages should worship her.'" **This is a supernatural legend recorded by the gazetteer**: the spirit speech, the possession and the title "Auntie Lightning" are all reported as hearsay. This library marks them **as legend recorded in the text, not as fact**, and draws no conclusion from them.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records that she lived in Zanxian township and was a descendant of the Song top laureate Yu Li; her vow, her gifts for the bridge and the nunnery, and her burial beside her parents all lie in the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct and benefactions there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth year is given; the death is dated to the tenth month of the dinghai year of Chenghua, which this entry keeps without conversion and from which it does not derive a birth year.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the text gives only her residence in Zanxian township, and residence is not birthplace.

Third, of her paternal family the text gives only her father Yu Xu, mother Chen-shi, seven brothers and her rank among the daughters; the grandparents and the brothers' names are unrecorded, and being unmarried she had no descendants.

Fourth, the licentiate Zhou Lu, to whom she was betrothed, is not otherwise described, and this library cannot fill in his life, death or dates.

Fifth, "several tens of cash" is approximate, and the nunnery and bridge of Shenshan cannot be located in any other material held here.

Sixth, the final notice of the spirit speaking through a man is a supernatural legend recorded by the gazetteer and is marked as hearsay; the title "Auntie Lightning" and the village worship are not treated as fact.

Seventh, the text records no court commendation for her; it notes only the bridge, the nunnery gift and the gravestone, and gives no number or date of any honour.

Eighth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
