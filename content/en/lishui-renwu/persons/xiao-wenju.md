Xiao Wenju is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him three items — the route of presentation, one clause of kinship, and the office held — and nothing else. This entry writes only those three items and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 蕭文舉叔裕孫。由太學，任翰林院孔目。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"By way of the Imperial Academy" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces it and does not convert it into "jinshi" or any other degree, and offers no account of how this route worked or what it involved — the section says nothing of it. "Served as a keeper of records in the Hanlin Academy" is the office as the gazetteer words it, and it lay in the Hanlin Academy, not in Lishui; what the post involved, and when he took it up and left it, is nowhere recorded. This block gives no years at all, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of appointment can be known.

## The kinship clause the section itself supplies

Between the name and the route into office the section inserts the clause "a grandson of Shuyu"; this is the gazetteer's own statement of kinship. On this basis the section takes Xiao Wenju as a grandson of Xiao Shuyu, whose name appears elsewhere in the same block, in the line reading "Xiao Shuyu, by way of persons of talent, served as recorder of Kuizhou prefecture in Sichuan"; this library has a separate entry for him, and this entry links to it. Three points must be stated. First, "a grandson of Shuyu" is the gazetteer's claim; this entry reproduces it and marks it as such, and reasons from it to no birth or death year and to no sequence of dates between the two men. Second, both lines stand in the same Hongwu block, which carries no dates, and no sequence is inferred from the order in which they appear. Third, although this library now has an entry for Xiao Shuyu, there is no material outside this section against which the lineage can be checked, and only the section's own claim is recorded here.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his office lay in the Hanlin Academy and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available. The clause "a grandson of Shuyu" states his kinship with Xiao Shuyu, not his relation to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of the Imperial Academy", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "keeper of records in the Hanlin Academy" is an office title whose duties and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, "a grandson of Shuyu" is the section's own claim and the gazetteer records nothing of what it rests on. This line writes only "Shuyu" while the other line writes the full name, and this entry takes the two references to be one and the same man, but that judgement rests on the section's own wording alone and no other material allows it to be checked.

Fifth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Sixth, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block and takes one further line of the same block only for the kinship clause; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
