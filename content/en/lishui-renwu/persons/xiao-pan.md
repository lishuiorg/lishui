Xiao Pan is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography tells a journey in search of his father and its lifelong consequence, then appends a county man's poem and a claim that someone wrote his biography. This entry separates what the gazetteer records from what it merely reports, and places everything unrecorded under Points of doubt.

## The gazetteer text

> 蕭泮，年十九，父賓外賈久不歸，號泣辭母，涉江淮，歷汴冀，蹤跡不得，數年而後返，致疾終其身。事母陳盡孝，邑人陳鳳舜有「劉殷痛哭天地寒，孝感芹生德相似」之詩，閣部吳甡為之傳。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Age and family

The gazetteer gives one age only, "nineteen", and it attaches to the moment he set out in search of his father, not to his birth; this entry infers no birth year from it and converts nothing. No township, no native place and no origin are recorded. His father is named only as "father"; his mother is given a surname, Chen, and nothing else.

## The search for his father

The gazetteer records that his father was "a guest abroad in trade" and did not return for a long time — the gazetteer's own wording, whose exact sense this library does not settle, reproduced unchanged — that Xiao Pan wailed, took leave of his mother and set out, that he crossed the Jiang and Huai regions and passed through Bian and Ji, that he found no trace of him, that he returned only after several years, and that he thereby contracted an illness which lasted his whole life.

What the text gives and what it withholds must be set out. It gives: the age at setting out, the leave-taking, the broad range of the journey, the failure to find him, a return after several years, and the lifelong illness. It withholds: why the father had gone and where, the actual route and places reached, the course of the search, how many years "several" is, the year of return, and what the illness was. "Crossing the Jiang and Huai, passing through Bian and Ji" is a range as the gazetteer states it; this entry uses it neither to locate the father nor to establish where the traveller actually was.

## What the gazetteer reports: a county poem and a biography by Wu Shen

Two items are appended at the end and their nature must be stated. First, a man of the county named Chen Fengshun wrote a poem comparing his weeping to that of a certain Liu Yin and praising his filial feeling; this is a poem the gazetteer reports from a fellow county man — reported text and praise, not verifiable record. The name inside the quoted line is a name in the gazetteer's quotation; this library has no material on who it refers to, and this entry supplies no explanation of any allusion. Second, the gazetteer states that Wu Shen wrote his biography, calling him by the term "of the cabinet". What office that term denotes is beyond this library's material; what kind of text the biography was, where it was collected and whether it survives are unrecorded, and this entry supplies nothing. The source card of this library separately notes that juan 8 of this gazetteer preserves a eulogy by Wu Shen for a widow of the Zhang family of Lishui; whether that is the same text or concerns the same person cannot be determined from this library's material, and this entry does not merge the two. This library has no entry for Wu Shen or for Chen Fengshun.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Xiao Pan no township and no native place; it ties him to this county only through being named in the filial sons section and through the one place where a fellow county man's poem is quoted. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is that he was recorded here and praised by men of this county, not that he was born here, so it does not record him as "born locally". The search itself crossed regions away from Lishui, and this entry says so.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; "nineteen" is his age when he set out and cannot yield a birth year, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, township, native place and origin are all unrecorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Third, his father is not named; the sense of the gazetteer's phrase for his being away trading is not settled here, only reproduced.

Fourth, the route, the places actually reached, the course of the search, how many years "several" covers, and the year of return are all unrecorded; "Jiang and Huai" and "Bian and Ji" are ranges only, and this entry does not use them to locate the father.

Fifth, what illness "an illness that lasted his whole life" was, and when it began, are unrecorded.

Sixth, the birth, death and later life of his mother of the Chen lineage are unrecorded; the biography names no other relative.

Seventh, the county man's poem and the statement that Wu Shen wrote his biography are reported text and reported claim, not fact; whether Wu Shen's biography survives is unrecorded, and this library cannot determine its relation to the eulogy by Wu Shen preserved in juan 8.

Eighth, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Ninth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
