Madam Jin is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Jin, her husband Li Mo, and three matters: keeping chastity and raising an orphan, her death at seventy-two, and her ennoblement through her son. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 靳氏，李模妻。年二十夫亡，守節撫孤，壽七十二歲終。子茂春任燕山左衛經歷，考滿封母太孺人。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is short, in two layers.

"Madam Jin, wife of Li Mo" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"Widowed at twenty, she kept her chastity and raised an orphan, and died at seventy-two" gives the start, the substance, and the end of her widowhood: her husband died when she was twenty; she kept her chastity and raised the orphan; and she died at seventy-two. "Twenty" and "seventy-two" are the gazetteer's ages, and keeping chastity and raising the orphan are its record of her conduct; her son's name, the beginning and end of her widowhood, and her husband's years are unrecorded.

"Her son Maochun served as registrar of the Yanshan Left Guard, and on completion of his term she was ennobled as a titled lady" gives her son's office and her honour through him. The office is as the gazetteer records; the ennoblement came through her son's completed term of service, not through any rank of her own; the year and the text of the patent are unrecorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Li Mo, widowed young, keeping chastity and raising her orphan to seventy-two, her son rising to registrar of the Yanshan Left Guard and her being ennobled through him, her home and her husband's family being the Lishui area. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Jin", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". The record concerns her husband's family and her son's office; she herself held no office, no recommendation, and no writing, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at twenty and died at seventy-two; it gives no year of birth and no exact year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Jin"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her husband or son.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Li Mo and her son Maochun; her husband's courtesy name and status are unrecorded, and whether she had other children is unrecorded.

Fourth, the record says only that "on completion of his term she was ennobled as a titled lady"; the year and patent text are unrecorded, and "the Yanshan Left Guard" is the post the gazetteer records for her son, its history not supplied here.

Fifth, the gazetteer records nothing that would connect her to other entries for cross-reference, and this entry draws no such links.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
