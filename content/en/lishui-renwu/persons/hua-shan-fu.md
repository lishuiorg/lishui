The Huashan Woman is one of the figures in the Yuan section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer states that her surname is lost, calls her only "the Huashan Woman", and records her capture, her poem and her death by drowning, together with the four lines of her poem. This entry reproduces the gazetteer, supplies no name and decides no present location.

## The gazetteer text

> 花山婦，失其姓氏。至元丙子，值亂兵被執，不得死，至崇賢鄉碑亭橋齧指瀝血題詩於橋，赴水死。其詩曰：「君王有難妾當災，棄子離夫被擄來。遙望花山何處是，存亡兩地亦哀哉。」
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her name, place and station

"The Huashan Woman" is the gazetteer's designation: a woman of Huashan; and the gazetteer states "her surname is lost" — the surname was already lost and this is the gazetteer's own note of the gap, not this library's omission. This entry keeps the gazetteer's own form, "Huashan Woman", as its title, with the slug hua-shan-fu. The gazetteer records neither her husband's nor her son's name: although her poem has the phrase "abandoning son and leaving husband", that records only the voice of the moment of her capture and not her family. "Huashan" is the place the gazetteer names for her; this entry reproduces the name without deciding where it is today and without supplying its township.

## Capture, poem and drowning

"In the bingzi year of the Zhiyuan era she met rebel troops and was taken, and could not die" is the gazetteer's account of how it began: in the bingzi year of the Zhiyuan era she met rebel troops and was captured, and could not die (that is, taken alive and unable to take her own life). The era and the sexagenary year are the gazetteer's own and are reproduced without conversion into a Common-Era year. "At Beiting Bridge in Chongxian Township she bit her finger, wrote a poem in blood on the bridge, and drowned herself" is the gazetteer's account of how it ended: at Beiting Bridge in Chongxian Township she bit her finger, wrote in blood on the bridge, and threw herself into the water and died. "Chongxian Township" and "Beiting Bridge" are the place names the gazetteer gives, reproduced without deciding their present position.

## Her poem

The gazetteer gives her bridge poem in full, four lines, and this entry reproduces it word for word. The poem is what the gazetteer records as written by her on the bridge and belongs to the gazetteer; this entry neither fills out its background from the poem's phrases nor decides on whose side the troops fought or of whom the poem's "sovereign" speaks. The line "gazing far, where is Huashan" echoes the gazetteer's "Huashan", and this entry records only that echo without deciding the correspondence between Huashan and any present place.

## Relation to Lishui

Of the Huashan Woman the gazetteer records her capture, her poem and her drowning. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer records no birth place or native place, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is her death keeping her chastity, and her place of death, Beiting Bridge in Chongxian Township, lies within Lishui, while the biography belongs to this county's gazetteer, so this library takes the relation "active in Lishui". The limits must be stated: the place "Huashan" is the place the gazetteer names for her, and this entry does not decide where it is today.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; "the bingzi year of the Zhiyuan era" dates only the event, and nothing is converted into a Common-Era year or used to infer her age.

Second, her surname the gazetteer states is lost and her name is lost; this library supplies and infers no name, and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Huashan Woman.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer calls her only "the Huashan Woman", a name from her place of residence, but to what township Huashan belonged and where it lies are unrecorded; her place of death, Beiting Bridge in Chongxian Township, is in Chongxian Township, and this entry does not decide their relative positions.

Fourth, neither her husband nor her son is named; though her poem speaks of "abandoning son and leaving husband", that records only her words at the moment of capture, and nothing is supplied about her family.

Fifth, the four lines of the poem are reproduced here without collation against other editions and without any judgement as to the sovereign and the troops the poem refers to.

Sixth, whether "Huashan" is the same as any place of that name today, this library has no material to decide.
