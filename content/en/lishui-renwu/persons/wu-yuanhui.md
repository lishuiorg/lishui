Wu Yuanhui is one of the Ming figures in the "Loyalty and Integrity" biography of juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His entry belongs to the appended biographies of that volume, not to the main sequence. It is a single paragraph recording his family, his appointment, his two temporary postings, his encounter with the Japanese and his death in battle, closing with "and no aid arrived, and he was killed in the battle, at the age of thirty-eight", which is the closing phrase of the whole category. This entry writes only what the gazetteer records and supplies no life for him.

**Note on the name of this entry.** The gazetteer writes only "Wu, courtesy name Yuanhui". A bare surname "Wu" cannot be identified within this library, so the entry is established under his courtesy name as Wu Yuanhui; the gazetteer's own characters stand unaltered, and this library neither changes his surname nor lets a courtesy name take the place of one.

## Family and early years

> 武，字元晦，蒲村人。父滋，里中號為長者。幼孤，棄舉子業，長有丈夫氣，嘉靖間由台史授台州府知事。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Five points must be stated. First, "Wu, courtesy name Yuanhui, of Putou" gives his surname, his courtesy name and his locality; "Putou" is the place the entry names, and this library records the name on the authority of the gazetteer without deciding where it is today. Second, "his father Zi, called a worthy in the neighbourhood" records that his father Wu Zi was spoken of approvingly by his neighbours; the phrase is the gazetteer's own account of his neighbours' estimate, reproduced as such, and his father's name and life amount to these characters alone. Third, "orphaned young, he gave up the examination track" records that he lost his father young and abandoned the path of the examinations; the phrase stands joined with what follows and is the gazetteer's account of his purpose, reproduced without deciding when his studies were broken off. Fourth, "he grew into a man's resolution" is the gazetteer's appraisal of his bearing, and what it rests on is not recorded. Fifth, "in the Jiajing era he was appointed commissioner of Taizhou by way of the censorate" records how he received that office; "by way of the censorate" is the gazetteer's wording, reproduced without deciding what the office was or in what year he received it.

## The circuit inspector entrusts him, and his two temporary postings

> 時監司林應箕廉其能，俾攝仙居，再攝黃岩，皆稱治辦，兩邑士民交頌之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"The circuit inspector Lin Yingzhi, appreciating his ability" records the inspector's esteem for his capability, the verb used being the gazetteer's own and read here as valuing; "had him administer Xianju, then Huangyan" records two temporary postings as magistrate, "to administer" being the gazetteer's word and no decision made here whether they were acting or delegated appointments; "both were said to be well handled" is the gazetteer's and the two populations' word of praise; and "the people of both places praised him together" records their acclaim. These sentences record his work in the two counties; the gazetteer lists no particular deed, and this entry supplies none either.

## The Japanese at the boundary, and the fall of the city: the sequence the gazetteer gives

> 會倭犯黃岩界，率兵迎敵，倭為之卻。初所率兵猝募民間子弟，雅勿習戰，又以倭即引去，備稍弛。越五日，倭突至，城遂陷。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This is the part of the entry in which the gazetteer gives a sequence of causes, and it must be taken sentence by sentence. First, "when Japanese forces crossed into Huangyan's bounds he led troops out against them, and the Japanese withdrew for the time"; no era is given, and no year is inferred. Second, "the troops he first led were hastily gathered from among the villagers and were quite unused to fighting" records that his men were villagers snatched up at short notice; "hastily gathered" is the gazetteer's wording, reproduced. Third, "and because the Japanese then withdrew, the defences slackened somewhat" records the easing of the guard once they withdrew, and "slackened" is the gazetteer's own account and is precisely the ground of what follows; this entry reproduces it and points out that the sequence of causes is the gazetteer's own statement. Fourth, "after five days the Japanese came suddenly and the city fell" records the sudden arrival and the fall; "five days" is reproduced without deciding whether it means five days after the Japanese withdrew or anything else.

## Reporting to the inspector, who blames him not at all

> 馳報監司，不之譴，益加顧勞，曰：「吾頃聞於諸大夫，如非棄城者，吾將再試之。」感激自誓曰：「不肖，無能一士卒。心誠未敢以必勝許，所可許者此身也。」遂毅然往。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Four points must be stated. First, "he rode to report to the inspector, who blamed him not at all but increased his regard and care" records that the inspector censured him in no way but rather showed him more consideration; both phrases are the gazetteer's words, reproduced. Second, what the inspector said is given as the inspector's own words and is reproduced in his voice without alteration; it means that he had heard from the other officers that, had it not been for the one who abandoned the city, he would try the man again, and this entry does not decide what the officers had said. Third, "he was moved and swore an oath" records his oath, and the words of it are his own and are reproduced unaltered and without repunctuation; "what I can promise is this body of mine" is the gazetteer's record of what he claimed of himself, reproduced without deciding how his end turned out. Fourth, "and he went resolutely out" records how he set out decisively.

## Aoling and Diaoyuling: fighting to the death, and the volume's closing phrase

> 御倭於拗嶺，以奇兵掩殺之。至釣魚嶺，倭亦以奇兵還擊之，眾潰。獨殿，與寇相持者越日，手刃若干級，援勿至，遂陣沒，年三十八。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This is the closing section of the entry and the closing words of the whole category, and it must be taken sentence by sentence. First, "he met the Japanese at Aoling and killed them by surprise" records that he engaged them at Aoling and cut them down by an unexpected move; "Aoling" is a place name, recorded here without deciding where it is today, and the wording of the killing is the gazetteer's own. Second, "at Diaoyuling the Japanese too returned a surprise attack, the ranks broke" records that at Diaoyuling the Japanese answered with their own sudden stroke and the troops broke; "the ranks broke" is the gazetteer's account of the collapse, reproduced, and it is from that collapse that what follows proceeds. Third, "he alone held the rear, and contended with the enemy for more than a day" records that he alone covered the rear and fought the enemy across more than a day; "held the rear" is taken here in its plain sense of covering the retreat, and the size of his command is not inferred. Fourth, "cutting down a number of them with his own hand" records that he killed a certain number of the enemy with his own hand, "a certain number" being the gazetteer's wording and no figure inferred, and the counting of the enemy reproduced as it stands. Fifth, "no aid arrived, and he was killed in the battle, at the age of thirty-eight" is both the close of this entry and the last words of the category: no reinforcements reached him, he died where he fought, at thirty-eight. This entry reproduces it unaltered and adds nothing about his grave, his burial place or his descendants.

## Relation to Lishui

He enters the persons site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: what the gazetteer records of Wu Yuanhui is his family, his appointment as commissioner of Taizhou, his two temporary postings at Xianju and Huangyan, and his death in the fighting against the Japanese, none of it in Lishui; although "Putou" is recorded as his locality, which county it lay in and how far from Lishui is nowhere given, so "born in Lishui" does not follow; his offices, the Taizhou commission and the two temporary posts, were not Lishui posts, so "official service" does not apply; no writings of his are recorded, so "writings" does not apply; no recommendation out of Lishui is recorded, so "recommended from Lishui" does not apply; and "sojourn" has nothing to rest on. The relation recorded is therefore "active in Lishui", meaning only that he appears in the gazetteer of Lishui: juan 6 lists him among the Ming figures of its appended biographies, and that is the sole basis on which he is included. It carries no implication that he acted in Lishui or held office there, and a reader must not infer from this entry that he ever came to Lishui or served there. This judgement runs parallel to that in the entry for Wei Ze in the same volume — Wei Ze's native place lay inside Lishui while his office lay elsewhere, so that entry takes "born in Lishui"; here the locality is not in Lishui either, so "active in Lishui" is used instead. The two are of different levels and must not be read as the same.

## Points of doubt

First, his year of birth is unknown; the entry gives only that he was thirty-eight at his death, and no birth year is inferred from it and nothing is converted into a Common-Era year. His own year of death is likewise given no era.

Second, although his locality is recorded as "Putou", which county it belonged to and its distance from Lishui are unrecorded, and this library holds no material to decide them; the entry does not treat Putou as within this library's Lishui on that ground.

Third, the exact years of his appointments, what "by way of the censorate" refers to, and whether the postings at Xianju and Huangyan were acting or delegated appointments are nowhere recorded.

Fourth, as to his work in the two counties the gazetteer says only that both were handled well and that both populations praised him, listing no deed; this entry supplies none.

Fifth, the year of the campaign against the Japanese and the present locations of Aoling and Diaoyuling are unrecorded, and what "five days" counted from is not stated, so nothing is inferred.

Sixth, what the inspector's forbearance rested on, what troops Wu led after his oath, and what the officers had said are all unrecorded.

Seventh, where the reinforcements should have come from, and how many there would have been, are unrecorded; his grave, his burial place, his descendants and his heirs are nowhere recorded, and this entry adds nothing about them.
