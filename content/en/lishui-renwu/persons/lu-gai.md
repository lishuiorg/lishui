Lu Gai is one of the thinnest entries in the Tang part of the notable-officials biography in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*: the whole biography is a single sentence, and this entry goes no further than it does.

## Origin and term

> 陆该，吴县人，象先孙也。为令倬有时誉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was a native of Wu county and is called "a grandson of Xiangxian" — the gazetteer gives his grandfather's given name but supplies no surname for him, and records no father. His office is given only as "ling"; the gazetteer never states the formal title of the post he held in Lishui, and records no year of appointment.

## What the gazetteer records

The only thing said of his career is the clause "as ling he was distinguished and had a reputation of his time". The gazetteer offers no gloss on the word rendered here as "distinguished", and this entry adds none. The sentence cites no act and records no measure; this entry has no deeds to report because the source contains none, not because any have been left out.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of holding the office of "ling", and this entry records him through official service. The volume records nothing he did in Lishui and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, the year of his appointment is not recorded, and this entry does not infer one from the dynasty.

Third, the gazetteer gives his office only as "ling" and never states the formal title of his Lishui post; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, the details of the lineage below "Xiangxian" — in particular his own father — are not recorded, and this entry supplies none.

Fifth, what earned him "a reputation of his time" is not recorded.

Sixth, this volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
