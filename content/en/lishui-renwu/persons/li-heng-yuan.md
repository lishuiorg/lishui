Li Heng is one of the Yuan officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The volume contains two entries named Li Heng, and this is the one whose appointment is given as the first Zhiyuan year; the other is a Song official whose biography gives the courtesy name Yanping, a native place of Jiangdu, and appointment in the first Longxing year, and which this library holds as a separate entry. This entry writes only what the Yuan entry contains.

## Origin and term

> 李衡，至元元年任溧水州。政务既举乃修学宫，益生徒，建尊道、明德二堂，以诱后进。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui *zhou* in the first year of the Zhiyuan era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His own biography states his post only as "appointed to Lishui *zhou*": *zhou* is the name of the administrative unit as the gazetteer gives it, and the biography never states his formal title. That title appears in the annals of juan 1 of the same book, and this entry records it as prefect on that basis while noting where it comes from. The Zhiyuan year is kept as the gazetteer gives it; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the annals of the same book say

> 至元三年丁丑，溧水知州李衡言跕戶消乏，請於溧陽僉補，從之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 1

The annals therefore call him prefect of Lishui and record that in the third year of Zhiyuan he reported that households attached to a labour duty were depleted, asked that they be made up by conscription from the neighbouring county of Liyang, and was granted. This entry records the matter on that basis and states four things plainly. First, both the title and this act come from the annals, not from his biography, which covers only his appointment and the school works. Second, the character the transcription gives for the household class is reproduced as it stands; this entry does not silently read it as the usual term for relay households and does not emend it from other books, and it makes no judgement about what class of duty is meant. Third, "depleted" and "conscription to make up" are the gazetteer's wording; how many households were conscripted, on what terms, and with what consequences are nowhere recorded. Fourth, the matter is dated to the third Zhiyuan year, two years after the appointment given by his biography; both dates are reproduced unconverted, and neither is used to fix the start or end of his term.

The annals also give the date of the change in status:

> 成宗元贞元年乙未，升溧水为州，等为中。仍设达鲁花赤，出知州上。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 1

This does not agree with "appointed to Lishui *zhou* in the first Zhiyuan year": the appointment his biography gives is earlier than the year the annals give for the promotion, yet the biography already calls the place a *zhou*. Two juan of one book disagree, and this library keeps both: the annals are not used to alter the year in his biography, his biography is not used to alter the year in the annals, and no judgement is made about whether Lishui was a prefecture or a county between those two dates. This entry therefore avoids any wording that folds the administrative status and the appointment year into a single statement.

## What the gazetteer records

The biography opens with the general statement that "his administration being already in order" he turned to the school; what the administration consisted of is nowhere recorded, and this entry infers no specific measure from that phrase. Four items follow. He repaired the school, with no year and no description of the work. He increased the students, with no number and no account of how it was done. He built two halls whose names the gazetteer gives as Zundao and Mingde, reproduced here as transcribed, while their location, their year of construction and their later fate are unrecorded. The closing clause "in order to encourage those who came after" states the purpose the gazetteer assigns to the two halls.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of "appointed to Lishui *zhou* in the first Zhiyuan year", and this entry records him through official service. Beyond his appointment and the three school matters, the volume records no year of leaving and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as the first Zhiyuan year; this entry converts nothing, fixes no start or end of his term, and records no year of leaving.

Third, his own biography gives his post only as "appointed to Lishui *zhou*" and states no title; the annals of the same book call him prefect, and this entry records the title on that basis while noting where it comes from. The two places it comes from are kept distinct rather than merged into one statement.

Fourth, status and appointment year disagree: his biography says he was appointed to Lishui *zhou* in the first Zhiyuan year, while the annals date the promotion of Lishui to *zhou* status to the first Yuanzhen year — the appointment is earlier than the promotion, yet already described as being to a *zhou*. Two juan of one book disagree; both are kept, and no judgement is made about whether Lishui was a prefecture or a county in between.

Fifth, the volume contains two entries named Li Heng. The one used here gives appointment to Lishui *zhou* in the first Zhiyuan year; the other is a Song official given the courtesy name Yanping, a native place of Jiangdu, and appointment in the first Longxing year (this library's separate entry on Li Heng). The two biographies differ in everything they state, and this library holds no material that decides whether they are one person or two, or any relationship between them. The two entries therefore stand separately: they are not merged, and neither is said to be the other. The link this entry carries to the Song entry is a cross-check on the shared name only, not a claim that they are the same person.

Sixth, the measures meant by "his administration being already in order" are unrecorded, as are the year and extent of the school repairs, the number and arrangement of the added students, and the location, construction date and later history of the Zundao and Mingde halls. In the annals' entry, what the household class means and how many households were conscripted and on what terms are likewise nowhere recorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in the biography volume and two entries in the annals; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. The gazetteer was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
