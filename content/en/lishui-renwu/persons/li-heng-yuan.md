Li Heng is one of the Yuan officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The volume contains two entries named Li Heng, and this is the one whose appointment is given as the first Zhiyuan year; the other is a Song official whose biography gives the courtesy name Yanping, a native place of Jiangdu, and appointment in the first Longxing year, and which this library holds as a separate entry. This entry writes only what the Yuan entry contains.

## Origin and term

> 李衡，至元元年任溧水州。政务既举乃修学宫，益生徒，建尊道、明德二堂，以诱后进。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui *zhou* in the first year of the Zhiyuan era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His post is stated only as "appointed to Lishui *zhou*": *zhou* is the name of the administrative unit as the gazetteer gives it, and the gazetteer never states his formal title, so this entry does not render him as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Zhiyuan year is kept as the gazetteer gives it; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

The biography opens with the general statement that "his administration being already in order" he turned to the school; what the administration consisted of is nowhere recorded, and this entry infers no specific measure from that phrase. Four items follow. He repaired the school, with no year and no description of the work. He increased the students, with no number and no account of how it was done. He built two halls whose names the gazetteer gives as Zundao and Mingde, reproduced here as transcribed, while their location, their year of construction and their later fate are unrecorded. The closing clause "in order to encourage those who came after" states the purpose the gazetteer assigns to the two halls.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of "appointed to Lishui *zhou* in the first Zhiyuan year", and this entry records him through official service. Beyond his appointment and the three school matters, the volume records no year of leaving and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as the first Zhiyuan year; this entry converts nothing, fixes no start or end of his term, and records no year of leaving.

Third, the gazetteer gives his post only as "appointed to Lishui *zhou*" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no other title.

Fourth, the volume contains two entries named Li Heng. The one used here gives appointment to Lishui *zhou* in the first Zhiyuan year; the other is a Song official given the courtesy name Yanping, a native place of Jiangdu, and appointment in the first Longxing year (this library's separate entry on Li Heng). The two biographies differ in everything they state, and this library holds no material that decides whether they are one person or two, or any relationship between them. The two entries therefore stand separately: they are not merged, and neither is said to be the other. The link this entry carries to the Song entry is a cross-check on the shared name only, not a claim that they are the same person.

Fifth, the measures meant by "his administration being already in order" are unrecorded, as are the year and extent of the school repairs, the number and arrangement of the added students, and the location, construction date and later history of the Zundao and Mingde halls.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
