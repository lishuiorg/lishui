Yi Wuyi is one of the Yuan officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*; the Yuan part of the volume holds only two entries, and this is one of them. His biography has one sentence of appointment and three items of construction, which makes it one of the more concrete entries in the volume — yet none of the three items is given any detail.

## Origin and term

> 仪武义，元贞初任溧水州。修建学校，易斋扁，铸祭器，焕然一新。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui *zhou* early in the Yuanzhen era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His post is stated only as "appointed to Lishui *zhou*": *zhou* is the name of the administrative unit as the gazetteer gives it, and the gazetteer never states his formal title, so this entry does not render him as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. "Early in the Yuanzhen era" is a span marked by an era name, not a year; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

Three items are recorded for his term: building and repairing the schools, changing the boards of the dining hall, and casting ritual vessels. Each is only a heading. Which school was repaired, where it stood and what work was done is not recorded. For the boards, the gazetteer writes the character transcribed here as *bian* and this entry reproduces it as transcribed without substituting the variant commonly used for a inscribed board; which hall was re-named and what the inscriptions said are unrecorded. Which vessels were cast, how many, of what shape and where they are now is nowhere stated. "Entirely renewed" is the gazetteer's verdict on the three items, recorded here as the gazetteer's judgement rather than as a verifiable fact.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of "appointed to Lishui *zhou* early in the Yuanzhen era", and this entry records him through official service. Beyond his appointment and these three items, the volume records no year of leaving and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as "early in the Yuanzhen era", a span rather than a year, and the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his post only as "appointed to Lishui *zhou*" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no other title.

Fourth, the school concerned in "building and repairing the schools", its site and the work done are unrecorded; the name of the hall and the wording of the boards are unrecorded; the names, number, shape and location of the ritual vessels are unrecorded.

Fifth, "entirely renewed" is the gazetteer's judgement, and the grounds for it are unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
