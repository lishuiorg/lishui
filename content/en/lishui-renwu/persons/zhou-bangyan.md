Zhou Bangyan is an entry in the Song part of the notable-officials biography in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. Among the Song officials in this volume he is the one whose writings produced in Lishui are recorded at most length.

## Origin and term

> 周邦彦，字美成，钱塘人。元祐八年任，雅娴于文词，一时称为才吏。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was courtesy-named Meicheng, a native of Qiantang. His appointment is dated precisely, "the eighth year of the Yuanyou era" — one of the entries in this volume that gives a fixed year. The source dates by reign period, and this entry reproduces that dating without adding a year of the common era. His Lishui post is given only as "appointed", and later referred to as "in the county"; no formal title is stated, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county". "Refined and versed in literary composition" and "called a talented official of his time" are the gazetteer's assessments, quoted as such.

## What the gazetteer records

> 在县作《萧闲堂》《插竹亭》《题名》诸记，及他什甚富，而不妨于政。其为人落魄不羁，多涉经史。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

While in the county he wrote the records of the Xiaoxian Hall, the Chazhu Pavilion and the inscribed names, together with many other pieces, and this did not hinder his administration. "Unconventional and unrestrained" and "widely read in the classics and histories" are the gazetteer's remarks on his character.

> 先是，元丰中献《汴都赋》万余言，多用古文奇字，神宗异之，自诸生召为太学正。后至徽猷阁待制，知顺昌府。有文集二十四卷行于世。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

With the words "earlier than this" the gazetteer goes back to events before his Lishui post: during the Yuanfeng era he presented the *Rhapsody on the Capital of Bian*, over ten thousand characters long, much of it in archaic and rare graph forms; the Shenzong emperor thought him remarkable, and he was summoned from the status of a student to be a standard-bearer of the Grand Academy. "During the Yuanfeng era" is a span, not a fixed year, and this entry converts nothing. His later offices — attendant of the Huiyou Pavilion, and administrator of Shunchang prefecture — and his collected works in twenty-four juan circulating in the world are all matters after Lishui, whose years and sequence the gazetteer does not give.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in Lishui through his appointment in a fixed year and through what he wrote "in the county", and this entry records him through official service. His main connection with Lishui is his writing during the post: three titles are named — the Xiaoxian Hall, the Chazhu Pavilion and the inscribed names — all composed while he was in the county. Whether these pieces survive, and where, is not recorded.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, the gazetteer never states the formal title of his Lishui post; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Third, the survival and location of the records of the Xiaoxian Hall, the Chazhu Pavilion and the inscribed names are not recorded; "many other pieces" are named nowhere, and this entry lists none.

Fourth, "during the Yuanfeng era" is a span with no fixed year, and the years and order of his later offices are not given.

Fifth, the editions and survival of the collected works in twenty-four juan are not recorded.

Sixth, this volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
