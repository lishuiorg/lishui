Madam Huang is one of the figures in the Yuan section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice records her husband, the words with which she cut off her hair after his death, her raising of her orphan and keeping her chastity, and her honouring in the Zhizheng period. This entry follows the gazetteer's layers and supplies no name, home place or date.

## The gazetteer text

> 黃氏，張和妻。年未三十而寡，即截髮自明，曰，「吾未可死者，以孤在耳。」卒能撫孤完節終焉。至正間旌表之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her station and her husband's house

"Madam Huang" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname and no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Huang" as its title. "Wife of Zhang He" is the husband the gazetteer records, whose name is Zhang He, reproduced here without alteration. The gazetteer gives neither her home place nor the places of her paternal or marital house, and no residence or native place is supplied.

## Widowhood, cut hair and the raising of her orphan

"Widowed before thirty" is the gazetteer's account of her lot: she was widowed before reaching thirty. "She at once cut off her hair to show her resolve" is the gazetteer's account of her chastity: she cut off her hair to show her determination never to remarry. Cutting off the hair is a stock act in the Chaste and Staunch biography of this gazetteer, and this entry reproduces it as the gazetteer's account. The gazetteer quotes her words, "I have not yet been permitted to die, because the orphan is here"; this is the notice's record of what she said in showing her resolve, reproduced as the gazetteer's quotation of her speech and not this library's judgement. The "orphan" she speaks of the gazetteer does not name, count or account for. "To the end she was able to raise her orphan and keep her chastity" is the gazetteer's summary of her life: in the end she was able to bring up the orphan and keep her chastity until death. Her age at death and whether the orphan prospered are unrecorded.

## Her honouring in the Zhizheng period

"In the Zhizheng period she was honoured" is the gazetteer's account of the honour she received: in the Zhizheng period she was honoured. In what year of that period, on whose memorial, and under what title are unrecorded, and nothing is inferred here from other juan. The era is the gazetteer's own and is reproduced without conversion into a Common-Era year.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as the wife of Zhang He, and records her widowhood, her cutting off her hair, her raising of her orphan and her honouring. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer records no birth place, native place or township, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; and the biography belongs to the "Chaste and Staunch" section of this county's gazetteer and records her chastity and the honour she received, which this library treats as her activity in Lishui, so the relation taken is "active in Lishui". The limits must be stated: the gazetteer records no home place for her, so "active in Lishui" here rests only on her appearing in this county's Chaste and Staunch biography and carries no explicit statement that she acted in Lishui.

Second, neither her personal nor her courtesy name is recorded, and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Madam Huang.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives only that she was the wife of Zhang He and no home place, so no native place can be decided.

Fourth, the life of her husband Zhang He is unrecorded, and Zhang He has no entry in this library.
