Zeng Jiuyi is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is built on two episodes of uncovering villainy, together with his regret at leaving and his later exceptional selection; it is one of the few biographies in this volume whose subject is order and litigation.

## Origin and term

> 曾就义，字双南，江西赣州人。由乙丑进士，天启七年任。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Shuangnan, a native of Ganzhou in Jiangxi, entered as a *jinshi* of a *yichou* year, and was appointed in the seventh year of the Tianqi era. The cyclical date is left as it stands: this entry converts nothing and does not infer its relation to the year of appointment. The gazetteer writes only "appointed" and gives no office title.

## What the record says about his acts

**Acuteness in uncovering villainy: gambling.**

> 居官多善政，尤以摘奸为精敏。市徒夜聚赌博，至明则勾摄已在门矣，赌风一杜自公始。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Market men gathered at night to gamble, and by dawn the arresting runners were already at their door. "In office he gave many beneficial policies and was especially acute in uncovering villainy" is the gazetteer's general judgement; "the complete stopping of the gambling habit began with this lord" is its reported judgement and attribution, not a verifiable record.

**Anonymous complaints and the runners from above.**

> 民有以夥状吓骗良善者，不遂其欲辄以无名状阴告，各上司差役下乡如虎狼，经年不决。公廉得其状，必请于府，且奋髯而起，曰：『不活磔此辈，无以安良民也！』
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The gazetteer records that some people used mass-name accusations to intimidate and cheat the good, and when their desires were not met filed anonymous complaints in secret, so that runners from the higher offices went down into the countryside like tigers and wolves and cases went undecided for years. Zeng Jiuyi detected their game, always reported the matter to the prefecture, and rose with his bristles astir and spoke. The words "unless these men are torn limb from limb while alive, the good people cannot have peace" are recorded here as a speech the gazetteer attributes to him, quoted and marked as such, not judged on its own account; "like tigers and wolves" is the gazetteer's figure.

**Departure, regret and later selection.**

> 乃以丁艰去，犹以未尽除此害为憾。当公之时，市棍莫不敛戢，公之政不可枚举，举其要者。崇祯间破格甄用，选入翰林，然用掩其长矣。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He left office for a parent's mourning and still regretted not having fully removed this harm. "In his time none of the market bullies failed to keep themselves in check" is the gazetteer's judgement; "his policies cannot be listed one by one — here only the main ones are given" is its own note that the biography selects. The gazetteer further records that in the Chongzhen period he was picked out for exceptional employment and chosen into the Hanlin, and adds "yet his employment overshadowed his qualities" — the gazetteer's assessment, transcribed as it stands and left unexplained.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his appointment in the seventh year of the Tianqi era, the two episodes of uncovering villainy, the account of his regret at leaving with the harm not fully removed, and the reported praise that the market bullies kept themselves in check. His biography records no building, no fiscal matter and no teaching.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded.

Second, the year he left Lishui is unrecorded, only "he left for a parent's mourning"; the era year gives only his arrival, and nothing is converted.

Third, the cyclical date of his degree is left unconverted, and its relation to his year of appointment is not explained by the gazetteer.

Fourth, his Lishui office is given only as "appointed", with no precise title.

Fifth, the specific cases behind the mass-name and anonymous accusations, the people involved and the final verdicts are unrecorded, as are the offices whose runners came down to the countryside.

Sixth, the date and mechanism of his exceptional selection and the specific Hanlin post he received are unrecorded.

Seventh, "the end of gambling began with him", "none of the market bullies failed to keep themselves in check" and "yet his employment overshadowed his qualities" are the gazetteer's reported judgements, attributions and assessment.

Eighth, the speech with bristles astir is a saying the gazetteer attributes to him, not a fact verified by this library.

Ninth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
