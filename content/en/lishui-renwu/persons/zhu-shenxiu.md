Zhu Shenxiu is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography gives his style of government and his later ranks: less than a year in Lishui, no single act named, yet a reputation as a god among officials.

## Origin and term

> 朱身修，字安伯，江西进贤人。由万历丁未进士任溧水，以爱民为心，不滥受词，坦然以农桑是劝。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Anbo, a native of Jinxian in Jiangxi, and held a Lishui post as a *jinshi* of a *dingwei* year of the Wanli era. The cyclical date is left as it stands: this entry converts nothing and does not infer his year of arrival from it. The gazetteer writes only "held a post in Lishui" and gives no office title; this entry follows that wording. "He took care for the people as his heart, took no litigation complaints indiscriminately, and openly urged farming and sericulture" is the gazetteer's three-clause summary of his conduct.

## What the record says about his acts

**Style of government.**

> 吏胥左右无敢一有犯者，公不威而民畏，有礼而士亲。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Not one of the clerks and runners around him dared offend" and "without sternness he was feared by the people, with courtesy he was close to the scholars" are the gazetteer's judgements, set in balanced pairs, naming no act.

**Less than a year, the reputation, and his later ranks.**

> 未期年，而邑之利弊了然，搜剔殆尽，称为神明。擢南礼部主事，转南吏部考功司郎中，终于福建布政使。邑有祠焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

In less than a full year in Lishui he is said to have known the county's advantages and harms clearly and to have searched them out almost completely, and so "was called a god" — the gazetteer's reported praise of his contemporaries, not a verifiable assessment record. The three clauses "promoted", "transferred" and "ended" give his later ranks with no dates; "ended as provincial administration commissioner of Fujian" states his final rank, not the year or place of his death. "The county had a shrine" is its record of posthumous worship.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is only a term of less than a year, the reputation attributed to him and the county shrine. His biography names no building, no fiscal item and no works in Lishui, so this entry supplies no list of acts for him.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded; "ended as provincial administration commissioner of Fujian" gives his final rank, not the year or place of his death.

Second, the year of his Lishui post is unrecorded; the cyclical date is left unconverted and is not used to infer his arrival.

Third, his term in Lishui is given only as "less than a year"; its beginning and end are unrecorded.

Fourth, the gazetteer writes only "held a post in Lishui" and gives no office title; this entry follows that wording.

Fifth, "was called a god" is reported praise, and "knew the county's advantages and harms, searched them out almost completely" is the gazetteer's judgement; the items it refers to are unrecorded.

Sixth, his promotion, transfer and final rank are all undated.

Seventh, the location, date and later history of the county shrine are unrecorded.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
