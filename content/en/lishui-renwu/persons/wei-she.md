Wei She is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is the shortest kind in the volume: one sentence of appointment, one clause of judgement, one clause of popular reaction, and nothing else. This entry writes only what that entry contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 卫社，绍定四年任。清而仁，民悦之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui in the fourth year of the Shaoding era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Shaoding year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

The only thing said of his career is the clause "clear and benevolent; the people were pleased by him". The first part is the gazetteer's judgement of his clarity and his benevolence; the second is its account of how the population reacted — a claim about reputation rather than a verifiable record. The sentence cites no act and records no measure: this entry has no deeds to report because the source contains none, not because any have been left out.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of "appointed in the fourth Shaoding year", and this entry records him through official service. The volume records nothing he did in Lishui, no year of leaving, and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as the fourth Shaoding year, which is the year of arrival; the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, "clear and benevolent" is the gazetteer's judgement and "the people were pleased by him" its account of reputation; the grounds for both are unrecorded.

Fifth, any concrete acts in Lishui, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
