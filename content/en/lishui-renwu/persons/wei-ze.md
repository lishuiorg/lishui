Wei Ze is a minister of justice of the early Ming recorded in the "Loyalty and Integrity" biography of juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His entry belongs to the appended biographies of that volume rather than to the main sequence. The gazetteer places his native place inside Lishui County while recording that the place had already been divided away into Gaochun, and it records a stele raised for him in Lishui, so this library builds him an entry and states plainly in the open questions that the place now belongs to another county rather than glossing over it.

## His person and career

> 魏澤，字彥恩。少有學行，洪武中官刑部尚書，建文時謫為海寧尉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Courtesy name Yan'en" is his courtesy name as the entry gives it. "He showed learning and conduct while young" is the entry's remark on his youth; nothing is recorded about what it rests on. "He served as minister of justice in the Hongwu period" gives his office without a year. "In the Jianwen period he was demoted to magistrate of Haining" records the most consequential episode of his life. The entry gives neither his birth or death year nor the year he became minister.

## Shielding Fang Xiaoru's clan: the crucial part of the entry

> 時文皇逮方孝儒械繫獄，以姚廣孝言欲勿殺，乃捕其族黨輒下獄，以怵方，而方卒勿屈，故方氏族黨殆盡。諸嘗善方者人人危懼，莫不諱言方氏。澤獨以一尉犯不測威，傾身庇焉，竟能存方氏。後天下義之，謝文肅所稱「孫枝一葉」者是矣。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Several points must be stated. First, "the Emperor Wen" is the gazetteer's own designation for the Yongle emperor, the adversary of the Jianwen reign; "Fang Xiaoru was seized and put in irons" is what the gazetteer records, and this entry reproduces the words "Emperor Wen" rather than substituting another title. Second, "on the words of Yao Guangxiao he wished not to kill them" is the gazetteer's account of what Yao said; it is reproduced as such, and no motive is decided from it. Third, "they arrested the clan and dragged them all to prison" and "the Fang clan was all but extinguished" record the consequence; "all but" is the gazetteer's own wording, reproduced here. Fourth, "Wei Ze alone, a mere clerk, braved the immeasurable danger and shielded them, and so the Fang clan was preserved" is the gazetteer's narrative of his act, reproduced; the phrase rendered "immeasurable danger" is the gazetteer's, and this entry does not rewrite it as a modern construction. Fifth, "the empire held it right, in the words of Xie Wensu, 'a single branch of the tree'" is the gazetteer quoting another man, reproduced and marked as his, not written as fact.

## The stele raised in Lishui, and his native place: the basis of this entry's relation

> 後直指黃紀賢代巡至溧，建石碑於北門橋，表澤大義，知澤為練城人，在溧水分去高淳縣境。高淳志載澤家世詳甚。澤所著有《綿山集》藏於家。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This sentence is the whole of his recorded connection to Lishui, and it must be taken clause by clause. First, "later the circuit inspector Huang Jixian, touring Lishui, raised a stele at the North Gate Bridge to commemorate his righteousness" is the gazetteer's record of a stele erected for him in Lishui, and it is the one direct deed this library knows of him in the county; "North Gate Bridge" is where the stele stood, and this entry names the place without deciding where it is today. Second, "it is known that Wei Ze was a man of Lianshan, in the territory taken from Lishui County into Gaochun County" is the gazetteer's own statement of his native place and its administrative affiliation, and on that basis this library decides his birthplace lay inside Lishui; the phrase "taken from Lishui County into Gaochun County" says exactly that the place was carved out of Lishui and assigned to Gaochun, hence "born in Lishui"; but this is this library's decision drawn from the gazetteer, and the open questions record that the place now lies in Gaochun County and no inference is made about its village. Third, "the Gaochun gazetteer records his family descent at length" points to another gazetteer, which this library has not consulted, so nothing is taken from it about his family. Fourth, "what he wrote includes the *Collection of Mian Mountain*, kept in the family" gives the title of his work and its keeping; this entry records the title but has not transcribed the text, and says nothing about whether it survives.

## A judgement quoted from an older gazetteer, and the lamenting poem

> 舊志吳仕詮曰：「魏澤無意於死建文君難，而顧汲汲為方氏後者，何烈也！豈其不欲此身以眾人死耶？鄭莊簡乃附澤於死難諸臣後，有旨哉！」澤過方故宅有悼詩，載《藝文志》。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This part of the entry quotes a judgement by Wu Shiquan from an older gazetteer and closes by recording that Wei Ze wrote a lamenting poem at Fang's former dwelling, listed in this gazetteer's arts-and-literature section. Four points must be stated. First, this part quotes an older gazetteer and is not the same layer as the first part of the entry; it is another man's opinion, reproduced and marked as such. Second, "he had no intention of dying in the Jianwen disaster, and yet strove for Fang's line; how fiery!" is Wu Shiquan's question, and "could it be that he wished only to leave this body to die with the multitude?" is his own rhetorical question; both are the older gazetteer's opinion and neither is written here as fact. Third, "Zheng Zhuangjian did indeed append Wei Ze to the ministers who died in the disaster, and there was purpose in it" is Wu Shiquan's remark on Wei Ze's placement among the dead, reproduced and marked as his, and this entry does not decide what Zheng Zhuangjian in fact appended. Fourth, "Wei Ze passed Fang's old dwelling and wrote a lamenting poem, listed in the arts-and-literature section" records the poem and its place in this gazetteer; this library holds neither its text nor any account of it and adds nothing from it to his life. This library already has an entry for Qi Tai, whose gazetteer text also records his own death in the Jianwen disaster, but nothing here brings the two men together beyond what the gazetteer does not record.

## Relation to Lishui

He enters the persons site with the relation "born in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the entry itself says he was a man of Lianshan, in the territory taken from Lishui County into Gaochun County, so his birthplace lay inside this library's Lishui; his offices, minister of justice and magistrate of Haining, were both held elsewhere and neither was a Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the entry records no recommendation out of Lishui, so "recommended from Lishui" is not available; it credits him with the *Collection of Mian Mountain* but this library holds none of its text and nothing in the title connects it to Lishui, so "writings" is not asserted; the entry does record that a circuit inspector raised a stele for him in Lishui, and since his birthplace is already in Lishui, "born in Lishui" alone expresses the relation and "sojourn" is not used. The limit of this entry must be stated: the place now lies in Gaochun County, so the Lishui recorded here is his historical native territory, and it does not mean that all of Gaochun today belongs to Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are nowhere recorded; "He showed learning and conduct while young" and "He served as minister of justice in the Hongwu period" carry no year, and nothing is converted into a Common-Era year and no age is inferred.

Second, his place "Lianshan" now belongs to Gaochun County; this entry rests only on the gazetteer's own statement and infers no village location, and because "taken from Lishui County into Gaochun County" is the gazetteer's own phrase and the Gaochun gazetteer has not been consulted, nothing is drawn from its account of his family.

Third, this entry belongs to the "appended biographies" and not to the main sequence; whether he can be ranked with the main-sequence figure Qi Tai cannot be decided from this library's material, and the related link is a cross-reference only.

Fourth, "on the words of Yao Guangxiao he wished not to kill them" is the gazetteer's account and no motive is decided from it; "what Xie Wensu called a single branch of the tree" and "Zheng Zhuangjian did indeed append Wei Ze" are other men's opinions, reproduced and marked as such.

Fifth, whether the *Collection of Mian Mountain* survives, its extent and its contents are not held by this library; whether it concerns Lishui cannot be decided.

Sixth, "he passed Fang's old dwelling and wrote a lamenting poem, listed in the arts-and-literature section": this library knows only that the poem and its place exist, holds no text, and takes nothing from it into his life.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single appended biography, which is short and undated; the outline of his career cannot be decided from this library's material and must not be filled in from other books.
