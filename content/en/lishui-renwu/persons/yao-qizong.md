Yao Qizong is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is one of the shortest in the volume: one sentence of appointment, two clauses of judgement, and nothing else. This entry writes only what that entry contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 姚耆宗，绍兴十五年任。爱民戢吏，详明不苛，邑称大治。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui in the fifteenth year of the Shaoxing era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Shaoxing year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

Two clauses describe how he governed: "loving the people and restraining the clerks" judges both his treatment of the population and his control of his subordinates, and "detailed and clear without harshness" judges the way he handled business. The further clause "the county was said to be well governed" is a verdict on the county as a whole — the verb "said" is the gazetteer's own, and the clause is the gazetteer's evaluation rather than a verifiable record. The biography cites no act and records no measure, and this entry infers no building, no case and no tax matter from the judgements.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records no native place for him, and this entry enters him through official service. What ties him to the county is the sentence giving his appointment and the judgements on his government, including the overall verdict on the county; nothing he did in Lishui is recorded beyond that, and neither his leaving nor his later movements are given.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as the fifteenth Shaoxing year, which is the year of arrival; the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, the judgements and the claim that the county was well governed are the gazetteer's, and the grounds for the verdict are unrecorded.

Fifth, any concrete acts in Lishui, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him.
