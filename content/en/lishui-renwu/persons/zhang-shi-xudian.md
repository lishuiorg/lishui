Madam Zhang is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of the county stipendiary Xu Dian. Her notice is one of the fuller ones in the Ming section. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 張氏，邑廩生許點妻。年二十，生二女，點死。立志守節，有丁姓者陰為謀娶，氏剔目自誓，丁懼而止。蓬首垢衣，不窺閫外者四十九年，屢受旌獎焉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of the county stipendiary Xu Dian" names husband and wife. A county stipendiary was a stipend-supported student of the county school, which the gazetteer uses to define Xu Dian's status.

Second, "she was twenty and had borne two daughters when Dian died" dates the husband's death.

Third, "she resolved to keep her chastity; a man surnamed Ding schemed in secret to marry her, so she put out her own eye as a vow, and Ding desisted in fear" records her resolve. Putting out her eye was her violent act of refusal, reproduced here as the gazetteer tells it.

Fourth, "with matted hair and filthy clothes, she did not cross her inner door for forty-nine years, and was repeatedly honoured" records her manner of life and her commendations. The text does not name those who honoured her, nor the dates.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Xu Dian, whose household was her place of residence; her widowhood and the honours she received attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty" dates her husband's death, not her birth.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the fate of her two daughters and whether she had other children are unrecorded, as are the life and death year of Xu Dian.

Fourth, the man surnamed Ding is unnamed, and the scheme to marry her rests only on the gazetteer's account.

Fifth, "repeatedly honoured" gives no exact number, no names of those who honoured her, no dates and no tablet inscription.

Sixth, "forty-nine years" is the gazetteer's figure, reproduced without conversion to a Western date.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
