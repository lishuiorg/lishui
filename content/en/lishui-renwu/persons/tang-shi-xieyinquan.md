Madam Tang is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Tang, her husband Xie Yinquan, and three matters, her chastity, her care for her mother-in-law, and her teaching of her son, closing with the words of a gate honour. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 湯氏，謝胤銓妻。二十七歲守節，家無儲粟，誓死存孤。事舅姑克盡婦道，姑壽八十餘，湯亦年逾六旬左右，奉養一如初嫁時。居家刻苦勤儉，教子有方。宗族稱為孝婦，復稱為賢母，府經歷、署縣事龔龍見旌其廬曰「苦節可風」。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is layered.

"Madam Tang, wife of Xie Yinquan" gives her natal surname and her husband's name. Her given name, courtesy name, residence, and paternal family are all unrecorded, as are her husband's courtesy name and status.

"From twenty-seven she kept her chastity; with no grain in the house she vowed to raise her orphan to the death" gives the start of her widowhood and its setting. "No grain in the house" shows her poverty, and her vow to raise the orphan to death is the gazetteer's record. Her son's name and her husband's years are unrecorded.

"She served her parents-in-law as a wife should; when her mother-in-law reached over eighty and she herself over sixty, she cared for her as on the day she married" gives the substance of her filial service. This is the gazetteer's record of her conduct.

"A frugal householder, she taught her son well" is the gazetteer's summary of her thrift and her teaching.

"Her clan called her a filial wife and again a worthy mother" records the clan's judgement, as the gazetteer reports it.

"Gong Longjian, a prefectural registrar acting as magistrate, honoured her gate with the words 'A model of bitter chastity'" gives the honouring man and the plaque text. His office is recorded; the plaque bears the words "A model of bitter chastity", a rare case in this volume where the text is given. The gazetteer does not give his native place or the dates of his service, and this entry supplies nothing.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: wife of Xie Yinquan, keeping chastity from twenty-seven, serving her mother-in-law and teaching her son, and finally having her gate honoured with "A model of bitter chastity" by Gong Longjian, acting magistrate — her household and the honouring official both in Lishui. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Tang", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of keeping her home, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she kept her chastity from twenty-seven and cared for her mother-in-law past sixty; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Tang"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives only her husband Xie Yinquan; his courtesy name and status are unrecorded, as are the orphan's name and later career.

Fourth, the year of the gate honour, and the native place and dates of service of the honouring Gong Longjian, are unrecorded; the plaque text, however, is given as "A model of bitter chastity".

Fifth, other items in this volume name men such as Xie Yinshou and Xie Yingang, and this entry draws no link to them, nor judges whether the three bearing the name Xie Yin belong to one household.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
