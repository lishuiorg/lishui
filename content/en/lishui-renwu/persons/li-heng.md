Li Heng is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Distinguished Officials" biography section. His biography is a single short passage. Everything it says about his Lishui term is judgement of his conduct; the only concrete information it gives concerns his later career. This entry transcribes the passage in parts, marks every judgement as a judgement, and adds nothing the gazetteer does not record.

## Name and origin

> 李衡，字彦平，江都人。绍兴中进士，隆兴元年任。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Yanping, a native of Jiangdu, entered office through the *jinshi* degree, and took up Lishui in the first year of the Longxing era. "In the Shaoxing period" gives only a span, not the year of his degree; "appointed in the first year of Longxing" gives only the year of arrival. This entry converts neither and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the record says about his Lishui term

> 博学能文，操笔立就。专以诚意化民，注情学校，诸生向风。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This whole passage is the gazetteer's judgement and narration: "broad in learning and able in letters, writing at once without drafting" judges his literary gifts; "he devoted himself to moving the people with sincerity" judges his method; "he set his heart on the school" states his attitude toward the county school; "the students turned to the wind of it" states the students' response. The biography records no measure, no building, no case, no fiscal act during his term in Lishui, so this entry does not infer any act from the praise.

## After Lishui

> 累官至副御史，改起居郎，以秘书修撰致仕。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The gazetteer links three later ranks with the words "accumulated promotions", "changed to" and "retired", and gives no year for any of them. The rank names are transcribed as the gazetteer writes them; this entry neither substitutes other official titles nor supplies dates.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is the single statement of his appointment and the conduct described during it. "He set his heart on the school" is the only sentence in the biography that points at a Lishui matter, and it states an attitude rather than an act.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Jiangdu.

Second, his degree is dated only as "in the Shaoxing period". This entry does not convert that into a year, and does not estimate how many years separated his degree from his Lishui term.

Third, the year he left Lishui is not recorded; only the year of his arrival is given.

Fourth, the dates and places of the three later ranks — vice censor, the diaries office, and the secretariat compiler from which he retired — are nowhere recorded. "Vice censor" is the gazetteer's wording, and this entry does not guess at the formal title behind it.

Fifth, what "he set his heart on the school" actually involved — repairs, estate, teaching — is not recorded.

Sixth, where he went after retirement, and the year and place of his death, are not recorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him; his career before Lishui, his examination cohort and his associations have no support.
