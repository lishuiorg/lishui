Yu Zhou is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography runs to two clauses: one of status and date, one of cutting his thigh to cure his mother. This entry writes nothing beyond those two clauses and places everything the gazetteer does not record under Points of doubt rather than supplying it.

## The gazetteer text

> 虞周，天順間為邑庠生。母史氏患蠱，諸醫不效，乃割股愈焉。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Status and date

"County school student in the Tianshun period" is the only date and the only status the biography gives. "Student of the county school" is the gazetteer's own wording, and this entry keeps it rather than converting it into "licentiate" or any other term. "Tianshun" here dates his status as a student; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers neither his birth and death nor the span of his activity from it.

## What the gazetteer records

His mother, of the Shi lineage, fell ill — the gazetteer writes the single character for a wasting or venomous ailment — the physicians were of no effect, and Yu Zhou cut his own thigh, after which his mother recovered. The cutting is a deed as recorded by the gazetteer, and this entry marks it as such; "she recovered" is the gazetteer's closing judgement on the episode. What the illness actually was, how the wound was treated, in what year she recovered and how long she lived, are nowhere recorded. The biography says nothing about any award, nothing about his later life and nothing about his death.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no township and no native place; it ties him to Lishui only by making him a student of the county school. That proves study and activity in Lishui, not birth there, so this entry enters him as "active in Lishui" and not as "born locally". Apart from being named in the biography section, the entry touches no other Lishui affair.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; neither township nor native place is recorded.

Second, "Tianshun" dates only his standing as a student; this entry converts nothing and infers no birth, death or active period from it.

Third, the route by which he entered the school roll is not recorded, and this entry supplies no examination or licentiate title.

Fourth, the nature of his mother's illness, the course of the cutting and its treatment, the year of her recovery and her later life are all unrecorded.

Fifth, the biography does not say whether he received an award; this entry does not infer one from other entries in this volume in which a district magistrate commended a filial son.

Sixth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
