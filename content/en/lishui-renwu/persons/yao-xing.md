Yao Xing is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him a courtesy name, the route of presentation, and two successive offices, and nothing else. This entry writes only those four items and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 姚行字本達。由儒士，任戶科給事中，累宦湖廣左參政。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

Most lines of this section give no courtesy name, and this is one of the few that does: his courtesy name is Benda, recorded here as the gazetteer words it. "By way of confucian scholars" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces it and does not convert it into "jinshi" or any other degree. One clause places a post in the Revenue section; the other says he held office in due course as a left provincial vice-commissioner of Huguang. "In due course" is the section's summarising word: in what years he served, through what ranks, and how many steps are nowhere recorded, so this entry supplies no intermediate offices and arranges no sequence. Neither post lay in Lishui. This block gives no years at all, so neither the year of presentation nor the years of either post can be known.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; both of his posts lay away from Lishui, one in a central section and one in Huguang, and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years and his native place are nowhere recorded; his courtesy name is known to this library only from this line.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and this block gives no years at all. His later service in Huguang falls after the period under which the section groups him, and nothing is inferred from that.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of confucian scholars", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "supervising secretary of the Revenue section" and "left provincial vice-commissioner of Huguang" are office titles whose duties and terms are unrecorded.

Fourth, the ranks and years behind "held office in due course" are unrecorded, and this entry supplies and orders none.

Fifth, the same block carries another line of the same surname and the same route but a different man, "Yao Jingzhong, courtesy name Keren, by way of confucian scholars, served as a censor-in-chief", for which this library has a separate entry. The section says nothing of any relationship between the two, and no kinship is decided here, so this entry declares no related link.

Sixth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Seventh, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
