Li Kezhi is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography records few acts but one unusual notice: an official from elsewhere who could not return home because of disorder and ended up settling in Lishui.

## Origin and term

> 李可埴，字夔龙，湖广善化人。由进士，天启二年任溧水。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Kuilong, a native of Shanhua in Huguang, entered as a *jinshi*, and was appointed to Lishui in the second year of the Tianqi era. The gazetteer writes only "appointed to Lishui" and gives no office title. The era year gives only his arrival; the later phrase "in the fifth year" is transcribed as it stands, its dating depending on the second year of Tianqi given above, and this entry neither converts it nor completes it into a named year.

## What the record says about his acts

**Style of government.**

> 赋性淳良，不为察察，与民相安。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"By nature honest and good, not given to sharp scrutiny, at peace with the people" is the gazetteer's judgement, naming no act.

**Military provisions, the tax conversion and his plea for exemption.**

> 至五年，以兵饷欲借改折暂征，公有伸免文移，甫行而丁艰去。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

In the fifth year, the gazetteer records, military provisions were about to be collected temporarily under the name of a tax conversion; Li Kezhi had documents pleading exemption, and no sooner had this begun than he left office for a parent's mourning. What the exemption achieved, how large the provision demand was, and what the "conversion" here refers to are all unrecorded.

**After the mourning period.**

> 服阕，补他县，惠政亦如在溧焉，后擢户部，升凤阳知府。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

After the mourning period ended he was appointed to another county, with beneficial policies the same as in Lishui, and later went to the Ministry of Revenue and was raised to prefect of Fengyang. None of this is dated, and which county he was sent to is not recorded.

**He settled in Lishui.**

> 时楚寇乱，不能归，以遗爱在溧水，遂家焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

At that time, the gazetteer records, there was disorder from banditry in Chu, and he could not return home; because the love he had left behind was in Lishui, he made his home there. "The love he left behind" is the gazetteer's retrospective term for his Lishui record, and "he made his home there" is its record of his settlement. Where he settled, when, and what became of his descendants are all unrecorded.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service, but his tie to the county has two layers: office, and settlement — the gazetteer records that disorder kept him from returning home and that, because the love he had left was in Lishui, he settled there. This is, within what this library knows, one case in this volume of an outside official taking up permanent residence in Lishui; the settlement layer reaches only the three characters "he made his home there", and this entry supplies nothing after it.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded.

Second, the year he left Lishui is unrecorded, only "no sooner had this begun than he left for mourning"; the era year gives only his arrival, and "in the fifth year" is kept as it stands, unconverted and uncompleted.

Third, his Lishui office is given only as "appointed to Lishui", with no precise title.

Fourth, the amount of the military provision demand, what the "conversion" here means, and the outcome of his plea for exemption are unrecorded.

Fifth, which county he took after mourning, and the dates of his Ministry of Revenue and Fengyang appointments, are unrecorded.

Sixth, what the disorder in Chu refers to and when it happened is unrecorded.

Seventh, the place and date of his settlement in Lishui and his descendants are unrecorded.

Eighth, "by nature honest and good", "beneficial policies the same as in Lishui" and "the love he left behind" are the gazetteer's judgements and retrospective terms, not verifiable records.

Ninth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
