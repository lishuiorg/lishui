Yao Jingzhong is one of the men presented from Lishui and served elsewhere listed in the "Ming, Hongwu period" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The section gives him four items — a courtesy name, the route of presentation, the office held, and a posthumous name — and nothing else. This entry writes only those four items and supplies no life for him.

## Route into office and office held

> 姚敬重字克仁。由儒士，任都御史。諡文林先生。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

Most lines of this section give no courtesy name, and this is one of the few that does: his courtesy name is Keren, recorded here as the gazetteer words it. "By way of confucian scholars" is the category of presentation as the gazetteer words it; this entry reproduces it and does not convert it into "jinshi" or any other degree. "Served as a censor-in-chief" is the office as the gazetteer words it — a bare title with no post, no year of taking it up and no year of leaving it, and this entry supplies no duties and no post. The section adds a posthumous name, "Master Wenlin", and this entry reproduces it; whether it was granted by the court or given privately, in what year, and on what grounds are nowhere recorded, so this entry neither decides the question nor attaches a year to it. This block gives no years at all, so neither the year of presentation nor the year of appointment can be known.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place and records no act of his in Lishui. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the only tie the section makes between him and Lishui is that he was presented from Lishui and served elsewhere — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui and held office away from it. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; his office lay away from Lishui and he held no Lishui post, so "official service" is not available; the section records no activity of his in Lishui, so "active in Lishui" is not available.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years and his native place are nowhere recorded; his courtesy name is known to this library only from this line.

Second, the era value "Ming" here only records that the section places him in its "Ming, Hongwu period" block. That grouping dates his presentation and service, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Hongwu era nor that he was active in any particular Hongwu year, and this block gives no years at all.

Third, his route into office is given only as "by way of confucian scholars", which must not be rewritten as "jinshi" or any other degree; "censor-in-chief" is a bare office title, and its duties, post and term are unrecorded.

Fourth, the nature and date of the posthumous name are unrecorded: whether it was granted by the court or given privately, in what year, and for what reason cannot be decided from this library's material. Whether the name "Master Wenlin" has any connection with rank names of the kind "Wenlin lang" found elsewhere in this library is not decided here.

Fifth, whether he had a house, descendants or remains in Lishui is nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide it.

Sixth, this entry rests on one roster line in the Hongwu block; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
