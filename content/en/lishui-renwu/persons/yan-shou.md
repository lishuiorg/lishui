Yan Shou is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography gives his origin, his term, his manner of governing and the reason he left — but records no affair in any detail. This entry records it as it stands, and keeps judgement as judgement.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 燕寿，字本仁，咸宁人。成化间由举人任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Benren, a native of Xianning, reached by the *juren* degree, and appointed "during the Chenghua era". The gazetteer gives no definite year, and this entry keeps its wording as an era span: nothing is converted into a Common Era year and no year is inferred from the dynasty. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui. The gazetteer also never gives his title in Lishui, and this entry does not assume that of county magistrate.

## What the gazetteer records

> 临下以庄，退居无惰容，簿书秩然，政平讼理，吏不能欺，民鲜不法。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

All six clauses are judgement: dignity toward those below him, no slack countenance in private retirement, records in good order, administration balanced and suits rightly settled, clerks unable to practise deception, and the people seldom breaking the law. The biography records no building, no fiscal measure, no school measure and no case in Lishui; "records in good order" speaks only of the handling of documents and names no register or book, and nothing is inferred from the judgement.

> 后丁艰去，众深惜之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"He later left on a mourning obligation" is the gazetteer's statement of why his term ended. For whose mourning, and in which year, are nowhere recorded; this entry supplies no year and does not describe the institution behind the phrase. "The crowd deeply lamented it" is the gazetteer's report of opinion at his departure, a narration rather than a verifiable record.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the gazetteer says "a native of Xianning" — and enters this library through official service. Three things tie him to the county: his appointment "during the Chenghua era" by way of the *juren* degree; the six clauses on his manner of governing; and the opinion reported at his departure. The tag "Education and culture" answers only to the *juren* origin the gazetteer records; the biography records no school building, school administration or educational installation in Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is given only as Xianning.

Second, precision of the term: the gazetteer says only "during the Chenghua era", gives no definite year, and this entry neither converts it nor infers a year from the dynasty.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate.

Fourth, for whom he kept the mourning obligation and in what year is nowhere recorded, so the year he left office cannot be fixed.

Fifth, the six clauses and "the crowd deeply lamented it" are the gazetteer's judgement and reported opinion, and the acts behind them are nowhere recorded.

Sixth, what registers and books "records in good order" refers to is not recorded.

Seventh, any concrete act in Lishui, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Eighth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
