Zhu Xudeng is one of the two Song figures in the category of loyal martyrs in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His entry is a single paragraph: the first half records his own examination, his office and the manner of his death; the second half looks back to the filial piety of his great-grandfather and to the degrees of his descendants; and it closes with the compiler's own judgement. This entry keeps those layers apart — his own career in one section, his family and descendants in another, and the closing remark marked as the gazetteer's comment rather than as fact.

**Note on the identifier of this entry.** When it was first built, the slug and id were written as `zhu-wengdeng`, the character 虙 being misread as *weng*. That character is read **xū**, so on 2026-10-03 the entry was moved to `ls:person:zhu-xudeng` and the file renamed to `zhu-xudeng.md`. The old id `ls:person:zhu-wengdeng` is withdrawn at once and must not be referred to by any entry. Not one character of the entry's content was altered by the move.

## Life

> 朱虙登，政和八年進士，建（延）［炎］二年官左從事郎、潭州瀏陽令，四年軍賊杜彥等陷瀏陽，虙力戰三日死之。湖南安撫司聞諸朝，贈通直郎，與其子柔嘉恩澤，因嘉歷仕，贈至大中大夫。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·忠节传

On his examinations: the gazetteer records that he "passed the metropolitan examination in the eighth year of the Zhenghe era". The era name is quoted as it stands; this entry converts it to no year of the common era and infers no birth year from it.

On his offices: the gazetteer records that "in the second year of the Jianyan era he held the posts of Left Attending Gentleman and magistrate of Liuyang in Tanzhou". The era name here carries the editorial brackets "[...]" of two readings, which this entry reproduces without alteration and without choosing between them. "Left Attending Gentleman" and "magistrate of Liuyang" are the two office titles the gazetteer gives; their functions, the seat of each, and the length of each tenure are recorded nowhere. It should be noted that this office was at Liuyang in Tanzhou, not at Lishui.

On his death: the gazetteer records that "when the military rebels Du Yan and others took Liuyang in the fourth year, Zhu fought hard for three days and died". The "fourth year" follows on from the era name above and the gazetteer does not repeat it; this entry quotes "the fourth year" as it stands, converts it to no year of the common era, and does not settle which year exactly is meant. Of Du Yan the gazetteer's only words are "the military rebel Du Yan and others": it does not say whose command he served under. "Fought hard for three days" is the length of the fighting as recorded; what followed for him after the third day, and what became of his body, are not recorded.

After his death: the gazetteer records that "the Hunan pacification office reported it to the court, which bestowed on him the rank of Tongzhi Lang" — that is, the office memorialized the matter and the court granted him a posthumous rank. This is an honour after his death, not an office he held in life. The words that follow, "and with his son Roujia received favour, and because Roujia served in office, was granted as far as the rank of Dazhong Dafu", are quoted here exactly as they stand: the punctuation of the sentence, who petitioned for which grant, and whether Roujia was his son or stood in some other relation are all left unmarked by the gazetteer, so this entry neither repunctuates nor decides, and does not rewrite the preceding clause in the light of it. This library holds no other material on Roujia.

Native place and dates: the entry records no native place for Zhu Xudeng, no courtesy name or style name, and no year of birth or death.

## The family and the descendants recorded in the gazetteer

Everything in this section is the gazetteer's retrospective account of Zhu Xudeng's family and his descendants, not matter of his own career.

> 三世孫紹遠、明遠，四世孫用泰、棟俱擢科第，五世孫立之、祐之以《易》學授徒於家。虙高祖壽昌，揚州天長人，七歲失母，行求五十年，得母完聚。母卒，泣涕喪明，有白烏集其墓，天下皆知其孝。仕至司農少卿。嗚呼！虙之忠節蓋有源矣。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·忠节传

The degrees and the teaching of his descendants: the gazetteer records that his third-generation descendants Shaoyuan and Mingyuan, and his fourth-generation descendants Yongtai and Dong, "were all advanced to degree in the examinations", and that his fifth-generation descendants Lizhi and Youzhi "taught the Study of the Changes to pupils at home". These are the degrees and the learning of his descendants. They are not Zhu Xudeng's own examinations — his own is the "eighth year of the Zhenghe era" given in the Life section, and the two must not be run together. "Advanced to degree" records admission to office by examination; the gazetteer gives no year, no subject of examination and no placing. "Taught the Study of the Changes to pupils at home" records teaching the Yijing at home; the gazetteer records neither his pupils nor the substance of his teaching nor where the home was.

His great-grandfather Shouchang: "Zhu's great-grandfather Shouchang, a native of Tianchang in Yangzhou". Shouchang was his great-grandfather, and his native place was Tianchang in Yangzhou. He lost his mother at seven, sought her for fifty years and was reunited with her; after her death he wept until his sight failed, and a white crow gathered at his grave, whereupon the gazetteer says "all under heaven knew his filial piety"; he rose as far as deputy director of the bureau of agriculture. On the gazetteer's own layering, what belongs to Shouchang is family matter. It is **not** Zhu Xudeng's filial piety, and this entry does not mix it into his own conduct or offer it as evidence of his own virtue.

One point must be marked explicitly: the closing sentence, "Alas! Zhu's loyalty had its source", is the gazetteer's own judgement, and is marked here as **the gazetteer's comment, not as fact**. Its sense is to trace Zhu Xudeng's loyalty back to the filial piety of his family and the degrees of his descendants; but "had its source" is a remark by the compiler, not a verifiable fact. This entry does not use it to establish what the family or the descendants were in fact like, nor treats it as a tribute to Zhu Xudeng himself among the facts.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is left empty here, and the reason must be stated. As to Zhu Xudeng himself the gazetteer records three things: that he passed the examination in the eighth year of the Zhenghe era, that he held the office of magistrate of Liuyang in Tanzhou, and that he died after fighting for three days at Liuyang. All three belong to Liuyang in Tanzhou and not to Lishui. It records no office, no posting, no residence, no shrine and no descendants' service in Lishui, and no intercourse with anyone there. Of the six values available, "native of Lishui" would require the gazetteer to record his birth at Lishui, which it does not; "official service" would require his office to have been at Lishui, whereas it was at Tanzhou, which it does not support; and "writings", "sojourn", "recommended from Lishui" and "active in Lishui" have nothing in the gazetteer to rest on. This library therefore holds no material to determine his specific relation with Lishui, and `relation` is not filled. This section supplies, as the site's validation rules require, the ground on which he enters this library: the gazetteer's category of loyal martyrs in juan 6 lists Zhu Xudeng among the two Song figures there, and that is the sole basis on which he is included.

One further point, to prevent a misreading. The gazetteer records his great-grandfather Shouchang as "a native of Tianchang in Yangzhou". That is Shouchang's native place, not Zhu Xudeng's, and this entry does not treat Zhu Xudeng's relation with Lishui as established on the strength of his great-grandfather's origin. Nor does the gazetteer record any relation between Shouchang and Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unknown. The gazetteer gives his examination in the eighth year of the Zhenghe era, his appointment in a year of the Jianyan era and his death in "the fourth year", all in era names or in a bare ordinal; this entry converts none of them into a year of the common era and infers no birth or death year from the examination or the appointment.

Second, his native place is unknown. The entry records none; "a native of Tianchang in Yangzhou" is his great-grandfather's native place, not his own. Whether he too was from Tianchang or from elsewhere is not recorded, and this entry does not infer it.

Third, the two readings of the era name "Jian (Yan) [Yan]". The gazetteer gives two readings at this point; this entry reproduces them unchanged, altering no character and choosing neither, and the "fourth year" that follows is quoted in the gazetteer's own order without being attached to an era name of this entry's own.

Fourth, the punctuation and the reference of the sentence "and with his son Roujia received favour, and because Roujia served in office, was granted as far as the rank of Dazhong Dafu". The words are quoted exactly; this entry neither repunctuates nor decides, and the gazetteer leaves unmarked whether Roujia was his son or stood in another relation, and on whose petition the grant was made.

Fifth, the scope of "were all advanced to degree". The phrase follows immediately on "his fourth-generation descendants Yongtai and Dong", and the gazetteer does not mark whether it also covers the third-generation descendants Shaoyuan and Mingyuan named above. This entry does not decide.

Sixth, the filial piety of his great-grandfather Shouchang and the degrees and teaching of his descendants are the gazetteer's retrospective account of his family and descendants, not matters of Zhu Xudeng's own life; this entry has set them in a separate section, and no reader may take this entry to say that Zhu Xudeng himself lost his mother at seven or sought her for fifty years.

Seventh, the closing sentence "Alas! Zhu's loyalty had its source" is the gazetteer's comment, marked as such and not entered as fact.

Eighth, whether he had a house, a shrine or any remains in Lishui, and whether his descendants had any relation with Lishui, is recorded nowhere.
