Li Chaozheng is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is longer than most Song entries in the same volume, recording two recommendations and one audience with the throne. Yet what he actually did in Lishui is present only as another man's retrospective remark inside a recommendation; the rest is praise and a list of ranks. This entry transcribes each part by its source and marks every recommendation and judgement as such.

## Name and origin

> 李朝正，字治表。由太学登第，历敕令所删订官，绍兴间出知溧水县。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Zhibiao, took his degree from the Imperial Academy, held in turn an editing office in the imperial edict-and-statute bureau, and was out-appointed to know Lishui County in the Shaoxing period. The gazetteer gives no native place. "In the Shaoxing period" gives a span, not the year of arrival.

## What the record says about his Lishui term

> 有异政，秩满民泣留。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"He left extraordinary administration" is the gazetteer's general judgement; "when his term ended the people wept and tried to keep him" is its account of the people's reaction. Neither states what he did or how the weeping happened, and this entry supplies no concrete act.

## The recommendation of Ye Mengde and the audience

> 叶梦得荐于朝，高宗曰：『近时县令以政绩被荐，辄别除差遣，莫若进秩久任之，庶几民安其政。』乃召对，迁一官，赐五品服遣还。陛辞，乞易所得章服封母，从之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The words attributed to Emperor Gaozong are quoted as the gazetteer reports them, without paraphrase or extension. The audience, the promotion of one rank, the grant of fifth-rank robes and the sending back are strung together in sequence with no dates. His request at takeleave — to exchange the robes he had received for a rank conferral on his mother — is recorded together with its granting, but the title his mother received is not given.

## The recommendation of Wang Fu and tax equalisation

> 户部侍郎王复荐之，言朝正昨任溧水县，曾措置均税，简易不扰，至今并无词诉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

This is the only sentence in the biography that touches a concrete matter in Lishui, and it is the words of a recommender, Wang Fu, Vice Minister of Revenue. It is recorded here as his claim. What the equalisation involved, what "simple and undemanding" rested on, and what period and scope "to this day there have been no complaints" covers are all unrecorded; "to this day" is the moment of the recommendation, and this entry does not use it to state anything about Lishui's later fiscal condition.

## After Lishui

> 除太府寺簿，累官至户部侍郎，奉祀，调知平江府。卒年六十，官至朝奉大夫。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Four later posts are listed, none dated. What the "sacrificial office" was and at which temple or palace, and the year of his transfer to know Pingjiang Prefecture, are not recorded. "He died aged sixty" gives an age only, not a year or a place.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. Two passages tie him to the county: his out-appointment and the remark at the end of his term, and Wang Fu's retrospective account of his tax equalisation there. The second is the only content-bearing record this library has of his work in Lishui, and it is a recommender's sentence.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unknown. The gazetteer says only that he died aged sixty, giving neither year nor place; his birth year cannot be derived from his age at death.

Second, no native place is recorded.

Third, the precision of his dates is low. "In the Shaoxing period" gives no year for his arrival, and the year he left Lishui is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing from the dynasty.

Fourth, the year of his academy degree and the dates of his editing office are not recorded.

Fifth, the content of the "extraordinary administration" is not recorded, and this entry does not guess at it.

Sixth, the dates of Ye Mengde's recommendation, the audience, the promotion and the grant of robes are not recorded.

Seventh, the method and year of the tax equalisation are unrecorded; the matter appears only in a recommendation, never in the gazetteer's own voice.

Eighth, the title conferred on his mother and anything following that request are not recorded.

Ninth, the meaning and location of the sacrificial office, the year of the transfer to Pingjiang, and the date of the court rank he reached at the end are not recorded.

Tenth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
