Su Kai is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography has one sentence of origin, one of appointment, one clause of judgement and one sentence about the summons that ended his term, and records no particular measure of his in Lishui. This entry writes only what it contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 苏楷，字彦平。隆兴元年任，强明有吏术，诸司列荐，召知江阴军。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was courtesy-named Yanping and appointed to Lishui in the first year of the Longxing era. The gazetteer gives no native place and no account of how he entered office. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Longxing year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

The only judgement of his career is the clause "strong, clear and skilled in the craft of an official", which praises his firmness, his clarity and his competence as an administrator; it cites no act and records no measure. "The various offices recommended him in turn" is the gazetteer's narrative: it does not say which offices recommended him, for what, or in which years. "He was summoned to be *zhi Jiangyin jun*" is the only sentence in the biography that gives him a destination; the title is the gazetteer's own wording, reproduced here as a transliteration, and neither the year of the summons nor anything after it is recorded. This entry infers no act in Lishui from the judgements.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records no native place for him, and this entry enters him through official service. What ties him to the county is the sentence giving his appointment, the judgement on his conduct, and the narrative of the recommendations; nothing he did in Lishui is recorded, and the summons that follows already lies outside his Lishui term.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, and neither is his native place.

Second, the appointment is given only as the first Longxing year, which is the year of arrival; neither the year he left nor the year of the summons is recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, which offices recommended him and for what, and the grounds for the judgement on his skill, are all unrecorded.

Fifth, *zhi Jiangyin jun* is the gazetteer's wording and this entry makes no guess about its formal name; his later career, final office and place of death are unrecorded.

Sixth, another Song entry in this volume — Li Heng, held separately in this library — is also given the courtesy name Yanping. The gazetteer says nothing about any relationship between the two, and this entry neither infers one nor merges them.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him.
