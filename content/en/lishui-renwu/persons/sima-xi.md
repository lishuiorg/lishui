Sima Xi is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography has one sentence of origin, one of appointment and one clause of judgement, and records no particular measure of his in Lishui. This entry writes only what it contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 司马僖，字季和，温公光之后。淳熙三年任，明敏有才，为政不苛。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was courtesy-named Jihe and is called "a descendant of Wengong Guang": here the gazetteer refers to his ancestor by an honorific and gives only the given name, supplying no surname, and this entry reproduces the wording as transcribed without supplying a surname, recasting the honorific, or recounting any deeds. No father is recorded. The gazetteer also gives no native place for Sima Xi himself and no account of how he entered office. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Chunxi year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

The only thing said of his career is the clause "quick, sensitive and talented, governing without harshness": the first part judges his gifts, the second his manner of government. The sentence cites no act and records no measure; this entry has no deeds to report because the source contains none, not because any have been left out, and infers no building, no case and no tax matter from the judgements.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records no native place for him, and this entry enters him through official service. What ties him to the county is the sentence giving his appointment and the judgement on his government; nothing he did in Lishui is recorded beyond that, and neither his leaving nor his later movements are given.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, and neither is his own native place.

Second, the appointment is given only as the third Chunxi year, which is the year of arrival; the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, the gazetteer supplies no surname for the ancestor it calls "Wengong Guang"; that ancestor's surname and career, and the details of Sima Xi's own lineage including his father, are all unrecorded, and this entry supplies none.

Fifth, "quick, sensitive and talented, governing without harshness" is the gazetteer's judgement, and the grounds for it are unrecorded.

Sixth, any concrete acts in Lishui, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him.
