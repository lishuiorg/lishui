Cen Zhonglin is an entry in the Tang part of the notable-officials biography in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is three sentences long, and this entry goes no further than they do.

## Origin and term

> 岑仲林，邓州人，中书令文本之孙，为令有政绩。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was a native of Dengzhou. The gazetteer calls him "the grandson of Wenben, the Zhongshu Ling": it gives his grandfather's given name and his office but supplies no surname for him, and it records no father of his own. His office is given only as "ling"; the gazetteer never states the formal title of the post he held in Lishui, and it records no year of appointment.

## What the gazetteer records

> 时兄羲令金坛，仲翔令长洲，皆有声，宰相宗楚客嘱监察御史曰『勿遗江东三岑』。仲林居溧水尤见表树，不愧家风。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

His brother Xi is recorded as magistrate of Jintan and one Zhongxiang as magistrate of Changzhou; all three had a reputation, and the chancellor Zong Chuke accordingly charged a censor "not to overlook the three Cens of Jiangdong". "The three Cens of Jiangdong" is the gazetteer's own phrase, and this library holds no other source to set beside it. The closing words — that in Lishui he "stood out most of all" and "did not fall short of the family tradition" — are the gazetteer's judgement rather than a record of fact, and are quoted as such.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in Lishui with the words "Zhonglin resided in Lishui", and this entry records him through official service. Beyond these three sentences the volume records no particular act of his in Lishui: "had meritorious results as ling" and "stood out most of all" are general assessments that point to no specific deed.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, the year of his appointment is not recorded, and this entry does not infer one from the dynasty.

Third, the gazetteer gives his office only as "ling" and never states the formal title of his Lishui post; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, the biography names "his brother Xi" and "Zhongxiang" without fixing Zhongxiang's place in the birth order or supplying his surname; this entry does not speculate.

Fifth, the name "the three Cens of Jiangdong" and the words attributed to Zong Chuke appear only in this biography, with no second source in this library to check them against.

Sixth, this volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
