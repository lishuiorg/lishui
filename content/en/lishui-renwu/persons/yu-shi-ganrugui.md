Madam Yu is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" (zhenlie zhuan) of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer. The gazetteer names her as the wife of the licentiate Gan Rugui and records her resolve through a question and an answer, closing with an official commendation. This entry rests on that single notice.

## Gazetteer text

> 俞氏，茂才甘如桂妻。如桂死，俞尚少，或謂，「姑老子幼，奈何？」俞泣曰：「此未亡人責也。」卒能勤苦完其志。萬曆間監司獎其廬，博士丁永曉為之序。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of the licentiate Gan Rugui" gives the husband's and wife's status. "Maocai" is an alternative term for a licentiate or county-school student; the gazetteer uses it here for Gan Rugui, and this entry keeps the wording rather than replacing it with another term. Whether Gan Rugui appears elsewhere in the gazetteer this library has not established.

Second, "when Rugui died, Yu was still young" records that she was young at her husband's death; her exact age is not given.

Third, "someone asked, 'with your mother-in-law old and your children young, what will you do?'" is a question attributed by the gazetteer to others. The text frames it only as a question and never says remarriage was urged, so this entry does not add such words.

Fourth, "Yu wept and said, 'this is a widow's duty'" is her recorded reply. "The bereaved" (weiwangren) is her self-designation, and the sense is that keeping chastity and raising the orphans was her own obligation. This is the gazetteer's report of her speech.

Fifth, "in the end she was able, through hardship, to fulfil her resolve" is the gazetteer's summary. No deed is given, and this entry draws no incident from the phrase.

Sixth, "in the Wanli era a provincial officer honoured her gate, and the instructor Ding Yongxiao wrote a preface for her" records those who honoured her. "Provincial officer" and "instructor" are titles; the provincial officer is unnamed, while the instructor is named Ding Yongxiao. The preface itself has not been obtained.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Gan Rugui, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity and the honoured gate belong to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "still young" describes only her age at her husband's death and is not an exact figure.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the fuller life and death year of her husband Gan Rugui are unrecorded, as are the number and fate of any children.

Fourth, the words attributed to the questioner are a report of others' speech, not an established fact; this entry quotes them and marks them as such.

Fifth, the only commendation recorded is the honouring of her gate by a provincial officer, with Ding Yongxiao's preface; whether there were other commendations, and how many and when, is not recorded.

Sixth, the provincial officer is unnamed, and Ding Yongxiao cannot be traced in this library; his preface has not been obtained.

Seventh, "in the Wanli era" dates only the commendation; this entry does not convert it to a Western year or infer the length of her widowhood from it.

Eighth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
