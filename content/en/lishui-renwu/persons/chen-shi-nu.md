Miss Chen of Wushan is one of the martyred women of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer calls her only "the Chen daughter", places her at Wushan, and records that her father was a tenant of the Marquis of Xining, with a single episode: throwing herself into a well while being carried off by soldiers. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 陳氏女，烏山人。父為西寧侯莊戶。女年十五，已字人，未適。兵過其家，欲殺其父，索金，女跪泣救父，兵舍父，挾之去。行經一村，遙見一井，詐言口渴，兵亦渴，欲得水飲。入村屋取汲具，女投井死。兵出，失女所在，追屋後，遍覓不得，始汲水，則女屍與汲桶觸，兵知已死，碎桶而去。村人出，見井有碎桶，視井得女，不知為何許人。女父尋至，乃知井主即夫家，泣曰：「女已于歸矣。痛哉！」奇也。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item is one of the longer martyrdom narratives in this volume. Taken sentence by sentence:

"Miss Chen of Wushan" gives her natal surname Chen and the style "a certain daughter", marking her as unmarried, and records her residence as Wushan. Wushan is one of the Lishui place names seen in this volume; this entry keeps the text's wording, and this library has no material linking it to a present-day settlement.

"Her father was a tenant of the Marquis of Xining" records her father's status. Who the Marquis of Xining was is not stated, and this entry supplies nothing; "tenant" is the household status the gazetteer records, kept as such.

"At fifteen she was betrothed but not yet married" gives her age and betrothal: at fifteen she had been promised in marriage but had not yet gone to her husband's house. "Betrothed" means the engagement, "married" means the wedding, and the text's "not yet married" marks her as still at home.

"Soldiers came to her house to kill her father and demand gold; the girl knelt and wept to save her father, and the soldiers left the father and carried her off" opens the episode: soldiers passing her house meant to kill her father and demand gold, and her kneeling and weeping won his release, while she was taken away.

"On the road, at a village, she saw a well in the distance and feigned thirst; the soldiers too were thirsty and wanted a drink. As they went into a village house for a bucket, the girl threw herself into the well and died" records her ruse and her death: she pretended thirst, the soldiers wanted water, and while they fetched a bucket she threw herself in. "Feigned" marks her cunning, part of the recorded deed.

"The soldiers came out, missed her, searched behind the house, found nothing, and only when they drew water did the corpse touch the bucket; knowing her dead, they smashed the bucket and left" records the discovery.

"The villagers came out, saw the broken bucket at the well, looked in and found the girl, not knowing who she was. Her father came searching, and only then knew that the well belonged to her husband's family" records that the well she had chosen stood on her husband's family's ground.

"He wept and said, 'Our daughter is already gone to her husband's house. How bitter!'" is the father's lament, reproduced as such. It is reported speech, not a verifiable record.

"Strange indeed" is the gazetteer's closing judgement, admiring the strangeness of the affair, not a factual statement.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her deed in Lishui: a Wushan daughter who threw herself into a well while being carried off, her father a tenant of the Marquis of Xining, and the well itself on her husband's family's ground. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "the Chen daughter", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of a local calamity, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer records only that she was fifteen and died by the well; it gives no year of birth or death and no year for the disorder, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "of Wushan" and her father a tenant of the Marquis of Xining; the family's long residence and native place are not stated, and this entry does not infer a county from the name Wushan.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives no husband's name, and "the well belonged to her husband's family" says only where the well stood; who her husband was and whether they had children are unrecorded.

Fourth, who the Marquis of Xining was, and her father's name, are not stated, and this entry supplies nothing from other volumes or other books.

Fifth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award after death, and this entry infers none from other items.

Sixth, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Seventh, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
