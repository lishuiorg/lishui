Madam Duan is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Ding Jia. Hers is one of the notices in the Ming section that records a son's entry into office. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 端氏，丁檟妻。年二十三夫死，子在襁褓，立志撫孤，教子煒歷仕，貞操六十餘載。直指屢旌獎之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Ding Jia" names husband and wife.

Second, "her husband died when she was twenty-three, leaving a son in swaddling clothes, and she resolved to raise the orphan."

Third, "she taught her son Wei, who held office, and kept her chastity for over sixty years." "Wei" is the son's name as the gazetteer gives it; "held office" summarises his career without naming posts.

Fourth, "the circuit censor repeatedly honoured her." The number and dates of the honours are not given.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Ding Jia, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her teaching of her son and the honours she received attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings of her own, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty-three" and "over sixty years" are the gazetteer's figures.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Ding Jia are unrecorded.

Fourth, her son Ding Wei's career beyond "held office" is unrecorded, with no posts or dates given.

Fifth, the repeated honours by the circuit censor give no number, year or tablet inscription.

Sixth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
