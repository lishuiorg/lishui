Zhang Ge is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is one of the shortest in the volume: one sentence of appointment, one clause of judgement, and nothing else. This entry writes only what that entry contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 张革，大观二年任。才而有操，以强明清白称。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui in the second year of the Daiguan era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Daiguan year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

The only thing said of his career is the clause "talented and principled, known for strength, clarity, purity and uprightness". The first part judges his ability and his conduct; the second reports the reputation he bore, and the verb "known for" is itself the gazetteer's own statement. The whole clause is the gazetteer's judgement, not a verifiable record. It cites no act and records no measure: this entry has no deeds to report because the source contains none, not because any have been left out.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of "appointed in the second Daiguan year", and this entry records him through official service. The volume records nothing he did in Lishui, no year of leaving, and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as the second Daiguan year, which is the year of arrival; the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, "talented and principled, known for strength, clarity, purity and uprightness" is the gazetteer's judgement and report of reputation, and the grounds for it are unrecorded.

Fifth, any concrete acts in Lishui, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
