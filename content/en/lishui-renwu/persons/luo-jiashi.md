Luo Jiashi is one of the Qing officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is the longest of the four Qing entries in this volume: first his successful resistance to a stationed garrison, then his imprisonment and execution after banditry in a neighbouring area, both closed with the people's reaction.

## Origin and term

> 罗佳士，字□□，北京□□人。由贡士，顺治二年任。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He entered as a tribute student and was appointed to Lishui in the second year of the Shunzhi era. His courtesy name and the exact locality of his native place appear in the transcription as blank squares; this entry keeps them exactly as they stand and supplies no character. The gazetteer writes only "appointed" and gives no office title.

**"Our dynasty" means the Qing.** This volume lists Luo Jiashi together with Yang Guozhen, Wang Dingyin and An Yingsui under the heading "our dynasty". The gazetteer was written by men of the Kangxi period, who called the Qing "our dynasty"; this entry treats him as a Qing official and notes the original wording.

## What the record says about his acts

**Refusing a garrison.**

> 时以新制，各县俱欲设镇兵以防土贼，公居此深察溧民淳良，既以归服可无用兵马，恐骚动百姓。力申上，勿烦设镇，从之，民大欢戴。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Under the new order, the gazetteer records, every county wanted a garrison of stationed troops to guard against local bandits. Luo Jiashi, having closely observed in his post that the people of Lishui were honest and good, held that since they had submitted there was no need for troops, and feared that a garrison would disturb the people; he pressed the matter upward and asked that no garrison be imposed, and this was granted. "The people greatly rejoiced and supported him" is the gazetteer's account of popular reaction. What the "new order" was and how such garrisons were organised are unrecorded.

**The yichou winter, imprisonment and execution.**

> 乙酉冬，适邻境土贼煽乱，公加防守，甫欲申报上，而贼忽焚掠而去。公挟印造府，亲道所由，各上台俱察非其过，独操台有苛责。下狱经年，意有他也，公付之不（辨）［辩］，终置于法，民尽悲之。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

In a *yichou* winter — the cyclical date is left unconverted — bandits in a neighbouring area stirred up disorder; he strengthened the defence, and no sooner was he about to report upward than the bandits suddenly burned, plundered and left. He took the county seal and went to the prefecture, stating the course of events in person; the higher authorities all judged that this was not his fault, and only the "cao tai" found harsh fault with him — the gazetteer writes only that term and does not explain which office is meant, and this entry supplies nothing. He was then imprisoned for a year. "Some other reason was at work" is the gazetteer's surmise; it records that he left the matter without defence — the transcription here carries its own collation bracket, a wrong character in round brackets and the corrected one in square brackets, reproduced as it stands, and the surrounding text follows the corrected character — and that he was finally executed by law. "The people all grieved for him" is the gazetteer's account of popular reaction; the charge and the year of the execution are unrecorded.

**The gazetteer's closing judgement.**

> 然却兵安民之心，自昭日月也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Yet his heart in turning troops away and settling the people shines as clearly as the sun and moon" is the gazetteer's closing assessment, recorded as its judgement, not as fact.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native — the exact locality of his origin is left blank in the transcription — and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his appointment in the second year of the Shunzhi era, his successful resistance to a stationed garrison, his imprisonment and execution, and the two reported popular reactions: great joy when the garrison was called off, and universal grief at his end. His imprisonment and execution are an unusual kind of notice among the biographies of this volume: a Lishui official imprisoned and executed by law over responsibility for banditry next door.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unrecorded; so are the year and the charge of his execution.

Second, his courtesy name and the exact locality of his native place appear as blank squares in the transcription; they are kept as they stand and nothing is supplied.

Third, the year he left office is unrecorded; the era year gives only his arrival, and nothing is converted.

Fourth, his Lishui office is given only as "appointed", with no precise title.

Fifth, what the "new order" was and how garrisons were organised are unrecorded.

Sixth, the cyclical date of the winter in question is left unconverted, and is not used to infer the length of his term.

Seventh, which office the "cao tai" is, and which offices the "higher authorities" were, are unrecorded.

Eighth, the beginning and end of his year of imprisonment and the charge against him are unrecorded; "some other reason was at work" is the gazetteer's surmise, not a verifiable fact.

Ninth, "the people greatly rejoiced", "the people all grieved" and the closing comparison to the sun and moon are the gazetteer's reported reactions and judgement.

Tenth, this volume lists him under "our dynasty", meaning the Qing; the original wording is noted above.

Eleventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, which is an online transcription not checked against the punctuated edition or the paper book; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
