Madam Ding is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of the shengji official Chen Yunkun. Hers is the only notice in the Ming section honoured for both chastity and filial piety. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 丁氏，省祭陳雲鵾妻。未字剜股愈母，既適刲肱救夫。年二十五守節撫子，勤苦機杼以佐其讀。萬曆四十三年撫按旌其閭曰「節孝兩全」。子道上，亦蚤亡，媳丁氏，年二十，以遺腹守節，竭力事姑，鄉閭以孝稱。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of the shengji official Chen Yunkun" names husband and wife; "shengji" is the office-title the gazetteer gives for Chen Yunkun, reproduced without change.

Second, "before marriage she cut flesh from her thigh to cure her mother; after marriage she cut flesh from her arm to save her husband." These acts of self-mutilation are recorded as the gazetteer tells them, and this entry reproduces them without any medical or factual judgement.

Third, "widowed at twenty-five, she raised her son and toiled at the loom to support his studies."

Fourth, "in the forty-third year of Wanli the governor and censor honoured her gate as 'Both Chaste and Filial.'" The offices, reign year and gate-name are reproduced as given; the reign year is not converted.

Fifth, "her son Daoshang also died young; her daughter-in-law, Madam Ding, aged twenty, kept her chastity with an unborn child, served her mother-in-law with all her strength, and was praised in the neighbourhood for filial piety." This is a supplement; the daughter-in-law is given no further detail and is not made a separate entry here.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Chen Yunkun, whose household was her place of residence; her acts of self-mutilation, her chastity and the honour to her gate all attach to the Chen family in Lishui. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty-five" dates only the beginning of her widowhood.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, "shengji" is the office-title given for Chen Yunkun, whose meaning, career and death year are unrecorded.

Fourth, the cutting of thigh and arm are recorded as the gazetteer tells them; the illnesses, times and circumstances are not detailed, and this entry marks them as the gazetteer's report.

Fifth, her son Chen Daoshang died young, with no further detail, and the daughter-in-law's chastity is only a supplement; no separate entry is made here.

Sixth, the forty-third year of Wanli dates only the honour; no conferring authority or tablet inscription is given, and the number is not stated.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
