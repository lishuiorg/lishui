Dou Shuxiang is an entry in the Tang part of the notable-officials biography in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. Among the Tang officials in this volume, his Lishui post is the one the gazetteer states most plainly.

## Origin and term

> 窦叔向，金城人。以左拾遗知溧水，优于治，善属文。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was a native of Jincheng. The gazetteer states his post outright: he "knew Lishui" while holding the office of Left Remonstrator. Whether the remonstratorship was the office he held at the time of coming to Lishui or an earlier one is not stated. No year of appointment is recorded. "Better than average in governance" and "skilful at writing" are the gazetteer's assessments, quoted as such.

## What the gazetteer records

> 其子常、牟、群、庠、巩五人，咸秉训诲，成显宦，而叔向之政独著。世以五窦称，叔向实先之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

Five sons are named — Chang, Mou, Qun, Xiang and Gong — all said to have taken their father's instruction and become distinguished officials; the gazetteer records no office and no later career for any of them. "The age calls them the five Dous, and Shuxiang was in fact the first of them" is the gazetteer's report of a contemporary name and of his place within it, recorded as such. The statement that "his administration stood out on its own" says his governance was the most conspicuous in the family but cites no particular act.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer states his Lishui post directly, and this entry records him through official service. The volume says only that his government of Lishui stood out; it records no specific act there. What fills the biography is his family's distinction — five sons as distinguished officials, and the contemporary name for the family — not his deeds in Lishui.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded.

Second, the year of his appointment is not recorded, and this entry does not infer one from the dynasty.

Third, the relation between "Left Remonstrator" and "knowing Lishui" — offices held at the same time or in sequence — is not stated.

Fourth, the offices, examination years and later careers of the five sons are recorded nowhere in this biography, and this entry supplies none.

Fifth, "the five Dous" is the gazetteer's report of a contemporary name; this library holds no second source to check it against.

Sixth, this volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
