The Chaste Widow Duan is one of the virtuous women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer; the notice opens by saying her surname is lost. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 端烈婦，逸其氏。嫁月余夫外賈，紡織養翁姑，八年無間。夫病癩歸，令婦他適，婦具酒食獻翁姑，曰：「今當去，願以為別。」舉家信之，夜遂投水死。視其屍，衣皆密縫。嗚呼！斷臂截耳，光耀史冊，以此較之，又何如耶？
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, the notice is headed "the chaste widow Duan" yet opens by saying her surname is lost. "Duan" is the surname the gazetteer uses, while "her surname is lost" records that her name is not preserved; whether Duan is her own or her husband's surname the text does not say, so this entry keeps the heading without deciding (see Points of doubt).

Second, "a month after marrying in her husband went away to trade, and she wove to support his parents without pause for eight years."

Third, "her husband returned stricken with leprosy and bade her remarry" (literally "go to another").

Fourth, "she prepared food and drink for his parents, saying 'I must go now; let this be our farewell'; the household believed her, and that night she drowned herself."

Fifth, "when her corpse was examined, its clothes were all tightly sewn" — a detail recorded by the gazetteer.

Sixth, "alas! arms cut off and ears sliced — those shone in the histories; compared with this, how do they stand?" is the gazetteer's praise, invoking the famous martyrs of old. It is the gazetteer's verdict, not a fact, and is marked as such.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as a virtuous woman of Lishui, whose husband's household was her place of residence; her support of his parents and her self-drowning took place in the Lishui countryside. The text gives neither her own surname nor her birthplace, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, the notice says "her surname is lost"; this entry uses the gazetteer's heading "the chaste widow Duan."

Second, "Duan" is the word the gazetteer prefixes, yet it also says her surname is lost; whether Duan is her own or her husband's surname is not made clear, and this entry keeps the heading without deciding.

Third, no birth or death years, birthplace or native place are given.

Fourth, the husband's name and family surname are unrecorded; his trade, his disease and the year of his return are all unstated.

Fifth, "eight years without pause" is the gazetteer's phrase, not an account of deeds.

Sixth, the closing reference to "arms cut off and ears sliced" invokes ancient martyrs for comparison and is not an act of the widow herself; this entry marks it as the gazetteer's verdict.

Seventh, the gazetteer does not record whether she was ever honoured.

Eighth, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
