Madam Chen is one of the figures in the Yuan section of the Chaste and Staunch biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, sharing a notice with Madam Wang. The gazetteer names the two together as "Madam Chen and Madam Wang, the wife and the concubine of Wang Rong", then records their care for his parents after his death, their starving to death in refusing dishonour during the Huai troubles, the court's honouring of them as "Chaste and Staunch", and their son Liangxian. This entry deals with Madam Chen alone and links to the entry for Madam Wang.

## The gazetteer text

> 陳氏、汪氏，王容之一妻一妾也。夫亡，俱善養舅姑。至正間淮兵亂，迫之弗從，並餓而死。事聞於朝，表曰「貞烈」。其子良顯。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Chaste and Staunch biography

## Her station: one of the wife and concubine of Wang Rong

The subject here is the Madam Chen of "Madam Chen and Madam Wang", recorded as one of the "wife and concubine" of Wang Rong. The gazetteer names Chen and Wang jointly as "wife and concubine" but does not say which was the wife and which the concubine; in the order Chen precedes Wang, suggesting Chen was the wife, which the gazetteer does not make explicit, and this entry sets both readings side by side without deciding. This library holds a separate entry for Madam Wang (`ls:person:wang-shi-wangrong`), and this entry is linked to it. "Madam Chen" is the designation the gazetteer gives, with only a surname and no personal or courtesy name, so this entry keeps the gazetteer's "Madam Chen" as its title. The gazetteer gives neither her home place nor her paternal house, and no native place is supplied.

## Care for the parents, refusal and death by starvation

"After their husband's death both cared well for his parents" is the gazetteer's account of her chastity: after the husband died both cared well for his parents. "When Huai troops rioted in the Zhizheng period and pressed them, they refused and starved to death together" is the gazetteer's account of her death: in the Zhizheng period Huai troops rose in disorder, pressed the two, and when they would not submit, both starved to death. The era is the gazetteer's own and is reproduced without conversion into a Common-Era year; the words "Huai troops" are reproduced without deciding on whose side they fought. The deaths of the two are one event and one act of chastity, and this entry reproduces "starved to death together" without inventing separate circumstances for each.

## The report to the court and the honouring as "Chaste and Staunch"

"The matter reached the court, and they were honoured as 'Chaste and Staunch'" is the gazetteer's account of the honour they received: their deed was reported to the court, and their gate was honoured as "Chaste and Staunch". In what year of the Zhizheng period this was done, and on whose memorial, is unrecorded. "Their son was Liangxian" is the gazetteer's record of their son's name, reproduced here; this entry does not decide whether he was born of Madam Chen or Madam Wang (the gazetteer names the two jointly and does not say whose son he was), nor records his life.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer records her as one of the wife and concubine of Wang Rong, and records her caring for the parents, her refusal, her death by starvation and her honouring. She enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer records no birth place, native place or township, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office she held in Lishui, no writings of hers and no recommendation of her out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is her death keeping her chastity in Lishui, and the biography belongs to this county's gazetteer, so this library treats it as her activity in Lishui and takes the relation "active in Lishui".

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are not recorded; "in the Zhizheng period" dates only the Huai troubles and her death, and nothing is converted into a Common-Era year or used to infer her age.

Second, neither her personal nor her courtesy name is recorded, and the entry stands under the gazetteer's own form, Madam Chen.

Third, her birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives only that she was one of the wife and concubine of Wang Rong and no home place, so no native place can be decided.

Fourth, the life of her husband Wang Rong is unrecorded, and Wang Rong has no entry in this library.

Fifth, the gazetteer names Chen and Wang jointly as "wife and concubine" but does not say which was which; this entry sets both readings side by side without deciding, though in the order Chen precedes Wang, suggesting Chen was the wife, which the gazetteer does not make explicit.

Sixth, "their son was Liangxian" does not say whether he was born of Madam Chen or Madam Wang, and this entry does not decide.

Seventh, the year of the honouring and the official who memorialised it are unrecorded.

Eighth, this entry rests on a single notice in the Chaste and Staunch biography of juan 7, and this library has nothing further to add; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
