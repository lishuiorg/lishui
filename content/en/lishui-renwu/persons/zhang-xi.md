Zhang Xi is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His biography is very short: two clauses of name and origin, one of appointment, two of judgement — and it is one of the few Ming entries in the volume that gives no native place. This entry records it as it stands, and separates out the question of a shared name raised by the biography of Zhang Sui in the same volume.

## Origin and term of appointment

> 张锡，字惟范。由进士，正德元年任
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Weifan, a *jinshi* holder, and was appointed in the first year of the Zhengde era, a definite year in this volume covering only his arrival; nothing is converted into a Common Era year. Two things must be marked. The biography gives no native place, and this entry supplies none. And it says only "appointed in the first year of the Zhengde era", never giving his title in Lishui, so this entry does not assume that of county magistrate. The biography never uses the word "Lishui"; his service there follows from the volume itself, which lists the officials of Lishui.

## What the gazetteer records

> 宽猛相济，爱畏人民。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Both clauses are judgement: leniency and severity balancing each other. The second clause, as transcribed, can be read two ways — that the people loved and also feared him, or that he loved the people and feared them; the gazetteer does not resolve it, and this entry quotes the wording as it stands without forcing a reading. The biography records no building, no fiscal measure, no case and no school measure in Lishui, and none is inferred from the judgement.

## Relation to Lishui

His native place is not recorded, and he enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is the statement of his appointment and the two clauses of judgement.

One relation to the biography of Zhang Sui in the same volume must be stated. That biography adds "later there was Zhang the magistrate, named Xi, a kinsman of his clan". Whether that "Zhang the magistrate named Xi" is the Zhang Xi of this entry is nowhere stated by the gazetteer, and this entry does not identify them; the two are kept side by side without choosing (see the entry on Zhang Sui). The word "magistrate" in that clause does not come from this biography, and is not used to supply Zhang Xi's title in Lishui; the generation meant by "kinsman" and the career and sequence of the man named Xi are also nowhere recorded.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; his native place is not recorded either, and this entry supplies none.

Second, precision of the term: "the first year of the Zhengde era" is a definite year in this volume and covers only his arrival; the year he left Lishui is not recorded, and nothing is converted into a Common Era year.

Third, his title in Lishui is not given, and this entry does not write it as county magistrate.

Fourth, the shared-name question: whether "Zhang the magistrate named Xi, a kinsman" in the biography of Zhang Sui is this Zhang Xi is nowhere stated, and this entry does not decide; the word "magistrate" there is not used to supply a title here.

Fifth, "leniency and severity balancing each other" and the second clause are the gazetteer's judgement, and the second clause admits two readings the gazetteer does not resolve.

Sixth, any concrete act in Lishui, his later career, his final rank and his place of death are all unrecorded.

Seventh, this entry rests on one biography in this volume, an online transcription not checked against a punctuated or printed edition; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
