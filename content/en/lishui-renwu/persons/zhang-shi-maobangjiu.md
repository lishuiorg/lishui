Madam Zhang is one of the Ming-era chaste women of Lishui in the "Biographies of the Chaste" of juan 7 in the Kangxi Lishui County Gazetteer, recorded as the wife of Mao Bangjiu. Hers is the fullest notice in the Ming section, and the only one naming several officials who petitioned on her behalf. This entry rests on that single record.

## Gazetteer text

> 張氏，毛邦久妻。歸三載夫卒，年二十一，子之麒甫二齡。家酷貧，事舅姑以孝，處伯叔以禮，凡舉九喪，皆出一未亡人之手。嚴於教子，每夜一燈相對，辟課讀，慈母若嚴師焉。苦節四十四年，宗黨無間言。有司屢獎其廬，知縣游應龍、司李董梅鼎、巡按賀登選、督學陳良弼題請旌表，值國變，寢。太守陳琯、大學士吳甡俱有詩讚，載《藝文》。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading of the text

First, "wife of Mao Bangjiu" names husband and wife; "her husband died three years after she married in, she then being twenty-one, and her son Zhiqi barely two" gives her marriage, widowhood and son.

Second, "the family was extremely poor; she served her parents-in-law with filial piety and dealt with her husband's uncles with propriety" is the gazetteer's summary of her household conduct.

Third, "she saw to nine funerals, all from the hand of one widow" records her burden. The text does not enumerate the nine deaths.

Fourth, "she was strict in teaching her son, sitting opposite him by a single lamp each night to urge his studies, a kind mother like a stern teacher."

Fifth, "forty-four years of bitter chastity, with no word of reproach from the clan" is the gazetteer's summary.

Sixth, "the authorities repeatedly honoured her gate; the magistrate You Yinglong, the judicial officer Dong Meiding, the circuit censor He Dengxuan and the education commissioner Chen Liangbi petitioned to honour her, but amid dynastic upheaval it was dropped." The officers' names and titles are reproduced as given. Since the petition lapsed, she received no honour in her lifetime.

Seventh, "the governor Chen Guan and the grand secretary Wu Shen both wrote poems in praise of her, recorded in the 'Literature' chapter." Those poems have not been obtained.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer identifies her only through her husband, Mao Bangjiu, whose household was her place of residence; her chastity, her management of funerals, her teaching of her son, and the petition on her behalf all attach to the Lishui countryside. The text neither states her birthplace nor calls her a native of Lishui, so this entry does not mark her as "native of Lishui." She held no office, received no recommendation and left no writings of her own, so the association is recorded as "active in Lishui," based on her conduct recorded there.

## Points of doubt

First, no birth or death years are given; "twenty-one" and "forty-four years" are the gazetteer's figures, reproduced without conversion.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not stated; the gazetteer ties her only to her husband's name.

Third, the life, rank and death year of Mao Bangjiu are unrecorded, as is whether her son Zhiqi prospered.

Fourth, the nine funerals are not enumerated, and the text gives no details.

Fifth, the officials who petitioned are named, but the "dynastic upheaval" is not identified by the gazetteer, and this entry does not specify it; because the petition lapsed, the number and dates of honours are not complete.

Sixth, the poems by Chen Guan and Wu Shen are recorded in the literature chapter but have not been obtained here.

Seventh, this entry rests on one notice in juan 7 of the Kangxi gazetteer, a network transcription not checked against a critical or printed edition.
