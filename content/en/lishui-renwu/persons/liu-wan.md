Liu Wan is one of the two Song figures in the category of loyal martyrs in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. His entry is two sentences: the first records his own office and the manner of his death, the second his descendant Ying Yan. This entry keeps the two apart, and does not mix Ying Yan's doings into Liu Wan's own career.

## Life

> 劉綰，靖康間官安撫使，金兵至，綰以抗敵死。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·忠节传

His office and his death: the gazetteer records that "during the Jingkang era he held the office of pacification commissioner". The era name is quoted as it stands; this entry converts it to no year of the common era and infers no birth year from it. "Pacification commissioner" is an office title; the gazetteer does not say to which prefecture or circuit it attached, and it records nothing about where that office sat in this period or over what territory it had authority. This entry does not supply the seat of the office from elsewhere.

"When the Jin forces arrived, Liu died resisting them" is everything the gazetteer records of his death: the Jin forces came and he died resisting them. When and where the fighting took place, how it began, and the manner of his death are not recorded. "Died resisting them" is the gazetteer's own wording and is quoted here without embellishment. Whether any posthumous rank, any honour or any shrine followed his death is not recorded.

Native place and dates: the gazetteer records no native place for Liu Wan, no courtesy name or style name, and no year of birth or death.

## The descendant recorded in the gazetteer

> 其後曰應炎者，任台諫，有聲，挺立不附賈似道，被謫，拂衣歸隱，克承先志。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·忠节传

The gazetteer opens this sentence with "after him there was one named Ying Yan" and records that Ying Yan held office in the censorate and "had a reputation"; that "he stood firm and did not attach himself to Jia Sidao"; that "he was demoted"; that he "returned and lived in retirement, brushing his robes aside"; and closes that "he carried on the earlier's resolve".

What must be stated: **this library holds no material establishing that Ying Yan was Liu Wan's son.** The gazetteer says only "after him there was one named Ying Yan", and "after him" points to a descendant; this entry does not decide whether that descendant was a son, a grandson, or a brother of the clan, and the "earlier's resolve" whose continuation the gazetteer notes is not identified either, so no judgement on the line of descent is made here. This section accordingly quotes only what the gazetteer records of Ying Yan's own conduct and reaches no conclusion about his genealogy.

Ying Yan himself: for "office in the censorate" the gazetteer gives only the two characters, without saying whether it was the censorate proper or the remonstrance office, and without any years of service. The reason for his demotion, the place to which he was demoted, and his residence in retirement are all recorded nowhere. "Had a reputation" is the gazetteer's own appraisal and is reproduced here without deciding what its weight and substance were.

## Relation to Lishui

The `relation` field is "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. As to Liu Wan himself the gazetteer records one thing: that during the Jingkang era he held the office of pacification commissioner, and that when the Jin forces arrived he died resisting them. It does not say to which prefecture his commission attached, so this library does not determine where his post was; it gives no native place, so no relation of birth can be established; and it records no office, no posting, no residence and no shrine of his in Lishui, nor any intercourse with anyone there. Thus "native of Lishui" does not apply, the gazetteer not recording his birth at Lishui; "official service" does not apply either, the seat of his office being unrecorded and so indeterminable in Lishui; and "writings", "sojourn" and "recommended from Lishui" have nothing in the gazetteer to rest on. What is recorded is only that he appears in the gazetteer of Lishui: the category of loyal martyrs in juan 6 lists Liu Wan among the two Song figures there, and that is the sole basis on which he is included in this library. "Active in Lishui" here rests only on his appearance in the Lishui gazetteer and carries no implication that he acted in Lishui or held office there; a reader must not infer from this entry that he ever came to Lishui or served there. This is the same judgement as in the entry for Zhu Xudeng in the same volume, and the two cross-reference one another.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are unknown. The gazetteer gives only the span "during the Jingkang era" and no fixed year; this entry converts it to no year of the common era and infers no birth or death year from the era name.

Second, his native place is unknown. The gazetteer does not record it, and this entry does not infer it.

Third, the seat of his pacification commission is unknown. The gazetteer gives only the office title, without attaching it to a prefecture, and this entry supplies the seat from neither other sources nor conjecture, nor concludes that his post was in Lishui.

Fourth, the line of descent between Ying Yan and Liu Wan is unknown. The gazetteer says only "after him there was one named Ying Yan"; this library holds no material establishing that Ying Yan was Liu Wan's son, and the "earlier's resolve" of the closing phrase is likewise unidentified, so the words are quoted and no judgement is made.

Fifth, the scope and length of Ying Yan's office in the censorate are unknown. The gazetteer gives only the two characters, without saying which of the two offices it was or how long he served.

Sixth, "had a reputation" and "stood firm and did not attach himself to Jia Sidao" are the gazetteer's own record and its own appraisal. What is a comment of others is quoted and not entered as fact; the reason for his demotion, the place of his demotion and his residence in retirement are recorded nowhere.

Seventh, whether he had a house, a shrine or any remains in Lishui is recorded nowhere.
