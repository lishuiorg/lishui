Xie Yingang is one of the Qing filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography gives one habit of conduct, one act of cutting, one sentence of popular opinion and one commendation; its commender already has an entry in this library, so this entry makes clear which text the commendation belongs to.

## The gazetteer text

> 謝胤綱，自幼克孝，勤苦以供甘旨。母病，刲股和藥進之，遂復甦，後廿三年壽終，人以為孝感焉。知縣王鼎胤嘉獎曰「孝懿誠篤」。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## What the gazetteer records

"Truly filial from childhood" is the gazetteer's summary, and "from childhood" is a span of conduct as the gazetteer states it, not a birth record; this entry infers no birth year from it. He worked hard and patiently to provide "sweet and savoury" food — the gazetteer's term for pleasant food used in the language of supporting one's parents — and what he provided and for which relative are not recorded.

His mother fell ill; he cut his thigh, compounded it into medicine and gave it to her, and she revived, then lived a further twenty-three years and died in old age. Doubt must be kept here: the subject of "after twenty-three years died in old age" is not fixed by the text — following the previous clause it may be read of the mother, but it may refer otherwise; this library keeps both without deciding, and the endpoints of the span are unrecorded, so this entry converts and infers nothing. What the illness was and how the cutting and treatment were carried out are nowhere recorded. "People took this as the effect of filial feeling" is popular opinion the gazetteer reports, a matter of local belief rather than verifiable record; this entry reproduces it and marks its nature.

## The commender Wang Dingyin: this deed comes from the Filial Sons section, not from his own biography

"The district magistrate Wang Dingyin commended him, saying 'sincere and firm in virtuous filial piety'" is the only honour the biography gives, and it supplies the words of the commendation. Three points must be made clear against an existing entry of this library.

First, this library already has an entry for Wang Dingyin (`ls:person:wang-dingyin`), resting on the officials' biography in juan 6, and that biography records no commendation of a filial son. The commending of Xie Yingang therefore reaches this library only through the Filial Sons section, and this entry records it as such rather than treating it as something Wang Dingyin's own biography says.

Second, the four characters are the words of the commendation as the gazetteer records them; the gazetteer writes only "commended, saying", so whether these were a tablet, an archway or another form is not made clear, and this entry does not fix the form. The year of the commendation is unrecorded, and this entry does not date the matter from any year attached to Wang Dingyin's own biography.

Third, the `related` link to the Wang Dingyin entry is a cross-reference for checking only and merges no deeds of the two men.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Xie Yingang no township and no native place; it ties him to this county through being named in the filial sons section, through the reported opinion of people about him, and through the commendation of a magistrate of this county. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is activity and commendation in this county, not birth here, so it does not record him as "born locally".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; township and native place are unrecorded; the biography carries no dates, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, "from childhood" is a span of conduct as the gazetteer states it and cannot yield a birth year.

Third, the subject of "after twenty-three years died in old age" is not fixed by the text — read of the mother following the previous clause, or otherwise — and this library decides neither; the endpoints of the span are unrecorded.

Fourth, the nature of the illness, the course of the cutting and treatment, and his mother's name, birth and death are all unrecorded; the biography names no other relative.

Fifth, what the "sweet and savoury" food was and for which relative it was provided are unrecorded.

Sixth, "people took this as the effect of filial feeling" is reported popular opinion, not fact.

Seventh, the form of the commendation is not made clear — the gazetteer writes only "commended, saying" — and this entry does not fix it as a tablet or an archway; the year is unrecorded.

Eighth, Wang Dingyin's own biography records no commendation of a filial son; this deed comes only from the Filial Sons section, and this entry does not merge the two texts; the `related` link is a cross-reference only.

Ninth, his origin is not recorded, and this entry supplies no examination title.

Tenth, the era field "Qing" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "present dynasty" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Eleventh, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
