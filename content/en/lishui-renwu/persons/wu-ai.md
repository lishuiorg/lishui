Wu Ai is one of the martyred women of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. Her life is the longest narrative in this block: it gives her family and her talents, then the coming of soldiers in the yiyou year, her capture and hanging, an appended note on her younger sister Ju, and finally a dream omen attached to her death. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 武艾，蒲村庠生武可偮之長女。年十六，侍父母能孝，工織紝女紅，暇則讀《女論語》，旁及《琵琶記》諸書、古詩《木蘭詞》，母曰：「女學宜習，《琵琶》劇也，烏用此為？」艾曰：「記中趙五娘之婦道不足法耶？」母頷而聽之。武為詩禮望族，女子多習訓教者，而艾尤慧善。歲乙酉，蔓兵至村，艾被執。妹橘亦在劫，年甫十二，罵兵受刃，傷項臂，赴水，人救得蘇。艾巧伺一隙，以膝帶自縊，氣絕墮地，趺坐，微有笑容。兵去，人咸訝之，倘亦死得其所，乃含笑入地耶？先是，聞變，即自知不免。夜夢庭中青煙起處，恍有神助書劍，旦告家人，皆以為妄。越月，果值此死，豈非數與？
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

**Family and talents.**

"Wu Ai, eldest daughter of the Pucun county-school student Wu Keji" gives her name, her place, and her family: Wu Ai, of Pucun, daughter of Wu Keji, a student of the county school, and his eldest daughter. Pucun is one of the Lishui place names seen in this volume, and this entry keeps the text's wording.

"At sixteen she served her parents with filial piety, was skilled at weaving and needlework, and in her spare time read the Analects for Women, and also the Story of the Pipa and other books and the old poem the Ballad of Mulan" gives her age and disposition. All these books are as the gazetteer records.

"Her mother said, 'A girl's education should be attended to; the Pipa is a play, what use is it?' Ai said, 'Is the wifely virtue of Zhao Wuniang in that story not worth following?' and her mother nodded and let her" gives the exchange between mother and daughter. It is reported dialogue, and "Zhao Wuniang" and "wifely virtue" are the gazetteer's words.

"The Wu were a clan of poetry and ritual, whose women mostly received training, and Ai was especially clever and good" is the gazetteer's summary of the family and of her gifts.

**The yiyou year, capture, and hanging.**

"In the yiyou year soldiers came to the village and Ai was taken" gives the year and the outset. "Yiyou" is a sexagenary term only, kept as written and not converted to a Western year. "Soldiers" here renders the gazetteer's own unusual wording, kept without emendation or explanation.

"Her younger sister Ju was also in the raid; barely twelve, she cursed the soldiers, was cut, wounded on the neck and arm, leapt into the water, and was rescued and revived" is an appended note. This entry keeps the layers apart: Wu Ai is the subject, while her sister's story is an appendix, and the wound, the rescue, and the revival are all the gazetteer's words.

"Ai seized a moment's gap, hanged herself with a knee-band, and fell dead to the ground, sitting cross-legged, with a faint smile" is the account of her death. All of it is as the gazetteer records, and this entry keeps it as the gazetteer's statement.

"The soldiers left, and all were astonished, wondering whether one who died so well might indeed smile as she entered the ground" is the bystanders' astonishment and the gazetteer's sigh, a judgement rather than a fact.

**The dream omen, marked as legend.**

"Earlier, when she heard of the trouble, she knew she could not escape. By night she dreamed that where blue smoke rose in the courtyard, it seemed a god helped her with sword and book; at dawn she told the family, and all thought it vain. A month later she met this death; was it not fate?" is the dream omen with which the gazetteer closes. This is a **dream omen as the gazetteer records it**, and this library marks it as legend, not fact. "It seemed a god helped her with sword and book" is what she saw in a dream, not a verifiable event; "all thought it vain" records the family's disbelief; "was it not fate?" is the gazetteer's rhetorical judgement. This entry infers nothing from the omen about the date, manner, or cause of her death.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: a daughter of the Wu of Pucun, taken when soldiers reached the village in the yiyou year, who hanged herself, her father a county-school student and her family a clan of poetry and ritual, the village being her home. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, only that she was a daughter of Pucun, so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her — her reading and weaving are neither writings nor rank — so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply; since the record is of a local calamity, not of lodging abroad, "sojourn" does not apply either. Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was sixteen and died in this affair, and the year of the disorder is a sexagenary term only, kept as written and not converted; this entry infers no birth or death year.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was the eldest daughter of the Pucun student Wu Keji, and the family's long residence and native place are not stated.

Third, beyond her husband's name, her paternal family and offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer gives no husband and names only her father and sister; whether she married and whether she had children are unrecorded.

Fourth, the wound, rescue, and revival of her sister Ju are appended matter, and her later fate is unrecorded; this entry supplies nothing.

Fifth, "by night she dreamed that where blue smoke rose in the courtyard it seemed a god helped her with sword and book" is a dream omen the gazetteer records; this library marks it as legend, not fact, and infers nothing from it about the cause of her death.

Sixth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item does not say whether she received an award after death, and this entry infers none from other items.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Eighth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
