Wu Youwen is one of the Song officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is one of the shortest in the volume: one sentence of appointment, two clauses of judgement, and nothing else. This entry writes only what that entry contains and supplies no life for him.

## Origin and term

> 吴友闻，淳熙十六年任。清勤详察，狱折片言。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之六·名宦传

He was appointed to Lishui in the sixteenth year of the Chunxi era. The gazetteer gives no courtesy name, no native place, and no account of how he entered office. His Lishui post is stated only as "appointed": the gazetteer never gives the formal title, so this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county" and supplies no title of its own. The Chunxi year given is only the year of arrival; this entry converts it to no Western year and infers nothing from the dynasty.

## What the gazetteer records

The only thing said of his career is two clauses. "Clear, diligent, thorough and observant" judges his government and is a judgement. "Cases settled on a few words" is reproduced here as transcribed; on its face it touches the hearing of cases, but the gazetteer records no case, no verdict and no year, and this entry adds no gloss to the four characters and supplies no judging episode. Neither clause cites an act or records a measure: this entry has no deeds to report because the source contains none, not because any have been left out.

## Relation to Lishui

He is placed in the volume of notable officials on the strength of "appointed in the sixteenth Chunxi year", and this entry records him through official service. The volume records nothing he did in Lishui, no year of leaving, and nothing of his movements afterwards.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded, nor his courtesy name, native place, or route into office.

Second, the appointment is given only as the sixteenth Chunxi year, which is the year of arrival; the year he left is not recorded. This entry converts nothing and infers nothing.

Third, the gazetteer gives his Lishui office only as "appointed" and never states the formal title; this entry does not render it as "magistrate of the county".

Fourth, the two clauses are the gazetteer's judgements, and their grounds are unrecorded; whether the clause about cases refers to his judging in Lishui is not made explicit.

Fifth, any concrete acts in Lishui, his later career, his final office and the place of his death are all unrecorded.

Sixth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume; searches of the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could supplement him. This volume was read from an online transcription, not from a punctuated edition or the printed book, so every quotation is marked "from the online transcription".
