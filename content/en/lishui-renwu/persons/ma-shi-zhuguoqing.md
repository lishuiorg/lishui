Madam Ma is one of the chaste widows of Lishui recorded in juan 7, the "Chaste and Martyred Women" biography, of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The gazetteer gives only her natal surname Ma and records her as the mother of Zhu Guoqing, a student of Zanxian Township, with two matters: keeping her chastity and raising her son, and settling at Jintan. The passage sits under a heading "Sojourners"; this library judges that word a suspect heading, as explained in its own section below. This entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer and adds nothing the text does not record.

## The gazetteer text

> 馬氏，贊賢鄉庠生朱國卿母也。年二十四夫亡，時國卿方在襁褓，氏誓守教子，弱冠游庠。嘗卜居金壇，壇紳王有三、周簡臣重其節，各有詩以贊之。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·贞烈传

## Reading

The item records two matters, taken sentence by sentence.

"Madam Ma, mother of Zhu Guoqing, a student of Zanxian Township" gives her natal surname and her son. Zanxian Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, kept as written. The gazetteer names her through her son and gives no husband's name, so his surname, courtesy name, and status are all unrecorded, and this entry supplies nothing.

"When she was twenty-four her husband died, and Guoqing was then an infant" gives the year of her widowhood and the situation: her husband died when she was twenty-four, and her son was still a babe in arms. The gazetteer uses this to show the child was young.

"She vowed to keep her chastity and raise her son, and he entered the county school as a young man" gives her vow and the fruit of her teaching: her son came of age and entered the county school as a student. This is the result of her motherly instruction as the gazetteer records it.

"She once settled at Jintan, where the gentlemen Wang Yousan and Zhou Jianchen admired her chastity and each wrote a poem in praise" records her move and the regard she won. Jintan is a neighbouring county, a place name outside the Lishui names seen in this volume, kept as written; "settled" means she chose a place to live, and the year and reason of the move are unrecorded.

## The suspect heading "Sojourners"

The passage this item belongs to carries a heading "Sojourners" in the transcription. This library judges the word a suspect heading: what follows (Cheng Tianmin's daughter, the Chen daughter, Wu Ai, Madam Ma, and others) are still chaste widows and martyred women, and does not match the sense of "sojourners". This library keeps the word as recorded and marks it a suspect heading, and does not read this item as belonging to a sojourner biography; whether the passage continues the Chaste and Martyred Women biography or is another biography must be settled against the printed edition.

## Relation to Lishui

This entry enters her as "active in Lishui", and the reason must be stated. What the gazetteer records is her chastity in Lishui: mother of Zhu Guoqing of Zanxian Township, widowed at twenty-four, raising her son to the county school, the township being her home. But the gazetteer records no birthplace or native place for her, naming her only "Madam Ma", so this entry does not record her as "born locally". There is no office, recommendation, or writing to attach to her, so "official service", "recommended from Lishui", and "writings" do not apply. Although the text records that she "settled at Jintan", what is recorded is the praise of Jintan gentlemen for her chastity, not sojourn beyond Lishui as her defining status, so this entry does not use "sojourn". Of the five categories only "active in Lishui" holds, and this entry uses it.

## Points of doubt

First, her birth and death years are unrecorded. The gazetteer gives only that she was widowed at twenty-four; it gives no year of birth, no year for her husband, and no year of death, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, her birthplace and native place are not explicitly recorded. The gazetteer calls her only "Madam Ma"; her paternal family and residence are unrecorded, and this entry does not infer a native place from her son's township.

Third, her husband's name is unrecorded. The gazetteer names her through "mother of Zhu Guoqing", and her husband's surname, courtesy name, and status are unrecorded.

Fourth, her offspring are not fully recorded. The gazetteer names only her son Guoqing; whether she had other sons, and Guoqing's dates and later career, are unrecorded.

Fifth, the year, reason, and duration of the move to Jintan are unrecorded, and the poems of the Jintan gentlemen, with their contents and source, are not copied in this volume.

Sixth, the honours the gazetteer records may not be complete. This item records only poems of praise from Jintan gentlemen, not whether she received an official award, and this entry infers none from other items.

Seventh, this entry rests on a single item in this gazetteer, in the "Sojourners" (suspect heading) block of the Chaste and Martyred Women biography; the volume is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.

Eighth, searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to her.
