Yang Chong is one of the figures in the Ming section of the Righteous Men biography in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*. The notice records his place, his dispersing his wealth at the end of the Yuan to keep fighting men and shield his township, and the people's wish to fight and his holding it wrong after the Ming founder crossed the river. It must be stated in particular that the gazetteer's online transcription breaks off in the middle of his notice, so his end cannot be known from this library.

## The gazetteer text

> 楊寵，思鶴鄉人。時元末，群盜起，寵散家養敢死士，障一鄉雲。及明太祖渡江，首命大將軍徐達取溧水，眾請戰，寵則曰：「不可。今有行仁義而起者，聖人也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 7, Righteous Men biography

It must be stated that the text quoted above stops at the words "for one who rises to practise benevolence and righteousness is a sage"; the gazetteer's online transcription breaks off there, and what follows is not available, so the quotation here is limited to what the transcription preserves and its continuation is not supplied from other books.

## His place and time

"Yang Chong" is the name the gazetteer gives, written here in its simplified form and with the slug yang-chong. "Of Sihe Township" is the residence the gazetteer gives; Sihe Township is one of the Lishui townships named in this juan, and this entry reproduces the gazetteer's characters without matching it to any present settlement, for which this library has no material. "At the end of the Yuan, when rebels rose on every side" is the gazetteer's account of his time: at the end of the Yuan rebels rose on every side. This entry assigns no year and converts nothing into a Common-Era year.

## Dispersing his wealth to keep fighting men and shield his township

"Chong dispersed his household wealth to keep fighting men, and shielded his township" is the gazetteer's account: Yang Chong dispersed his family's wealth to maintain men willing to die, shielding one township. The trailing particle is the gazetteer's own and is reproduced without altering or glossing it. The year of the dispersal, the number of men kept and the results of his defence are unrecorded, and nothing is supplied.

## The Ming founder crossing the river and Xu Da taking Lishui

"And when the Ming founder crossed the river, he first ordered the Grand General Xu Da to take Lishui, and the people wished to fight, but Chong said, 'No. For one who rises to practise benevolence and righteousness is a sage'" is the gazetteer's account of what followed: when the Ming founder crossed the river, he first ordered the Grand General Xu Da to take Lishui, and the people wished to fight, but Yang Chong held it wrong, on the ground that "one who rises to practise benevolence and righteousness is a sage". "The Ming founder" and "the Grand General Xu Da" are the gazetteer's own words and are reproduced without supplying their dates or background; "the people wished to fight" and "Chong said" are the gazetteer's account of the people's request and Yang Chong's answer, reproduced and marked as the speech the gazetteer records, with no further inference beyond it.

## Where the transcription breaks off, and this library's limit

The limit must be stated plainly: the gazetteer's online transcription breaks off in the middle of the "Yang Chong" notice, stopping at the words "for one who rises to practise benevolence and righteousness is a sage", and what follows is not available. So this library cannot know what Yang Chong did afterwards — whether he surrendered, whether he was employed, his death and its aftermath — and nothing is supplied, nor is anything inferred from his counsel against fighting. Whatever this entry does not state is limited by what the transcription preserves.

## Relation to Lishui

Of Yang Chong the gazetteer records his place, his dispersing his wealth to keep fighting men and shield his township, and his holding it wrong after the Ming founder crossed the river. He enters this site with the relation "active in Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: the gazetteer records that he was of Sihe Township, a residence and not a birth record, so "born in Lishui" does not apply; it records no office he held in Lishui, no writings of his and no recommendation of him out of Lishui, so "official service", "writings" and "recommended from Lishui" do not apply; what it records is his keeping fighting men, shielding his township, and his stance when the Ming forces took Lishui, which is his activity in Lishui, so the relation taken is "active in Lishui". His role is taken as "local worthy" because he belongs to this county's Righteous Men biography and appears there for the righteous deed of protecting a township, not on any ground for "official" or "teacher or scholar".

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; "at the end of the Yuan" dates only the time, and nothing is converted into a Common-Era year or used to infer his age.

Second, neither his courtesy name nor his style name is recorded.

Third, his birth place and native place are not explicitly recorded: the gazetteer gives only that he was "of Sihe Township", his residence; to what sub-unit the township belonged and where his house stood are unrecorded.

Fourth, the year of his dispersal of wealth, the number of fighting men kept, and the results of his defence are unrecorded.

Fifth, the trailing particle in the gazetteer's phrase is reproduced without glossing and without supplying what follows from it.

Sixth, the gazetteer's online transcription breaks off in the middle of his notice, so his end after his counsel against fighting — whether he surrendered, whether he was employed, his death and its aftermath — is unavailable, and nothing is supplied; see above.

Seventh, the value "Ming" for his birth era is taken from the "Ming" dynastic grouping of the Righteous Men biography in this gazetteer (his notice standing under "Ming"), and is not a birth record; his activity falls at the turn from the late Yuan to the early Ming, and both statements are kept side by side here.

Eighth, this entry rests on a single notice in the Righteous Men biography of juan 7, and that notice is itself incomplete; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription.
