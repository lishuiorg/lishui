Zhang Xianwen is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. The biography opens with one clause of family, continues with several clauses of conduct and closes with the gazetteer's judgement; it carries no dates and records no award. This entry writes nothing beyond that clause and those clauses.

## The gazetteer text

> 章憲文，南昌府通判甫詔之子也。幼穎秀能文，性正直，為庠士所推重。稚年喪母，事繼母曹至孝，曹不以子子之，百計為害，憲文甘受不出片語。父病，禱天祈保全，盡子道不形於事跡，孝至純矣。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## The clause of family: kept as written and held in doubt

"He is the son of Fuzhao, vice-prefect of Nanchang Prefecture" is the only family information the biography gives. Three points must be clear. First, "vice-prefect of Nanchang Prefecture" is his father's post, not his, and this entry does not transfer it to Zhang Xianwen. Second, "Fuzhao" is the name the gazetteer gives for his father in these two characters only; it gives no courtesy name and no surname — as Zhang Xianwen's son the father may share the surname, but the gazetteer does not say so and this entry supplies nothing. Third, "Nanchang Prefecture" is a prefecture name as the gazetteer gives it; this library has no material on its location or administrative attachment, and this entry does not fix it anywhere. This library has no entry for Fuzhao, and no material on his years of office or career.

## What the gazetteer records of his conduct

The gazetteer records that he was clever and able to write as a child, upright by nature, and esteemed by the students of the schools. Note carefully: the text says only that the students esteemed him — that is the attitude of school students towards him — and does not record that he himself held scholastic status; this entry does not write him as a "student of the county school", a "student in the schools" or any other rank on the strength of that clause.

Two deeds follow, neither dated. First, he lost his mother while young and served his stepmother of the Cao lineage with the utmost filial care; she would not treat him as a son, harmed him by every device, and he accepted it willingly without uttering a word. Second, when his father fell ill he prayed to heaven for his preservation. The gazetteer gives only that clause: where he prayed, whether any sign followed, and what became of the illness are all unrecorded. The surname of his birth mother, how the stepmother of the Cao lineage entered the household and what became of her are nowhere recorded; "while young" is not dated.

"He discharged the way of a son without it showing in deeds; his filial piety reached pure perfection" is the gazetteer's judgement and summary, citing no act, and this entry reasons back from the judgement to no specific deed.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives Zhang Xianwen no township and no native place; it ties him to this county only by placing him in the filial sons section, and by the esteem of this county's school students. This entry enters him as "active in Lishui": what the text proves is that he is recorded in this county's biography section and esteemed by men of this county, not that he was born here, so it does not record him as "born locally". His father's post at Nanchang is family information recorded by the gazetteer; this entry uses it neither to fix a birthplace for Zhang Xianwen nor to establish where he himself was active.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; township and native place are unrecorded; the biography carries no dates at all, and this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, his own scholastic status is not recorded — the text writes only that the students esteemed him — and this entry supplies no such title.

Third, his father's name appears only as two characters; his surname, courtesy name, years of office and career are unrecorded, and this library has no material on the location of Nanchang Prefecture.

Fourth, the surname of his birth mother, when the stepmother entered the household and her later fate are unrecorded; "while young" is undated.

Fifth, what the stepmother's "every device" consisted of, and the course of his silent acceptance, are nowhere given.

Sixth, where he prayed, whether any sign followed and the outcome of his father's illness are unrecorded.

Seventh, "his filial piety reached pure perfection" and "he discharged the way of a son without it showing in deeds" are the gazetteer's judgement and summary.

Eighth, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Ninth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Tenth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
