Xu Bida is one of the Ming officials of Lishui listed in juan 6 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and his biography is one of the two longest in the volume, listing more than a dozen acts during his Lishui term. The biography itself notes that he has a separate, fuller biography and that this volume only praises the outline of his government of Lishui, so this entry uses only what this volume records. Almost nothing is dated: his arrival, his departure, the success of the tax conversion, the academy, the abolition of the towmen — all undated.

## Origin and term

> 徐必达，字德夫，别号玄仗，浙江嘉兴府秀水人。万历壬辰进士，初授太湖县，多惠政，以丁内艰，服阕补溧水。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He was courtesy-named Defu, with the style name Xuanzhang, a native of Xiushui in Jiaxing Prefecture, Zhejiang, passed the *jinshi* examination in a *renchen* year of the Wanli era, was first appointed to Taihu county, left office for his mother's mourning, and was reappointed to Lishui after the mourning period ended. The gazetteer records only the reappointment and gives no year; the cyclical date is left as it stands and this entry converts nothing. "Many beneficial policies" is the gazetteer's judgement of his Taihu term, whose content is not recorded.

## Acts in Lishui

**Land survey and the petition for tax conversion.**

> 甫下车，问民疾苦，举最重者莫如田亩丈量之务。先是，明永乐初溧居苏常上游，吴下每受湖水之患，因筑东坝以塞湖水，为吴都利则溧害生，南湖之漂没泛滥，十万余亩化为汪洋，不可问粮税矣。前令陈子贞不得已，以每亩加钞弓一分有奇，以充前数，则李代桃（薑）［僵］，民不堪命。且丁、条二项悉重于他县，而协济银尚三千余两措办尤艰，公心恻之。比例高淳改折以苏民困，代邑民张晓等草疏，使叩阍以请。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The transcription carries its own collation brackets inside the idiom about one tree dying in another's place: a wrong character is bracketed and the correct one follows in square brackets, and this entry reproduces the passage as transcribed without altering it. This is the most concrete fiscal matter in the biography. The gazetteer states that in the early Yongle period Lishui lay upstream of Suzhou and Changzhou, that the Wu region suffered from lake flooding and so the East Dam was built to shut the lake water off, that what benefited the Wu capital generated harm for Lishui, that the Nanhu was drowned and flooded and over a hundred thousand mu became open water with no way to assess grain tax; that the earlier magistrate Chen Zizhen, with no alternative, added a little over one fen of paper currency per mu to make up the lost total — which the gazetteer likens to one tree dying in another's place; and that the ding and tiao categories were heavier in Lishui than in other counties, with over three thousand taels of supplementary silver especially hard to raise. Xu Bida cited the precedent of Gaochun's conversion to relieve the people's distress, drafted a memorial for the county people including Zhang Xiao and others, and had them petition at the throne. The causal account of the dam and Lishui's injury, and the figures of a hundred thousand mu and three thousand-odd taels, are all the gazetteer's; their statistical basis and dates are unrecorded.

> 公后历迁至应天府尹、操江都御史，始终以溧为意，每与诸当道激切言之，卒获改折，皆公之力也。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The gazetteer credits the eventual conversion to Xu Bida and places it among his promotions after leaving Lishui. The year the conversion was obtained is not recorded. "It was all his doing" is the gazetteer's attribution, recorded here as the gazetteer's claim.

**Collection.**

> 征催有法，弗事烦刑，不经胥役，民自投柜登名，因以验其轻重，民乐输早完，岁无逋欠。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

**Judging and compositions.**

> 听讼虚公，使各尽其词，随以片言折之，无一冤者。又宽锾赎，公移内开填百不及十。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

"Not one person was wronged" is the gazetteer's judgement, not a verifiable record. "Of a hundred items entered in his official documents, fewer than ten were collected" states the degree to which he relaxed monetary compositions; how that was counted is not recorded.

**Teaching, the academy and school estate.**

> 与士子课文讲艺，建图南书院，有期有礼，士彬彬以兴，置学田以赡贫生。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He set exercises and discussions with the students, built the Tunan Academy with fixed dates and observances, and established school land to support poor students. The year the academy was built, its hall names, its site and its later fate are not given, nor is the acreage of the estate.

**Relief of the ward heads' supply burden.**

> 凡各上官经过，供亿之烦往往取办坊长，公恤其苦，乃于衙舍旷地蓄鸡豚，治蔬菜，以备陈献，累不及民，上官亦习闻其贤而无有苛责。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

**The garrison registration of Qi Tai's clan associates.**

> 县民杨保元、骆应鹏等坐齐泰族党，编戍军四十五户，泰子孙蒙赦还而族党远戍者犹故，公为力请豁籍，且请恤录裔孙以慰忠魂。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

County people including Yang Baoyuan and Luo Yingpeng had been enrolled as garrison military in forty-five households because of their association with Qi Tai's clan; Qi Tai's own descendants had been pardoned and returned, but those clan associates serving in distant garrisons remained as before. He pressed hard for the striking of their registration and for posthumous recognition of Qi Tai's descendants. The year and the outcome of the petition are not recorded in this volume.

**Two household counts, kept side by side.** This volume says "forty-five households enrolled as garrison military". This library's entry on Qi Tai records the annals of juan 1, under the first year of the Tianqi era, as giving "thirty-eight households". The two juan of the same gazetteer give different numbers; this entry keeps both, chooses neither, and rewrites no figure from either.

**Other benefits.**

> 他若恤马户，佃草场，修圩埂，治街道，兴利除害不可胜纪。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

Four categories are named — care for horse-keeping households, tenancy of grasslands, repair of polder banks, work on the streets — with no content and no dates for any of them.

**Abolishing the Yangtze towmen.**

> 长江故有牵挽水手，岁计工食银二千四百余两。实则江帆风便，无事此役，公为裁革，以抵各属县夫马驿递之应增者、赔偿之应恤者，条款井井，抚按允行，勒石著为永规。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

The Yangtze had long had towmen for hauling, costing over twenty-four hundred taels of wages a year; in fact, the gazetteer says, sailing depended on wind and the service was unnecessary, so he had it abolished and the money applied to what the subordinate counties needed added in courier men and horses and to what should be compensated, with orderly clauses approved by the governors and inspectors and cut in stone as a permanent rule. This passage stands in the biography after "he left office on being drawn up, and later rose to Prefect of Nanjing", and the office he held when he did it is not made explicit; this entry follows the gazetteer's order without rearranging it. Whether the stone still exists is not recorded.

**Donated salary and the closing verdict.**

> 又捐俸三百余两，为溧水增置学田。凡可以为溧计者，莫不留意焉，溧民至今颂之不衰。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

He also donated over three hundred taels of his salary to add school land for Lishui. "There was nothing that could be done for Lishui's benefit that he did not attend to, and the people of Lishui still praise him without diminishment" is the gazetteer's judgement and its account of posthumous memory.

## After Lishui

> 以行取去任，后历升至南京应天府尹。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

> 公位至南兵部侍郎，终于家。别有传，兹特褒其治溧之大略焉。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 6

His later rank appears three times in the biography: "later promoted in turn to Prefect of Yingtian Prefecture and Commander of the Jiangnan Coast", "later rose to Prefect of Nanjing", and "he reached Vice Minister of War in the Nanjing capital, and died at home". None is dated; this entry records all three, does not merge them into one career line, and fixes no order. The biography ends by noting that a separate fuller biography exists and that this volume only praises the outline of his government of Lishui; that other biography has not been obtained by this library.

## Relation to Lishui

He was not a Lishui native and enters this library through official service. What ties him to the county is his appointment, the more than dozen acts recorded during it, the statement that after leaving he "always kept Lishui in mind", and the claim that the people still praise him. This is the longest biography of any Lishui official in this volume known to this library. His petition concerning the garrison registration of Qi Tai's clan associates is one of the important records this library has of the price Lishui paid in the Qi Tai case and of its later remedy, which is why this entry is linked to the entry on Qi Tai.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded; neither his arrival in Lishui nor his departure is dated, only "reappointed on the end of mourning" and "left office on being drawn up".

Second, the cyclical date of his examination is given only as cyclical; this entry converts it and uses it for nothing, and does not infer the start and end of his Lishui term from it.

Third, the year the tax conversion was obtained is not recorded, and "it was all his doing" is the gazetteer's attribution rather than an institutional fact stated by this entry.

Fourth, the household counts disagree: this volume says forty-five households, this library's entry on Qi Tai records thirty-eight from the annals. Both are kept. The year and outcome of the petition, and where the forty-five households went, are unrecorded.

Fifth, the year, hall names, site and later fate of the Tunan Academy are unrecorded, as is the acreage and location of the two school estates — the estate to support poor students and the one added with his donated salary.

Sixth, the figures — over a hundred thousand mu, over three thousand taels, a little over one fen per mu, over twenty-four hundred taels, over three hundred taels — are all the gazetteer's, with no stated basis, date or source.

Seventh, the office he held when the Yangtze towmen were abolished is not made explicit, and the survival of the stone recording the permanent rule is unrecorded.

Eighth, the content and dates of the four categories — horse households, grasslands, polder banks, streets — are unrecorded.

Ninth, the causal account of the East Dam and the Nanhu flooding is the gazetteer's; the history of the engineering and of the damage is not recorded in this volume.

Tenth, his later rank appears three times with different formulations, none dated; this entry fixes no order and decides none to be final.

Eleventh, the biography refers to a separate fuller biography, which this library has not obtained; this entry uses only this volume.

Twelfth, this entry rests on one biography in this volume. What this library records about the Qi Tai household counts and the annals belongs to other juan of the same gazetteer and is kept separate from this biography.
