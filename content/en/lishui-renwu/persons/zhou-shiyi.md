Zhou Shiyi is one of the Ming filial sons of Lishui listed in juan 7 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, in its "Filial Sons" biography section. His biography gives the fullest home and family details of any Ming entry in this volume, but one of its episodes belongs to recorded marvel. This entry keeps the checkable layer — township, family, livelihood — separate from the uncheckable one — the gold in the fish and what the gazetteer attributes to it.

## The gazetteer text

> 周什一，弘治間仙壇鄉一圖荷塘村人也。少孤貧，父用四早卒，事母朱氏至孝，負販以養。一日，母病思魚，時值大風，漁人罷釣，什一走湖濱遍覓，得一鯰，倍價購歸，剖之得黃金百兩。後以是起家，今子孫代游庠序，人以為孝子之報。
> —— 康熙《溧水县志》卷之七·孝子传

## Township and date

The opening clause places him in Hetang Village of the "first tu" of Xiantan Township in the Hongzhi period. Xiantan Township is one of the township names this volume records for Lishui, and this entry keeps the gazetteer's wording. "First tu" is a further subdivision the gazetteer adds below the township; this library has no material to determine its nature, its extent or how it was organised, and this entry does not equate it with any known system. Hetang Village is kept as written; this library has no material linking it to a present-day settlement. "Hongzhi" here is the period the gazetteer assigns to the man, not a year of award and not a birth record; this entry converts it to no Common-Era year and infers from it neither birth and death nor the span of his activity.

## Family and livelihood

The gazetteer records that he was orphaned young and poor, that his father Yongsi died early — "Yongsi" is the father's name as the gazetteer gives it — that he served his mother Zhu with the utmost filial care, and that he kept her by carrying and selling goods. The father's name and the mother's surname are the firmest layer of this biography; the gazetteer gives nothing else about them — no birth and death years, no marriage, no other relatives — and this entry supplies none. What he carried and sold, and where he plied his trade, are unrecorded; "died early" is not dated.

## The mother's craving and the gold in the fish: a recorded marvel

The gazetteer records that one day, while ill, his mother craved fish; that a great wind was blowing and the fishermen had stopped work; that Zhou Shiyi went along the lakeshore searching everywhere; that he obtained one catfish and bought it at double price; and that on cutting it open he found a hundred taels of gold.

Its nature must be stated: "cutting it open he found a hundred taels of gold" is a marvel recorded by the gazetteer, and this entry reproduces it while marking it as such, not as a fact. The three clauses that follow are likewise not verifiable record — "the family rose from this" is the gazetteer's attribution of the rise of the household, "now his descendants generation after generation attend the schools" is the gazetteer's own report of the situation at the time of compilation, and "people take it as the reward for filial piety" is popular opinion the gazetteer reports. This entry reasons back from none of the three to any property, degree or causal chain.

The gazetteer writes only "lakeshore"; this library has no material identifying which lake, and this entry does not name one. What the double price was, what became of the gold, and what "the family rose" actually consisted of are nowhere recorded.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer places him in a named township and village, and this entry enters him as "active in Lishui". A township is the place of residence the gazetteer records, not a birth record: the text says only that he was a man of such-and-such township and village, gives no birthplace and no birth and death years, so this entry does not record him as "born locally". What ties him to Lishui is his residence, his livelihood of carrying and selling, the search for fish for his mother, and the gazetteer's own note that his descendants attend the local schools.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years are not recorded. "Hongzhi period" is the period the gazetteer assigns to him, not a birth or death year and not a year of award; this entry converts and infers nothing.

Second, this library has no material on the nature or extent of the "first tu" of Xiantan Township, and none on the location of Hetang Village or its correspondence with any present-day settlement.

Third, "died early" is undated; the birth and death years and the marriage of his parents are unrecorded; the biography names no other relative and this entry supplies none.

Fourth, which lake the "lakeshore" is remains unrecorded, and this entry does not name one.

Fifth, "a hundred taels of gold" is a recorded marvel; "the family rose from this", "his descendants attend the schools" and "people take it as the reward for filial piety" are the gazetteer's attribution, its own report and reported popular opinion, and this entry treats them as such.

Sixth, his own route into the school roll is not recorded; only his descendants' schooling is, and this entry does not infer his own status from theirs.

Seventh, the biography does not say whether he received an award, and this entry infers none from other entries in this volume.

Eighth, the era field "Ming" here follows the dynasty grouping of the "Ming" block of this biography section, not a birth record.

Ninth, this entry rests on a single biography in juan 7; the volume used here is an online transcription, not checked against a punctuated edition or the printed text, so citations must be marked as from the transcription. Searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.

Tenth, another entry of the same biography places Zhou Jiaqing at "Hetang in Xiantan township", the same township as this entry's "Hetang village, first district of Xiantan township" but with a slightly different place name, and with a different father (Yongsi against Shangli) and a different mother (of the Zhu clan against of the Shen clan). The related link to that entry is a cross-reference for the same township and village only; whether the two are of one family, and whether the two place names are one place, cannot be decided from this library's material.
