Ma Caichen is one of the two men in the "Our Dynasty" block of the Recommendations examination in juan 2 of the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, and the only entry of the section that carries a narrative: besides the roster line the section appends two plaque inscriptions, recording his repeated refusals of recommendation and his coming forward with stored grain in a famine year. This entry writes only the roster line and those two inscriptions and supplies no life for him.

## Status and recommendation

> 馬采臣，邑庠廩生。康熙九年奉詔求賢，紳（矜）［衿］公舉，采臣辭不就聘。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

"Stipend student of the county school" is the status as the gazetteer words it; the neighbouring line for Shi Kexing words his status slightly differently, and this entry decides nothing about the difference. "Ninth year of Kangxi" is a year the section does give, reproduced without conversion to a Common-Era year. "An edict sought worthies" is the occasion and "the gentry publicly recommended him" the actors; the transcription's collation bracket in that phrase is reproduced exactly as given, neither deleted nor resolved. "He refused and did not accept the summons" is the section's clause of refusal, and the section assigns him no office.

## The two appended plaque inscriptions

After the roster line the section appends two pieces of text, the first signed "inscribed by Li Zuoji, district magistrate" and the second "inscribed by Cheng Zhiwang, school instructor":

> 予下車浹月，學博程君即以通庠推轂馬寅公茂才應求賢之詔，寅公遜不就。既而歲祲，首出儲糈佐予倡賑，境內翳桑之眾賴以存活。已匯請之台部諸公，勒石紀事矣。考邑乘，寅公之祖若父並號仁人善士，好義樂施，此其丕承先澤，彌廣作求，予深嘉之。因為之式其廬，以風邑俗，扁曰「世德堂」。知縣李作楫題。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

> 馬生采臣，宏才碩德，素隆月旦。前巡方衛及前邑令陳屢欲薦辟，力辭未就。茲康熙己酉冬，覃恩下詔求賢，檄傳瀨庠，通庠諸生復舉馬生懿行，列狀以聞，三上而馬生三辭，意欲樹幟文壇，以圖遠舉。予嘉其志，因顏其閭以表之，扁曰「鳳翔千仞」。司訓程之望題。
> — Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer*, juan 2

These are plaque inscriptions collected by the section, not the roster line: their first person and their ways of naming the man are the inscribers' own, and this entry reproduces them and marks their nature. The first calls him "Ma Yingong" and "Yingong", the second "the student Ma Caichen" and "the student Ma"; the entry never explains whether "Yingong" is a courtesy name, a style or an honorific, this entry decides nothing, and takes both pieces as belonging to this entry on the strength of where the section places them.

## Who the "I" of the inscriptions is: not stated by the entry

Both inscriptions speak in the first person — the first has "aided me in organising relief", "I greatly approve of him", "so I had his house decorated", the second "I approve of his intention", "so I had his gate inscribed". The entry never says who this "I" is. Only the signatures can be reasoned from: the first ends "inscribed by Li Zuoji, district magistrate" and the second "inscribed by Cheng Zhiwang, school instructor", so the "I" of the first should be the district magistrate who inscribed it and that of the second the school instructor who inscribed it. This entry reasons only to that level of office and does not point the "I" at a named man: a signature records who inscribed the plaque, and the gazetteer nowhere states that the "I" and the signatory must be the same person, so this entry decides nothing and cross-confirms the two inscriptions against each other for nothing.

## "Master Cheng the academic instructor" and "Cheng Zhiwang the school instructor" are this gazetteer's compiler

The first inscription says "Master Cheng the academic instructor" and the second's signature reads "Cheng Zhiwang the school instructor"; the two ways of naming differ, and this entry records both side by side without deciding which is his formal title. This person is the compiler of this gazetteer: this library's source card records the Kangxi *Lishui County Gazetteer* as revised by Liu Dengke and compiled by Cheng Zhiwang, Xie Wenyun, Wang Zhizao and others, and records Cheng Zhiwang as the county school instructor. This is the most direct record this library holds of the compiler's own activity in Lishui — that the man who pressed Ma Caichen to answer the edict, and who inscribed the second plaque, was the gazetteer's own compiler is the value of this passage beyond what it says about one man, and this entry states it. This library has no person entry for Cheng Zhiwang or Li Zuoji: the directory `content/lishui-renwu/persons/` was listed and no slug or id for either was found, so this entry declares no related link.

## Three refusals and the stored grain

What the gazetteer records of his refusal appears in three places: the roster line says "he refused and did not accept the summons"; the second inscription says an earlier circuit inspector and an earlier district magistrate surnamed Chen "repeatedly wished to recommend him and he pressed his refusal and did not accept", and that the students of the county school again listed his virtuous conduct, submitted it in a formal statement, "it was sent up three times and the student Ma refused three times"; the first inscription says "Yingong declined and did not accept". The three wordings and their counts differ (once, repeatedly, three times up and three refusals), and this entry records all of them side by side, choosing none and merging none into a single event. The second inscription also says he "intended to make his mark in literary circles in order to win a more distant reputation", which is the inscriber's account of his motive — a judgement, not a record of fact — and this entry reproduces it and marks its nature.

The relief matter comes from the first inscription: "then the year was bad with famine, and he took the lead in bringing out stored grain to aid me in organising relief, and the hungry of the region depended on it to stay alive. It has all been submitted to the officials of the higher boards, and carved in stone to record the matter." On this basis his coming forward with stored grain in a famine year is the only act the section records of him, and the tag "Famine" in this entry rests only on the phrase "the year was bad with famine" and this clause. The year of the famine is not recorded, and this entry infers it from neither "ninth year of Kangxi" nor "the winter of the jiyou year of Kangxi". The stone mentioned is not held or located by this library, and whether it survives is undetermined. The first inscription also says, consulting the county's older gazetteer, that "Yingong's father and grandfather were both called benevolent men and good gentlemen, fond of righteousness and glad to give", which is the inscriber's report of what he read in an earlier local gazetteer; this entry reproduces it, marks its nature, and supplies no names or careers for his forebears.

## Relation to Lishui

The gazetteer gives no native place. He enters the persons site with the relation "Recommended from Lishui", and the reasoning must be stated: what ties him to Lishui is that he was publicly recommended from Lishui to answer the edict — being named in the Recommendations examination of a Lishui gazetteer is itself the gazetteer's statement that he was recommended out of Lishui. This value is used only for this section, and differs from the others: the section records no birthplace, so "born in Lishui" is not available; he refused and never served and the section assigns him no office, so "official service" is not available; as for "active in Lishui" — unlike the other entries of this section, the two inscriptions do record matters of his in Lishui (the stored grain and relief, the inscriptions on his house and gate), making him the only man of the section for whom an act in Lishui is recorded, yet this library still uses "Recommended from Lishui", because the reason he stands in this section is the recommendation and the relief and the plaques are appended to that record. This entry states that difference in the body rather than replacing one value with the other. Both inscriptions speak of his house and his gate but name neither, and where he lived in Lishui cannot be decided from this library's material.

## Points of doubt

First, his birth and death years, his native place and his courtesy name are nowhere recorded; the "Yingong" of the first inscription is an unexplained form of address in this entry, and whether it is a courtesy name, a style or an honorific is not decided.

Second, the era value "Qing" here only records that the section places him in its "Our Dynasty" block. That grouping dates his recommendation, not his birth: it proves neither that he was born in the Qing, and "ninth year of Kangxi" is the year of the edict, from which this entry infers neither his birth, his age, nor any Common-Era year.

Third, two ways of dating coexist in this entry: the roster line gives "ninth year of Kangxi" and the second inscription gives "the winter of the jiyou year of Kangxi". This entry reproduces both, converts neither, and does not decide whether the stem-branch date and the era year name the same year.

Fourth, the counts of refusal are inconsistent: once in the roster line, "repeatedly" and "three times up, three refusals" in the second inscription, and "declined" in the first. These are recorded side by side, unresolved.

Fifth, the usage note on this section in this library's source card says the section gives dates in only two places, for Yang Yuanli and Shi Kexing, which does not match the "ninth year of Kangxi" plainly present in the verbatim "Our Dynasty" block of the same card; this contradiction is recorded side by side and the gazetteer text is not altered for it.

Sixth, the "I" of the inscriptions is not identified by the entry and can only be reasoned from the signatures to a district magistrate and a school instructor, never to a named man; the "an earlier district magistrate surnamed Chen" of the second inscription gives a surname only, and this library has no material to decide who it was.

Seventh, "academic instructor" and "school instructor" differ as designations and are recorded side by side without deciding which is formal; this library has no entry for Cheng Zhiwang or Li Zuoji, so no related link is declared.

Eighth, the year of the famine, the stone said to record it, and the location of the house and gate inscribed for him are nowhere recorded, and this library has no material to decide them.

Ninth, this entry rests on one roster line and the two inscriptions appended to it; searches across the other source layers of this library produced nothing that could add to him.
