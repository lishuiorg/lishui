## Listing

In its reply of 28 November 2023, the Lishui District Culture and Tourism Bureau states that Tai Village in Jingqiao, Luoshan in Hefeng, Yangjia Village in Sunjiaxiang and two other villages were included in the sixth batch of traditional villages of Jiangsu Province.

A notice published by the Nanjing Municipal Commission of Housing and Urban-Rural Development on 22 February 2023 records four villages for Lishui in the same batch, among them "Shangjie and Xiajie, Taicun Village, Jingqiao Town". The two sources disagree on the total for Lishui (five in the bureau's reply, four in the commission's list); both are set out here side by side and neither is used to rewrite the other, the formal list being authoritative as published by the provincial housing department. The official list names the settlements of the village as Shangjie and Xiajie within the administrative village of Taicun.

## Situation of the village

A report of Xinhua Daily Media Network of 17 June 2025 states that Tai Village lies on the border between Lishui and Gaochun and is an "ancient village of a thousand years". The report records that the Xiajie old street of the village "took shape in the Hongwu period of the Ming dynasty", that it is more than a kilometre long and retains on both sides more than ten old houses of grey brick and black tile, five of which are listed as "historic buildings of Nanjing", and that several wells of the Ming and Qing dynasties remain on the street. The era of this entry is given as Ming on the strength of the statement that the Xiajie old street took shape in the Hongwu period; that statement concerns the existing settlement and street, not the founding of the village.

## Legend and remains

The same report records that a memorial terrace to Yue Fei stands in a cultural gallery beside the old street, and states that, "according to the historical materials of Lishui", Tai Village is traditionally held to have been an important battlefield of Yue Fei's resistance against the Jin, where the Song army defeated the Jin in 1129 (the third year of Jianyan), and that remains traditionally attributed to his earthworks and watchtowers survive in the village.

These matters of Yue Fei, the Jin war and the "Yue city" come from local historical materials and village tradition, not from contemporaneous historical records; the entry keeps the wording "tradition" and separates them from the factual account.

A separate notice of the Civil Affairs Bureau of Lishui District, published for comment on 3 June 2026, lists a "site of Yue city" in Jingqiao Town among proposed entries for the fifth batch of the district's place-name cultural heritage. The notice attaches the site to the town without naming the village; whether it is the same as the Yue city described here is not stated in the sources used, and the two are set down side by side without a conclusion.

## Village scenery

The Xinhua report records that the village once had "eight views of Changshan" such as "spring water drawn at Shiquan" and "sitting in conversation at Jigu", and that ten new views have been laid out on the old themes. It also quotes a poem on the village's fields by Wu Jingzi, the Qing man of letters. Only two of the old eight views are named in the source, and they are not filled in here; the poem is quoted without title or source and is not used as evidence.

## Points of doubt

- The two sources give different totals for the Lishui villages of the sixth batch (five in the bureau's reply, four in the commission's list); they are presented side by side, and the formal list is authoritative as published by the provincial housing department.
- The report calls the village "of a thousand years" while also dating the old street to the Hongwu period; the two concern the age of the village and of the street respectively, and are kept apart without being used to corroborate each other. The founding date of the village is not established.
- The Yue Fei tradition and the earthworks rest on local historical materials and village tradition, with no contemporaneous record. The Qianlong gazetteer is quoted elsewhere as recording a "Yue city" with an inscription by Yue Fei, but only the catalogue entry of that gazetteer is held here, not its text, so it is not cited as gazetteer wording.
- The surname history, household numbers, population and origin of the village name are not recorded in the sources used here. Searches of the provincial gazetteers, academic literature and full-text classical sources held here (within the parts read of the Kangxi and Guangxu gazetteers) yielded no entry for the village.
