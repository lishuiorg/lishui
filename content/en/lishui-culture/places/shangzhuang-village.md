## Listing

A notice carried on the Nanjing municipal government website on 22 August 2025 states that the eighth batch of traditional villages of Jiangsu Province comprises seventy villages, five of them in Nanjing, among them "Shangzhuang, Shanggang Community, Honglan Subdistrict, Lishui District". The list was published by the Jiangsu Provincial Department of Housing and Urban-Rural Development.

A report of the Lishui District Media Centre dated 14 July 2025, during the period of public notice, states that Shangzhuang was on the notice list for the same batch. The report is a news item; the list is authoritative as published by the provincial department.

## Name and date

The district media centre report says that Shangzhuang "had already formed a village as early as the Ming dynasty" and gives its former name as "Shenxing village". A report of Xinhua Daily Media Network of 7 July 2025 likewise says its history "can be traced back to the Ming dynasty, evolving from 'Shenxing village'". The origin of the present name from "Shenxing village" rests on local media reporting and oral tradition; it is reproduced here and marked as such, and no precise founding year is given. The era of this entry is accordingly given as Ming.

## Ancestral halls and remains

The Xinhua Daily Media Network report states that two ancestral halls, the Fu and the Yong halls, attest to the lineage culture of the village. The Fu hall is said to have been built in 1735 and to carry a legend concerning Fu Yue, a worthy minister of the Shang dynasty, with Fu clansmen gathering to make offerings on the eighth day of the fourth lunar month each year. The Yong hall was built in the Qianlong period with funds raised by the lineage, in a layout of three bays, two kitchens and one courtyard.

The same report records traditional buildings including the Yong Mingfu house of 1942 and the Fu Chengsi house of 1953, forming a group with old cypresses and ancient wells. The district media centre adds that the Fu and Yong ancestors settled here and that the village once held more than five hundred households, and that the Fu and Yong halls, an inscription stele said to be nearly five hundred years old and ancient wells survive; the village adjoins the Fujiabian science park, neighbours Shuijing Village of Jingqiao Town and faces Putang Bridge, a nationally protected heritage site.

## Customs

The Xinhua Daily Media Network report records that the Ming-dynasty "chupusa" rite, in which villagers carry an image of a deity in procession and burn incense, continues to this day, and that traditional bamboo weaving once flourished. Whether the village holds other customs is not recorded in the sources used here.

## Points of doubt

- The formal publication notice of the eighth batch, with its document number, is not held here; the date of publication follows the municipal government website and the district media centre.
- The former name "Shenxing village", the 1735 date of the Fu hall, the Qianlong-period Yong hall and the two house dates all come from media reports, with no inscription or genealogy behind them, and are not treated as settled.
- The figures "more than five hundred households" and "nearly five hundred years" are the wording of a report, are approximate, and are not used for calculation.
- The early history of the village, its surname origins and a complete inventory of its surviving buildings are not recorded in the sources used here.
- Searches of the provincial gazetteers, academic literature and full-text classical sources held here (within the parts read of the Kangxi and Guangxu gazetteers) yielded no entry for Shangzhuang.
